Abstract Populations of seagrasses such as eelgrass ( Zostera marina ) have been declining worldwide within the last century. This loss has partly been attributed to mechanisms associated with increases in nutrient loading from anthropogenic sources. Furthermore, the systems are kept in a degraded state through feedback mechanisms caused by loss of the eelgrass habitat’s nutrient-related ecosystem functions. In this article, we reviewed the available published data and methods used for the quantification of nutrient-related ecosystem functions. We focused on three pathways of nutrient retention/export: (1) Assimilation in the eelgrass biomass, (2) nutrient burial and sedimentary stocks, and (3) microbially driven nutrient cycling.
