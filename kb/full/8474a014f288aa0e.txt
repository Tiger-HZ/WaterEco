Mineral-associated organic carbon (MAOC) is an important component of stable soil carbon pools, yet its distribution and controlling factors in karst ecosystems remain unclear due to strong soil heterogeneity. This study investigated the relationships between vegetation type, microbial communities, soil physicochemical properties, and MAOC amount in a karst watershed in Guizhou, China. Surface soils (0–20 cm) were collected from limestone-derived Leptosols under three vegetation types: grassland, arboreal forest, and shrubland. Soil physicochemical properties were analyzed in combination with high-throughput sequencing of bacterial 16S rRNA genes and fungal ITS regions.
