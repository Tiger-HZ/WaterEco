 Abstract The panicle initiation stage is critical for determining rice yield potential and grain quality formation. In a two-year field study, foliar application of ornithine to the japonica rice cultivar Ningxiangjing 9 increased grain yield by 8.54% and 3.95% in 2024 and 2025, respectively, compared with the control. The yield improvement was mainly associated with enhanced sink capacity, reflected by more grains on secondary branches and a higher 1000-grain weight. Ornithine treatment also improved grain quality by increasing brown rice, milled rice, and head milled rice rates, while reducing chalky rice rate and chalkiness degree. In addition, ornithine maintained higher SPAD values and promoted dry matter and non-structural carbohydrate accumulation during grain filling. Leaf transcriptomic analysis indicated upregulation of genes related to nitrogen assimilation, sugar transport, and panicle development. Metabolomic profiling of young panicles showed enrichment of flavonoid- and phenylpropanoid-related pathways, accompanied by the accumulation of naringenin, naringenin chalcone, pinobanksin, and chlorogenic acid. These findings provide field evidence for the potential use of foliar ornithine during reproductive development, although its underlying regulatory mechanisms require further functional validation.

 Introduction In rice production, improving grain yield without compromising grain quality remains a major agronomic challenge, particularly under increasing pressure from population growth, climate variability, and limited arable land (Fu et al. 2023 ; Yin et al. 2022 ). Modern cultivation strategies therefore increasingly emphasize stage-specific agronomic regulation, including optimized water and nutrient management, to enhance productivity while maintaining grain quality (Mo et al. 2019 ; Potcho et al. 2021 ). Foliar application provides a rapid and flexible approach for supplying nutrients or bioactive compounds during key developmental windows (Wang et al. 2023 ). In rice, previous studies have shown that foliar application of nitrogen, micronutrients, and plant growth regulators can improve nutrient use efficiency, photosynthetic performance, panicle development, and grain filling when applied at appropriate growth stages (Tuiwong et al. 2022 ; Wang et al. 2016 ). Beyond conventional nutrient supplementation, exogenous bioactive compounds applied during reproductive development may regulate physiological and metabolic processes associated with panicle formation, assimilate allocation, and grain quality formation(Pan et al. 2013 ; Pantoja-Benavides et al. 2022 ; Zhang et al. 2024 ). Panicle initiation marks the pivotal vegetative-to-reproductive transition in rice, triggering the reallocation of nutrients to young panicles and a metabolic shift toward coordinated carbon and nitrogen use (Giaume et al. 2023 ). This metabolic equilibrium supports spikelet development and survival by limiting spikelet degeneration and structuring panicle branching (Giaume et al. 2023 ). The process ultimately governs grain yield and quality, as the hormonal and metabolic status at this stage directly influences subsequent sugar transport, endosperm formation, and source–sink carbon partitioning (Duan et al. 2025 ; Wu et al. 2026 ; Yu et al. 2024 ). Evidence indicates that targeted nitrogen supply at panicle initiation promotes the development of primary and secondary rachis branches, reduces their degeneration, and thereby raises branch and spikelet survival rates, grains per panicle, and final yield (Ding et al. 2014 ; Ju et al. 2021 ). Similarly, foliar application of plant growth regulators such as 6-benzylaminopurine (6-BA) and brassinolide (BR) at this stage has been reported to enhance panicle elongation, branch and spikelet differentiation, and seed-setting rate (Pan et al. 2013 ; Pantoja-Benavides et al. 2022 ; Zhang et al. 2024 ). Collectively, these findings underscore panicle initiation as a crucial window for agronomic manipulation to concurrently improve rice yield and quality. Amino acids function both as essential signaling molecules in plant development and stress responses, and as the dominant form of long-distance nitrogen transport, primarily as glutamine, glutamate, and asparagine (Guo et al. 2021 ). Enhancing source-to-sink amino acid allocation is therefore a promising strategy to improve nitrogen use efficiency and grain yield (Perchlik and Tegeder 2017 ). Ornithine (Orn), a basic amino acid, exerts pleiotropic roles in plants. It is integral to core metabolic pathways, including polyamine biosynthesis and nitrogen metabolism (Gholami et al. 2013 ). Notably, ornithine δ-aminotransferase facilitates nitrogen remobilization, a process critical for spikelet development and grain filling in rice (Liu et al. 2018 ). In fragrant rice, ornithine also serves as a precursor of 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (2-AP), and exogenous application has been reported to enhance the accumulation of this key aroma compound in grains (Luo et al. 2020 ). These observations suggest that the agronomic potential of ornithine may extend beyond aroma regulation. However, current knowledge of exogenous ornithine application in rice remains limited, particularly regarding its use at panicle initiation under field conditions. Previous studies have mainly focused on fragrant rice and aroma-related traits, whereas its effects on early sink establishment, source physiological status, yield components, and milling or appearance quality remain insufficiently understood. To address this gap, we conducted a two-year field experiment using the japonica rice cultivar ‘Ningxiangjing 9’. Physiological measurements, leaf transcriptomic analysis, and young-panicle metabolomic profiling were combined to evaluate the effects of foliar ornithine application at panicle initiation on rice yield and grain quality, and to explore the tissue-specific responses associated with ornithine treatment. Materials and methods Experimental design A two-year field experiment was carried out in 2024 and 2025 at the Experimental Farm of Henan Agricultural University, Xinyang, Henan Province, China (32°30′N, 114°01′E), using the japonica rice cultivar ‘Ningxiangjing 9’(N9). N9 is an elite fragrant japonica cultivar bred by Nanjing Agricultural University. This cultivar was selected because of its favorable grain-quality characteristics and agronomic potential, and because our ongoing field studies aim to develop suitable cultivation practices. The experimental soil was a typical paddy soil with pH 6.40, total nitrogen of 1.00 g kg⁻¹, available phosphorus of 16.50 mg kg⁻¹, organic matter of 30.00 g kg⁻¹, and available potassium of 139.30 mg kg⁻¹. The field schedule differed slightly between the two years. In 2024, seeds were sown on 23 May, seedlings were transplanted on 15 June, and plants were harvested on 5 November. In 2025, sowing, transplanting, and harvesting were performed on 13 May, 5 June, and 5 November, respectively. In both seasons, seedlings were transplanted at a spacing of 27 cm × 15 cm. Before transplanting, compound fertilizer (N–P₂O₅–K₂O, 15%–4%–6%) was incorporated into the soil at 750 kg ha⁻¹ as basal fertilizer. Urea ( N  ≥ 46.0%) was applied at 180 kg ha⁻¹ 7 days after transplanting. Foliar treatments were imposed at the panicle initiation stage. Four treatments were included: distilled water control (CK), 1 g L⁻¹ ornithine (Orn), combined foliar fertilizer consisting of 10.00 g L⁻¹ urea, 3.00 g L⁻¹ potassium dihydrogen phosphate, and 0.10 mg L⁻¹ brassinolide (N10 + K + BR), and the same combined foliar fertilizer supplemented with 1 g L⁻¹ ornithine ((N10 + K + BR) + Orn). Ornithine was supplied as L-ornithine hydrochloride (Macklin, CAS No. 3184-13-2). The trial was arranged as a single-factor randomized complete block design with three biological replicates. Each treatment was assigned to three plots, giving a total of 12 plots, and each plot covered 6 m² (2 m × 3 m). Crop management, including irrigation, weed control, and pest and disease control, followed local high-yield rice production practices. Measurement of yield components and grain yield Yield-related traits were assessed at maturity. In each plot, the number of productive panicles was recorded from 40 continuous hills and used to estimate the mean panicle density. Ten hills showing uniform growth and panicle number close to the plot average were then sampled for component analysis. After sun drying, panicle length, grains per panicle, filled grains per panicle, seed-setting rate, and 1000-grain weight were measured. These traits were used to calculate theoretical yield. For actual grain yield, plants within a 2.00 m² harvest area in each plot were collected separately, mechanically threshed, sun dried to a stable moisture level, and weighed. Evaluation of grain quality Grain quality was analyzed after harvest using air-dried grains that had been stored at room temperature for one month. For each replicate, approximately 20.00 g of rough rice was dehulled with a laboratory rice sheller, and the brown rice rate was calculated from the ratio of brown rice weight to rough rice weight. Milled rice rate was determined using a Sinograin rice milling tester. Head rice was separated manually from the milled rice fraction and weighed to calculate the head rice rate. Chalky grain percentage and chalkiness degree were quantified from scanned grain images obtained with a CanoScan 5600 F scanner, followed by image-based analysis. Three biological replicates were analyzed for each treatment. Determination of leaf chlorophyll content Leaf chlorophyll status was evaluated using a portable SPAD meter (SPAD-502, Konica Minolta, Osaka, Japan). Chlorophyll levels (SPAD values) were determined in the flag leaf (FL), penultimate leaf (SLB), and antepenultimate leaf (TLB) during the grain-filling (14 days after flowering) and dough stages (30 days after flowering) across two experimental years. Leaf SPAD values were measured independently in both the 2024 and 2025 field experiments. Three independent field plots per treatment served as biological replicates. At each sampling time, five representative hills were measured within each plot as subsamples, and the measurements were sequentially averaged to obtain one plot-level value. Data from the two experimental years were analyzed separately. Measurements were conducted between 16:00 and 17:00 to reduce the influence of diurnal variation. The three readings from each leaf were averaged first, after which leaf-level, hill-level, and plot-level means were calculated sequentially. Determination of dry matter accumulation and non-structural carbohydrates (NSC) Dry matter accumulation was determined at maturity, three days before harvest. After the productive panicle number had been recorded, three hills were randomly collected from each plot. The sampled plants were divided into stem-sheath, leaf, and panicle fractions. Each fraction was placed in a separate paper bag, heated at 105 °C for 30 min to stop metabolic activity, and then dried at 80 °C until constant weight. Organ dry weight was recorded after drying. The dried materials were ground with a high-speed grinder, passed through a 100 μm sieve, and stored in sealed bags for subsequent carbohydrate analysis. Non-structural carbohydrate content was determined as the sum of soluble sugars and starch using the anthrone-sulfuric acid method (Ai et al. 2017 ). Briefly, 0.1 g of dried powder was used for extraction. Soluble sugars were extracted with 80% ethanol, whereas starch was extracted sequentially with 9.2 mol L⁻¹ and 4.6 mol L⁻¹ perchloric acid. After dilution and color development, absorbance was measured at 620 nm using a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (Thermo Evolution 201, Waltham, MA, USA). Soluble sugar and starch concentrations were calculated from standard curves, and their sum was multiplied by the corresponding organ dry weight to estimate non-structural carbohydrate accumulation per hill. Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses For transcriptome analysis, leaf samples were collected from the CK and Orn treatments at 6 h after foliar application in the 2025 field experiment. Three independent biological replicates were collected for each treatment. Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA), and RNA quality was evaluated with an Agilent Bioanalyzer. Qualified RNA samples were used to construct cDNA libraries, followed by paired-end 150 bp sequencing on the MGI platform by Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). After quality filtering with fastp, clean reads were mapped to the Oryza sativa ssp. japonica cv. Nipponbare reference genome Os-Nipponbare-Reference-IRGSP-1.0 (IRGSP-1.0) using HISAT2. The reference genome sequence and gene annotation files were obtained from the Rice Annotation Project Database (RAP-DB). Gene expression was quantified with featureCounts and normalized as FPKM. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between CK and Orn were identified using DESeq2 with thresholds of |log₂FoldChange| ≥ 1 and FDR < 0.05. Functional classification and pathway enrichment of DEGs were performed using GO and KEGG annotations. For metabolomic profiling, young panicles were collected from the CK and Orn treatments at 7 days after foliar application in the 2025 field experiment. Three independent biological replicates were collected for each treatment. Freeze-dried samples were ground and extracted with 70% methanol containing internal standards. Widely targeted metabolomic analysis was conducted by Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd. using an ExionLC™ AD UPLC system coupled with tandem mass spectrometry in multiple reaction monitoring mode. Metabolite annotation was performed according to MS/MS spectra, retention time, and the Metware Database. The metabolomic dataset was processed by removing unstable signals and imputing missing values. Principal component analysis and orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis were used to evaluate metabolic differences between treatments. Differentially accumulated metabolites were defined using VIP > 1 together with fold change ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5. KEGG and MetMap annotations were used to interpret the metabolic pathways associated with ornithine treatment. Statistical analysis Data were organized and preliminarily processed using Microsoft Excel. For agronomic, grain-quality, and physiological traits involving the four foliar treatments, differences among treatments within each experimental year were evaluated by analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Duncan’s multiple range test at a significance level of P  < 0.05. Data from the 2024 and 2025 field experiments were analyzed separately. For comparisons involving only two groups, statistical significance was assessed using an independent-samples t-test where appropriate. Data are presented as mean ± SD. Results Grain yield and yield components The results (Table  1 ) indicated that foliar application of ornithine significantly promoted rice yield formation. Orn treatment had a significant effect on grain number, 1000-grain weight and grain yield. Compared with the control, Orn treatment increased yield by 8.54% and 3.95% in 2024 and 2025, respectively. In 2024, Orn treatment increased grains per panicle and 1000-grain weight by 8.67% and 6.15%, respectively, compared to the control; in 2025, the increases were 14.77% and 11.06%, respectively. The number of grains on secondary branches was the key factor contributing to the change in grains per panicle, which increased by 15.68% and 23.53% in 2024 and 2025, respectively, relative to the control. These results collectively demonstrate that the foliar application of ornithine effectively enhances yield by synergistically promoting panicle development and grain filling. Rather, the response of the (N10 + K + BR) + Orn treatment suggests that Orn may have potential as a complementary component of an existing foliar management strategy. Grain quality traits Foliar application of ornithine significantly improved rice processing and appearance quality (Table  2 ). Regarding processing quality in 2024, Orn treatment increased brown rice rate, milled rice rate, and head milled rice rate by 9.27%, 8.76%, and 5.38%, respectively, compared with the control. The combined treatment (N10 + K + BR) + Orn also raised these three indices by 5.43%, 6.31%, and 9.15%, respectively, relative to (N10 + K + BR) alone. For appearance quality, Orn treatment reduced chalky rice rate and chalkiness degree by 27.98% and 37.83% compared with the control, while (N10 + K + BR) + Orn decreased them by 27.16% and 22.41% compared with (N10 + K + BR). The 2025 results further confirmed the quality-enhancing effect of Orn: brown rice rate, milled rice rate, and head milled rice rate were increased by 4.11%, 5.49%, and 12.71%, respectively, and chalky rice rate and chalkiness degree were reduced by 36.80% and 23.72% compared with the control. In the combined treatment, (N10 + K + BR) + Orn improved brown rice rate, milled rice rate, and head milled rice rate by 1.60%, 4.42%, and 3.53%, respectively, and lowered chalky rice rate and chalkiness degree by 30.30% and 29.96% relative to (N10 + K + BR). The two-year data consistently demonstrate that Orn stably and significantly enhances rice quality, particularly in head milled rice rate and appearance traits. Upregulation of key genes involved in glutathione metabolism and flavonoid biosynthesis (e.g., OsCHS1 , OsF3H ) (Fig.  4 E) suggested enhanced antioxidant capacity in leaves, which aligns with the significantly increased chlorophyll content (SPAD value) observed during the grain-filling and dough stages in the field (Lam et al. 2022 ; Wang et al. 2025 ). At the level of key functional genes, Orn treatment altered the expression of several genes previously associated with nitrogen metabolism, leaf functional traits, assimilate transport, and reproductive development (Fig.  4 F). Notably, expression of OsNR2 —a positive regulator of nitrogen uptake and use—and OsDHAR1 , which contributes to stay-green traits, was significantly altered (Gao et al. 2019 ; Kim et al. 2013 ). These transcriptional responses identify candidate processes potentially associated with the subsequent agronomic responses to Orn, but their causal roles require further functional validation. Metabolomic analysis To investigate the effects of foliar ornithine application on panicle metabolism, young panicles were collected at 7 days after treatment for metabolomic profiling. Principal component analysis (PCA) showed a clear separation between the ORN-treated group (TORN) and the control group (TCK), with PC1 explaining 46.51% of the total variance (Fig.  5 A). The biological replicates within each group clustered closely, indicating good repeatability of the metabolomic data. Based on the criteria of VIP > 1 and |log2FC| ≥ 1, a total of 268 differential metabolites (DEMs) were identified, including 145 upregulated and 123 downregulated metabolites (Fig.  5 B). The clustered heatmap further showed distinct metabolic differences between TORN and TCK, with many flavonoids, terpenoids, phenolic acids, and amino acids and their derivatives exhibiting relatively higher abundance in the TORN group (Fig.  5 C). KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that the DEMs were mainly enriched in flavonoid- and phenylpropanoid-related secondary metabolism, lipid metabolism, and antioxidant-related pathways (Fig.  5 D). The highly enriched pathways included flavonoid biosynthesis, flavone and flavonol biosynthesis, tricin O-glycosides biosynthesis, sphingolipid metabolism, and monoterpenoid biosynthesis. In addition, linoleic acid metabolism, alpha-linolenic acid metabolism, glutathione metabolism, and plant hormone signal transduction were also significantly enriched. Together, these results show that Orn treatment altered the metabolic profile of young panicles, particularly pathways associated with secondary metabolism, lipid metabolism, and cellular redox processes. In young panicles, the relative abundances of naringenin, naringenin chalcone, and pinobanksin increased markedly following Orn treatment (Fig.  6 C). Multiple flavonoids and their derivatives also accumulated to higher levels under Orn treatment, and chlorogenic acid increased approximately 2.46-fold. Because the transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses were performed in different organs, however, these observations should be interpreted as parallel tissue-specific responses rather than evidence that transcriptional changes in leaves directly regulated flavonoid accumulation in young panicles. Significance was calculated using a two-tailed Student’s t-test (* P  < 0.05, ** P  < 0.01) Discussion Panicle initiation is a key developmental window for sink establishment in rice, during which primary and secondary branches are differentiated and the potential spikelet number is largely determined. In this study, foliar application of ornithine at this stage increased grain yield in both experimental years, with the most stable contribution arising from the increase in grains on secondary branches (Table  1 ). Because secondary branch development is highly sensitive to plant nutritional and physiological status during panicle differentiation, these results indicate that Orn treatment was favorable for sink establishment during early reproductive development (Hu et al. 2024 ; Wu et al. 2026 ). This response is agronomically relevant for japonica rice, in which grain number per panicle is an important component of yield potential. The yield response to Orn was accompanied by changes in several source-related physiological traits. Orn-treated plants maintained higher SPAD values and showed greater dry matter and non-structural carbohydrate (NSC) accumulation during reproductive growth (Figs.  1 , 2 and 3 ). These results indicate that Orn treatment was associated with improved leaf chlorophyll status and greater biomass and carbohydrate accumulation. However, because SPAD values provide only an indirect estimate of leaf chlorophyll status, the higher SPAD values observed under Orn treatment cannot be interpreted as direct evidence of enhanced photosynthetic performance. Future studies combining SPAD measurements with gas-exchange and chlorophyll-fluorescence analyses will be required to determine whether Orn directly affects photosynthesis. Carbohydrate availability and sugar transport from source tissues to developing grains are important for seed development and grain filling in rice (Li et al. 2022 ; Duan et al. 2025 ). In the present study, the increases in secondary-branch grain number and 1000-grain weight occurred together with greater dry matter and NSC accumulation, suggesting coordinated changes in source- and sink-related traits following Orn treatment. Nevertheless, because assimilate transport from leaves to panicles was not directly measured, these results should not be interpreted as direct evidence that Orn enhanced source-to-sink carbohydrate translocation. Another important finding is that Orn application improved grain quality while increasing yield. Brown rice rate, milled rice rate, and head rice rate were increased, whereas chalky grain percentage and chalkiness degree were reduced (Table  2 ). Chalkiness is closely associated with abnormal starch accumulation and insufficient or unstable assimilate supply during endosperm development (Zhou et al. 2025 ). The reduction in chalkiness under Orn treatment therefore suggests a more stable grain-filling process. Together with the increases in dry matter and NSC accumulation, these results indicate that Orn may enhance the efficiency of assimilate production and utilization during reproductive growth, thereby contributing to both higher yield and better milling and appearance quality. The transcriptomic and metabolomic results provide tissue-specific evidence supporting these physiological responses. In leaves, Orn treatment induced the expression of genes associated with nitrogen assimilation, assimilate transport, and reproductive development, including OsNR2 , OsSWEET6b , OsSPL13 , OsDREB1C , and OsMYB6 (Gao et al. 2019 , 2020 ; Si et al. 2016 ; Wei et al. 2022 ). Several genes previously linked to grain filling, grain weight, or panicle-related traits, such as OsSM1 , OsMKKK62/55 , OsJAZ11 , and OsABCG18 , were also upregulated (Li et al. 2022 ; Liu et al. 2021 ; Mehra et al. 2022 ; Xue et al. 2016 ; Zhao et al. 2019 ). In young panicles, metabolomic profiling showed that Orn treatment altered flavonoid, phenylpropanoid, lipid, and glutathione-related pathways, with increased accumulation of metabolites such as naringenin, naringenin chalcone, pinobanksin, and chlorogenic acid (Beasley et al. 2019 ; Elangovan 2024 ; Li et al. 2020 ; Niggeweg et al. 2004 ; Yildiztugay et al. 2020 ; Zang et al. 2022 ). These metabolic changes may reflect an improved redox and developmental status in young panicles, which could be beneficial for spikelet differentiation and subsequent grain filling (Fig.  7 ). However, because the transcriptome and metabolome analyses were performed using different organs, these omics results should be interpreted as complementary tissue-specific responses rather than direct evidence for a single integrated regulatory pathway. Although Orn has specific biochemical links to nitrogen remobilization, polyamine metabolism, and 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline biosynthesis, the present study did not include other amino acids as comparative treatments. Therefore, the observed agronomic and molecular responses cannot be attributed exclusively to Orn. Other amino acids, such as arginine or methionine, may also affect carbon–nitrogen metabolism or reproductive development following foliar application. Comparative studies using different amino acids at equivalent concentrations will be necessary to distinguish Orn-specific responses from more general effects of amino acid supplementation. Fig. 7 Full size image Possible Mechanistic Diagram of Improved Rice Yield and Quality by Foliar Application of Orn. Proposed model illustrating how foliar Orn application at the panicle initiation stage improves yield and grain quality in rice. Orn treatment may enhance antioxidant defense, stay-green performance, carbon–nitrogen metabolism, and NSC translocation in leaves, while promoting flavonoid-, lipid-, and glutathione-related metabolism in panicles. These coordinated source–sink responses may support panicle development and grain filling, thereby increasing secondary branches, grain number, and 1000-grain weight and reducing chalkiness From an agronomic perspective, the present study extends the potential use of Orn beyond aroma regulation. Previous studies have mainly emphasized the role of Orn in 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline biosynthesis and aroma formation in fragrant rice (Dai et al. 2024 ; Luo et al. 2020 ). Here, foliar Orn application at panicle initiation improved secondary-branch grain formation, 1000-grain weight, milling quality, and chalkiness-related traits in ‘Ningxiangjing 9’, indicating that Orn may also function as a reproductive-stage regulator of yield and grain quality (Zhou et al. 2022 ). Moreover, the combined treatment of (N10 + K + BR) + Orn produced favorable agronomic responses, suggesting that Orn can be incorporated into an existing foliar fertilization strategy for field production. However, the present study did not assess application costs, optimal dosage across environments, economic returns, or large-scale field performance. Its practical value therefore requires further evaluation in multi-cultivar and multi-environment field trials. Multi-environment and multi-cultivar trials are therefore needed to evaluate the stability of Orn responses. In addition, functional validation of the candidate genes identified here and direct measurement of aroma-related metabolites, especially 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (2-AP), will be necessary to clarify the broader regulatory role of Orn in rice reproductive development. Conclusion Foliar Orn application at panicle initiation improved grain yield and grain quality in ‘Ningxiangjing 9’ across two field seasons. The yield response was mainly associated with increased grain number on secondary branches and higher 1000-grain weight, while the quality response was characterized by improved milling quality and reduced chalkiness. Orn treatment was also accompanied by higher SPAD values and greater dry matter and NSC accumulation. Leaf transcriptomic and young-panicle metabolomic analyses further revealed distinct tissue-specific responses associated with Orn treatment. Together, these results suggest coordinated changes in source- and sink-related traits, although the underlying causal relationships remain to be established. The additional yield response observed when Orn was incorporated into the N10 + K + BR treatment suggests that Orn may have potential as a complementary foliar input. Further multi-cultivar and multi-environment studies, together with functional validation, are needed to evaluate the generality and practical value of this response (Fig.  7 ).
