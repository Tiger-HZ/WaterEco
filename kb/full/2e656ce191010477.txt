River water quality monitoring networks (WQMNs) are designed to meet specific objectives within given environmental settings, but the design constraints can be generalized. We frame WQMN optimization around four high-level design levers: sampling density (total sampling effort), spatial versus temporal allocation, monitoring site prioritization (evenness of sampling effort), and stream order allocation. We first characterize how these levers are positioned in current monitoring by analyzing a large-sample dataset for the contiguous United States. We then conduct a synthetic subsampling experiment, using a stochastic modelling approach for stream networks with different landscape configurations, to test how the observed designs, alongside alternatives, perform against different objectives.
