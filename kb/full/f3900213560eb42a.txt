Abstract Increasing helminth drug resistance necessitates novel anthelmintic agents. Lichens, historically used to treat parasitic infections, represent a promising natural resource. This study evaluated eight lichen extracts and 17 metabolites against the model organism Caenorhabditis elegans (wild-type strain N2). Extracts of Hypogymnia physodes, Nephroma arcticum, and Stereocaulon grande showed significant efficacy, while Sphaerophorus fragilis and Ophioparma ventosa caused 100% nematode mortality on day 11 (at 25 and 100 μg/mL, respectively). Chemical workup of S. fragilis yielded squamatic acid, sphaerophorin, and its monocyclic degradation products.
