Lobster shell waste is an underutilized marine by-product with strong potential for conversion into value-added alternative products such as nanofertilizers due to its unique composition. In this study, a modified hydrothermal method was used to synthesize carbon nanoparticles (CNPs) from lobster shell waste at 220 °C, with autoclave reaction times of 6, 12, 24, 36, and 48 h to evaluate the impact of processing time. The resulting CNPs were comprehensively characterized for their morphological and chemical properties using Fourier Transform Infrared Spectrometry (FTIR), X–Ray Diffraction (XRD), Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) analysis, Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM), zeta potential, hydrodynamic particle size, and mineral composition to assess their suitability as carbon nanofertilizers (CNFs). CNPs synthesized at 12 h exhibited pronounced FTIR and XRD peaks, with functional groups similar to chitin and chitosan and enhanced crystallinity. Highest BET specific surface area recorded was 51.12 m 2 /g. SEM and TEM imaging confirmed the breakdown of shell structure, yielding CNPs in the 1–10 nm range. Zeta potential values ranged from −13.50 mV to −20.40 mV. Increasing reaction time led to a reduction in total N, K, Mg, and S contents, while C, P, and Ca contents increased, exhibiting the potential of these CNPs as CNFs. Overall, hydrothermal treatment for 12 h reaction at 220 °C was identified as the optimal condition for producing CNFs from lobster shell waste.
