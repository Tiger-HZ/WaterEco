In Caenorhabditis elegans , a brief, sublethal heat shock (HS) induces a hormetic response that increases resistance to subsequent stress and extends lifespan. These benefits require hlh-30 , the ortholog of mammalian transcription factor EB ( TFEB ). Although HS induces robust HLH-30 nuclear translocation, how this response is regulated remains poorly understood. Nutrient- and energy-sensing pathways, including mTORC1 and AMPK, regulate HLH-30/TFEB subcellular localization under other physiological conditions, but whether they mediate its nuclear translocation during HS is unknown.
