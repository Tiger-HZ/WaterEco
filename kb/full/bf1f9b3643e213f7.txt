Abstract The increasing focus on ecosystem restoration in European environmental policy highlights the need for reliable methods to quantify the nature conservation value (NCV) of aquatic habitats. Current Water Framework Directive (WFD) metrics assess ecological quality mainly through the phytoplankton community composition and abundance. However, these approaches do not reflect properly the real conservation value of the habitats, because they overlook the contribution of rare and unique algal species. Currently, the method proposed by Coesel is the only microalgae-based framework explicitly incorporating rarity, using desmid assemblages to assess the conservation value of aquatic habitats.
