This study examined the effects of salinity on survival in the early life stages (planulae, polyps, and ephyrae) of Aurelia coerulea and on asexual reproduction (budding and strobilation) in polyps. Short-term tolerance experiments, gradual low-salinity exposure experiments, and a 90-day reproduction experiment were conducted across salinities of 0–33 psu. Planulae showed 100% survival at salinities ≥ 21 psu, but metamorphosis into polyps occurred only at salinities ≥ 25 psu. Polyps survived for 48 h at salinities ≥ 17 psu, but normal tentacle morphology and contraction responses were maintained only at salinities ≥ 25 psu.
