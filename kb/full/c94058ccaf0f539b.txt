We begin the discussion by reviewing the intersection of gender and vulnerability to extreme weather events attendant to climate change. We argue that such vulnerability is a relational phenomenon –i.e., that the disproportionate burden of extreme weather on women and female-heads-of-households emerges from processes of social disenfranchisement that emerge in the context of the web of interrelationships found in a place. But the relational view also holds the promise of intervention by turning social patterns around from exclusion to empowerment. We discuss how women on the frontlines, bearing the greatest burdens of extreme weather, can lead the way to building knowledge and initiatives that respond squarely to vulnerabilities found in each context. We end with a speculative discussion on how gender-led movements in the disaster preparedness field can have important implications for disaster risk analysis, communication, and planning.
