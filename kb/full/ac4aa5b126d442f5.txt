can mitigate the risk of 
eutrophication in large shallow 
lakes

& Hao Liu1,5
The role of aquatic vegetation restoration in mitigating lake eutrophication is well-recognized, yet the 
underlying nutrient dynamics-vegetation response relationship remains poorly quantified, hindering 
a mechanistic understanding of grass-algae regime shifts. To address this, the Wetland Eco-dynamic 
Model for Submerged Plants (WET) was developed to mechanistically simulate ecological processes 
within the water–sediment-plant continuum by coupling hydrodynamics, nutrient cycling, and plant 
growth modules. Applied to Lake Taihu (2005–2019), the model effectively simulated submerged 
plant responses to hydrological and physicochemical parameters, with simulated vegetation cover 
validation achieving an R2 of 0.68. A multi-scale analysis further identified a significant positive 
correlation between N:P ratios and aquatic vegetation cover. The model was subsequently employed 
to simulate vegetation dynamics under 121 distinct nutrient regulation scenarios, revealing that 
aquatic vegetation cover exerts a stronger influence on total phosphorus than on total nitrogen, a 
relationship characterized by steady-state nonlinearity. The findings demonstrate that under stable 
meteorological, hydrological, and pollution load conditions, managing aquatic vegetation is an 
effective eutrophication mitigation strategy for large, shallow lakes. Specifically for the phosphorus-
limited Lake Taihu, simulations indicate that increasing vegetation coverage to 27.7% would shift 
its trophic status from eutrophic to oligotrophic-mesotrophic. By systematically quantifying these 
nonlinear responses, this study provides operational thresholds and a scientific basis for lake ecological 
management, overcoming a key limitation of traditional qualitative descriptions.

Eutrophication prevention and control remain a major challenge for global aquatic environmental management1. 
This challenge is intensified by increased nutrient loading from anthropogenic activities and climate-induced 
alterations in hydrodynamics, which have exacerbated the degradation of lake ecosystems 2,3. Moreover, 
controlling pollutant loads alone has proven insufficient for restoring ecological function in many degraded 
lakes4. Consequently, ecosystems often remain impaired by persistent issues such as wetland degradation, 
eutrophication, and salinization5–8. These impacts pose significant threats to aquatic vegetation, water quality, 
and public health 8, thereby compounding the challenges of eutrophication management. This is particularly 
evident in China, where lacustrine ecosystems have undergone severe degradation due to the combined effects 
of anthropogenic pressures and natural drivers9. Over a recent decade, the proportion of assessed lakes classified 
as moderately eutrophic increased from 31.3 to 42.7%  10. A rapid shift from mesotrophic to mildly eutrophic 
status has also been observed in key water bodies, including major tributaries like the Hanjiang River and large 
reservoirs such as the Three Gorges Reservoir, particularly within their bays and estuaries 10. Nutrient-driven

eutrophication in Lake Taihu emerged in the 1980s, marked by the expansion of cyanobacterial blooms from 
Meiliang Bay to the broader northwestern region 11. Despite long-standing recognition of the issue, harmful 
cyanobacterial blooms persist, and anaerobic ‘black water’ events recur annually each summer 12,13. Given that 
N:P ratios are integrative indicators of nutrient limitation and eutrophic status 8,14, a refined understanding 
of their interaction with biotic components is critical. This study aims to provide this understanding, thereby 
offering a scientific foundation for the effective management of eutrophication in large, shallow lakes.
Several studies have shown that shallow lakes can shift from a turbid, phytoplankton-dominated regime to a 
clear-water, macrophyte-dominated regime, indicating a transition from eutrophic to oligotrophic conditions15. 
Submerged macrophyte restoration has been widely recognized as an effective strategy for restoring eutrophic 
aquatic systems16. The distribution and coverage of aquatic vegetation in Taihu Lake exhibited two distinct 
phases from 1980 to 2017: a gradual expansion followed by a rapid decline. The distribution area reached 
its peak between 1980 and 2014, followed by a sharp decrease from 2015 to 2017, declining to less than 50 
km2. Aquatic vegetation is mainly concentrated in East Lake, the East Area, and Gong Bay, with submerged 
species constituting the dominant vegetation type 17. Aquatic vegetation plays a critical role in mitigating lake 
eutrophication through multiple mechanisms, such as suppressing algal growth (Bakker et al., 2010), enhancing 
dissolved oxygen levels15, stabilizing aquatic ecosystems16, and enhancing physical habitat conditions. Beyond 
these direct interactions, the density and distribution of aquatic vegetation, along with their impact on altering 
hydrodynamics, also play a crucial role in modifying dissolved oxygen and carbon levels in water, thereby 
influencing biogeochemical dynamics 2,3. A substantial body of research has demonstrated that aquatic plants 
are key contributors to eutrophication mitigation in lakes 18,19. Restoring a balanced macrophyte–algae aquatic 
ecosystem has become a central focus of ecological restoration efforts in Taihu Lake 20. The rehabilitation of 
eutrophic water bodies using aquatic ecological methods offers a cost-effective and efficient approach to 
wastewater treatment and is increasingly adopted worldwide to enhance water quality. Lakes dominated by 
aquatic plants generally exhibit clear water and high transparency under low-nutrient conditions 21. Notably, 
clear-water conditions can be maintained in certain lakes through internal regulatory mechanisms, even 
under elevated nutrient inputs 22,23. Conversely, with increasing nutrient inputs, phytoplankton-dominated 
algal blooms and turbid conditions commonly emerge24. Large-scale aquatic vegetation, including submerged, 
emergent, and floating-leaf plants, plays a vital role in supporting the structural and functional integrity of 
lake ecosystems25. Large aquatic vegetation in lakes, particularly submerged species, is widely recognized as an 
effective means of managing eutrophication in ecological restoration efforts. As primary producers in aquatic 
ecosystems, submerged plants enhance water quality and reduce eutrophication in aquatic ecosystems 26. The 
transition between clear and turbid water states is primarily driven by competition between submerged plants 
and phytoplankton. Therefore, the adoption of water ecological restoration methods can better prioritize natural 
recovery and long-term benefits, overcoming the limitations of short-term solutions and the potential negative 
impacts of traditional governance approaches. It represents a more sustainable and environmentally friendly 
governance approach. This approach enhances the self-purification capacity of water bodies, reduces secondary 
pollution, lowers treatment costs, and demonstrates strong adaptability, aligning with the concept of ecological 
civilization construction. This paper models the ecological mechanisms of submerged plants in Taihu Lake, 
quantifying the effects of management measures and providing a scientific basis for formulating specific goals to 
restore the ecological functions of the lake and restore its clear water state.
Additionally, simulating aquatic vegetation requires comprehensive environmental and biological data, 
which necessitate complex statistical analysis and model development. Experimental methods often encounter 
challenges in realistically replicating the evolution of lake ecosystems, while the lack of effective mathematical 
models hinders the accurate prediction and simulation of the grass-algae transition process. Experimental 
methods often concentrate on specific species, such as subfossil mosquitoes, zoobenthos, and zooplankton, 
leading to a highly complex and labor-intensive workload 27,28. The impact of aquatic plants on eutrophication 
can be assessed through experiments involving these plants 29. These experiments can be conducted indoors or 
in small lake areas, which facilitates ease of implementation and reproducibility. However, these experiments 
are time-intensive and spatially constrained, thereby limiting their representativeness of the entire site and 
watershed30. Statistical analysis methods are commonly used to identify trends in large datasets over extended 
periods. These methods are primarily utilized to determine thresholds. However, statistical approaches that do 
not incorporate mechanistic models to represent the underlying mechanisms of individual components may 
be susceptible to error propagation during data processing 31. In contrast to statistical methods, eco-dynamic 
models can predict changes in lake ecosystems and aquatic vegetation using mechanistic modeling. Several 
mathematical models are employed to elucidate the mechanisms underlying changes in aquatic ecosystems 32. 
The Vollenweider model was the first to establish the relationship between phosphorus nutrient loading and 
the eutrophic status of lakes 33. The PCLake (Princeton Lake Model), an integrated model of complex aquatic 
ecosystems, was used to simulate state changes with delayed effects in bifurcation analyses of a dataset 
comprising 43 shallow temperate lakes34,35. It has been widely used in studies of regime change, climate change, 
and submerged plant restoration36,37. The WET model (Water Ecosystems Tool) integrates the hydrodynamic 
GOTM (General Ocean Turbulence Model) with the PCLake model. Compared to the standalone PCLake 
model, this coupled model more accurately simulates the gradual response of ecosystems to changes in external 
forcing. It is particularly useful for studying the responses and changes in aquatic vegetation in shallow lakes, 
especially in scenario simulations38,39.
However, the quantitative mechanisms through which aquatic vegetation regulates the clear-turbid state 
transition in large, shallow lakes remain poorly understood, particularly the ecological response processes to 
coupled environmental stressors. This knowledge gap impedes the development of precise eutrophication control 
strategies based on vegetation restoration. To address this, a process-based eco-dynamic model for submerged 
aquatic vegetation (the WET model) was developed. Integrating long-term observational data, the model couples

the identification of key ecological thresholds and provides a theoretical and methodological foundation for 
transitioning from qualitative description to quantitative prediction in the ecological restoration of large, shallow

patterns of aquatic vegetation cover in Lake Taihu over a 15-year period and identify its key drivers using 
long-term time-series data; (2) to establish the relationship between hydrological/physicochemical drivers and

(3) to quantify the nonlinear response and control thresholds of aquatic vegetation cover to eutrophication in 
large, shallow lakes by evaluating N:P ratio-based indicators across multiple eutrophication scenarios. These 
objectives are achieved through scenario-based modeling of the Lake Taihu ecosystem, elucidating the critical 
role of vegetation cover in eutrophication dynamics. The findings provide a scientific foundation for developing 
targeted vegetation restoration strategies to control eutrophication. Furthermore, they offer critical insights into 
eutrophication recovery management and elucidate the mechanisms governing the macrophyte-to-algae regime 
shift in large, shallow eutrophic lakes.
Materials and methods
Study area
Taihu Lake, the third-largest freshwater lake in China, has an average depth of 1.9 m. It is classified as a typical 
shallow lake, covering an area of 2338 km 2 and supporting a basin population of approximately 60 million 20. 
Taihu Lake, located at the junction of southern Jiangsu Province and northern Zhejiang Province in China, 
lies between latitudes 30°55′40″–31°32′58″N and longitudes 119°52′32″–120°36′10″E. Taihu Lake is located in 
the heart of the Y angtze River Delta, the most industrialized and urbanized region in China26. Since the 1980s, 
cyanobacterial blooms have posed a significant environmental challenge in Taihu Lake40. Human activities are the 
primary drivers of accelerated water eutrophication, with agricultural non-point source pollution representing 
the largest proportion, while point-source discharges from industrial and domestic wastewater collectively 
elevate nitrogen and phosphorus concentrations41. Increased eutrophication and degradation of the underwater 
environment in Taihu Lake have led to the loss of vegetation, thereby disrupting the balance of the lake’s aquatic 
ecosystem and contributing to its continued degradation37,42. The highest density of aquatic vegetation is found 
in the eastern region of Taihu Lake, predominantly composed of submerged macrophytes, while the southern 
region supports a mixed community of submerged and floating-leaf macrophytes. In contrast, aquatic vegetation 
has largely disappeared from the open-water zones of the central and northwestern regions17.
Data sources and processing
The historical meteorological data for this study were obtained from the Resource Service System (cern.ac.cn), 
with measurements collected at the Taihu Lake Station (Wuxi Headquarters), including daily records of air 
temperature, dew point temperature, barometric pressure, wind speed, and cloud cover. Additionally, historical 
flow data were obtained from the Taihu Lake Basin Management Bureau under the Ministry of Water Resources 
(MWR), which provided monthly rainfall and discharge data. Water quality data covering the period from 2005 
to 2019 were also collected. This dataset includes 15 years of monthly data from 32 sampling points in Taihu 
Lake, facilitating continuous monitoring of 27 ecological indicators. Key indicators include water temperature, 
dissolved oxygen, total nitrogen, total phosphorus, and chlorophyll-a. The distribution of sampling points is 
shown in Fig. 1c, and the samples were analyzed in accordance with the “Technical Specification Requirements 
for Surface Water and Wastewater Monitoring” (People’s Republic of China Ministry of Environmental 
Protection, 2002). For satellite data, observations were made in February, May, August, and November of 
each year from 2005 to 2019. The remotely sensed data were acquired from Landsat TM/ETM+/OLI surface 
reflectance datasets. Field data on the dry weight of aquatic plants were collected biannually from 2000 to 2019 
to validate the remotely sensed data.
WET model principles
The WET (Water Ecosystems Tool) model consists of six key modules that work together to simulate 
various aquatic processes. These modules include the abiotic-water, abiotic-sediment, phytoplankton-water, 
phytoplankton-sediment, macrophytes, and auxiliary modules43. In addition to these core modules, the model 
incorporates essential physicochemical processes, such as sedimentation, deposition, resuspension, diffusion, 
adsorption, mineralization, and burial, which are crucial for accurately simulating aquatic interactions. Primary 
producers, such as macrophytes and phytoplankton, are central to the model’s simulations. Oxygen dynamics, 
such as oxygen produced by photosynthesis and consumed by mineralization in both the water column and 
sediment, are also considered, providing a comprehensive understanding of the ecological balance in aquatic 
environments44.
The aquatic plant module primarily simulates submerged plants and incorporates key processes such as 
photosynthesis, nutrient uptake, respiration, excretion, mortality, migration, and seasonal growth and emergence 
cycles. These processes are essential for accurately modeling the life cycle and ecological interactions of aquatic 
plants within the ecosystem43. Figure 2 presents a conceptual diagram of the WET model, adapted from Hu et 
al.44. The left side shows the model’s input data, while the right side presents the primary output data. The inflow 
of phosphate, dissolved and particulate organic phosphorus, ammoniacal nitrogen, dissolved and particulate 
organic nitrogen, and nitrate nitrogen into the lake acts as a boundary condition. The output data include key 
indicators such as dissolved oxygen, lake water quality, aquatic plant cover, and water temperature39.

Eutrophication scenario construction in Taihu Lake
To assess the impact of external nutrient loading on aquatic ecosystems, this study used the years 2015–2019 as a 
baseline for analyzing year-to-year changes in the modeled scenarios. Scenario changes were made by adjusting 
the boundary and input conditions for the baseline years. The N:P ratios can, to some extent, characterize the 
eutrophic status of lakes. As the chlorophyll-a concentration increases, the N:P ratio significantly decreases. 
The corresponding mean N:P ratios are 54.1, 26.8, and 18.1 when the lake is in oligotrophic, eutrophic, and 
hyper-eutrophic states, respectively 45. These values were used as boundary conditions to simulate different 
eutrophication states.
To investigate the ecosystem response to nutrient changes, two distinct regulatory scenarios were developed: 
one for phosphorus regulation and one for nitrogen regulation. Scenario boundaries were defined using the 
observed minimum and maximum concentrations of TN, TP and the resulting N:P ratios from historical 
monitoring data at Lake Taihu (2005–2019). For the P-regulation scenario, TP concentration was varied from 
0.052 to 0.332 mg/L in 0.005 mg/L increments, while TN was held constant at its historical average (2.859 mg/L). 
This generated 56 scenarios with N:P ratios ranging from 9 to 57. For the N-regulation scenario, TN was varied 
from 1.527 to 7.927 mg/L in 0.1 mg/L increments, with TP fixed at its historical average (0.129 mg/L), yielding 
65 scenarios with N:P ratios between 12 and 61. All scenarios were simulated over a 60-year period (2020–2089) 
to ensure the lake ecosystem reached a stable, dynamic equilibrium. Model outputs for aquatic plant cover and 
nutrient concentrations were evaluated based on summer averages, as this season best represents peak biological 
activity and the critical conditions for regime shifts. The N:P ratios served as a key integrative indicator to 
simulate eutrophication status under varying levels of aquatic plant cover, thereby providing a quantitative basis 
for management recommendations (Table 1).
Results
Characterization of spatial and temporal changes in nutrients and aquatic vegetation cover 
in Taihu Lake
Analyzing long-term trends in N:P ratios and aquatic plant cover is crucial for understanding the ecohydrological 
dynamics of Lake Taihu. The average interannual N:P ratio in Lake Taihu exhibited an overall decreasing trend 
from 2000 to 2019. This period began with an increase from 19.5 in 2000 to a peak in 2003, followed by a decline 
until 2010. A secondary peak then occurred before ratios declined again through 2019. This overall decline in 
the N:P ratio suggests a general deterioration in trophic status, consistent with the association of lower N:P 
ratios with more severe eutrophication in this system. Similarly, aquatic plant cover, which is predominantly

submerged vegetation, displayed a decreasing trend. A particularly sharp decline commenced in 2015. This 
abrupt decline coincided with the initiation of centralized, high-intensity mechanical salvage operations. These 
operations directly removed aboveground biomass and fragmented rhizome networks, thereby impairing 
regenerative capacity. Concurrently, the repeated disturbance reduced sediment seed banks and increased water 
turbidity, which further inhibited seedling establishment and vegetative regrowth17. A highly significant positive 
correlation was identified between N:P ratios and aquatic plant cover in Lake Taihu (p < 0.001), reflecting 
their synchronous temporal dynamics. These correlated long-term trends are essential for understanding the 
ecohydrological dynamics of Lake Taihu. The analysis confirmed a significant decreasing trend in the average 
interannual N:P ratio over the study period (Fig. 3).
The spatial patterns of aquatic plants and N:P ratios in Taihu Lake were analyzed using data from 30 
monitoring points for aquatic plant dry weight and 32 hydrological monitoring stations. The distribution patterns 
of both variables across the divisions of Taihu Lake were examined. The spatial distribution of aquatic plant dry 
weight is shown in Fig. 4a, with the highest dry weight found in the East Lake and East Area. The concentration 
decreases from the southeast to the northwest. The N:P ratios of Taihu Lake, shown in Fig. 4b, exhibit a similar 
spatial trend, increasing from the southwest to the east. The N:P ratios are higher in the eastern part of the lake 
and lower in the western part. The spatial trends of both variables generally follow the same pattern from the 
Southwest Area to the Eastern Area, showing a decreasing trend as illustrated in Fig.  4c. The average values of 
sampling points in each region were calculated to analyze the N:P ratios and dry weight of aquatic plants in the 
Southwestern Area, Gong Bay, Eastern Area, and East Lake. The concentrations of both indicators were highest 
in the Eastern Area. Both indicators exhibited a linear increasing trend from the Southwestern Area to the 
Eastern Area, demonstrating similar spatial changes.
Analysis of spatiotemporal patterns revealed a consistent coupling between N:P ratios and aquatic plant 
biomass in Lake Taihu. This coupling indicates that aquatic vegetation is a significant driver of N:P ratios, which 
in turn governs the eutrophication status of the lake.

Discussion
Temporal and spatial patterns of changes in nutrients and aquatic vegetation in large 
shallow lakes and their impact relationships
The spatiotemporal dynamics of aquatic vegetation cover and N:P ratios in Taihu Lake exhibit remarkable 
consistency. A severe cyanobacterial bloom occurred in Taihu Lake in 2007, followed by repeated bloom-
induced eutrophication, with N:P ratios gradually decreasing 47. Data analysis indicates that the aquatic 
vegetation cover in Taihu Lake exhibited an overall decreasing trend with interannual variations. By 2015, the 
cover had been reduced to about 5%, consistent with trends observed in remote sensing data from Taihu Lake 
between 1980 and 2017. As shown in Fig. 4b, the N:P ratios in Taihu Lake exhibited a decreasing trend from east 
to west48. Additionally, the water quality in the western part of the lake was more polluted than in the eastern 
part, consistent with findings from previous studies. The injection of large nutrient loads into the northwestern 
part of Taihu Lake due to human activities, combined with the closed geomorphological features in the northern 
region, is the primary factor contributing to the spatial heterogeneity in water quality49. As shown in Fig. 4a, the 
aquatic vegetation cover gradually decreased from the eastern part to the middle and western sections of Taihu 
Lake50. Remote sensing results revealed that aquatic vegetation was primarily concentrated in the eastern and 
southeastern regions17, consistent with the trend observed in this study.
A significant relationship was observed between aquatic vegetation and the trophic status of Taihu Lake. 
Persistent water eutrophication and changes in hydrological conditions contribute to the ongoing decline of 
aquatic vegetation in Taihu Lake50. The large-scale reduction of aquatic vegetation has disrupted the ecological 
balance and steady state of the lake, further contributing to the degradation of the water ecosystem26. Spatially, the 
western part of the lake is dominated by plankton, with sparse aquatic vegetation and frequent algal blooms12,13, 
whereas the eastern part of the lake is characterized by dense aquatic vegetation and relatively good water

Model construction and advantages of aquatic vegetation in the shallow lake Taihu Lake
Aquatic ecological models are primarily used to study eutrophication, bioaccumulation, and trophic interactions 
in aquatic ecosystems 52. Several large-scale aquatic ecosystem models, including PCLake, WET, EFDC, CE-
QUAL-W2, W ASP , DELFT3D, AQUATOX, and MIKE, are well-developed and commercially available53. The 
WET model is an aquatic ecosystem model that allows flexible, modular configuration of the food chain, making 
it suitable for a wide range of aquatic ecosystem applications. It integrates hydrodynamic, biogeochemical, and 
ecological processes to comprehensively simulate lake ecosystems, with multiple modules capable of simulating 
complex ecosystem interactions. This complexity enables a more accurate simulation of changes in aquatic 
vegetation43. The WET model can simulate biotic and abiotic transport and transformation processes, as well as 
climate and nutrient loading change scenarios in a lake ecosystem. For example, Nielsen et al. applied the model 
to Arreskov Lake, a shallow Danish lake, to predict the impacts of future climate change and nutrient loading 
on lake water quality. Chen et al. used the WET model to assess the impacts of extreme climatic events on 
eutrophication levels in Bryrup Lake, Denmark54. Similarly, Andersen et al. employed the WET model to predict 
changes in ecosystem states of shallow lakes38.
The WET model provides mechanistic explanatory capabilities for simulating aquatic vegetation in lakes 
and aligns with the scientific principles governing growth and changes in aquatic biodynamic processes. It 
incorporates the vertical gradient from the hydrodynamic model GOTM, analyzed in conjunction with the 
aquatic ecosystem model PCLake, and is implemented within the framework of Aquatic Biogeochemical 
Modeling (FABM). This approach more accurately models the delayed growth and cumulative effects of aquatic 
vegetation, better aligning with the observed phenomenon in which aquatic vegetation does not decline 
immediately after an algal bloom outbreak. Compared to using PCLake alone, the WET model offers better 
lag identification capabilities and more accurately reflects the actual conditions of the lake. During practical 
validation, the WET model successfully simulates the coverage and growth processes of aquatic vegetation in 
shallow lakes. Phytoplankton limitation by nutrients is modeled using the Droppe equation, which also accounts 
for oxygen dynamics, including reaeration, oxygen production via photosynthesis, oxygen exchange with the 
water column, and oxygen consumption during sediment mineralization38. The effect of temperature on aquatic 
vegetation growth can be represented by two exponential functions, which describe its impact on production 
respiration and maintenance respiration, respectively34.
Nonlinear inflection thresholds and cause identification for aquatic vegetation restoration 
and eutrophication scenarios in shallow lakes
The increase in aquatic vegetation coverage to control lake eutrophication and inhibit algal blooms aligns with 
the results of the scenario simulation presented in this study 55. Phosphorus limitation in lake ecosystems is 
governed by its distinct biogeochemical behavior at the sediment–water interface and its long-term accumulation. 
Aquatic vegetation exhibits a pronounced uptake capacity for phosphorus, which is sustained by a dynamic 
release-uptake cycle at the sediment interface that maintains a stable recycling pathway. In contrast, nitrogen is 
typically non-limiting due to significant gaseous losses via denitrification, constraints on biological fixation, and 
complex transformations among its various chemical species. These processes collectively result in lower and 
less predictable nitrogen bioavailability compared to phosphorus. The Pettitt mutation test indicated that the 
curve’s inflection point occurred when aquatic vegetation cover reached 15.28%, at which point the lake entered 
a state of hyper-eutrophication in response to the simulated phosphorus. The results of the Pettitt mutation test 
are presented in Fig. S3 of the Supplementary Material, indicating that increasing aquatic vegetation cover to 
15.28% is insufficient to improve the health of Taihu Lake. However, increasing the aquatic vegetation cover to 
27.7% resulted in an increase in the N:P ratio to 56.1, causing the lake to transition from a hyper-eutrophic state 
to an eutrophic one and ultimately to an oligotrophic state. The 27.7% threshold aligns with the lower bound of 
the optimal range identified in the Baiyangdian study, likely indicating that a self-sustaining, resilient clear-water 
state has been established56.
The model simulates the varying effects of aquatic vegetation on the dynamics of nitrogen and phosphorus. 
Phosphorus absorption by aquatic vegetation increases linearly, while nitrogen absorption is more sensitive to 
vegetation, initially declining before increasing. In lakes, phosphorus uptake begins with the direct absorption 
of phosphate, followed by the accumulation of phosphorus as aquatic plants develop. As aquatic vegetation wilts 
and decays, phosphorus stored in plant tissue is released back into the water, causing a short-term increase in 
phosphorus concentration. However, this process is generally slow, with phosphorus changes occurring as a long-
term, steady-state increase. As aquatic vegetation decays, phosphorus is released back into the water. Additionally, 
the root system of aquatic vegetation plays a role in influencing phosphorus dynamics in the sediments 57. 
Additionally, increased aquatic vegetation cover enhances water transparency and reduces plankton growth, 
thereby indirectly limiting phosphorus cycling. The influence of aquatic vegetation on nitrogen is primarily 
associated with its polymorphic nature and complexity. The scenario simulation of the WET model currently 
simplifies ecological processes, with the simulation results mainly reflecting the response characteristics driven 
by nitrogen-phosphorus structure changes. Due to data acquisition limitations and the preliminary positioning 
of the model, the current framework is simplified. Future research will incorporate remote sensing technology 
and water quality monitoring data to supplement variables such as transparency, water depth, and other key 
factors, ultimately building a more ecologically complete multi-factor coupling model to enhance both its 
ecological adaptability and universality.

Moreover, submerged vegetation plays a crucial role in maintaining clear water conditions in lakes through 
various mechanisms 58. These plants reduce turbidity and nutrient concentrations, including total nitrogen 
and phosphorus, by acting as a barrier to particle dispersal and filtering suspended particles from the water. 
Submerged plants also directly absorb nitrogen and phosphorus through their roots and leaves, thereby 
improving water quality by lowering the concentrations of soluble nitrogen and phosphorus 50. Additionally, 
by secreting allelopathic substances, they modify algal cell physiology, inhibiting phytoplankton growth and 
reducing algal biomass. The leaf type of submerged plants plays a crucial role in enhancing water quality. Leaf 
shape is a key phenotypic trait that reflects their adaptive strategies to environmental stress. Establishing a 
submerged plant system not only improves water transparency, but the resulting clarity further supports plant 
growth, creating a positive feedback loop. It provides food for microorganisms and shelter for zooplankton, 
reducing suspended nutrients and inhibiting algal growth through zooplankton grazing 59. Therefore, large-
scale cultivation of submerged vegetation in lakes can improve aquatic ecosystem health and help mitigate 
eutrophication in Taihu Lake. In the future, emphasizing the ecological role of aquatic vegetation may alleviate 
the burden of lake management.
This study establishes a 27.7% aquatic vegetation coverage threshold for transitioning Lake Taihu from a 
eutrophic to an oligotrophic-mesotrophic state, providing a quantifiable management target that bridges 
scientific research and practical application. This threshold serves as a key indicator for assessing ecosystem 
status and restoration effectiveness. The WET model serves as a predictive decision-support tool, simulating 
vegetation response to management actions—including pollutant load reduction and hydrological regulation—
to optimize strategies and forecast eutrophication trends. Furthermore, these findings enable the development 
of an early-warning system that triggers intervention when vegetation coverage nears critical levels, facilitating 
a shift from reactive treatment to proactive, preventative management.
Conclusions
This study developed a novel WET-based ecological model for large, shallow lakes, establishing a nonlinear 
relationship between long-term hydrological/physicochemical drivers and submerged vegetation cover in Lake 
Taihu. A key contribution is the quantification of critical ecological thresholds, demonstrating that achieving 
approximately 27.7% vegetation cover can shift the system from a eutrophic to an oligotrophic-mesotrophic 
state under specific N:P ratios. The results confirm phosphorus as the primary limiting nutrient, with nitrogen 
exerting a secondary influence on vegetation dynamics. A primary limitation is the model’s parameterization 
for a single lake system, necessitating validation across diverse lacustrine environments to ensure broader 
applicability. Future work should elucidate the underlying biogeochemical mechanisms—particularly sediment-
phosphorus feedbacks and nitrogen fixation—and evaluate the impacts of climate change on restoration 
resilience. For management, this study provides quantifiable vegetation targets and a predictive tool to simulate 
ecosystem responses to interventions, thereby enabling proactive eutrophication control and optimized resource 
allocation in shallow lakes.
