Abstract Cities must balance the housing needs of growing populations with planetary boundaries. Technological improvements alone cannot meet climate targets, and demand-side strategies that reduce private dwelling size are critical for achieving absolute reductions in material and energy demand. However, the socio-spatial conditions that enable compact living remain poorly understood. Prior theoretical work suggests that social infrastructure, including cafés, parks, libraries, and community centres, can reduce demand for private residential space, but empirical evidence linking social infrastructure to dwelling size has been virtually absent. &amp;#xD;We address this gap through a large-scale, neighbourhood-level analysis across Vancouver, Toronto, and Montréal, Canada.
