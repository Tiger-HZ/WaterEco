Abstract Phosphorus (P) cycling underpins productivity and fertiliser use efficiency in grazed pasture systems. However, the effects of regenerative practices such as enhancing pasture diversity and alternative grazing management remain poorly quantified. We investigated regenerative and conventional system effects on soil-plant-animal P dynamics in a three–year dryland grazed experiment. The regenerative system comprised a 12-species diverse pasture mix with short grazing periods and extended recovery, while the conventional system used best-practice grazing of Medicago sativa L. Both systems also included high and low P fertiliser treatments.
