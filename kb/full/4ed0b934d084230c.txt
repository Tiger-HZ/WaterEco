This study evaluated the adsorption performance of hazelnut shells and their derived biochar for removing paracetamol (PCM), amoxicillin (AMX), and triclosan (TCS) from synthetic solutions and wastewater matrices. Batch and fixed-bed experiments were conducted using hazelnut shell/biochar mixtures (0–100%), a combination rarely examined in continuous systems. The materials were characterized by XPS, SEM-EDS, FTIR, and TPD-MS. In batch tests, hazelnut shell kinetics were empirically better described by the pseudo-second-order than the pseudo-first-order model (R2 = 0.78–0.98). Pyrolysis of the raw biomass increased the adsorption capacity by up to 113-fold.
