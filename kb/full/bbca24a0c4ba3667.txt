 Abstract For several decades, the valorization of plastic waste has been a top concern for the scientific community. Informed by prior research, this work explores the viability of reusing bottle caps as bio-carriers after their surface modification. The High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) bottle caps were modified using KMnO 4 , H 2 SO 4 , HCl and NaH 2 PO 4 solutions, then employed as bio-carriers in the fixed bed bioreactor for treating nutrient rich domestic wastewater (NH 4 + in = 50 mg/L − 162 mg/L; COD in = 490 mg/L − 570 mg/L). The initial results revealed that the removal efficiencies for organic matter and ammoniacal nitrogen of these modified carriers were 81 ± 1.9% and 86 ± 9.5%, respectively. The 4.5% increase in specific surface area contributed to slightly higher densities of ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB) and nitrite-oxidizing bacteria (NOB) on the modified carriers. The addition of modified plastic caps as carriers in both anoxic and oxic tanks helped improved the total nitrogen reduction efficiency from 77.9 ± 4.0% to 82.3 ± 7.3%. Full-length 16 S rRNA metagenomic barcoding analysis revealed that the microbial communities within the modified carriers from oxic tank were highly diverse, abundant, and evenly distributed with 399 bacterial genera, 626 bacterial species detected. In addition to Caldilinea and Nitrospira , other prevalent species included Romboutsia (3.02%) and Thermomonas (2.96%), the latter known for its nitrate-reducing capabilities. The metagenomic analysis and treatment efficiency confirmed the possibility of using this kind of carriers for nitrogen removal from nutrient-rich wastewater.

 Introduction Given the substantial and continuously increasing plastic consumption over recent decades, the reuse and recycling of plastic waste have garnered significant attention from policymakers, entrepreneurs, and researchers globally. According to a World Bank report, approximately 3.90 million tons of polyethylene terephthalate (PET), low-density polyethylene (LDPE), high-density polyethylene (HDPE), and polypropylene (PP) are consumed annually in Vietnam (World Bank Group 2021 ). The volume of plastic waste generated has shown a rising trend, with estimated figures of 2.7 million tons in 2018, 2.83 million tons in 2019, and approximately 2.93 million tons in 2021 (WWF 2023 ). In 2021, around 0.89 million tons of plastic waste were classified for recycling purposes; however, only 0.77 million tons were actually recycled, indicating an approximate 10% material loss. In contrast, many European nations and advanced Asian countries such as Denmark, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Germany, and Singapore have implemented stringent source-separation policies for waste, particularly plastics. Notably, Japan achieves a 100% plastic waste recycling rate through rigorous waste management and recycling systems. For the past decades, plastic waste has got attention in some researches to be reused as raw materials for construction materials (Lamba et al. 2022 ), adsorption materials (Singh et al. 2017 ; Maitlo et al. 2022 ; Yuan et al. 2020 ; Zhang et al. 2020 ) or bio-carriers, filter media in wastewater treatment (Oliveira et al. 2014 ; Hong Anh et al. 2024 ; Muliyadi et al. 2025 ). The plastic biocarriers have some limitations such as low hydrophilicity, poor bioaffinity, which makes biofilms grow slowly and peel off easily. The specific surface area of the reused plastic waste as bio-carriers was also a constraint. For instance, the bottle caps had specific area of 255.4 ± 5.3 m 2 /m 3 , much lower than that of commercial high-density polyethylene K3 carriers of 500 m 2 /m 3 (Hong Anh et al. 2024 ). Therfore, surface modification to increase roughness, surface area and biocompatibility of biocarriers have been conducted to improve the biofilm forming ability of the material via chemical methods and/or physical methods. Chemical modification to improve the surface have been proved effective by chemical reactions, such as chemical oxidation and surface grafting with gelatin (CO-SGG) (Chen et al. 2012 ), polymer grafting by radiation or plasma and enzyme techniques (Bhattacharya et al. 2009 ), grafting amino functional groups (-NH 2 ) onto PE and PP carriers (Lackner et al. 2009 ), embedding in polymers (e.g., with toluene diisocyanate, polyether polyol, foam stabilizers, etc.) to increase positive charge and hydrophobicity (Mao et al. 2022 ), embedding in polylactic acid, CH 2 Cl 2 , NaOH solution (Yu et al. 2022 ), or combining embedding in polylactic acid solution and bio-aggregates (Li et al. 2022 ). Physical surface modification methods usually use physical processes such as mechanical surface modification or irradiation. In 2019, Sonwani et al. 2019 studied cutting a 12 mm long PP sheet; 25 mm in diameter and attaching it to LDPE and PUF materials cut into the required size, forming new LDPE-PP and PUF-PP carriers. Physical surface modification can precisely control the modified area and improve the surface micro-friction properties of the material, however, the factors that directly affect the ability to form a biofilm layer are poor bioaffinity and surface hydrophilicity, which have not been improved. Since the chemical surface modification method has many advantages such as selective grafting, tight bonding, little effect on the surface, easy operation, and can be produced on a large scale at low cost, it shall be selected to apply in this study. The novelty of this study lies in the application of surface modification technique to waste plastic caps in order to assess their effectiveness in treating nutrient-rich domestic wastewater. Previously, bottle caps were identified as having potential for reuse as biofilm carriers (Hong Anh et al. 2024 ); however, this study hypothesizes that chemical surface modification can increase specific topographical features or specific surface area and embed functional moieties, which may accelerate biofilm formation, cultivate a more robust and specialized microbial community, and ultimately enhance the efficiency of nitrogen removal from a nutrient rich flow. A key contribution of this research was the comprehensive microbial community analysis for the plastic carriers, which provided valuable insights into the biofilm composition and its role in the treatment process. Materials and methods Bio-carrier’s surface modification and analysis Modification of bottle caps Chemicals for surface modification include concentrated KMnO 4 solution (YoungJin, Korea), 95–97% H 2 SO 4 solution (China), 0.1 M HCl solution (Samchun, Korea), 0.2 M NaH 2 PO 4 or 0.2M H 3 PO 4 buffer solution (GH Tech, China) and 0.1 M FeCl 3 solution (Samchun, Korea). The bio-carriers used in this study were HDPE bottle caps collected from plastic recycling facilities in Phan Boi Village, My Hao Commune, Hung Yen Province. These caps, made from high-density polyethylene (HDPE), are buoyant and chemically stable in aquatic environments. The HDPE caps were thoroughly washed with water and air-dried for 24 h prior to surface modification. The modification process was designed to oxidize the surface using a KMnO₄/H₂SO₄ system. The oxidation step cleaned, slightly corroded, increased micro-roughness, and created additional oxygen-containing functional groups such as –OH, C = O, and –COOH. Subsequently, the materials were rinsed three times with 6 M HCl and then neutralized using either 0.2 M NaH₂PO₄ or 0.2 M H₃PO₄ buffer solution. After neutralization, the carriers were soaked in 0.1 M FeCl₃ solution for 30–60 min and dried at 105 °C before being introduced into the bioreactor for wastewater treatment (Fig.  1 a). The FeCl₃ treatment aims to bring Fe³⁺ to the oxidized surface, increasing its interaction with EPS and supporting the adhesion and growth of microorganisms. The ratio of concentrated KMnO₄ : H₂SO₄ : water = 1 : 2 : 18, temperature 50 °C, and time 4 h were selected to ensure effective surface activation without destroying the matrix structure of the material. A FeCl₃ concentration of 0.1 M and soaking times of 10 min, 20 min, 30 min, 1 h, and 2 h were chosen to investigate the effect of contact time on the degree of denaturation and biofilm-bearing capacity. It was found that a soaking time of less than 30 min resulted in an insufficient Fe³⁺ coating, limiting improvements in biocompatibility. Conversely, soaking for more than 2 h led to excess Fe³⁺ deposition, which may compromise the surface stability of the carriers and negatively impact microbial activity within the treatment system. Bio-carriers’ analysis The surface area, specific surface area, porosity, and density of the bottle caps before and after surface modification were measured following methodologies described in previous studies (Oliveira et al. 2014 ; Hong Anh et al. 2024 ). To gain deeper insight into the surface morphology of the bio-carriers, scanning electron microscopy coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM–EDS) was employed. This technique generates high-resolution, magnified images by scanning the sample surface with a focused electron beam. Analyses were performed using a Tabletop Microscope (TM4000Plus, Hitachi, Japan). For biofilm characterization, at the end of each treatment cycle, ten representative pieces from each type of bio-carrier were collected and sent to Microlab for microbial and metagenomic analysis. Experimental setup and testing procedure A laboratory-scale experimental set-up (Q = 20 L/day) was installed in the lab of the Water Supply and Sanitation Division, Hanoi University of Civil Engineering (Fig.  1 b). The setup included 05 small plastic tanks: feed tank (D = 390 mm, H = 420 mm), Anoxic tank (B × L × H = 385 × 230 × 285 mm), Oxic tank (B × L × H = 385 × 230 × 285 mm), clarifier (D = 200 mm, H = 500 mm) and the treated water tank (D = 200 mm, H = 500 mm). Two peristaltic pumps (MP-2000, EYELA, USA) were used, in which one was used to pump water from the feed tank to the anoxic tank and the other was used to pump the recycled water (NO 3 rich flow) from an oxic tank to an anoxic tank for denitrification. The concentration of dissolved oxygen in the oxic and anoxic tanks was maintained at 3.2±0.6 mg O 2 /l and 0.75±0.25 mg/L O 2 /l, respectively. The experimental setup was seeded with activated sludge from the another wastewater treatment plant with a mixed liquor suspended solid (MLSS) of 2.1–2.5 g/l to accelerate the microorganism growth or MLSS increase. The experiment was conducted from February 2024 to December 2024. To speed up the stable condition, activated sludge from nearby wastewater treatment plant was collected for seeding the oxic tank. The modified plastic carriers were put in the oxic tank with 30%-50% filling ratio in different testing batches. The purpose of adding carriers in the anoxic tank was to enhance the biogrowth and attachment of denitrifying bacteria on the carriers, thereby improving the denitrification process. It is important to note that a mechanical mixer was employed in the anoxic tank to maintain uniform suspension of the activated sludge. TN, COD, NH4-N were measured twice a week using analytical method mentioned elsewhere (APHA 2005 ). Additionally, the bottle cap samples (carriers) were sent to Microlab for microbiological analysis focusing on ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB), nitrite-oxidizing bacteria (NOB), and overall microbial density. The analytical methodology employed has been described in detail in a previous study (Hong Anh et al. 2024 ). The objective was to assess the presence and abundance of these bacteria on the carriers, as they potentially influence nitrogen removal efficiency. Finally, sequencing was performed on the Oxford Nanopore Technologies platform using the Flongle flow cell. Result and discussions Characteristics of modified bio-carriers The modified bio-carriers exhibited physical characteristics largely comparable to those of the original carriers (Hong Anh et al. 2024 ). Specifically, the average diameter was 2.3 ± 0.31 cm, porosity measured 82.6% ± 1.35, density ranged from 0.93 to 0.968 g/cm³, and the specific surface area was 268.4 ± 4.12 m²/m³. The primary distinction was the presence of a yellowish discoloration and a rougher surface texture compared to the smooth surface of the original carriers (see Fig.  2 ). This alteration was attributed to the chemical coatings applied, as detailed in the previous section. Furthermore, evidence of surface aggregations, likely resulting from chemical deposition, was observed on the modified caps. Fig. 2 Full size image SEM images with magnification of 2000 of ( a ) unmodified and ( b ) modified plastic caps Fig. 3 Full size image EDX analysis information for ( a ) original plastic caps and ( b ) modified plastic caps The aggregations deposited on the surface likely contributed to an increased surface roughness compared to the original carriers. Energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) analysis (Fig.  3 ) clearly demonstrated the significant presence of ions such as Cl⁻, K⁺, Mn²⁺, and Fe³⁺ on the surface of the modified bio-carriers following coating with potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) solution and ferric chloride (FeCl₃) chemicals. Characteristics of feed water The feed water was sourced from an office building, with the primary wastewater contributions originating from centralized toilets and washrooms, excluding any shower effluents. The wastewater temperature averaged 28.5 ± 8.5 °C, and the pH was 6.95 ± 0.39. Ammonium nitrogen (N-NH₄⁺) concentrations ranged from 50 to 162 mg/L, while the influent chemical oxygen demand (COD) varied between 490 and 570 mg/L. To enhance nitrogen removal efficiency, sugar was supplemented at a rate of 0.4 g/L of wastewater to increase the COD concentration whenever influent COD fell below 300 mg/L. This dosing rate was adopted based on a previous study (Dang et al. 2020 ). During the experimental phases, temperature, pH, and dissolved oxygen (DO) fluctuated but remained within acceptable operational ranges. Specifically, the DO concentration in the anoxic tank averaged 0.82 ± 0.21 mg/L, providing favorable conditions for denitrification. Conversely, in the oxic tank, DO was 6.41 ± 1.25 mg/L, predominantly within the 5–6 mg/L range, ensuring sufficient oxygen availability to support aerobic microbial activity (Techobanoglous et al. 2014 ). Additionally, the sludge volume index at 30 min (SVI30), overall SVI, and mixed liquor suspended solids (MLSS) in the oxic tank were 79 ± 5.4 mL/g, 115 ± 48 mL/g, and 2.45 ± 0.8 g/L, respectively, indicating favorable conditions for biological treatment processes. Table 2 AOB, NOB and total microorganism density Full size table The results presented in Table  2 indicate that the densities of both ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB) and nitrite-oxidizing bacteria (NOB) increased in proportion to the type of carrier material, following the order: commercial carriers (K3), unmodified caps, and modified caps. This suggests that chemical modification of the caps enhanced their attractiveness as substrates for bacterial colonization. It is noteworthy that the specific surface area of the modified caps was 268.4 ± 4.12 m²/m³, which was approximately 4.5% greater than that of the unmodified caps (255 m²/m³). Typically, a higher surface area or increased surface roughness provides more niches for bacterial attachment, which supports this observation. However, both types of recycled plastic carriers exhibited higher AOB and NOB densities than the K3 commercial carrier, despite the fact that the K3 carrier has a considerably larger specific surface area of 500 m²/m³. Direct observation revealed that while the caps had rougher surfaces, they possessed fewer angles and edges compared to the K3 carriers. Therefore, factors beyond surface area alone likely influence biomass growth on carriers. These factors may include the carrier material composition, wastewater characteristics, substrate concentration, dissolved oxygen levels, and aeration conditions (Morgan-Sagastume et al. 2018 ; Madan et al. 2022 ; Dölle 2024 ). Importantly, all materials tested were composed of high-density polyethylene (HDPE), which Dölle ( 2024 ) identified as a preferred medium for biofilm development. The results presented in Table  2 also indicate that the population of nitrite-oxidizing bacteria (NOB) was slightly higher than that of ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB). Specifically, Nitrobacter spp. were more dominant compared to Nitrosomonas spp. , Nitrospira spp ., and other AOB genera. This observation aligns with the findings of Oliveira et al. ( 2014 ), who reported that Nitrospira spp. (AOB) are better adapted to low ammonia concentrations, whereas Nitrobacter spp. (NOB) thrive under higher ammonia levels. In their study, the influent NH₄⁺-N concentration ranged between 42 and 54 mg/L, resulting in a greater abundance of Nitrospira spp . compared to Nitrobacter spp. throughout all experimental phases. In contrast, the present study involved wastewater with a relatively high average NH₄⁺-N concentration of 108 mg/L, which likely contributed to the observed predominance of the NOB population. Total microorganisms encompass a diverse range of bacterial groups, including both beneficial and potentially harmful populations. Beneficial bacteria are those involved in the metabolism and removal of environmental pollutants, such as nitrogen-oxidizing bacteria (e.g., AOB, NOB), sulfur-oxidizing bacteria, and others. In contrast, harmful groups typically consist of pathogenic microorganisms capable of spreading disease. Fig. 4 Full size image Images of total bacteria community growth on petri disks As shown in Table  2 ; Fig.  4 , the total bacterial community on the commercial carriers (S3) was 1.5 to 4 times greater than that observed on the bottle caps, depending on the measurement scale. However, the concentrations of beneficial bacterial groups, particularly nitrogen-oxidizing bacteria (AOB and NOB), were 5 to 30 times higher on the recycled plastic carriers (S1 and S2) compared to the commercial carriers. This suggests that the bottle caps offer an advantage by supporting a higher abundance of beneficial microorganisms. Furthermore, total microbial density on the modified carriers was significantly greater than that on the unmodified carriers across all measured ranges, indicating that the modification process effectively enhanced biomass attachment on the carriers. Microbial richness and diversity To further characterize the microbial composition on the test carriers, samples were subjected to metagenomic analysis as described above. The analysis yielded 78,198 sequences, totaling 117,004,684 bases. After quality control procedures, including the removal of unidentified bases, adapter sequences, and low-quality bases from the 5’ and 3’ ends, 73,740 high-quality sequences comprising 114,194,700 bases were retained. Bases with a quality score (Q score) above 10 accounted for 91.7% of the total sequenced bases, indicating reliable data quality. Full-length 16 S rDNA metabarcoding analysis was performed to assess species diversity, using operational taxonomic units (OTUs) and the Chao1 richness estimator. The modified carrier exhibited 636 OTUs and a Chao1 index of 636, reflecting high species richness. Diversity and evenness metrics, represented by the Shannon index (7.89) and the Simpson index (0.9909), further confirmed a diverse, abundant, and evenly distributed microbial community within the modified carrier. These results suggest that the modified material provides favorable conditions for microbial adhesion and proliferation. Fig. 5 Full size image Microbial diversity (phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species) in the modified carrier Microbial communities are essential in biological wastewater treatment because their diversity, distribution, and functional specialization directly impact the system’s efficiency. The microbial biofilm adhering to the cap carrier was sequenced and identified at the phylum, genus, and species levels. The results indicate the presence of 24 microbial phyla, with 11 dominant phyla (≥ 1.0%) depicted in Fig.  5 . These phyla include Pseudomonadota , Planctomycetota , Chloroflexota , Bacillota , Bacteroidota , Actinomycetota , Acidobacteriota , Nitrospirota , Ignavibacteriota , Bdellovibrionota , Thermodesulfobacteriota , and Thermomicrobiota . These phyla are commonly found in biological wastewater treatment systems, such as IFAS-MBBR processes used in municipal wastewater treatment (Godzieba et al. 2022 ). Among them, three dominant phyla— Pseudomonadota (32.5%), Planctomycetota (16.7%), and Chloroflexota (11.5%)—account for the majority (Fig.  5 ). In wastewater treatment, Pseudomonadota (Proteobacteria) encompasses facultative and obligate anaerobic bacteria capable of degrading organic compounds and reducing nitrate (Huang et al. 2020 ). Some Planctomycetota species are slow-growing autotrophic bacteria involved in anaerobic ammonium oxidation (anammox) (Kündgen et al. 2025 ). According to Petriglieri et al. ( 2023 ), Chloroflexota is widespread in wastewater treatment facilities globally, comprising heterotrophic bacteria with diverse metabolic capabilities, utilizing various carbon substrates such as sugars and amino acids. Consequently, Chloroflexota competes strongly in wastewater treatment systems, including anaerobic-anoxic-aerobic nitrogen removal and phosphorus bioaccumulation processes. The abundance of these three phyla in this study (approximately 39.3%) is comparable to the findings of Godzieba et al. ( 2022 ). Their prevalence underscores the efficacy of the anoxic moving-bed biofilm reactor (MBBR) using HDPE carrier for treating nitrogen-rich wastewater. This confirmed that the biofilm on this modified plastic carriers allowed simultaneous nitrification and denitrification process as findings in some previous studies (Wang et al. 2019 ; Liu et al. 2020 ; Massoompour et al. 2020 ; Raj Deena et al. 2022 ). Additionally, other bacterial phyla within the biofilm community contribute to various processes, including the degradation of different carbon substrates, nitrite oxidation, complete nitrification, biological phosphorus accumulation, and nitrate reduction. Table 3 Functional Roles of Dominant Bacterial Phyla in the modified carrier Full size table Beyond the phylum-level analysis, the 16 S rRNA sequencing data were further examined at genus and species levels, providing a detailed characterization of the dominant bacterial populations within the modified carrier (Fig.  6 ). These findings contribute to assessing microbial richness and multifunctionality, particularly in organic compound degradation and nitrogen cycling. At the genus level, 399 genera were identified in the microbial community. Genera with low prevalence (below 1.5% of the bacterial community) constitute 64.55%. The most abundant genera included Caldilinea (4.5%), unclassified- Planctomycetaceae -genus (3.81%), Nitrospira (3.23%), Romboutsia (3.02%), Thermomonas (2.96%), unclassified- Betaproteobacteria -genus (2.84%), Gemmata (2.33%), Ferruginibacter (2.05%), Clostridium (2.03%), Hyphomicrobium (2.02%), Stenotrophobacter (1.72%), Azoarcus (1.72%), Pelolinea (1.68%), and Terrimonas (1.53%). At the species level, 626 bacterial species were identified within the modified carrier. Species classification confirmed the presence of a diverse, abundant, and relatively balanced microbial community. The species identified were highly similar to those found in conventional wastewater treatment systems, particularly in biofilm-based processes. These microorganisms include species capable of degrading various carbon substrates, performing partial and complete nitrogen transformations, and supporting phosphorus bioaccumulation. Among the sequenced bacterial genera, Caldilinea was the most abundant, accounting for 4.50% of the total microbial population. This filamentous genus, belonging to Chloroflexota, plays a significant role in forming activated sludge flocs (Zhang et al. 2018 ) and is capable of degrading diverse organic compounds, including carbohydrates, amino acids, and fatty acids (Petriglieri et al. 2023 ). Within the study, Caldilinea aerophila (4.48%) and Caldilinea tarbellica (0.02%) were the two identified species. Caldilinea aerophila is a facultative anaerobe that thrives in low-oxygen environments, utilizing various carbon substrates (Sekiguchi et al. 2003 ). Its presence enhances organic carbon degradation in the studied wastewater treatment systems, creating optimal conditions for other bacteria to continue biological treatment processes. Another significant genus was Nitrospira (3.23%), recognized as nitrite-oxidizing bacteria (NOB), playing a vital role in nitrification by converting nitrite (NO₂⁻) to nitrate (NO₃⁻). Moreover, particular Nitrospira species perform comammox (complete ammonia oxidation), converting ammonia directly to nitrate. Within the HDPE cap biocarrier, multiple Nitrospira species were identified, including Nitrospira defluvii (2.61%), Nitrospira spp. (0.3%), Nitrospira inopinata (0.13%), Nitrospira moscoviensis (0.12%), Nitrospira lenta (0.04%), and Nitrospira japonica (0.03%). While Nitrospira defluvii is well-established as an effective nitrite-oxidizing bacterium, Nitrospira inopinata and newly founded Nitrospira spp. contribute to comammox. Additionally, Nitrospira moscoviensis exhibits simultaneous nitrification and denitrification, enhancing nitrogen removal in aerobic and anoxic conditions (Kuypers et al. 2018 ). Romboutsia (3.02%) was another dominant genus, consisting of Gram-positive bacteria typically found in intestinal microbiota. Five Romboutsia species were identified within the HDPE caps, including Romboutsia timonensis , Romboutsia lituseburensis , and Romboutsia sp. G12 , Romboutsia ilealis , and Romboutsia hominis . Romboutsia species have an intestinal origin, but their roles in the gastrointestinal tract of humans and mammals are less well characterized (Chang et al. 2023 ). They were the most prevalent (2.76%) in this study due to the wastewater source originating from an academic facility’s septic tank system. Additionally, Thermomonas (2.96%), a genus belonging to Pseudomonadota, was detected in notable abundance. These heterotrophic bacteria possess nitrate-reducing capabilities, allowing them to compete with autotrophic denitrifiers under conditions with high biodegradable organic matter (Xing et al. 2018 ). Among the seven identified Thermomonas species, Thermomonas sp. 7B-400 was the most prevalent (2.34%), while six additional species, including Thermomonas aquatica , Thermomonas fusca , Thermomonas beijingensis , Thermomonas sp. R039N , Thermomonas koreensis , and Thermomonas carbonis constituted the remaining 0.66%. Many Thermomonas species have previously been found in wastewater treatment plants (Ju et al. 2019 ), though Thermomonas sp. 7B-400 and Thermomonas sp. R039N remains a novel species with unverified roles in wastewater treatment systems. Full-length 16 S metagenomic barcoding analysis confirmed that the microbial communities within biocarriers derived from bottle caps were highly diverse, abundant, and evenly distributed. The identified bacterial taxa closely corresponded to those typically found in wastewater treatment systems, particularly in biofilm-based reactors. This microbial diversity included species involved in organic matter degradation, nitrogen cycling processes (partial and complete nitrification, denitrification), and phosphorus bioaccumulation. Notably, certain nitrate-reducing bacteria demonstrated high competitiveness under low dissolved oxygen (DO) conditions and in environments with elevated organic matter concentrations. The structural organization of the biofilm—with aerobic bacteria predominantly in the outer layers and anaerobic bacteria in the inner core—promotes efficient degradation of organic compounds and nitrogen removal, even in wastewater characterized by low carbon-to-nitrogen (C/N) ratios and high nitrogen loads. This study represents one of the first detailed species-level characterizations of microbial communities on biofilm carriers recycled from plastic waste, providing foundational data for future research on the application of such materials in wastewater treatment. Fig. 6 Full size image Relative abundance (%) of predominant microorganisms in the biofilm at the phylum level ( A ), genus level ( B ), and species level ( C ) Organic and ammonia removal performance The influent chemical oxygen demand (COD) ranged from an average of 490 mg/L to 570 mg/L across the three phases. The COD removal efficiency was 81 ± 1.9%, which was lower than that of 90 ± 3.1% observed when using unmodified bottle caps as carriers (Hong Anh et al. 2024 ). It is important to note that the influent COD in this study was higher than that reported in the previous study (Table  4 ). The removal rates of the modified ones, nevertheless, were comparable to those reported in earlier investigations employing recycled plastic carriers (Oliveira et al. 2014 ; Fauzi et al. 2023 ). Oliveira et al. ( 2014 ) previously explored the use of polyethylene terephthalate (PET) bottle caps as an alternative media support in submerged aerated filters, achieving an average COD removal of 78% under an organic loading rate below 2.6 kg COD/m³ media/day. Similarly, Fauzi et al. ( 2023 ) utilized shredded PET bottles with a specific surface area of 444 m²/m³ as supporting media in a fixed-bed bioreactor. Their study, conducted using synthetic domestic wastewater with COD concentrations of 400, 500, and 600 mg/L, reported COD removal efficiencies of 86.7%, 85.7%, and 85.0%, respectively. These findings supported the use of waste PET plastic bottles as sustainable support media for domestic wastewater treatment, offering the dual benefits of effective pollutant removal and plastic waste reduction, particularly in contexts such as Indonesia. A comparison between Phase 1 and Phase 2 showed that the average COD removal efficiency was slightly higher when a lower carrier filling ratio (30%) was used compared to a higher filling ratio (50%). However, when accounting for standard deviation, the difference was not statistically significant, suggesting that the filling ratio had a limited impact on COD removal. Similar findings were reported by Feng et al. ( 2012 ), who observed minimal effect of filling ratio on COD removal efficiency in their study on moving bed biofilm reactors (MBBRs) using polyurethane foam (PUF) carriers at filling ratios of 20%, 30%, and 40% for the treatment of synthetic wastewater. Conversely, Martín-Pascual et al. ( 2016 ) reported that the highest COD removal efficiencies were achieved at a 50% filling ratio for different types of commercial carriers—56.97% for Aqwise, 58.92% for Kaldnes, and 46.13% for BIOCONS, when tested at filling ratios of 20%, 35%, and 50%. In the present study, the addition of carriers to the anoxic tank did not appear to influence COD removal (see Fig.  7 ), suggesting that carrier presence in the anoxic zone may be more relevant for enhancing ammonia removal rather than organic matter degradation. Fig. 7 Full size image COD removal efficiency during three phases (Phase 1: 50% filling ratio in oxic tank; Phase 2: 30% filling ratio in oxic tank; Phase 3: 30% filling ratio in oxic tank and 30% filling ratio in anoxic tank) Figure 8 illustrates the ammonia removal efficiency observed in Phase 1 and Phase 3 (data from Phase 2 were unavailable due to internal technical issues). The influent ammonium nitrogen (NH₄⁺-N) concentrations ranged from 50 to 162 mg/L, while the effluent concentrations varied between 2 and 32 mg/L. Correspondingly, the ammonia removal efficiency ranged from 72% to 98%, with an average efficiency of approximately 86%. When compared with findings from previous studies (Oliveira et al. 2014 ; Fauzi et al. 2023 ; Hong Anh et al. 2024 ) as summarized in Table  4 , the nitrogen removal performance in this study is comparable to those reported in similar research, indicating the viability of using recycled plastic carriers for effective ammonia removal in wastewater treatment systems. Fig. 8 Full size image Evaluation of nitrogen control efficiency It is noteworthy that the treatment performance in Phase 3 was higher than in Phase 1. A likely explanation lies in the significantly lower influent ammonium concentration during Phase 3 (83.7 ± 16.5 mg/L NH₄⁺-N) compared to Phase 1 (132.3 ± 32.8 mg/L NH₄⁺-N). Additionally, the carbon-to-nitrogen (C: N) ratio during Phase 3 was more favorable for microbial growth, thereby supporting a more effective nitrification process. Moreover, as previously mentioned, modified carriers were introduced into the anoxic tank during Phase 3. This may have promoted enhanced biofilm development, thereby improving denitrification activity within the anoxic zone. Supporting this, the total nitrogen removal efficiency increased from 77.9 ± 4.0% in Phase 1 to 82.3 ± 7.3% in Phase 3. These results support the hypothesis that the addition of modified carriers in the anoxic tank facilitated greater biomass growth and enhanced nitrogen removal performance. Table 4 Review studies on reuse of plastic waste for wastewater treatment Full size table Further analysis of the data presented in Table  4 highlights promising trends in the pollutant removal efficiencies when various types of plastic waste are repurposed as filter media/carriers across different treatment technologies. For example, studies utilizing acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) in vertical flow filters and biological reactors consistently report high removal efficiencies for biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), chemical oxygen demand (COD), and total suspended solids (TSS), often exceeding 90% (Chmielowski et al. 2023 ). Likewise, polyethylene terephthalate (PET), especially when applied in fixed-bed reactors and anaerobic biofilters, has demonstrated effective removal of organic matter and suspended solids, with some studies also reporting notable nutrient removal (Dorji et al. 2020 ; Fauzi et al. 2023 ). Trickling filters employing unspecified plastic media also showed strong performance in the removal of BOD, COD, and TSS, as well as additional pollutants such as phosphates, sulfates, nitrates, and fecal coliforms—even under low-temperature conditions (Dölle 2024 ). Variations in removal efficiencies among different studies can be attributed to several factors, including the type and physical form of plastic used, pre-treatment or modification methods applied to the plastic waste, the design characteristics of the treatment system (e.g., hydraulic loading rate, retention time), the degree of biofilm development, and the specific properties of the domestic wastewater (e.g., organic loading, nutrient concentrations, pH, and temperature). Collectively, these findings reinforce the potential for recycling ABS, PET, and HDPE plastic waste as effective biofilm support media in wastewater treatment applications. Conclusions Surface modification of HDPE plastic waste used as biofilm carriers has yielded promising results in the treatment of nutrient-rich wastewater. Specifically, the removal efficiencies for organic matter and ammoniacal nitrogen were 81 ± 1.9% and 86 ± 9.5%, respectively. These results are comparable to those reported in previous studies employing either recycled plastic waste or commercial plastic carriers. With a 4.5% increase in specific surface area, the modified carriers demonstrated a slight improvement in the removal of organic compounds and ammoniacal nitrogen compared to unmodified carriers. It is noteworthy that the experiments were conducted under nutrient-rich domestic wastewater conditions, which typically present challenges in achieving high removal efficiencies. The marginal increase in surface area contributed to slightly higher densities of ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB) and nitrite-oxidizing bacteria (NOB) on the modified carriers. Interestingly, the abundance of AOB and NOB was significantly greater on the modified plastic caps than on the commercial carriers. Analysis of the biofilm community on the modified bottle cap biocarriers via 16 S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing revealed a highly diverse and evenly distributed microbial population. Among the 399 bacterial genera identified, the most dominant were Caldilinea (4.5%), associated with organic matter degradation; an unclassified genus within the Planctomycetaceae family (3.81%); and Nitrospira (3.23%), which plays a key role in ammonia oxidation. At the species level, a total of 626 bacterial species were detected. In addition to Caldilinea and Nitrospira , other prevalent species included Romboutsia (3.02%) and Thermomonas (2.96%), the latter known for its nitrate-reducing capabilities. The metagenomic analysis provided valuable insights into the microbial composition and contributed to a deeper understanding of the treatment mechanisms occurring within the bioreactor. Highlights 
 
 The 4.5% increase in surface area contributed to slightly higher densities of AOB and NOB bacteria on the modified carriers. 
 
 
 The addition of modified plastic caps as carriers in both anoxic and oxic tanks increased nitrogen reduction efficiency from 77.9±4.0% to 82.3±7.3%. 
 
 
 Full-length 16S rRNA metagenomic analysis detected 399 bacterial genera, 626 bacterial species in the modified carriers. 
 
 
 The most predominant bacteria were Caldilinea and Nitrospira, Romboutsia and Thermomonas species.
