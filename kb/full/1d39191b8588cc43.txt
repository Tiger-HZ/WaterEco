Abstract Effective hypoxia management requires disentangling natural (e.g., stratification) and anthropogenic (e.g., nutrient enrichment) drivers, which are often influenced by the same external factors, leading to temporally correlated effects. Here, we present a machine learning framework that integrates spatial monitoring data to enhance temporal analysis, enabling quantitative separation of stratification and eutrophication impacts on interannual oxygen depletion (measured as apparent oxygen utilization, AOU). We applied this framework to 29‐year water quality data from 69 stations in the lower Pearl River Estuary.
