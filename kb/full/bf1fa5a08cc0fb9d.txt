True-Cost Accounting (TCA) brings the invisible costs of an activity forward. Increasingly, TCA outcomes are being used to incorporate revealed societal costs into market prices to rectify market failures. This paper analyses the efficacy of True Pricing by analyzing the complexity of food price formation globally, including different stakeholders’ roles, both public and private. Reflecting the hidden environmental and social costs of food production into market prices marginalizes poor consumers, without affecting perverse subsidies and unsustainable corporate behavior. As a consequence, we argue how policy reforms using TCA are game changers in correcting food price distortion through appropriate incentives, fiscal and trade policies, more than True Pricing.
