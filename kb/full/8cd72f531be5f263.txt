Abstract Riverine dissolved silicon (DSi) and biogenic silica (BSi) are modulated along the estuarine gradient by several biotic and abiotic processes governed by physical forcings. An important area controlling silicon transport in alluvial estuaries with large intertidal mudflats is the benthic diatom-dominated biofilm system. Here, the hypertidal Severn Estuary, UK, has been used as a case study to improve our understanding of silicon transport in these benthic-dominated systems. We present the first time-series dataset of Si concentrations in the Severn. River and tidal hydrodynamics drove spatio-temporal changes in DSi.
