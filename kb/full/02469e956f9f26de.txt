ABSTRACT
 
This study was aimed to determine the effect of feeding diets containing graded levels of 
soybean husk on the nutrient intake, digestibility and growth
 
performance of Yankasa 
rams. Twelve (12) Yankasa rams with an average weight of 20.75kg were selected from the 
Livestock Unit of the Department of Animal Science, Bayero University, Kano and were 
randomly assigned to three (3) dietary treatment groups wit
h four animals per group in a 
randomized complete block design (RCBD). The treatment diets were; 0%, 15% and 30% 
soybean husk inclusion level. The result of the daily weight gain was not significantly 
(P>0.05) affected by the level of inclusion of the test
 
materials. The average daily feed 
intake did not differ significantly (P>0.05) among the treatments, with values ranging from 
770.75 to 821.00 g/day. However, the results of the nutrient digestibility revealed 
significant (P<0.05) differences in dry matt
er digestibility (DMD), crude fibre digestibility 
(CFD), nitrogen free extract digestibility (NFED) and neutral detergent fibre digestibility 
(NDFD) between treatment groups. It is concluded that Soybean husk can be fed for up to 
30% inclusion level to Yan
kasa Rams without any harmful effect on growth. It is therefore 
recommended that further feeding trial be conducted with other breed of sheep during feed 
scarcity.

Experimental animals
 
Twelve (12) Yankasa rams with an average 
weight of 20.75kg obtained from the Livestock

Bayero University, Kano were used for the

Experimental diets
 
Soybean husk, maize bran and rice bran were 
obtained from 
the Department of Agronomy, 
Bayero University, Kano. Three diets were 
formulated containing cotton seed cake, wheat 
offal, maize bran, brewers dried grain, rice 
bran and common salt (Table 1). The 
experimental diets were designated as T1, T2 
and T3 with 0%
, 15% and 30% inclusion 
levels of soybean husk respectively.
 
Experimental design
 
Twelve (12) Yankasa rams were randomly 
assigned into three dietary treatment groups 
with four animals per group in a randomized 
complete block design (RCBD).
 
Management of the
 
Experimental Animals
 
Prior to the commencement of the experiment, 
the animals were treated against ecto and endo 
parasites using ivermectin injection at 2ml per 
10kg body weight and albendazole at 1ml per 
50kg body weight. The animals were 
randomly alloca
ted to three (3) groups of four 
(4) animals each. Adjustment period of one 
week was allowed for the animals before the 
commencement of the data collection.

Data Collection
 
Performance indices
 
The experimental animals were allocated to 
the three treatments groups comprising four 
(4) animals per treatment and housed 
individually. The experimental diets 
formulated were given to the animals every 
morning througho
ut the period of the 
experiment which lasted for eight weeks. 
 
The animals were weighed at the beginning of 
the experiment and on weekly basis to 
determine average weight changes. Data on 
feed intake and body weight gain were 
evaluated. Feed intake was obt
ained by 
subtracting left over feeds from the feeds 
offered the previous morning. The live weight 
gains were determined by difference between 
final weight and Initial weight (Final 
–
 
Initial).

The average daily weight gain was determined 
by dividing weight
 
gain by the number of 
sheep and the number of days of the feeding 
trial.
 
Digestibility trial
 
At the end of the feeding trial which lasted for 
eight weeks, digestibility trial was conducted. 
The animals were harnessed with harness bags 
for adaptability to 
the bags. Faecal output was 
collected and weighed. It then mixed 
thoroughly and weighed, some portion were 
taken to the laboratory for analysis (AOAC, 
2005).
 
Chemical Analysis
 
Each sample of the experimental diet was 
milled to pass through a 1.00mm screen 
using 
a telator cyclotee 1093. The milled samples 
were used for proximate analysis to determine 
Nitrogen (N) for use in crude protein 
determination (N x 6.25), crude fibre (CF), 
ether extract (EE) and ash according to AOAC 
(2005). Neutral Detergent fibre a
nd acid 
detergent fibre (ADF) were determined 
according to procedures outlined by Van Soest 
et al
.
 
(1991).
 
Statistical analysis
 
Data obtained were subjected to analysis of 
variance (ANOVA) using SPSS package 
(1999).
