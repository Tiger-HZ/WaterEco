This chapter presents a rigorous evaluation of technologies employed to dispose of and recover heavy metal ions adsorbed on solid materials after water and wastewater treatment processes. It compares traditional modes of disposal, such as landfills and incineration, with environmental hazards, control issues, and sustainability concerns. This chapter also discusses enhanced recovery and regeneration methods, which include: chemical leaching, electrochemical regeneration, thermal desorption, solvent extraction, ion-exchange, and bioremediation. There is an emphasis on strategies that enable the recovery of metals, reuse of adsorbents, and valorization of resources in accordance with the requirements of the circular economy. Selected case studies in various treatment contexts prove the technical feasibility and performance of the disposal and extraction methods. Other crucial issues discussed in the chapter include the efficiency of the process, secondary pollution, and economic feasibility, as well as future research perspectives on the sustainable end-of-life management of metal-filled adsorbents. Overall, this chapter is a valuable resource for making environmentally responsible decisions in heavy metal management.
