Research on northern cod (Gadus morhua) from 1983 to 1994 indicated that a southward shift in distribution in the early 1990s was real and not an artifact of sequentially fishing down local populations. In the early 1990s, seasonal fishery and survey data showed distribution changes where there was no fishery, and large tonnage and densities (450 000 t, densities fourfold higher than 1980s levels) appeared in the south concurrent with declines in the north. All fishery, acoustic, and trawl survey indices increased in the south, while the stock declined.
