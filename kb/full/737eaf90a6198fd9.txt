This study characterized biochemical changes during pea seed (Pisum sativum L. cv. Aksary) germination, quantified thermolabile nutrient retention (vitamin C, thiamine, lysine) during infrared (IR) drying relative to grain temperature, optimized drying time and roller gap for maximal flake yield, and assessed physicochemical, nutritional, and microbiological quality of final flakes. Seeds were germinated (18–22 °C, 36–42 h, radicle 3–5 mm), IR dried (0.8–3.0 µm; heat flux 6.7 kW/m²; 0–180 s), and roll flaked (gap 0.3–0.8 mm) using the response surface methodology (central composite design); AOAC/ISO methods were used for nutrient analyses.
