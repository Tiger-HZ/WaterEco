Effect of integrated nutrient management on growth and
yield attributes of Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.)
under Chhattisgarh plains
Ravindra Paikra1, PC Chaurasiya2, Deo Shankar3, Himanshu Thakur4, Sweta Singh5
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/26174693.2026.v10.i10Sb.10263
A BSTRACT
A field study evaluated the effect of Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) modules combining inor-
ganic fertilizers, organic amendments, and bio-stimulants on the yield of tomato (Solanum lycopersicum
L. cv. Arka Samrat) under the sub-humid agro-climatic conditions of the Chhattisgarh plains. The ex-
periment was conducted during the Rabi season of 2025–26 at the College of Agriculture and Research
Station, Kanpa, Mahasamund, Chhattisgarh, using a Randomized Block Design (RBD) with nine treat-
ment combinations replicated thrice. Treatments comprised combinations of Recommended Dose of
Fertilizers (RDF: 213:150:120 kg/ha NPK), Farmyard Manure (FYM @ 10 t/ha), Vermicompost (VC @
4 t/ha), and foliar application of Seaweed Extract (D @ 3.0 ml/L). The application of 75% RDF +
Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract @ 3.0 ml/L (T_{5} T5) significantly enhanced yield parame-
ters compared to 100% inorganic RDF (T_{1} T1) and sole organic/bio-stimulant regimes (T_{9} T9).
Treatment T_{5} T5 recorded the highest fruit set (79.52%), fruit diameter (5.91 cm), average fruit
weight (88.30 g), yield per plant (2.61 kg), marketable yield (60.92 kg/plot), and total yield (40.84 t/ha).
Keywords: Tomato, Integrated Nutrient Management, Vermicompost, Seaweed Extract, Arka Samrat,
Yield attributes
Introduction
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L., 2𝑛 = 24) is one of the most widely cultivated vegetable crops globally
and holds a pivotal role in India's horticultural economy. Rich in essential minerals (K, Ca, Mg, P, Fe), or-
ganic acids (citric, malic), vitamins (A, B, C), and antioxidants like lycopene, tomatoes contribute signifi-
cantly to human nutrition and income generation for smallholder farmers. In India, tomato is grown across
approximately 854 thousand hectares with an annual production of 21.32 million tonnes. In the state of
Chhattisgarh, tomato cultivation spans 60.34 thousand hectares with a total production of 1208.70 thousand
tonnes. (Anonymous, 2024-25).
Despite high production potentials, the continuous and exclusive reliance on synthetic chemical fertilizers
has led to adverse ecological consequences, including soil structure degradation, secondary nutrient deficien-
cies, reduced fertilizer-use efficiency, and ecological imbalances. Conversely, exclusive organic farming of-
ten fails to meet the immediate nutrient demands of high-yielding crop varieties. Integrated Nutrient
Management (INM) addresses these limitations by optimizing the combined application of mineral fertiliz-
ers, organic manures (such as FYM and vermicompost), and biological/bio-stimulant inputs. This balanced
approach maintains soil physical, chemical, and biological properties, enhances microbial nutrient cycling,
and provides steady nutrient release throughout the crop cycle. (Purkayastha et al., 1998; Kumar et al.,
2001).
While several studies have explored INM strategies across various agro-ecological zones, location-specific
calibration is critical for regions like the Chhattisgarh plains, characterized by Vertisols ("Kanhar" soils) with
neutral pH and distinct nutrient balances. This investigation evaluates the interactive effects of reduced inor-
ganic fertilizer doses paired with vermicompost, farmyard manure, and seaweed biostimulants on tomato
vegetative growth, phenological development, fruit yield, post-harvest quality, and economic efficiency.
Materials and methods
Experimental site and agro-climate
The field experiment was conducted during the Rabi season of 2025–26 at the College of Agriculture and
Research Station, Kanpa, Mahasamund, Chhattisgarh. The site falls under the Eastern Plateau and Hills
Region. The climate is sub-humid, with an average annual rainfall of 1385 mm. Meteorological parameters
recorded during the cropping period are presented in the experimental records.

Experimental design and treatment allocations
The experiment was laid out in a Randomized Block Design (RBD)
with 9 treatments and 3 replications using the high-yielding tomato
variety Arka Samrat. Net plot size was 3.0m × 3.0m (9.0m2) with a
spacing of 60cm × 45cm.
𝑇1: 100% RDF (only NPK 213:150:120 kg/ha) control
𝑇2: 75% RDF + FYM 10 t/ha
𝑇3: 75% RDF + FYM 10 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @
3.0 ml/L
𝑇4: 75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha
𝑇5: 75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @
3.0 ml/L
𝑇6: 50% RDF + FYM 10 t/ha + Vermicompost 4 t/ha
𝑇7: 50% RDF + FYM 10 t/ha + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed
Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/L
𝑇8: 100% RDF + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/L
𝑇9: FYM 10 t/ha + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D)
@ 3.0 ml/L
Crop management
Seedlings were raised in protrays filled with cocopeat, sown on
November 6, 2025, and transplanted on December 3, 2025. Organic
manures (FYM and Vermicompost) were incorporated into the desig-
nated plots 30 days prior to transplanting. Half of the total Nitrogen
along with 100% Phosphorus and Potassium were applied basally at
transplanting via Urea, DAP, and MOP. The remaining half of
Nitrogen was top-dressed at 40 DAT following earthing-up. Seaweed
extract foliar sprays were applied per schedule.
Observations
The observations were recorded on 25 characters under yield attribut-
ing traits in tomato i.e. Fruit set percentage, Fruit diameter, Fruit
weight per plant, Fruit Yield per hectare. Fruit length, Total Number
of fruits per plant, Time of maturity, Average fruit weight, Marketable
yield, Unmarketable yield.
Result and discussion
The highest fruit set percentage (79.52%) was recorded in treatment
T5 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @
3.0 ml/litre of water), which was found to be significantly superior to
all other treatments. In contrast, the lowest fruit set percentage
(59.91%) was observed under treatment T9 (FYM 10 t/ha +
Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).
The highest fruit weight per plant (kg) (2.61) was obtained in the
treatment T5 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract
(D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water). The minimum fruit weight (1.70) per
plant was observed under the treatment T9 (FYM 10 t/ha +
Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).
The maximum fruit yield per hectare (40.84 t/ha) was recorded under
treatment T5 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract
(D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water), which was significantly superior to all
other treatments. The minimum fruit yield (32.42 t/ha) was recorded
in treatment T9 (FYM 10 t/ha + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed
Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).
The highest fruit length (9.44 cm) was recorded in treatment T5 (75%
RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of
water), which was found to be significantly superior to all other treat-
ments. On the other hand, the lowest fruit length (5.71 cm) was ob-
served under T9 (FYM 10 t/ha + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed
Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).
T5 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @
3.0 ml/litre of water) recorded the highest total number of fruits per
plant (41.45), which was found to be significantly superior to all other
treatments. In contrast, the lowest total number of fruits per plant
(26.46) was recorded under T9 (FYM 10 t/ha + Vermicompost 4 t/ha
+ Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).
The maximum average fruit weight (88.30 g) was recorded in treat-
ment T5 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @
3.0 ml/litre of water). The minimum fruit weight (60.25) was ob-
served in treatment T9 (FYM 10 t/ha + Vermicompost 4 t/ha +
Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).
The treatments, T5 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed
Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water) recorded the highest marketable
yield (62.92 kg plot−1), which was found to be significantly superior
to all other treatments. In contrast, the lowest marketable yield
(40.85 kg plot−1) was observed under treatment T9 (FYM 10 t/ha +
Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).
Conclusion
The highest gross returns (Rs. 612600 Rs. /ha) and net return
(446435.5 Rs/ha) was recorded under treatment T5 (75% RDF +
Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water)
followed by T4 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha) which were ap-
proximately similar trend in gross and net return.
The minimum gross return (Rs. 486300 Rs/ha) and lowest net return
(Rs.229143 Rs/ha) was recorded under treatment T9 (FYM 10 t/ha +
Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water).

On the basis of above findings, treatment T5 (75% RDF +
Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract (D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water)
stand first in position and T4 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha) stand
in second order of preference. Therefore, it may be concluded that
treatment T5 (75% RDF + Vermicompost 4 t/ha + Seaweed Extract
(D) @ 3.0 ml/litre of water) may be prefer for integrated nutrient
management in tomato. Farmers recommended to adopt T5 under
similar agro-climatic conditions for obtaining better yield from
tomato cultivation.
