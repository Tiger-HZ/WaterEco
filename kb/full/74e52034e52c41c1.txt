Abstract Transboundary rivers transport plastic pollution across political boundaries, yet the evidence base available for characterizing this movement remains dominated by concentration measurements that cannot resolve where contamination originates or how it is redistributed. This study develops and applies a coupled hydrodynamic-ecological modeling framework for a major transboundary river system, integrating field observations with discharge-controlled particle transport to evaluate how microplastic contamination moves from upstream catchments toward a coastal receiving environment. Field monitoring was conducted at 12 locations across two seasons and two matrices (surface water and bed sediment).
