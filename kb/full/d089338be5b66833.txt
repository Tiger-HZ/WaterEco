Operational safety is an important consideration in the design of mechanical systems because failures and repair durations directly influence their ability to perform the required function under operating conditions.However, operational-safety assessment is often conducted after equipment commissioning, limiting its usefulness during the design decision process.This study proposes a probabilistic and design-oriented approach for integrating operational safety into mechanical design.Reliability and maintainability are respectively modeled using Weibull and Gumbel distributions, while the parameters of the reliability model are estimated using the maximum likelihood method.A distinct operational-safety formulation is then developed from these probabilistic quantities and used to derive operational-safety, probability-density, risk-rate and confidence-rate functions.The approach is applied to three fire pump units using historical time-between-failure and time-to-repair data.The results demonstrate the applicability of the proposed formulation and show differentiated operationalsafety behavior among the studied units.A design-oriented framework is subsequently proposed to use the operational-safety indicator for evaluating, comparing and improving mechanical design solutions.The study provides a quantitative link between reliability, maintainability and operational safety and establishes a basis for incorporating operational-safety considerations earlier in the mechanical design process.
