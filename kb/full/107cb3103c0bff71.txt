Abstract A weight standard is the system of weights with which a coinage was issued. The weight standards used by different issuing authorities (cities, ethnē, federal states, kings, and satraps) during the Archaic and the Classical periods either derived from weight standards in use before the invention of coinage or were created afterward, sometimes by adopting a reduced version of one of the main monetary standards. The main monetary standards before the Hellenistic period were the Lydo-Milesian, the Persian, the Euboian (and Attic-Euboian), the Aiginetan, and the Corinthian. Evidence for weight standards derives from the coins themselves, as well as epigraphic documents and literary sources.
