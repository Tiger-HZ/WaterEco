Land use planning is routinely presented as the principal instrument through which societies can protect soil and water resources, yet the evidence connecting planning practice to measurable resource outcomes remains uneven and, in several respects, contested. This review examines the state of knowledge on sustainable land use planning for soil and water conservation in rural and peri-urban landscapes, spanning plot, catchment and regional scales. Literature was identified through open scholarly databases and indexes, supplemented by citation searching and by documents from recognised intergovernmental bodies, and was appraised for design quality, transparency of assumptions, and the strength of the inferential link between planning intervention and resource outcome.
