ASO Author Reflections: Beyond the Cut Surface—Rethinking R1 
Margins Through Recurrence and Salvageability
Kizuki Yuza, MD, PhD1,2 · Timothy M. Pawlik, MD, PhD, MPH, MTS, MBA3 
Received: 26 August 2026 / Accepted: 31 August 2026 
© The Author(s) 2026
Past
Microscopically positive (R1) margins have traditionally 
been viewed as a technical failure after resection of colo-
rectal liver metastases. However, earlier studies yielded 
conflicting findings regarding whether R1 margin status 
independently influences survival, particularly in the era 
of effective systemic therapy and parenchymal-sparing sur-
gery.1–3 Moreover, the relationship between margin status, 
the anatomic pattern of first recurrence, and the opportunity 
for subsequent liver-directed salvage remained incompletely 
defined.
Present
In an international multi-institutional cohort of 1060 
patients, R1 margin status was associated with worse crude 
survival outcomes, although the association was weaker 
after adjustment for primary lymph-node status and hepatic 
tumor burden and varied according to how missing data 
were handled. 4 Importantly, recurrence after R1 resection 
was less often confined to the liver. Among patients who 
developed recurrence, isolated liver recurrence occurred in 
31.1% of patients after R1 resection versus 47.6% after R0 
resection. Repeat hepatectomy was also less common after 
R1 resection (16.4% vs. 31.4%), with an even larger differ -
ence among patients with node-negative primary tumors. 
These findings suggest that the clinical meaning of an R1 
margin extends beyond the cut surface and may reflect a 
broader oncologic context that influences both recurrence 
distribution and the feasibility of liver-directed salvage. R0 
resection should remain the operative goal; however, an R1 
margin may be better interpreted as a context-dependent 
prognostic marker rather than as a purely technical endpoint.
Future
Future studies should distinguish planned R1 vascular 
detachment from parenchymal or otherwise unintentional R1 
margins and incorporate margin distance, molecular tumor 
characteristics, and standardized assessment of recurrence 
location. Further investigation is also needed to determine 
whether surveillance and multidisciplinary salvage planning 
informed by margin status and recurrence risk can increase 
opportunities for repeat liver-directed treatment. Ultimately, 
integrating technical margin status with tumor biology, dis-
ease burden, and anticipated salvageability may provide a 
more clinically meaningful framework for operative deci -
sion-making and post-resection management.
