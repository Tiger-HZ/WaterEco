LLALS is a concept white paper proposing a lunar surface infrastructure architecture that uses high-power laser energy beaming to assist compatible spacecraft during lunar descent and ascent. Rather than relying on photon pressure, LLALS frames laser assistance as an energy-transfer system: surface-generated power is stored, released in short high-power bursts, absorbed by an armored vehicle receiver, and converted into thermal propulsion assist while conventional chemical propulsion remains available for control and redundancy. The paper presents the LLALS-H baseline architecture, first-order power and propellant trade calculations, beam geometry considerations, receiver flux constraints, surface energy storage logic, dust/plume mitigation, operational safety requirements, and a staged demonstration roadmap. It positions LLALS as a mature-base lunar logistics technology intended to reduce propellant demand, preserve lunar water resources, support reusable cargo transport, and integrate with broader lunar wireless power-transfer infrastructure.
