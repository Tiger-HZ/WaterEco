The presence of parasites in the host is sensitive to changes in the aquatic environment, making them useful as bioindicators of water quality. Their reliance on the life cycle and environment of their hosts makes parasites sensitive to the health of aquatic ecosystems. The study examined 40 Epinepheliida grouper samples from five species: Cephalopholis miniata, Epinephelus longispinis, Epinephelus merra, Epinephelus ongus, and Epinephelus tauvina. The fish were obtained from fishermen who caught and landed them at Kedonganan beach. Parasites infecting the five grouper species included Rocinela sp., Acanthocephalus sp., and Lecithochirium sp.
