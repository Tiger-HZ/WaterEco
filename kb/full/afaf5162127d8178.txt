Abstract Harmful algal blooms (HABs) in coastal waters are an increasing ecological and socio-economic concern in Northwestern Patagonia (NWP). One widely accepted mechanism proposes that reduced freshwater input weakens water-column stratification and increases residence time, allowing nutrient-rich oceanic waters to intrude and favor HAB development. Here, we demonstrate that riverine controls exerted on HABs extend beyond low-flow conditions to include shifts in the timing and magnitude of high-flow pulses.
