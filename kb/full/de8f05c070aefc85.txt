ABSTRACT Chlorinated organic compounds (COCs) are persistent contaminants that are difficult to remove by conventional oxidation and biological treatment because of the stability of C–Cl bonds. UV/sulfite advanced reduction processes have emerged as promising technologies for reductive dechlorination because sulfite photolysis can generate hydrated electrons, hydrogen atoms, and sulfite anion radicals. However, the roles of these reactive species are strongly dependent on pollutant structure, pH, dissolved oxygen, and water‐matrix constituents, making the degradation mechanisms and practical applicability difficult to generalize.
