Abstract
The amount of atmospheric water vapour and its transport were analysed to rate the intensity and main directions of 
moisture fluxes incoming to Poland. The total column water vapour (TCWV) was used to show the total amount of 
water vapour in the entire atmosphere, whereas the specific humidity (SH) allow to show vertical variation of the mois -
ture properties. Also, the SH fluxes were applied to verify the main directions of water vapour transport at the pressure 
level of 925 hPa, 850 hPa and 700 hPa over Europe. The Atlantic Ocean is the relevant source of water vapour, which is 
advected to Europe due to the western circulation dominant in mid-latitudes. Huge supplies of water vapour and higher 
wind speeds affect the strongest fluxes there. Thus, the strongest SH fluxes come from the west (the monthly mean in July 
is up to 61 g∙kg− 1∙m∙s− 1 in Lodz). It is also the most frequent direction of atmospheric water vapour supplies over Poland. 
Simultaneously, long-term trends in TCWV and SH are mostly insignificant. There is a slight increase in the amount of 
water vapour in the subpolar region in the colder half of the year, and over Central, Eastern and Northern Europe in sum -
mer. These results show that the increasing saturation deficit and more frequent dry events are not caused by the change 
in the amount of water vapour. This provides the ground for claiming that the drying of the atmosphere is affected mainly 
by increasing temperature.

1 Introduction
Water vapour is an important component of the atmosphere. 
Its amount is differentiated spatially and dependent on the 
altitude (Palarz and Celiński-Mysław 2020). In general, the 
water vapour amount is decreasing as the altitude increases. 
Its amount is mostly higher in warmer regions due to stron-
ger evaporation and an increasing capacity as the air temper-
ature increases (Kunkel et al. 2013). It also means that the 
amount of atmospheric water vapour is strongly related to 
air temperature (Jackson et al. 2006; Scholz and Lora 2024); 
more water vapour is observed in the warmer air horizon -
tally and vertically. According to Wypych and Bochenek 
(2018), the relevance of water vapour in the atmosphere 
is based on its properties of thermal absorption as a green -
house gas. The atmospheric moisture also affects the heat

The research on atmospheric moisture properties over 
Europe shows significant relations with conditions over 
the Atlantic Ocean and the Arctic. Wypych and Bochenek 
(2018) point to the impact of atmospheric circulation on 
moisture properties over Europe, especially in winter. It 
determines the direction of water vapour advection and 
causes anomalies in the amount of atmospheric water in 
the higher troposphere related to the properties of incom -
ing air masses. They show the domination of western water 
vapour transport from the Atlantic Ocean to Europe in the 
entire vertical profile of the troposphere. A part of water 
vapour from the Atlantic Ocean is also transported by the 
atmospheric rivers to Western Europe (Ramos et al. 2016; 
Zavadoff and Kirtman 2020). According to Zavadoff and 
Kirtman ( 2020), higher temperatures and the intensifica -
tion of evaporation might lead to the strengthening of atmo-
spheric rivers by additional water vapour resources. The 
location of Europe defines the main properties for incoming 
air masses. The western advection to Europe is more humid 
than the eastern advection of continental air masses (Phil -
lips and McGregor 2001). Besides, the relation in vapour 
transport between Europe and the Arctic is considered in 
detail. The evaporation is quite low in the Arctic; most of 
the water vapour comes by advection from lower latitudes 
(Jakobson and Vihma 2010; Nygård et al. 2019). Hocke and 
Wang (2023) stated that the northern advection brings low 
supplies of atmospheric water.
The previous research described contrary trends, which 
might be an effect of the different long-term periods used. 
The amount of water vapour in the atmospheric air column 
(precipitable water) is mostly increasing in Poland, accord -
ing to Wibig and Siedlecki ( 2007). Ojrzyńska et al. ( 2022) 
stated that the significant trends are mostly observed in 
lower layers of the troposphere in spring (negative trends) 
and summer (positive trends). Whereas, Degirmendžić and 
Kożuchowski ( 2017) point to the decreasing trend of the 
average precipitable water.
The research described in this paper is focused on the 
amount of atmospheric water vapour and its transport over 
Europe. The key aim is to show the main sources of atmo -
spheric water vapour advected to Poland and their relations 
with general moisture transport over Europe. This analy -
sis improves the research by a wider data range and a dif -
ferent approach to the described issue. Also, the research 
will allow for improving recent research on the humidity 
conditions in Poland (Krawczyk 2026) by the analysis of 
moisture sources. Moreover, the rarely applied methods and 
the observations of relations between the amount of water 
vapour and evaporation improve the recent research on 
moisture content over Europe. These observations are based 
on the differences between chosen pressure levels of the 
troposphere in changes of moisture content and horizontal 
transport of water vapour, instead of the integrated water 
vapour in columns within pressure levels applied in many 
analyses. The trends in water vapour might allow to detect 
dependencies with climate change and detect whether the 
drought episodes are affected by the deficit of water vapour 
in the atmosphere or are an effect of increasing air tempera-
ture. The spatial variation is analysed in different altitudes to 
compare the range of changes and define their reason.
2 Data and methods
The longitudinal range of the research area is between 30°W 
and 45°E, and the latitudinal range is from 25°N to 75°N 
(Fig. 1). Therefore, the area considered in this research 
includes a slightly wider spatial range than Europe. The 
reason for choosing this area range and the simultaneous 
aim of this research is to show the impact on atmospheric 
water vapour amount and sources of its advection to Poland. 
Therefore, the impact of marine air from the Atlantic Ocean 
and continental air from Western Asia was considered. Also, 
more probable directions of water vapour fluxes are included 
(especially a pretty huge part of the Atlantic Ocean).
The monthly data from ERA5 reanalysis were used to verify 
spatial distribution and trends in atmospheric water vapour 
properties in 1966–2020. The datasets at a resolution of 0.25° x 
0.25° were prepared by the European Centre for Medium-Range 
Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) and came from the Copernicus 
Climate Change Service (C3S) (Hersbach et al. 2020). For the 
total column water vapour and actual evaporation analysis the 
single level database was used, whereas the data from three 
pressure levels (925, 850 and 700 hPa) were used for specific 
humidity amount and horizontal fluxes analysis (Copernicus 
Climate Change Service 2023a; 2023b).

2.1.1 Amount of water vapour in the atmosphere over

most of the year, the TCWV is much higher over the ocean, 
especially in lower latitudes, where it can reach up to 32 kg/
m2 (Fig. 2). Additionally, the second important source of 
atmospheric water vapour is the Mediterranean Sea basin 
(Wypych et al. 2018). However, its impacts strengthen sea-
sonally from May to October, when the TCWV might be 
about 30 kg/m2. Over the land, the amount of atmospheric

4 Long-term changes in the amount of 
atmospheric water vapour
In the long-term perspective, significant changes in TCWV 
occur seasonally and regionally in limited areas. Mostly, 
the trends are increasing, but these changes are rarely 
strong (Fig. 7). The increase in TCWV is observed in the 
subarctic region from September to February, usually up to 
0.5 kg/m2/10 years. In most months of the year, increasing 
trends are also visible over the Atlantic Ocean. There, 
higher trends over larger areas are observed in the warmer 
half of the year, often exceeding 0.4 kg/m2/10 years. 
The slight increase in TCWV (up to 0.4 kg/m2/10 years) 
in Southern and Central Europe is developing gradually 
from April to August. Decreasing trends in TCWV are 
observed seasonally, usually from November to April in 
the Mediterranean region and near the Black Sea (up to 
-0.5 kg/m2/10 years).
Long-term trends in SH at the pressure levels also show 
changes in the subarctic region in the colder half of the year 
(Fig. 8). However, the area with statistically significant 
changes and the magnitude of trends shrink in higher 
altitudes. At the pressure level of 925 hPa, the SH increases 
slightly, up to 0.3 g/kg/10 years, but these changes don’t 
exceed 0.2 g/kg/10 years in most cases. These trends also 
include Northern Europe. At the pressure levels of 850 
and 700 hPa, trends of SH in the subarctic region are low, 
rarely exceeding 0.1 g/kg/10 years. The decreasing SH 
is observed in the Mediterranean Sea basin in winter and 
summer months at the pressure levels of 925 and 850 hPa. 
However, these changes are not stronger than 0.3 g/kg/10 
years, and they are isolated to small regions. These changes 
are not as visible at the pressure level of 700 hPa. In the 
warmer half of the year, SH trends at the pressure levels of 
925 and 850 hPa are similar to TCWV trends. Additional 
changes are observed in the Iberian Peninsula and in 
the Black Sea basin. The decrease in SH at previously 
mentioned pressure levels is more often observed than a 
change in the amount of water vapour for the total column 
of the atmosphere. The summer trends in SH over Central 
and Western Europe at the pressure level of 850 hPa are 
widely expanded in comparison to the pressure level 
of 925 hPa, but they are weaker than in the near-surface 
atmosphere. At the pressure levels of 850 and 700 hPa, the 
SH does not change as intensively as at the pressure level 
of 925 hPa. The trends are rarely stronger than 0.1 g/kg/10 
years (both increasing and decreasing) at the pressure level 
of 700 hPa. They are mostly concentrated over the Atlantic 
Ocean and the subarctic region, and also in different parts 
of the Mediterranean Sea basin seasonally.

5 Discussion
In general, the highest total column water vapour and specific 
humidity are observed over the Atlantic Ocean, especially in 
lower latitudes (TCWV > 30 kg/m2 and SHUM > 10 g/kg). 
Spatial variation is similar for the total column water vapour 
and specific humidity at each pressure level and in all sea -
sons. More water vapour is observed over the ocean and 
decreases gradually in the northern and northeastern direc -
tions (Figs. 2 and 4). Therefore, the prominent supplies of 
water vapour for inland Europe are transported by advec -
tion from the Atlantic Ocean. Trenberth et al. ( 2011) stated 
that the hydrologic cycle is based on the intensive evapora-
tion over the oceans and precipitation in the inland regions. 
Because of the stronger evaporation over water reservoirs 
(Fig. 3), the amount of atmospheric water vapour is larger 
over the Atlantic Ocean than over Europe (Fig. 2). Accord-
ing to Held and Soden ( 2006), most of the water vapour in 
the atmospheric column is concentrated in the lower parts 
of the atmosphere. It is related to the vertical profile of air 
temperature and its impact on saturation water vapour pres-
sure, and also the intensive water vapour exchange between 
Earth’s surface and the atmosphere. Therefore, the specific 
humidity is the highest at the level of 925 hPa compared 
with levels of 850 hPa and 700 hPa (Fig. 4). The amount 
of water vapour gradually decreases in the higher altitudes, 
which also impacts lower transport of the atmospheric water 
despite the stronger wind speed.
The most intensive specific humidity fluxes are observed 
over the Northern Atlantic Ocean, which is determined not 
only by the amount of water vapour but also by stronger 
advection at these latitudes (Fig. 5). The lower horizontal 
wind speed impacts weaker fluxes near the tropic and sub -
polar latitudes. Additionally, weaker fluxes are over inland 
Europe as a result of lower water vapour supplies and lower 
wind weakened by the friction. Seager et al. (2010) point to 
mechanical and thermodynamic factors affecting the water 
vapour transport. From a mechanical point of view, most 
of Europe is exposed to the western circulation, which is 
dominant in midlatitudes due to global circulation mecha -
nisms. Therefore, the western water vapour fluxes are more 
frequent, especially above the boundary layer where the 
friction is negligible (Fig. 6). On the other hand, the ther -
modynamic conditions are related to evaporation processes, 
which affect the atmospheric water vapour accumulation. 
The type of Earth’s surface and its land cover impact the 
atmospheric water vapour resources by the water availabil -
ity and its potential evaporation capability. Nygård et al.

Long-term trend in specific humidity and TCWV over 
the research area are mostly insignificant. However, there 
are a few visible dependencies in the seasonality of sig -
nificant trends, which are slightly increasing in most cases. 
In autumn and winter, the increasing trend is relevant in 
the subarctic region and locally over the Atlantic Ocean. 
Whereas in summer, the increase in specific humidity and 
TCWV is noticed over the Central, Eastern and Northern 
Europe (Figs. 7 and 8). Humidity properties are dependent 
on the thermal conditions. Higher air temperature favours 
evaporation strengthening, and so the precipitable water is 
larger in warmer seasons (Fig. 2) (Rinke et al. 2019). Also, 
the increasing air temperature in the long-term perspective 
are in relation with positive trends in evaporation strength -
ening and growth in the amount of water vapour (Fig. 7). In 
the multiyear perspective, these changes lead to modifica -
tions in the entire hydrological cycle (Seager et al. 2010). 
The positive trends in precipitable water (Fig. 7) are mostly 
related to stronger evaporation in the warmer atmosphere 
(Zavadoff and Kirtman 2020; Tabassum et al. 2024). How-
ever, it is worth noticing that the evaporated water vapour 
is delivered mostly to lower parts of the atmosphere, so the 
amount of specific humidity varies vertically (Fig. 4). Held 
and Soden ( 2006) highlighted that the stronger evapora -
tion in warmer climates causes an increase in the amount of 
water vapour mostly in the lower atmosphere. In agreement 
with them, the increase in specific humidity at the pressure 
level of 925 hPa is relevant in summer over central, eastern 
and northern regions of Europe, which in a large part cor -
relates with an increase in precipitable water (Figs. 7 and 8). 
The processes over the Atlantic Ocean are particular; they 
play a crucial role in water vapour transport to Europe. In 
the long-term perspective, the increase in precipitable water 
and specific humidity is observed there. Serreze et al. (2012) 
also mentioned that this trend is visible in the northern parts 
of the Atlantic Ocean, which might support the advection of 
water vapour to the Arctic.
The moisture advection in Poland is dominated by the 
western directions. Their frequency increases at higher alti-
tudes, where the impact of friction and surface properties 
is much lower than in the near-surface layer of the atmo -
sphere. Specific humidity fluxes come to Poland also from 
the southern (in April and October) and northern (in July) 
regions (Fig. 6). Poland’s location causes the simultaneous 
impact of different factors on the climate. Western advec -
tion is dominant in midlatitudes and affects the moisture 
transport from the Atlantic Ocean. From this direction, the 
intensive specific humidity fluxes come to Central Poland 
(Fig. 6). Therefore, each change in humidity exchange in 
Western Europe might impact moisture properties in Poland. 
The trends in the amount of water vapour in the atmospheric 
column are slightly increasing, but mostly insignificant in 
most cases, which is in agreement with Wibig and Siedlecki 
(2007). The exception is the southeastern region of Poland, 
where the precipitable water significantly increases in sum-
mer (Fig. 7). According to Araszkiewicz et al. ( 2021), it 
might be an effect of stronger convective processes in the 
continental climate of Southeastern Poland during summer, 
which boost the evaporation.
6 Conclusions
The atmospheric water vapour participates in thermody -
namic and circulation processes, so the moisture issues 
are extensive and complex. The water vapour transport 
is an important factor regulating not only the air humid -
ity conditions, but also the entire hydrological cycle. In 
general, the moisture conditions over Europe have been 
mostly dependent on the humidity fluxes incoming from 
the Atlantic Ocean. Large atmospheric water supplies in 
oceanic air masses and western circulation in mid-latitudes 
are favourable to water vapour advection to Europe. The 
general increasing trend in the total column water vapour 
is supposed to be an effect of increasing air temperature 
and stronger evaporation. It is contributed by changes in 
specific humidity in the near-surface atmosphere, whereas 
the amount of atmospheric water at higher altitudes is less 
changeable. Simultaneously, the significant trends in the 
amount of water vapour are mostly observed over large 
reservoirs. Only in summer, the increase in specific humid -
ity or precipitable water is significant over the inland areas 
of Central, Eastern and Northern Europe. Therefore, the 
changes in the amount of atmospheric water vapour or its 
transport are mostly insignificant and do not have a large 
impact on the hydrological cycle.
