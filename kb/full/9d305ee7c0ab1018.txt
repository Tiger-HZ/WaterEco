Harmful algae blooms occur regularly in Lake Winnipeg. We apply a two-dimensional hydrodynamic and biogeochemical model, to investigate bloom causes and develop lake management tools. The model simulated hydrodynamics with root-mean-square errors in temperature and currents &lt;2.5 °C and &lt;4 cm s −1 , respectively. Biogeochemical parameters were reproduced to the same order as observed. Light and nitrogen (N) limited algae growth were simulated within the Red River plume and North Basin, respectively, in agreement with observations. We model low ratios of N to phosphorus (P), which favours growth of N-fixing cyanobacteria.
