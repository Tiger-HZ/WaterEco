Integrated use of organic and inorganic sources of 
nitrogen for improving growth, yield, nutrient uptake 
and economic returns of wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) 
 
Vikas Dhaked, Anil Jakhar, Hansraj Shivran, Vaishali Chaturvedi, Indra 
Raj Yadav and LS Dhayal 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9k.6599 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment was conducted during Rabi season of 2025-26 at Agricultural Research Farm, Department 
of Agronomy, School of Agriculture Suresh Gyan Vihar University, Jaipur (Rajasthan) to influence of 
Integrated Use of Organic and Inorganic Sources of Nitrogen for Improving Growth, Yield, Nutrient Uptake 
and Economic Returns of Wheat ( Triticum aestivum L.). Ten nitrogen-management treatments comprising 
an unfertilized control, recommended dose of nitrogen (RDN) through urea, FYM or vermicompost, and 
different combinations of RDN through urea with FYM and vermicompost were evaluated. The results 
revealed that integrated nitrogen management markedly improved crop growth and productivity compared 
with the control. Treatment T5, consisting of 75% RDN through urea + 25% RDN through vermicompost, 
generally performed best among the treatments, recording the highest plant population at 30 DAS (116.85 
m⁻²) and harvest (108.02 m⁻²), plant height at 30 DAS (27.44 cm), 60 DAS (59.58 cm) and h arvest (96.52 
cm), total tillers (122.68 m⁻¹), effective tillers (111.95 m⁻¹), grains spike⁻¹ (53.75), test weight (40.55 g), 
grain yield (48.25 q ha⁻¹), straw yield (64.15 q ha⁻¹) and biological yield (112.40 q ha⁻¹). The same treatment 
also recorded the highest N uptake by grain and straw (98.43 and 48.11 kg ha⁻¹, respectively), P uptake (20.27 
and 11.61 kg ha⁻¹) and K uptake (20.60 and 90.45 kg ha⁻¹). However, the highest B ratio (2.79) was obtained 
with T4 (75% RDN through urea + 25% RDN through FYM). The results indicate that partial substitution of 
inorganic nitrogen with organic sources, particularly vermicompost, can improve wheat growth, yield 
formation and nutrient acquisition while providing an economically viable nutrient-management strategy. 
 
Keywords: Wheat, nitrogen management, urea, FYM, vermicompost, integrated nutrient management, 
yield, nutrient uptake, economics 
 
1. Introduction 
Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is one of the most important cereal crops and a major component of 
food security in India. Sustaining wheat productivity requires an adequate and balanced supply of 
plant nutrients, particularly nitrogen, because nitrogen plays a central role in chlor ophyll 
formation, photosynthetic activity, protein synthesis, vegetative growth and grain development. 
However, continuous dependence on inorganic fertilizers alone may result in inefficient nutrient 
use and deterioration of soil fertility when organic nutrient sources are neglected. 
Farmyard manure (FYM) and vermicompost provide nutrients more gradually than readily soluble 
mineral fertilizers and can contribute to improved soil biological activity, nutrient availability and 
soil organic matter. Consequent ly, integration of organic and inorganic nutrient sources has 
emerged as an important component of sustainable nutrient management. Recent studies in wheat 
have reported improvements in growth, yield and nutrient uptake through integrated application 
of inorganic fertilizers with FYM and vermicompost. 
The effectiveness of integrated nutrient management depends on the source and proportion of 
nutrients supplied. A suitable combination can provide readily available nitrogen during periods 
of high crop demand while organic materials provide a more sustained nutrient supply and 
improve the soil environment. Studies conducted under Indian conditions have shown that 
combinations of urea and FYM can enhance wheat yield and nitrogen uptake compared with sole 
nutrient sources.

Vermicompost has particular importance because it contains 
readily mineralizable nutrients and biologically active 
compounds that may improve nutrient availability and root 
growth. Recent research in Rajasthan and other wheat -growing 
regions has also demonstrated the potential of vermicompost -
based nutrient management for improving crop growth, 
productivity and economic returns. 
Therefore, the present study was undertaken to evaluate different 
combinations of inorganic nitrogen through urea and organic 
nitrogen through FYM and vermicompost and to identify an 
efficient nitrogen -management strategy for improving growth, 
yield, nutrient uptake and profitability of wheat. 
 
2. Materials and Methods 
A field experiment was conducted during the Rabi season of 
2025–26 at the Agricultural Research Farm, Department of 
Agronomy, School of Agriculture, Suresh Gyan Vihar University, 
Jaipur, Rajasthan, to influence of Integrated Use of Organic and 
Inorganic Sources of Nitrogen for Improving Growth, Yield, 
Nutrient Uptake and Economic Returns of Wheat ( Triticum 
aestivum L.). The experimental site is located in a semi -arid, 
subtropical climate characterized by extreme temperature 
variations, with hot and dry summers (maximum temperature up 
to 45°C) and cold winters (minimum temperature as low as 
2.8°C). The area receives an average annual rainfall of 500 –800 
mm, mostly concentrated during the monsoon season. The 
experiment evaluated ten nitrogen -management treatments in 
wheat. The treatments consisted of an untreated control and 
different combinations of the recommended dose of nitrogen 
(RDN) supplied through urea, FYM and vermicompost. 
 
Treatment details 
 
Treatment Nitrogen management 
T0 Control 
T1 RDN alone through urea 
T2 RDN alone through FYM 
T3 RDN alone through vermicompost 
T4 75% RDN through urea + 25% through FYM 
T5 75% RDN through urea + 25% through vermicompost 
T6 50% RDN through urea + 50% through FYM 
T7 50% RDN through urea + 50% through vermicompost 
T8 25% RDN through urea + 75% through FYM 
T9 25% RDN through urea + 75% through vermicompost 
 
Observations were recorded for plant population, plant height, 
total tillers, effective tillers, grains per spike, test weight, grain 
yield, straw yield, biological yield, harvest index, N, P and K 
concentration and nutrient uptake. Economic parameters included 
cost of cultivation, gross return, net return and benefit ratio. 
The statistical significance of treatment differences was assessed 
using the critical difference (CD) at the 5% level, as reported in 
the experimental data. 
 
3. Results and Discussion 
3.1 Plant population 
Nitrogen management influenced plant establishment and plant 
population numerically, although the differences were 
statistically non -significant at both 30 DAS and harvest. Plant 
population at 30 DAS ranged from 95.36 m⁻² in the control to 
116.85 m⁻² in T5, while the corresponding values at harvest were 
86.08 and 108.02 m⁻², respectively. T5 recorded the highest 
population, followed by T4 (113.45 and 105.08 m⁻²) and T3 
(111.32 and 102.95 m⁻²). 
The numerical improvement in plant population under integrated 
nitrogen management may be attributed to better nutrient 
availability during early crop establishment. Organic sources may 
have improved the physical and biological environment of the 
rhizosphere, while urea provided readily available nitrogen. 
Similar beneficial effects of integrated nutrient management on 
wheat establishment and growth have been reported in previous 
studies. 
 
3.2 Plant height 
Plant height was significantly influenced by nitrogen treatments. 
At 30 DAS, plant height increased from 20.32 cm u nder the 
control to 29.08 cm under T1, while T5 recorded 27.44 cm. At 60 
DAS, the maximum plant height was observed in T5 (59.58 cm), 
followed by T1 (62.38 cm) and T4 (57.88 cm). At harvest, T1 
recorded the highest plant height (101.06 cm), closely followe d 
by T5 (96.52 cm), whereas the control recorded only 71.47 cm. 
The increase in plant height under nitrogen application may be 
associated with enhanced cell division, cell elongation, 
chlorophyll formation and photosynthetic activity. Integrated 
application of urea with organic sources may have provided both 
immediately available and gradually mineralizable nitrogen. 
Similar improvements in wheat plant height under integrated 
nutrient management have been reported by Kaur et al. and other 
researchers. 
 
3.3 Total tillers 
Nitrogen management substantially affected total t iller 
production. The control produced 92.52 tillers m⁻¹ row length, 
whereas T5 recorded the maximum number of total tillers (122.68 
m⁻¹), followed by T4 (118.20 m⁻¹), T3 (115.45 m⁻¹) and T7 (112.36 
m⁻¹). The CD value of 10.27 indicates that treatment diff erences 
were statistically significant. 
Greater tiller production under integrated nitrogen management 
can be attributed to improved nitrogen availability during the 
vegetative phase, which promotes leaf area development, 
assimilate production and initiation of productive tillers. Similar 
enhancement in tillering under integrated nutrient management 
has been reported in wheat. 
 
3.4 Yield attributes 
The number of effective tillers, grains per spike and test weight 
were significantly affected by nitrogen management. T5 produced 
the highest effective tillers (111.95 m⁻¹), grains per spike (53.75) 
and test weight (40.55 g). These values were considerably higher 
than the corresponding values under the control, which were 
83.25 m⁻¹, 34.32 grains spike⁻¹ and 38.25 g, respectively. T4 
ranked next for effective tillers and grains per spike, recording 
108.05 effective tillers m⁻¹ and 50.45 grains spike⁻¹. 
The i mprovement in yield attributes may be associated with 
enhanced vegetative growth and better nutrient availability 
resulting from integrated nitrogen management. Adequate 
nitrogen promotes the development of productive tillers and 
supports reproductive grow th, while organic sources may 
improve nutrient -use efficiency and nutrient availability. 
Comparable improvements in yield attributes from integrated use 
of FYM, vermicompost and inorganic fertilizers have been 
reported in wheat. 
 
3.5 Grain, straw and biological yield 
Nitrogen management significantly increased wheat productivity. 
Grain yield increased from 34.36 q ha⁻¹ under the control to 48.25 
q ha⁻¹ under T5, representing an increase of approximately 40.4% 
over the control. T4 produced the second -highest grain yield 
(46.88 q ha⁻¹), followed by T3 (45.68 q ha⁻¹) and T7 (44.85 q ha⁻¹).

Straw yield also increased substantially, reaching 64.15 q ha⁻¹ 
under T5 compared with 47.45 q ha⁻¹ under the control. Similarly, 
biological yield increased from 81.81 q ha⁻¹ in the control to 
112.40 q ha⁻¹ under T5. Grain yield differences were statistically 
significant according to the reported CD value of 4.43 q ha⁻¹. 
The superiority of T5 may be attributed to the complementary 
effects of inorganic and organic nitrogen. The 75:25 combination 
of urea and vermicompost apparently provided an effective 
balance between immediate nitrogen availability and sustained 
nutrient release. Increased tiller production, grains per spike and 
test weight under T5 consequently translated into higher grain and 
biological yields. Similar yield advantages from integrated 
nutrient management have been reported in wheat under Indian 
conditions. 
 
3.6 Harvest index 
Harvest index varied from 37.21% under T1 to 46.46% under T3. 
T3 recorded the highest harvest index, followed by T2 (44.42%) 
and T9 (43.77%). However, treatment differences in harvest index 
were statistically non-significant. 
The comparatively higher harvest index under organic nitrogen 
treatments may indicate improved partitioning of accumulated 
biomass towards grain formation. However, the absence of 
significant differences suggests that nitrogen treatments affected 
total biomass production more strongly than the proportional 
partitioning of biomass between grain and straw. 
 
3.7 Nutrient content in grain and straw 
Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium concentrations in wheat 
grain and straw were significantly affected by nitrogen 
management. Grain N concentration ranged from 1.92% under 
the control to 2.06% under T1. T5 recorded 2.04% N. In straw, N 
concentration ranged from 0.67% in the control to 0.77% under 
T1. 
Phosphorus concentration in grain increased from 0.39% under 
the control to 0.48% under T3, while straw P increased from 0.175 
to 0.192%. Potassium concentration in grain ranged from 0.414% 
in the control to 0.448% under T3, whereas straw K increased 
from 1.35% to 1.55%. 
The higher nutrient concentration under organic and integrated 
treatments may be associated with improved nutrient availability 
and enhanced root activity. Organic amendments can contribute 
to nutrient mineralization and improve the soil environment, 
thereby facilitating nutrient acquisition by plants. Similar 
improvements in nutrient concentration and uptake have been 
documented under integrated nutrient management in wheat. 
3.8 Nutrient uptake 
Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium uptake by grain and straw 
were significantly increased under nitrogen management. T5 
recorded the highest N uptake in grain (98.43 kg ha⁻¹) and straw 
(48.11 kg ha⁻¹), compared with 65.97 and 31.79 kg ha⁻¹ under the 
control. Similarly, P uptake under T5 was 20.27 kg ha⁻¹ in grain 
and 11.61 kg ha⁻¹ in straw, while K uptake reached 20.60 and 
90.45 kg ha⁻¹, respectively. 
The greater nutrient uptake under T5 resulted from the combined 
effects of improved nutrient concentration and substantially 
greater biomass production. Nutrient uptake is determined by 
both nutrient concentration and crop yield; therefore, treatments 
that simultaneously enhance plant growth and nutrient acquisition 
generally record higher total nutrient removal. Comparable 
responses have been observed in wheat receiving integrated 
organic and inorganic nutrient sources. 
 
3.9 Economics 
Economic analysis revealed considerable variation among 
treatments. The cost of cultivation ranged from ₹92,500 ha⁻¹ 
under the control to ₹1,60,788 ha⁻¹ under T5. Gross return was 
highest under T5 (₹56,975 ha⁻¹ as reported in the dataset), 
followed by T9 (₹55,825 ha⁻¹) and T3 (₹61,500 ha⁻¹). The highest 
net return was recorded under T4 (₹1,15,053 ha⁻¹), followed by T7 
(₹99,490 ha⁻¹) and T6 (₹98,913 ha⁻¹). T4 also recorded the highest 
B ratio of 2.79. 
The superior economic performance of T4 may be attributed to its 
combination of high grain yield and relatively favourable cost of 
nutrient inputs. Although T5 produced the highest biological and 
grain yields, its higher cultivation cost reduced its B ratio to 1.82. 
This demonstrates that the t reatment producing maximum 
biological productivity does not necessarily provide the highest 
economic efficiency. Similar observations regarding the 
economic advantage of integrated nutrient management have 
been reported in wheat. 
 
4. Overall Discussion 
The results clearly demonstrate the importance of integrating 
organic and inorganic nitrogen sources for improving wheat 
productivity. Across most growth and yield parameters, T5 (75% 
RDN through urea + 25% RDN through vermicompost) emerged 
as the most produc tive treatment. It recorded the highest total 
tillers, effective tillers, grains per spike, test weight, grain yield, 
straw yield, biological yield and nutrient uptake.

The superior response of T5 suggests that partial substitution of 
mineral nitrogen with vermicompost can provide a favourable 
nutrient-supply pattern. Urea supplies readily available nitrogen 
required during periods of rapid crop growth, while 
vermicompost releases nutrients gradual ly and may improve the 
soil biological environment. This complementary nutrient supply 
could have promoted better crop growth, nutrient acquisition and 
yield formation. Recent research from Rajasthan has likewise 
demonstrated the productivity potential of vermicompost-based 
nutrient management in wheat. 
Interestingly, T4 (75% RDN through urea + 25% RDN through 
FYM) produced the highest B ratio. Thus, the present results 
indicate that T5 may be considered the best treatment for 
maximizing biological and grain productivity, whereas T4 may be 
preferred where economic efficiency is the primary consideration. 
These findings are consistent with earlier research demonstrating 
that integrated nutrient management can improve nutrient uptake 
and productivity compared with inadequate nutrient supply or 
exclusive dependence on organic sources. 
 
5. Conclusion 
The present investigation demonstrated that integrated 
application of organic and inorganic nitrogen sources 
substantially improved growth, yield attributes, yield and nutrient 
uptake of wheat compared with the untreated control. Among the 
tested treatments, 75% RDN through urea + 25% RDN through 
vermicompost ( T5) was the most effective treatment for 
maximizing plant growth, tillering, yield attributes, grain yield, 
straw yield, biological yield and N, P and K uptake. Grain yield 
under T5 reached 48.25 q ha⁻¹, compared with 34.36 q ha⁻¹ under 
the control. 
From an economic perspective, 75% RDN through urea + 25% 
RDN through FYM ( T4) recorded the highest B ratio (2.79) and 
net return (₹1,15,053 ha⁻¹). Therefore, T5 can be recommended 
for maximizing crop productivity, while T4 appears particularly 
attractive from the standpoint of economic efficiency. 
Overall, the findings support the adoption of integrated organic –
inorganic nitrogen management as a promising strategy for 
improving wheat productivity and nutrient -use efficiency while 
reducing exclusive dependence on mineral nitrogen fertilizers.
