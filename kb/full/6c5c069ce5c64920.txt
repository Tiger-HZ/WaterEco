While it is understood that the linkages of water and energy are important, they are complex and difficult to understand. The nexus must be addressed in a coordinated manner. Therefore, quantification of the water–energy nexus (WEN) must be understood to enable its optimization and informed decision-making. The identification and classification of indicators revealed that no single metric can adequately describe the complexity of the nexus. The methodological overview highlighted the diverse analytical tools available, from life cycle assessment and input–output models to multi-criteria decision analysis and emerging artificial intelligence-based techniques, each offering distinct strengths while also exhibiting notable limitations, particularly concerning data needs, interoperability, and representation of uncertainty. A wide range of frameworks, decision-support systems, and integrated modeling platforms have been developed to operationalize nexus thinking in real planning environments. The climate-oriented analysis underscored that WEN security cannot be achieved without explicit attention to climate risks, resilience strategies, governance mechanisms, and social equity considerations. By combining improved data systems, advanced modeling, coordinated governance, and adaptive planning, WEN can evolve from a conceptual challenge into a practical framework for achieving sustainable development in the future.
