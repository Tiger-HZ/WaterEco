Crude oil contamination of surface water remains a major environmental challenge in oil-producing regions, particularly in developing countries where conventional remediation technologies are often constrained by high costs and limited field applicability. This study evaluated the effectiveness of a solar-activated rice husk–TiO₂ (RH–TiO₂) nanocomposite for the remediation of crude oil-contaminated surface water, with emphasis on the removal of Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH), Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD), and Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD). A controlled batch experimental design compared four treatment systems: sunlight only (control), rice husk, TiO₂, and the RH–TiO₂ nanocomposite under natural solar irradiation. Initial water quality assessment confirmed severe petroleum contamination characterized by elevated hydrocarbon and organic pollutant levels, depleted dissolved oxygen, and slightly acidic conditions. Among all treatments, the RH–TiO₂ nanocomposite demonstrated the highest remediation efficiency, achieving 88.7% TPH, 82.4% COD, and 79.8% BOD removal after 21 days, significantly outperforming the individual rice husk and TiO₂ systems. The enhanced performance was attributed to the synergistic interaction between the adsorption capacity of rice husk and the photocatalytic activity of TiO₂ under solar irradiation, resulting in accelerated hydrocarbon degradation. Kinetic analysis indicated pseudo-first-order degradation with excellent model fit, while post-treatment water quality showed substantial improvements in dissolved oxygen and pH, reflecting partial ecological recovery. Statistical analysis confirmed significant differences among treatment systems (p &lt; 0.001). The findings demonstrate that the RH–TiO₂ nanocomposite is an efficient, low-cost, and environmentally sustainable technology for treating petroleum-contaminated surface water. Its reliance on renewable solar energy and agricultural waste aligns with circular economy principles and highlights its potential for scalable application in oil-producing regions.
