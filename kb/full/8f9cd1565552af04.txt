Abstract
Introduction: Postmenopausal women often experience vasomotor and hormonal symptoms due to a decline
in estradiol levels, which affects their quality of life (QOL). Botanical extracts like Vitex
 
extract, known for
their estrogen- and progesterone-like actions, may offer a safe and effective approach for alleviating
postmenopausal symptoms and improving overall quality of life.
Methods: The current study was a prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, monocenter
clinical study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of Vitex extract in postmenopausal women aged 40-65
years. Sixty eligible participants were randomized to either the Vitex extract or placebo group equally and
instructed to take one capsule daily after breakfast for 60 days. The primary endpoints included changes in
the Blatt-Kupperman Index (BKI), Menopause-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (MENQOL), Menopause
Rating Scale (MRS), and overall QOL scores. The study was prospectively registered with the Clinical Trials
Registry of India (CTRI/2025/03/082025).
Results: After 60 days, Vitex extract supplementation led to clinically and statistically significant
improvements compared with placebo in all primary outcomes. Notable reductions were observed in BKI,
MENQOL, and MRS scores, reflecting better control of vasomotor, psychosocial, physical, and sexual
symptoms. Improvements were also seen in the reduction in joint and muscle pain, stress levels, and serum
cortisol. Hormonal regulation of luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), estradiol,
and thyroid function, along with reduced rheumatoid factor, indicated anti-inflammatory and endocrine
benefits. No adverse effects or lab abnormalities were reported.
Conclusions: In this single-center study, Vitex extract appeared effective and was well tolerated, suggesting
its potential as a safe, non-hormonal option for relieving menopausal symptoms and improving quality of
life in postmenopausal women. However, these findings should be confirmed in larger, multi-center trials
before broader clinical recommendations can be made.

Introduction
Menopause represents a significant metabolic transition in women, marking the lifelong cessation of
menstruation due to a decline in ovarian follicular activity, clinically defined as amenorrhea lasting for 12
consecutive months. This period is frequently accompanied by a complex array of physical, psychological,
and emotional symptoms that substantially impact a woman's overall quality of life (QoL) 
[1]
.
Globally, menopause and its associated disorders are rising public health concerns, driven by increased life
expectancy and a growing aging female population. Projections indicate that by 2030, the number of
postmenopausal women will reach 1.2 billion worldwide, with nearly 80% experiencing menopausal
symptoms 
[2]
. These symptoms, which can range from mild to severe, include mood swings, depression,
sleep disturbances, hot flashes, night sweats, anxiety, and fatigue. Furthermore, long-term health risks such
as cardiovascular diseases (CVD), osteoporosis, obesity, diabetes, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD),
and dyslipidemia are associated with this transition 
[3]
.

and progesterone. Estrogen deficiency directly contributes to vasomotor symptoms like hot flashes and

Vitex agnus-castus,
 commonly known as vitex, chaste tree, or chasteberry, is native to tropical and
subtropical regions of Asia. For over 2500 years, it has been extensively used in traditional medicine for
managing premenstrual syndrome (PMS), infertility, menstrual irregularities, and menopausal complaints.
The fruit of Vitex contains various bioactive constituents, including essential oils, flavonoids, iridoids, and
diterpenoids. These compounds are believed to be responsible for the plant's diverse pharmacological
activities 
[8]
.
Modern pharmacological and clinical studies indicate that the actions of Vitex extract can be explained
through multiple interconnected mechanisms. These include dopaminergic pathway modulation,
characterized by D₂ receptor activation and subsequent improvement of luteal function, support of the
hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis through modulation of luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-
stimulating hormone (FSH) activity, contributing to progesterone production and maintenance of hormonal
balance; selective estrogen receptor fine-tuning, particularly through preferential interaction with estrogen
receptor beta (ER
β
), which may help alleviate estrogen-dominant states, and regulation of neurotransmitter
networks involving dopamine, serotonin, and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), thereby promoting mood
stability and sleep quality 
[9-11]
.
Collectively, these mechanisms provide the physiological basis for the broad spectrum of Vitex extract
applications in endocrine-related disorders, including PMS, premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD),
mastalgia, menstrual irregularities, luteal-phase dysfunction, infertility associated with mild
hyperprolactinemia, perimenopausal and natural menopausal syndromes, and neuroendocrine-related
emotional and sleep disturbances 
[9-11]
.
In contemporary scientific research, Vitex extract is no longer viewed solely as a phytoestrogen but rather as
a multi-target neuroendocrine modulator. Unlike conventional hormone replacement therapy, it does not
provide exogenous hormones; instead, it influences the central-pituitary-ovarian axis and neurotransmitter
pathways to promote physiological synchronization of endocrine rhythms and maintain hormonal
homeostasis. Consequently, Vitex extract has emerged as a representative nutritional endocrine modulator
in modern integrative medicine, with the potential to improve endocrine dysfunction associated with stress,
sleep disturbances, nutritional inadequacies, and age-related hormonal fluctuations, ultimately
contributing to symptom relief and enhanced QoL 
[9]
.
The novelty of the present study lies in its comprehensive clinical assessment of Vitex extract, specifically
in postmenopausal women, a demographic where robust human trials remain limited. While Vitex extract
has been effectively studied for premenstrual and reproductive disorders (PMRD), its targeted role in
postmenopausal symptom relief and enhancement of QoL has not been fully established. This study aims to
delineate the multidimensional benefits of Vitex extract, including its potential for pain reduction,
vasomotor stability, mood regulation, and hormonal balance, through a standardized clinical trial.
Materials And Methods
This was a prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, two-arm study
conducted at Sri Venkateswara Hospital, Bengaluru, Karnataka, India, from March 18, 2025, to July 15, 2025,
to evaluate the safety and efficacy of Vitex extract in postmenopausal women. Eligible participants were
randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either Vitex extract or placebo for 60 days. The study protocol and
informed consent documents were reviewed and approved by the Sri Venkateshwara Hospital Ethics
Committee (protocol number RRS/CL/PM/VITEX/2024, dated October 30, 2024). The study was prospectively
registered with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI/2025/03/082025). Eligible participants were
informed about the study objectives, procedures, potential risks, and benefits by the investigator. Written
informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to study enrollment in a language understandable
to the participant.

Study participants
Inclusion Criteria
Women aged 40-65 years who were in menopause, defined as the absence of menstruation for 12 consecutive
months and having serum FSH levels >30 mIU/mL, were eligible for participation. Participants were required
to have a Blatt-Kupperman Index (BKI) score ≥25 or report clinically significant menopausal symptoms
despite a BKI score <25.
Exclusion Criteria
Women were excluded if they had used hormone preparations, plant-based hormone substitutes, thyroid
hormones, clonidine, anticoagulants, antithrombotic agents, or menopause-related supplements within the
past three months, or had a history of drug abuse. Participants receiving osteoporosis treatments such as
bisphosphonates, or those with a body mass index (BMI) >30 kg/m², hormone-related cancers, hysterectomy,
or severe cardiovascular, thromboembolic, or cerebrovascular diseases were also excluded. Participants with
psychiatric disorders, uncontrolled hypertension or diabetes, thyroid dysfunction, abnormal uterine
bleeding, significant hepatic or renal impairment (alanine transaminase (ALT)/aspartate aminotransferase
(AST) > 3 × upper limit of normal (ULN); estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 60 mL/minute/1.73
m²), or endometrial thickness ≥ 7 mm were excluded.

Investigational product (IP)
The IP was a 300 mg capsule containing Vitex extract, standardized to provide 4.5 mg of 100% agnuside,
manufactured and registered by Ingex Botanicals Pvt. Ltd., Deganahalli, Karnataka, India. The IP was
produced in compliance with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) and relevant regulatory standards.
Participants in the intervention (Vitex) group received one capsule orally once daily after breakfast for 60
days (two months). The placebo capsules were matched in appearance, size, color, and packaging and were
administered according to the same dosing schedule.
Randomization and blinding
The study followed a randomized, double-blind design. Eligible participants were randomly assigned to the
different intervention arms according to a computer-generated randomization schedule. Blinded
randomization codes were generated by an independent statistician using the R statistical software version
4.4.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), and the active and placebo products were
assigned randomly. Block randomization with a fixed block size of 2 was implemented to ensure balanced
allocation between the study groups throughout the trial period.
The study was conducted under double-blind conditions, wherein neither the investigators/site personnel
nor the participants were aware of the IP allocation. The randomization list was maintained in a password-
protected format and was accessible only to designated unblinded pharmacy personnel responsible for IP
management and dispensing. The IP was dispensed sequentially according to the predefined randomization
schedule as participants qualified for enrollment in the study.

Menopause-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (MENQOL)
The MENQOL is a validated instrument used to assess the impact of menopausal symptoms on QoL 
[14]
. It
evaluates symptoms across four domains, namely vasomotor, psychosocial, physical, and sexual. Each
questionnaire item is scored on a scale from 0 to 6, where 0 indicates absence of symptoms and higher scores
indicate greater symptom severity. Domain scores are calculated as the mean of questionnaire items within
each domain, with higher scores indicating poorer QoL. The license for the use of the MENQOL was obtained
from Mapi Research Trust.
Total Menopause Rating Scale (MRS)
The MRS is a tool used to evaluate the severity of symptoms associated with menopause and their impact on
a woman's QoL 
[15]
. It is specifically designed to measure the frequency and intensity of various menopausal
symptoms and to assess their effect on daily activities. The MRS score evaluated the severity of menopausal
symptoms across somatic, psychological, and urogenital domains, with each item scored on a 0 to 4 scale (0
= no symptoms, 4 = very severe symptoms). Tracking changes in MRS scores provides valuable insights into
the overall impact of Vitex extract on menopause-related QoL.
SF-36 (36-Item Short-Form) Health Survey Questionnaire
The SF-36 Health Survey Questionnaire is a widely used QoL assessment tool designed to evaluate an
individual’s overall health status and well-being across physical, emotional, and social domains 
[16]
. It
measures the impact of health on daily activities, pain, energy levels, emotional health, and general
perception of health over the previous four weeks. The scores are interpreted on a scale where higher scores
indicate better health-related QoL, improved physical and emotional well-being, and lesser impairment in
daily activities, whereas lower scores indicate poorer perceived health status and greater functional
limitations.
Biomarker analysis
Biomarker assessments were performed at baseline and Day 60 to evaluate endocrine and stress-related
physiological responses associated with the intervention. Changes in biomarker levels over the study period
were analyzed to assess the potential effect of the intervention on stress regulation and hypothalamic-
pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis stabilization. Serum concentrations of the hormonal biomarkers, including
FSH, LH, cortisol, and estradiol, were assessed to evaluate the endocrine and reproductive hormonal status
of postmenopausal women. FSH and LH were measured as indicators of pituitary gonadotropin activity
associated with menopausal status; estradiol was evaluated to assess circulating estrogen levels, and cortisol

Quantitative estimation of these biomarkers was performed using commercially available enzyme-linked

All biomarker assays were conducted according to validated analytical procedures with appropriate

clinical laboratory standards to ensure accuracy, reliability, and reproducibility of the results.
Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 25.0 (IBM Corp.,
Armonk, New York, United States). All statistical tests were conducted at a significance level of 0.05 using
two-sided tests unless otherwise specified. Categorical variables were summarized by treatment group using
frequency counts and percentages of participants. Continuous variables were summarized using descriptive
statistics, including number of participants, mean, standard deviation (SD), median, and range.
The normality of continuous data distributions was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. For inter-group
comparisons of continuous variables, an independent two-sample t-test was used when data satisfied the
assumption of normality. In cases where normality assumptions were not met, the Wilcoxon rank sum test
was applied. For categorical variables, Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was performed as
appropriate based on the distribution of the data. Within-group (intra-group) comparisons between baseline
and post-intervention measurements were analyzed using paired t-tests for normally distributed data or
Wilcoxon signed rank tests for non-normally distributed data.
Group differences were calculated as the value of the Vitex group minus that of the control group unless
otherwise specified. Change from baseline was determined by subtracting the baseline value from the post-
intervention value. The baseline value was defined as the last non-missing assessment obtained prior to the
first intake of the investigational product for efficacy endpoints and vital signs, or the value measured
during the screening visit for hematology and serum biochemistry parameters. All statistical results were
reported to two decimal places. p-values were reported to four decimal places, with values less than 0.0001
presented as “<0.0001.” The p-value threshold for statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion
This clinical study was conducted to evaluate the safety and efficacy of Vitex extract in managing symptoms
associated with postmenopausal syndrome compared with a placebo. The results clearly showed that Vitex
extract significantly improved menopausal symptoms, including vasomotor disturbances, anxiety, and
physical discomfort, after 60 days of treatment. On the other hand, the placebo group also showed a mild
reduction in symptoms, which may be explained by suggestibility or the placebo effect.
The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of Vitex extract in alleviating menopausal
symptoms using validated clinical assessment tools. A significant reduction in the BKI total score was
observed (Figure 
2
), decreasing from 26.92 at baseline at Day 0 to 18.70 at Visit 3 at Day 60 (p < 0.0001),
indicating a marked improvement in overall menopausal symptoms. Similarly, the MENQOL results (Figure
3
) demonstrated significant improvements across the major symptom domains, including vasomotor
symptoms, psychosocial symptoms, and sexual symptoms, reflecting an overall enhancement in
menopause-related QoL. Furthermore, the MRS results (Figure 
4
) showed a 37.42% reduction in the total
MRS score from baseline at day 0 to Visit 3 at day 60 (p < 0.0001), confirming a statistically significant
improvement in the severity of menopausal symptoms following Vitex extract supplementation.
Changes in health-related QoL were assessed using the SF-36 Health Survey, and participants receiving
Vitex extract demonstrated significant improvements in multiple SF-36 domains compared with the placebo
group by Visit 3. Physical Functioning showed a marked improvement, with scores increasing from 394.44 at
baseline at day 0 to 788.89 at Visit 3 at day 60, whereas the placebo group exhibited only a modest increase
(371.15 to 417.31). The between-group difference was highly significant (p < 0.0001). Role Limitations due to
Physical Health also improved substantially in the Vitex group, increasing from 133.33 to 311.11, compared
with a smaller increase in the placebo group (138.46 to 161.54), demonstrating a significant treatment effect

14
 of 
18
(p < 0.01). Similarly, Role Limitations due to Emotional Problems increased from 81.48 to 214.81 in the Vitex
group, while the placebo group showed minimal change (103.85 to 111.54), indicating significant
improvement (p < 0.001). The Vitality (Energy/Fatigue) domain demonstrated a significant increase in the
Vitex group from 215.22 to 230.37, whereas scores in the placebo group progressively declined from 209.50 to
168.46, suggesting improved energy levels and reduced fatigue following supplementation (p < 0.0001).
Emotional Well-being (Mental Health) improved significantly in the Vitex group, with scores increasing from
280.74 to 370.37, compared with only a slight increase in the placebo group (266.92 to 276.15) (p < 0.0001).
Although Social Functioning showed a numerical improvement in the Vitex group (92.31 to 101.85), the
difference between groups was not statistically significant. The General Health domain demonstrated a
significant increase from 274.04 at baseline to 397.22 at Visit 3 in the Vitex group, whereas placebo scores
remained essentially unchanged (262.96 to 274.04), indicating a significant enhancement in participants'
overall perception of health (p < 0.0001). Finally, the Bodily Pain domain showed significant improvement
in the Vitex group, with scores increasing from 122.78 to 172.41, while placebo scores changed minimally
(111.54 to 116.92), indicating reduced pain-related limitations (p < 0.0001). Based on the SF-36 Health
Survey graphs, the Vitex extract group showed a consistent improvement in several domains of health-
related QoL compared with the placebo group over the 60-day study period. Since higher SF-36 scores
indicate better health status, the observed increases reflect improvements in participants' physical and
mental well-being. Overall, these findings demonstrate that 60 days of Vitex extract supplementation
significantly improved both physical and psychological aspects of health-related QoL, with the greatest
benefits observed in physical functioning, general health, emotional well-being, vitality, role limitations due
to physical and emotional problems, and bodily pain compared with placebo. 
The secondary endpoints were to measure changes in the frequency and severity of vasomotor symptoms
like hot flushes and night sweats, musculoskeletal discomfort, and psychological stress, which were assessed
through serum cortisol levels. Similar to the present study, earlier randomized, double-blind, placebo-
controlled trials have examined the effects of Vitex extract in peri- and post-menopausal women. A study
involving 52 women found that eight weeks of Vitex extract led to significant reductions in overall
menopausal symptom scores, anxiety, and vasomotor complaints compared with placebo, while no major
changes were observed in depression, somatic symptoms, or sexual function 
[17]
. These results are in line
with the improvements seen in our study, particularly in emotional well-being, general health, and overall
quality-of-life outcomes.
The improvement in the Vitex extract group was much greater compared to placebo, with clinically and
statistically significant improvements, confirming the clinical effectiveness of the extract. The findings of
this study are consistent with earlier research. A randomized, double-blind study showed Vitex extract
significantly reduced total menopausal symptoms 
[17]
. Similarly, a double-blind clinical trial demonstrated
that Vitex extract effectively reduced vasomotor symptoms such as hot flushes 
[12]
. Vitex extract was also
found to be useful for improving menopausal symptoms 
[18]
. Consistent results from multiple studies
provide compelling evidence that Vitex extract possesses potential efficacy in alleviating menopausal
symptoms and enhancing QoL.
The mechanism behind these improvements may be related to the hormonal activity of the plant. Vitex
contains biologically active compounds, such as flavonoids, which are known to influence the pituitary
gland. This gland regulates the secretion of LH, which helps increase progesterone levels and restore
hormonal balance. The extract may also help reduce prolactin levels, as shown in earlier animal studies,
contributing to the relief of menopausal symptoms and better regulation of hormonal activity 
[19]
.
The hormonal findings of this study support these mechanisms, as thyroid hormones (T3, T4, and TSH)
remained stable while the Vitex extract group showed a modest but statistically significant decrease in FSH
levels (-5.64%) by Visit 3, compared with almost no change in the placebo group. This indicates that Vitex
extract may have an effect on gonadotropin regulation, which could be important in managing endocrine
changes in postmenopausal women. In addition, the marked reduction in overall symptom scores
(approximately 37% decrease in total MRS in the population) is comparable to the improvements reported in
earlier clinical trials 
[20]
.
Another clinical study reported that Vitex extract significantly reduced both the frequency and intensity of
hot flashes (vasomotor symptoms) over an eight-week period compared with placebo, indicating that it may
be particularly effective in managing early vasomotor symptoms in postmenopausal women 
[16]
. Beyond the
menopausal period, Vitex extract has long been used for various female reproductive conditions such as
PMS, luteal phase defects, cyclic breast pain, and other hormone-related disorders. A systematic review of
randomized controlled trials found that Vitex extracts were more effective than placebo, pyridoxine, or
magnesium in reducing PMS symptoms; in fact, well-standardized Vitex extract preparations were
associated with more than twice the likelihood of symptom improvement compared with placebo 
[21,11]
.
Pharmacological studies showed that Vitex extract contains a diverse range of phytochemicals-such as
flavonoids, iridoids, diterpenes, phenolic acids, and terpenes-that may produce estrogen-like, antioxidant,
dopaminergic, and neuroendocrine effects. The dopaminergic actions of Vitex extract, in particular, may

15
 of 
18
influence the pituitary gland’s release of prolactin, LH, and FSH, which can indirectly affect ovarian steroid
production and overall hormonal regulation 
[22]
. This type of hormonal modulation may help improve well-
being, increase energy levels, and reduce menopausal symptoms over time. The combined phytoestrogenic,
dopaminergic, antioxidant, and possible neuroendocrine actions of Vitex extract give it a broad and multi-
targeted therapeutic profile. With increasing interest in herbal and phytoestrogen-based treatments, the
findings of this study support the idea that Vitex can enhance several aspects of postmenopausal health and
QoL without causing major hormonal disturbances.
Although previous reviews show that Vitex extract is usually well tolerated and side effects are uncommon,
some reactions such as digestive discomfort, headaches, or mild hormonal shifts can occur, and its long-
term safety in postmenopausal women still needs more investigation 
[23]
. In this study, Vitex extract use in
postmenopausal participants produced notable and clinically meaningful improvements in menopausal
symptoms, overall QoL, emotional well-being, pain, energy, social functioning, and general health. The
hormonal assessments showed modest but favorable changes, including a reduction in FSH and stable
thyroid hormone levels, suggesting that Vitex extract may act as a phytoestrogenic and neuroendocrine
modulator rather than a direct hormone replacement. These outcomes are consistent with earlier clinical
trials and pharmacological studies indicating that Vitex extract can reduce menopausal symptoms,
especially vasomotor complaints, anxiety, and overall symptom burden. While these findings are promising,
larger and more robust studies with standardized extracts and long-term follow-up are required to confirm
the effectiveness and safety of Vitex extract as a treatment option for postmenopausal syndrome.
This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. The
intervention period was limited to 60 days, which may not fully capture the long-term efficacy and safety
profile of Vitex extract in postmenopausal women. In addition, the relatively modest sample size and single-
center study design may restrict the generalizability of the results to broader and more diverse populations.
The study also did not assess variations in treatment response based on menopausal stage, baseline
hormonal status, or lifestyle-related factors that could influence outcomes. Furthermore, the absence of
extended follow-up after completion of supplementation limits conclusions regarding the sustainability of
symptom improvement and long-term hormonal stability, highlighting the need for larger multicenter trials
with longer intervention durations.
Conclusions
This clinical study demonstrates that Vitex extract, administered for 60 days, provides meaningful clinical
benefits for postmenopausal women by improving menopausal symptoms and overall quality of life more
effectively than placebo. Participants receiving Vitex extract experienced notable improvements across
multiple domains, including vasomotor symptoms, psychological well-being, musculoskeletal discomfort,
physical functioning, social and emotional health, and general quality of life, with benefits emerging early
and strengthening over the study period. These symptomatic improvements were supported by favorable
hormonal trends, such as healthier gonadotropin regulation and mild enhancement of estradiol levels, along
with reduced cortisol values indicating better stress modulation and emotional stability.
The treatment was well tolerated, with vital signs, physical examinations, and safety assessments showing
no clinically significant abnormalities, confirming a strong safety profile for short-term use. Together, these
results suggest that Vitex extract may act through both hormonal and neuroendocrine pathways and may
serve as a safe, natural, non-hormonal alternative for managing postmenopausal symptoms. However, the
study also presents certain limitations, including a relatively short treatment duration, a modest sample
size, and the absence of long-term follow-up to assess sustained efficacy, safety, and hormonal stability.
Additionally, the study did not evaluate variations in response based on menopausal stage, lifestyle factors,
or baseline hormone levels, which may further influence clinical outcomes. Considering these gaps, future
research should involve large-scale sample sizes, multicenter trials with longer treatment periods and
extended follow-up to validate the therapeutic potential of Vitex and clarify its broader role in supporting
women’s health during menopause.
