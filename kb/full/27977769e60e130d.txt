1 Introduction
With rapid urbanization in China, more and more wastewater treatment plants (WWTP) were built during
past decades(Cao, Zhang, Zhang, & Wang, 2021). Wastewater treatment involves the formation of large
quantities of waste activated sludge (WAS), and is now becoming a big problem. Its large amount, high
water content, and richness in organics makes it dicult to be treated and disposed properly (Ai et al.,
2021). Dewatering is regarded as the rst step in reducing the volume of the sludge, which could improve
the eciency in disposal and reuse (Ai et al., 2019). However, the raw sludge commonly presents poor
dewaterability due to the colloidal structure formed by the macromolecular polymers produced by the
microorganisms. From previous research has indicated that the dewaterability of WAS is dependent on
the rheological properties, microstructure characteristics, and extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) of
the sludge (Wei et al., 2020). The higher anity between the water and organic matter commonly due to
the stronger viscosity of the sludge and the higher bound water content. Particle size distribution, surface
charge, as well as micromorphology of the sludge particles also pose strong impact on the dewaterability
(Cao et al., 2018). The microstructure features can be adjusted by chemical coagulants to achieve higher
eciency in water removal, and the chemical coagulants such as ferric or aluminum salts, and
polyacrylamide (PAM) have been widely used (Li et al., 2022). EPS is considered as the most important
parameter determining the dewaterability of WAS, since extremely hydrated EPS bind a signicant
volume of water. This boundary water can be rened into either sterically binding water (e.g. interstitial
water) or chemical boundary water (e.g. vicinal water)(Wu, Zhou, He, Chai, & Dai, 2019), the state of which
depends on water retention capability of EPS substrate.
Besides the conventional WAS treatment by chemical coagulants, EPS structure destruction would be an
alternative path to enhance water removal in dewatering process by means of releasing bound water
from the sludge (Chen, Dai, & Dong, 2022a; Liang et al., 2020a). The process of chemical oxidation poses
a high potential in decomposition of organic matters, and different kinds of oxidants such as ozone,
Page 3/27
H2O2, NaClO and ferrate have been adopted in conditioning WAS (Wen et al., 2022). The "green oxidizer"
potassium permanganate (KMnO4) has played attention for its well oxidizing properties, relative stability
over the broad range of pH (Feng et al., 2022; Kou, Wang, Zhao, Jiang, & Xu, 2021), safe storage and
delivery, and relatively low cost, as well as its nontoxic product of MnO2, which is insoluble in water. It
presents oxidative features towards organic matters with phenolic, olen, thiol, ketone groups and amino
groups, and it can also be applid in water disinfection (Zhang et al., 2019), algae growth controlling and
toxicant degradation. In recent years, the KMnO4 was adopted in sludge dewatering pretreating. Lidong
Kou (Kou et al., 2021) reported that the application of KMnO4 signicantly improved the sludge
dewaterability, in which the SRF and mud cake moisture content decreased by 38.7% and 32.6%,
respectively. Hua Wei (Yin et al., 2021) pointed that the SRF of the sludge were slightly improved under
the condition of low dose of KMnO4, and part of the soluble EPS was compressed due to the coagulation
effect of the reactive products of MnO2.
Furthermore, recent researches have indicated that the EPS structure is powerfully effected by EPS
interaction with metal ions, because Ca2+ or Fe3+ can form a bridge between polyanionic alginate
molecules, and thus enhance the strength and structural stability of sludge particulate (Bai et al., 2022;
Wei et al., 2020). The authors report that large quantities of metals were commonly bound to organic
matter by fractionation in sludge ocs (Yang et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2022), and removal of Fe3+ from
WAS induced the dissolution of EPS. In recent years, isoelectric point (PI) pretreat was introduced to
separate metal ions from sludge (Guo et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2019). If the net charge on a molecule is
equal to zero at its PI, the intermolecular or intramolecular electrostatic attractions and repulsions that
maintain the structure of the molecule could disappear, causing the molecules to become unstable (Chen
et al., 2022b; Hu et al., 2018). As widely acknowledged from literatures, the PI of WAS was usually less
than pH 4.0, which means that if the pH of sludge was adjusted to lower than that point, quantities of
heavy metals can be released from WAS into solution, and thus the structural stability of EPS can be
reduced (Citeau, Larue, & Vorobiev, 2011). In addition, the lowered pH condition in treatment is also
helpful for occulation of sludge particles due to the reduced electrostatic repulsion among each other. In
terms of EPS oxidation, the lower pH in PI treatment also facilitate KMnO4 oxidation due to the promoted
oxidative ability of KMnO4 under such acidic pH environment (Zhang et al., 2012), which can quickly
destroy the EPS structure and release the bound water.
Based upon analysis, the combination of PI and pre-treatment with KMnO4 may be doable to improve
decomposition and dewatering of drilling waste sludge in theory (Li et al., 2018). Until now, though the
effect of solution pH on KMnO4 oxidation has been reported (Li et al., 2019; Xiao, Dai, Wang, Chen, &
Dong, 2021b), the information about such kind of combination is still limited. Relative mechanism drives
the dewaterability of drilling waste sludge deserves deep exploration (Liang et al., 2020b; Wu et al., 2017).
Furthermore, it remains unclear if and how the combination pretreatment affected physiochemical
characteristics, as well as the organics constituents of WAS.

The aim of the present study is therefore to investigate the feasibility and mechanism of combining
KMnO4 and PI treatment on the enhancement of WAS dewaterability. The main objectives of this work are
to (i) explore the feasibility of pH/KMnO4 treatment in order to improve the dewaterability of sludge; (ii)
study variations in the physicochemical properties of slurries and biopolymers in EPS. This work will
propose a new method for sludge pretreatment to improve the dewaterability of sludge.
2 Materials And Methods
2.1 Sludge and reagents
WAS was collected from the secondary sedimentation tank of Jiguanshi WWTP in Chongqing, China. The
sludge were gravity settled to increase the sludge concentration to about 4.00 ± 0.03% (TS), and the
thickened samples were stored at 4°C before use. Characteristics of the sludge are presented in Table S1.
All chemicals were analytical reagent grade. All stock solutions were prepared with deionized water. The
KMnO4 were purchased from Chongqing Xingguang Chemical Glass Company, China. A KMnO4 stock
solution with the concentration of 3 g/L was prepared for subsequent use.
2.2 Experimental procedure
First, 150 mL of WAS with the TS of 4% was taken and placed into a 250 mL triangular ask. pH
adjustment was conducted rstly to achieve nal slurry pH of 3.0, 5.0, and 7.0 by using 0.1 M HCl. After
pH adjustment, different doses of potassium permanganate were added into the slurry, and then the ask
was stirred in a water bath agitator with a speed of 150 RPM for 30 min under 25 oC. The conditioned
sludge was then taken out for analysis.
2.3 Analytical methods
2.3.1 Dewatering performance
SRF (Specic Resistance of Filtration) was determined according to previous study (Chen et al., 2022b).
In each dewatering ltration experiment, 50 mL of sewage sludge was placed in an 8 cm Buchner funnel
under a ltration pressure of 0.7 MPa for ltration of the sludge. Detailed steps are shown in Text S1. CST
(Capillary Suction Time) is another common used parameter to evaluate sludge lterability. It was
analyzed by the DFC10A.
2.3.2 EPS extraction and analysis
EPS extraction of sludge samples was implemented by centrifugation and thermal method as described
by (Dai et al., 2017). The procedure was detailed in Supplementary material Text S2. Protein(PN) and
polysaccharide(PS) concentrations of the extracted EPS were determined according to (Dai et al., 2017).
Briey, PN was quantied by a modied Lowry method with bovine serum albumin. PS was measured
using an anthrone method with glucose as standard.

2.3.3 Water occurrence state characterized by the low eld
NMR relaxation time T2
Based on the previous study, because of the different anities of the water with the surrounding medium,
under the low-eld radio frequency pulse of NMR, the transverse relaxation time (T2) of protons differs. T2
can thus be used as an indicator that probes the mobility of water molecules (Wu et al., 2019).
In the present study, WAS sample was concentrated by vacuum ltration to yield a slurry lter cake. The
concentrated slurry sample was then placed ina cylindrical polytetrauoroethylene tube with a diameter
of 18 mm and subjected to measurement of the transverse relaxation time by low eld NMR (NMPQ001
NMR system, Niumag Electric Corporation, Suzhou, China). Detailed instrument tuning and data
processing referenced from earlier studies(Wu et al., 2019).
2.3.4 Free radicals analysis

then examined by EPR after mixing at a xed time interval. Details of the procedure were provided in
Supplementary material Text S4.
2.3.5 Zeta potential
Different sludge samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 5 minutes, and then the supernatant was
collected and measured with a Zeta potentiometer (Biue Label,Red Label).

3 Results And Discussion
3.1 Improvement in sludge dewaterability by using KMnO4
under different pH
WAS conditioning was conducted under diverse initial pH conditions (3.0, 5.0, 7.0) with different KMnO4
concentrations, and the dewaterability of the conditioned sludge was taken in Fig. 1. As can be proved
from the Fig. 1 that, the combination application of acidity and KMnO4 signicantly improves the
dewaterability of WAS. For the raw WAS without pH adjustment, the CST of the sludge were decreased
from about 167.3 s to 90 s, and the SRF dwindled from 4.6×1012 to 3.3×1012 m/kg with the increase of
the KMnO4 dosage from 5 to 20 mg/gTS, revealing the accepted performance of KMnO4 in WAS
conditioning, and higher dosage commonly brings better dewaterability. The adjustment of the sludge pH
to acidic condition improved the dewaterability, while the lower CST and SRF were observed at lower pH
conditions, suggesting that the individual acidity treatment poses signicant effect on the dewaterability
of the sludge. This is similar to previous studies, which have shown EPS to decay more rapidly as pH
levels drop(Lin et al., 2022; Ling et al., 2021). Further improvement of the WAS dewaterability was
observed when KMnO4 was applied under acidic pH conditions. When the KMnO4 was added at pH 5.0,
the CST decreased from 121.8s to 41.9s while the SRF dwindled from 2.5×1012 to 1.5×1012 m/kg with
the increase of dosage from 0 mg/gTS to 20 mg/gTS. The conditions under pH 3.0 presented the highest
promotion of the dewaterability. The CST and SRF at pH 3.0 with the KMnO4 dosage of 20 mg/gTS were
30.1 s and 9×1011 m/kg, respectively. In addition, there is a much higher separation rate of the water from
sludge (Figure S1).These results demonstrated that the acidic conditions further facilitated solid-liquid
separation during KMnO4 oxidation, and they presented signicant synergism.
3.2 Low-eld NMR T2 distribution tracking water state
In WAS the condition of occurrence of water is extremely potent to the water removal productivity of the
dewatering process. Therefore, the oxidative performance of KMnO4 in sludge conditioning was also
appraise by estimating the water fugacity state characterized by low eld NMR spectra of T2(Wen et al.,
2022). According to previous studies, four discrete peaks can be found in the T2 distribution spectrum,
indicating the continuance of four different states of protons T21, T22, T23, and T24, respectively, while the
more distinct peak in this paper is T21, T23 (Wu, Wang, Li, Dai, & Chai, 2022). T21 was found to be
designated as a proton in solid form, while T24, T23 and T22 agree to stable water that binds powerfully to
organic through hydrogen bonds (attached water). Water physically trapped in a biological occulant due
to steric hindrance or adsorption ((interstitial water), which is least inuenced by solid composition. The
range of 0 to 1.2 ms and 2.5 to 9 ms(Wu, Wang, Wang, Dai, & Chai, 2021), respectively, were remained T21
Page 7/27
and T22,indicating that the conditioning processes had little effect on the movability of solid-state
protons and vicinal water. Bonded hydrogen atoms dominate the vicinal water interaction with the solid
surface.Thus, conditioned-induced changes in bioocculants microstructure do not affect vicinal water
uidity and migration of T2 (Wu et al., 2022). It can be clearly seen in Fig. 2 that T21, T22 are nearly
unchanged and there is no sign of T2 motion. However, even a 4-fold or 6-fold change in the transverse
spin-spin relaxation time of T23 and T24 was observed (Table 1). The microstructure and macrocapillary
structure of bioocculants are the key determinants of the properties of interstitial water. High oxidation
may loosen the bioocs, and thus left transfer the conforming T2 by increasing the trapping force of the
interstitial water.
Suggesting the attendance of two main proton states in the crude sludge, Fig. 2 shows that the single
dominant peak and the single small peak show the original T2 spectrum of the sludge. In agreement with
previous studies, longer T2 times are indicative of lower binding energy and greater water mobility;
whereas the decreasing T2 stands for compact coupling between water and surrounding material(Wu et
al., 2021). The leftmost peak at 0.2 ms should thus be referred to as a solid proton(Wu et al., 2019), in
which the content is extremely small compared to that of a water molecule and does not change in any
substantial way at different doses of KMnO4. T22 and T23 comform, respectively, to xed water that is
tightly bound to the organic phase by a variety of physiochemical bonds and to water that is physically
xed in biological occulants only through spatial resistance (Wu et al., 2019). Total bound water content
in WAS is due to these two water fractions. In agreement with the data presented in Table 1, peaks in T23
at approximately 40 ms performanced in the spectra of the samples treated with 5 to 20 mg/gTS KMnO4.
In the total peak area, the fraction of the T23 peak area also continued to increase (A23%) as the dose
levels of KMnO4 were increased. These phenomena indicated that increasing KMnO4 addition shifted the
T2 peak farther from the T2 peak, indicating that the oxidation process is weakening the water-keeping
capacity of the bioocculant. Likewise, increasing acidity resulted in a change in the T2 peak, but the T24
peak was not seen, and the A23% peak was only 0.323%. Thus, it can be deduced that pH has a more
limited effect on the water occurrence statuts of WAS than oxidation.

3.3 Variations in sludge physicochemical properties
The sludge mixture’s many of the physicochemical properties, such as relative hydrophobicity and zeta
potential, are important in settling the dewaterability of the sludge. Therefore, the effects of the pH
condition and/or KMnO4 treatment on these properties were investigated. The electric potential Zeta
reects the surface charge of the contents of the sludge akes. In general, a stronger electrostatic
repulsion of sludge particles is indicated a higher absolute value of zeta potential, which is not conducive
to sludge dehydration. It is widely reported that the surface of the raw WAS is covered by a great many of
negatively charged groups, which lowers the zeta potential of the WAS (Li, Zeng, & Yu, 2021; Liu, 2019). In
our study, the Zeta potential of the WAS was about − 11.4 mV. The pH adjustment and the participation of
KMnO4 oxidation brought signicant increase of the Zeta potential, and the outcomes are presented in
Fig. 3(a). It has been proved from the Fig. 3(a) that the zeta potential of the WAS was signicantly
promoted from − 11.4 to -3.2 mV by adjusting the slurry’s pH values alone (Fig. 3(a)). Some previous
studies showed that the increase of zeta potential would cause the dropping of electrostatic repulsion
among negatively charged particles. According to the Derjaguin-Landau-Verwey-Overbeek theory, when
the surface charge is negative, the interaction decreases and ultimately improves the dewatering
properties(Kou et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2016; Liu, Ran, & Hua, 2021). Additionally, the participation of
KMnO4 further increases the zeta potential. At pH = 3.0, as the dosage of KMnO4 was increased from 5 to
20 mg/gTS, the zeta potential was increased more abruptly from − 2.80 to -0.38 mV. Strong oxidation of
organic polymers occurred when KMnO4 was adopted at acidic pH condition. The formation of ·O2−
radicals in KMnO4 participated reactor can also be observed in the EPR paramagnetic resonance results
shown in Fig. 3(b), which also indicates the strong oxidative properties of KMnO4. As we know, EPR
usually is convinced to clearly express electronic transfer. Works employed pH/KMnO4 in the
dewaterability of sludge, and common intermediate state is Mn(III) in Mn oxides (Zhang et al., 2021a).
The EPR test showed, as being seen from Fig. 3(b), that the characteristic spectra with four-peak relative
intensities of 1,2,4,6 performing force, and the 3,5 performing force peak weakened.Mn(III) has been
Page 9/27
known as extremely active site among the Mn oxides, and intensely determines the oxidation of Mn
oxides (Zhang et al., 2021a). As KMnO4 concentration increased, it can be seen that the signal of EPR
was obviously increased. There is a sharp upward trend appeared after the reaction, indicating that the
pH/KMnO4 combination is highly oxidative and can rapidly cleave the EPS structure, leading to the
dicharge of bound water. It clearly showed that ·O2− had been formed, which played a major role in
oxidation ability for pH/KMnO4 treatment. This result claimed that the enhanced degradation of EPS and
improvement in sludge dewaterability were impacted by free radicals generated in interactions between
KMnO4 and acidity.
3.4 Effect of EPS properties with pH/KMnO4 and pH
treatment
3.4.1 SCOD contents
Impact of the pH/KMnO4 treatment on sludge damage was studied by testing the SCOD after treatment
with supernatant (Fig. 4). SCOD showed a distinct uptrend with increasing KMnO4 dosage, as well as the
acidity. As the pH decreased from 7.8 to 3.0 without KMnO4 participation, the SCOD multiplied from 882.5
to 1270.0 mg/L, suggesting that the acidic pH condition is helpful in partial dissolution of organic
substance contained in the sludge. When KMnO4 was added to the acidied sludge samples, the SCOD
sharply increased to 2070.0 mg/L in the dosage of 20 mg/g TS. A similar result was obtained for sludge
decomposition following K2FeO4 treatme(Ning et al., 2015; Xiao et al., 2021a).
There are three possible reasons for these ndings: First, as the pH decreases, it destroys the extracellular
organic matter of the sludge making the SCOD concentration multiply. Second, The extracellular polymer
of the sludge was only slightly oxidized when KMnO4 was added to the raw sludge alone, as can be
derived from the small increase in SCOD. Finally, the sludge polymerized under the action of acid was
added with KMnO4. KMnO4 penetration through the cytoderm of the cell provided KMnO4 with the
opportunity to rupture the cell membrane. Both intracellular and cytoplasm material was released into the
supernatant, and the strong oxidizing effect made the already damaged sludge extracellular organic
matter break up more severely, so that the SCOD at KMnO4 of 20 mg/gTS increased sharply (Zhang et al.,
2022; Zhang, Dong, Dai, & Dai, 2021b). The Nernst equation shows that the H+ concentration has an
effect on the electric KMnO4, and the larger the H+ concentration is, the stronger the oxidation capacity of
KMnO4 will be. The Nernst equation is a unique criterion to determine the direction of redox reactions
(Zhen, Lu, Li, & Zhao, 2013). In essence, precipitation reactions, coordination reactions, and changes in
solution acidity may change the concentration of oxidized or reduced substances in the redox pair, which
in turn affects the rupture of sludge extracellular polymer and thus causes changes in sludge dewatering.
3.4.2 The content of Proteins and polysaccharides

EPS consists mainly of protein and polysaccharides. Within a certain pH range, due to anionic functional
groups’s ionization, the carboxyl groups, amino groups, and phosphate groups,mud particles are always
negatively charged (Zhu et al., 2015).Negative charges on the surface of the particle create electron
repulsion, keeping the colloidal system stable. (Zhang, Cao, Wang, Ma, & Yu, 2016). Acidic treatment not
only destroys the structure of EPS, but also causes protonation of EPS anion functional groups and
accumulation of sludge systems that convert the combined water into free water (Ye, Liu, & Li, 2012).
There are two ways in which low pH could impact proteins. First, the protein lysis constant reached a
minimum of 2–3 pH at the sludge the isoelectric point (Liu et al., 2022). At one time, protein solubility
was minimal and the extractable TB-EPS and LB-EPS levels decreased with pH value reduction (Xu et al.,
2018). On the other hand, low-molecular weight protein-like substances are dissolved in acidic
environments and released into supernatants, resulting in elevated SEPS concentration. Figure 5(a) and
(b) give the impact of pH on the content of polysaccharides and proteins, respectively. Proteinaceous
materials appeared to have been the leadership fraction and represented more than 85% of the whole
EPS lots of the slurry. In protein and polysaccharide content a similar pattern of changes was observed in
SEPS with reduction in pH. SEPS polysaccharide and protein concentrations gradually increased with
decreasing pH from pH 7.8 to pH 3.0 and got the minimum at neutral conditions. For TB-EPS and LB-EPS,
with increasing pH level the protein concentration increased,while that of the polysaccharide was
relatively constant. They noted that the pH has a greater impact on proteins visibly dissolving and being
released into the bulk sludge when the pH is less than 5.0. As the pH treatment, the density of proteins in
S-EPS increased from 132.7 mg/L(raw sludge) to 788.5 mg/L (pH = 3.0), 738 mg/L (pH = 5.0) and 273.9
mg/L (pH = 7.0), while in polysaccharides increased from 24.4 mg/L (raw sludge) to 29.1 mg/L(pH = 3.0),
30.4 mg/L(pH = 5.0) and 24.5 mg/L(pH = 7.0). Higher extractable EPS concentration, especially high
protein concentration, was studied to be consistently correlated to poor dewatering performance of the
sludge (Hu et al., 2020). It is also possible that protein hydrolysis and coagulation are the primary
mechanisms for improving the dewaterability of slurries in acidic environments.
As can be proved from Fig. 5 that without acidity treatment, the S-EPS increased smoothly with the
addition of KMnO4, and the TB-EPS/LB-EPS did not change dramatically. However, this sets forth the vital
fact that the change in content of SEPS and LB-EPS,TB-EPS showed the similar performance with
increasing KMnO4 dosage after acid treatment, which is agree with that of the index of SRF. The results
reveal that the SRF are intimately relevant with the extractable EPS concentration in sludge system, and
high extractable EPS content is related to high compression, the loose occulent structure and low
dewaterability of sludge system. There was a difference in the pattern of protein change in S-EPS and LB-
EPS,TB-EPS. The protein content of S-EPS multiply from 132.7 mg/L to 677.5, 606.9, 1020.5 mg/L
respectively and while TB-EPS dwindle from 970.0 mg/L to 233.3, 253.7, 253.7 mg/L when KMnO4 at pH 
= 3.0 was administered at 5, 10, 20 mg/gTS. The variation of EPS at pH = 5.0 was slightly worse than that
at pH = 3.0, but the variation of EPS at pristine sludge and pH = 7.0 was almost the same(in Fig.S5a,b,c,d).
The protein theme of the SEPS section increased as the quantity of KMnO4 was increased, whereas the
protein theme of both LB-EPS and TB-EPS was decreased. As the dose of KMnO4 was increased, the
polysaccharide theme of LB-EPS, TB-EPS, and SEPS initially multiply and then reduce. One possible
Page 11/27
explanation for these conditions is that KMnO4 may play a double function as both a coagulant and an
oxidant. Oxidation served a leader role in chemical conditioning at low doses of permanganate. The
oxidation process can lead to EPS release and the divalent manganese ion produced from the reduction
of KMnO4 was unable to suciently adsorb and remove the EPS that was released. Surprisedly,
coagulation might continue to take more vital part in during the regulation of high doses. The Mn(III) can
be electro-neutralised and cross-linked to compress the EPS structures, dropping the content of
extractable EPS. When KMnO4 was overexpressed, the protein content with protein-based organic
compounds in the SEPS fraction was increased, one possible explanation for this is that manganese ions
have coagulation effects on protein-based organic compounds(Hu et al., 2020). Filtrability of sludge was
reported to be passively correlated with protein concentration in SRF(Guo, Wang, & Wang, 2017; Kou et al.,
2021). In our study, the high MW protein was oxidized to smaller proteins, which, as aresult, increased the
resistance of the sludge to ltration.
As can be viewed from the Fig. 5(a,b,c) that the supplement of KMnO4 accelerated the release of inner
tightly adhered EPS layer to dissolved one. When the concentration of KMnO4 was controlled at about 5,
10 and 20 mg/gTS, the density of proteins and polysaccharides in S-EPS got extreme promotion with the
signicant dwindle of the density of proteins and polysaccharides in TB-EPS. The LB-EPS and TB-EPS of
proteins dwindle deeply while multiply in S-EPS after pH/KMnO4 treatment. These result was consistent
with CST, SRF and SCOD, indicating that he dewaterability of sludge was enhanced because EPS
structure were broken down, especially in terms of the proteins of TB-EPS.
3.5 EPS characterization
3.5.1 FT-IR
To further examine changes in protein components before and after different treatments, functional
groups in the activated sludge and the extracted EPS were identied using infrared spectroscopy.In order
to reveal the different hydrophilicity/hydrophobicity of EPS, FT-IR spectra of S-EPS, LB-EPS, and TB-EPS
were acquired in the range of 500–4000 cm− 1. For the extractable sludge organics from WAS, dramatical
absorption bands were observed at 1601 cm− 1 (carbonyl C = O vibrations), 1412 cm− 1(aliphatic C-O and
C-H vibrations), and 659 cm− 1 in the samples treated with PH/KMnO4, respectively(Bai et al., 2022).

respectively. For example, the spectral peaks around 2250 cm− 1 (-O-H vibrations), 1460 cm− 1 (C-H, C-H2
and C-H3 vibrations), and 1080 cm− 1 (vibrations) can be attributed to the organic material associated
with the hydrocarbon materials. For all of the sludge samples, the increase in spectral peak intensity at
around 1080 cm− 1 revealed that the polysaccharides in the EPS slurries could not be readily oxidised by
pH/KMnO4. In contrast, a signicant reduction in amides I, II, and III (protein-related) means that these
amide containing groups could be eciently degraded. Based on the signicant (humic acid-related)

decreasing trend after processing, which is relevant to the preferential breakage of C = O. The presence of
C-O and C-H bonds in the aromatic organic matter at the high molecular weights of the samples.

components and functional groups ,which in the spectra of TB-EPS fractions showed dramatically

3.5.2 3D-EEM
Fluorescence spectroscopy using three-dimensional EEM is a speedy and sensitive technique to detect
uorescent compounds in EPS and explore possible mechanisms to enrich the dewatering performance
of sludge. To further understand the feature of EPS content variation in treatment, the 3D-EEM was
applied and the results can be found in Fig. 7. As showed in the gure and supplementary material Figure
S4, the change in uorescence characteristic of pH/KMnO4 treatment sludge showed a similar type with
different dosage. Additionally, it was observed from uorescence intensity that the chemical composition
of TB-EPS and LB-EPS is decreasing and the chemical composition of S-EPS is increasing with adjusting
pH from 7.8 to 3.0. Four dominant peaks at excitation/emission (Ex/Em) of 225/350 (aromatic protein II),
275/350 (tryptophan-protein like substances), 250/275(tryptophan-protein like substances) and 275/300
(tyrosine-protein like substances) (Cao et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2022)were dicovered in the extract EPS
sections. Figure 7(a) shows the pH dependence of the EEM spectrum as well as the uorescence intensity
of the SEPS, LB-EPS and TB-EPS. First, we were able to detect four uorescent peaks in the SEPS
fraction: Peak I (ex/em = 250/275)-tryptophon like proteins, Peak II (ex/em = 225/350)-aromatic proteins,
Peaks III (ex/em = 275/350)-tryptophan protein, and Peak IV (ex/em = 275/300)-tyrosine protein whereas
LB-EPS, TB-EPS only appeared in Peak I and Peak II(Liu et al., 2022). Fluorescence intensities for peaks I,
II, III and IV were determined that was improved from 1450, 1650, 2115 and 2198 to 6200, 2305, 2310 and
2315nm when pH was reduced from 7.8 to 3.0. As the pH of the sludge was increased, the violence of the
four uorescent SEPS peaks was also expanded. For example, the change in the EEM and Fig.S4 spectra
of the SEPS and LB-EPS, TB-EPS after treatment under a different pH was showed in Fig. S4 in the SI.
Despite the lack of clear variation in two protein-related uorescent peaks following acid treatment,
whereas humic and fulvic uorescent peaks were signicantly increased at pH 3.0. For LB-EPS,TB-EPS,
uorescence peaks C and A intensied from 1550 and 1665nm to 2355 and 6200nm, and pH increased
from 3.0 to 7.8, but pH values for B and D peaks remained stable. It can be seen in Fig. 7 that the high
MW organic matter in SEPS was mostly removed following permanganate treatment with a pH < 5.0.
Thirdly, PN removal of organic compounds was improved by decreasing pH. When the pH was reduced to
3.0, only organic material with peak A and peak B could be detected, and other peaks were lost. As noted
above, the oxidative capacity of KMnO4 was increased with decreasing pH, resulting in the improved
removal eciency of organic matter. Since KMnO4 showed a high reactivity to acidic conditions, it allows
the dewatering performance of sludge to be enriched by oxidizing the divalent manganese to form a new
Page 13/27
form of manganese dioxide. The reduction in the intensity of the uorescence peak before and after
treatment is generally a sign of oxidation of the uorescent product. The uorescence intensities of the
peak in the EEM spectrum of oxidized EPS were dramatically reduced, which is regular with the tendency
of CST and SRF to vary in the dewaterability of the sludge. The uorescence intensity after
acidity/KMnO4 treatment was lower than the acidity treated one and in the raw sludge, which may be due
to the fact that the addition of KMnO4 allowed the EPS to be oxidize and decomposed and releasing the
bound water. The results showed that the uorescence components (mainly proteins) in the slurries of S-
EPS, LB-EPS and LB-EPS had an inuence on the dewaterability of the sludge. This implied that, in the
research conducted in this study, there was a close relationship between the improvement in sludge
dewaterability and the degradation and sweep of uorescence components present in the EPS in the
sludge.Indeed, potent pH/KMnO4 oxidation destroyed in the case of the specic functional groups of the
uorescent materials in the EPS slurries and resulted in the breaking of bonds in the polymer backbone,
causing EPS-bound water to be released and subsequently increasing the dehydration of the slurry.
3.6 Mechanism study
3.6.1 Correlation analysis
Establishing the kinship between sludge dewatering and T2, EPS, SCOD, and Zeta contents will aid in the
decision-making process to ensure maximum dewatering capacity of the activated sludge. Correlation
analysis of the data obtained for the pH/KMnO4 treated sludge (Fig. 8) demonstrated a forceful positive
relavant between the CST and EPS of the sludge for protein and polysaccharides, especially for TB-EPS(r 
= 0.58, p < 0.05) and TB-EPS(r = 0.67, p < 0.05). There was a powerful negative attachment between SRF
and EPS and the protein and polysaccharides of S-EPS(r=-0.73,p < 0.05),S-EPS(r=-0.64,p < 0.05)
respectively. Figure 8 also shows that PS in LB-EPS, TB-EPS, PN in LB-EPS, TB-EPS were positively jointed
with both SRF and CST. PS in S-EPS, on the other hand, and PN in S-EPS were negatively correlated with
CST and SRF. The reduction in TB-EPS and its chemical composition has been suggested to facilitate the
dewatering performance of the sludge. The correlation between T21, T23 and SRF and CST was negative,
whereas T22, T24 was positive with CST and SRF.
3.6.2 Mechanism of EPS
Combining the above results with the modies in the structure and features of the sludge, a summary and
explanation of the reasonable mechanism was obtained. Since the solids in the sludge do not affect the
free water, the pH/KMnO4 has no effect on the primary free water content. Bound water interacts with
solid particles to some degree. Therefore, the focus of the Oxidation methods is on sludge solids.
pH/KMnO4 changes the properties of water by breaking electrostatic bonds and hydrogen bonds to
change the shape of water's adhesion and its ability to bind in the slurry, separating it from the solid
particles and transferring it to the free water. Figure 9 shows that following the action of radicals with
high oxidation potential, leading to EPS breakdown of the sludge and the disruption of the cell membrane
(destruction of EPS proteins and oxidation of polysaccharide) and is the most common cause of. In this
Page 14/27
way, protein-and polysaccharide-bound water is released from EPS, and water within the cell is
subsequently emited into the external environment. In contrast, since the main EPS contents are
proteinaceous and polysaccharide components, carbon dioxide is an organic substance that is oxidized
to carbon dioxide and water as a result of (oxidation oxidation). Thus, the water content in the sludge
was increased. In addition, free water is increased in water content, which can be easily swpeed.
4 Conclusions
The work indicated that the raw sludge was treated by pH/KMnO4 that can extremely promote the
dewaterability of sludge. Compared with raw sludge,the pH = 3 and KMnO4(20 mg/gTS) treatment sludge
achieved 89% CST reductions, 75% SRF reductions and 50% Weight Loss reductions.The result shown
that pH and pH/KMnO4 conditioned trun out to be ecient for freeing of water from sludge. The theory to
promote sludge dewatering eciency by pH/KMnO4 would have to be based on the EPS structure by
oxidation of acidic potassium permanganate.The study may offer a new path for the sludge
preprocessing by pH/KMnO4.
