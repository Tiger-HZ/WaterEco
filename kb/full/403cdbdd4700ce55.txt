The removal of phenolic compounds from aqueous solutions is a crucial step in wastewater treatment. In this research, the performance of uncalcined eggshell and calcined eggshell at 200 °C, 400 °C, 600 °C and 800 °C denoted as; UES, CES200, CES400, CES600 and CES800 respectively were studied as sorbents for removal of 4-chlorophenol (4-CP) from pre-contaminated water samples. The morphology of CES800 before and after adsorption was examined using Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), while the structure and surface properties of UES, CES400 and CES800 were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD). Batch adsorption experiments were conducted to evaluate the effects of key parameters such as; pH, temperature, contact time and pollutant concentrations on the sorbents’ adsorption efficiency of 4-CP in aqueous medium. Adsorption isotherms were analyzed using the Langmuir and Freundlich models, with the Langmuir isotherm providing the best fit for all the adsorbents. The kinetics followed a pseudo second-order model. Additionally, thermodynamic analysis of CES800 indicated that the adsorption process was endothermic and governed by physical adsorption. The optimal pH for 4-CP removal was obtained between 4.0 – 7.0. The maximum adsorption capacities obtained for UES, CES200, CES400, CES600, and CES800 were 5.358, 4.581, 5.514, 5.625, and 5.679 mg/g respectively. The adsorption capacity followed the trend: CES800 &gt; CES600 &gt; CES400 &gt; UES &gt; CES200, with CES800 exhibiting the highest adsorption performance. This study indicated that monitored calcination possess potentials to boost the adsorbent efficiency of eggshell in the removal of 4-CP in aqueous medium.
