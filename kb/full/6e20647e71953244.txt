Abstract Mutational activation of the KRAS oncogene occurs in over 90% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) patients, and plays a crucial role in tumor initiation, progression, and tumor maintenance. Historically considered undruggable, there has recently been a surge in efforts to develop and improve drugs that target RAS. RAS inhibitors are well-tolerated, with promising clinical efficacy against RAS-driven PDAC; however, adaptive and acquired resistance develops upon inhibition of RAS alone. Multiple genomic- and signaling-based compensatory mechanisms have been proposed and account for approximately 50% of the cases in which resistance emerges.
