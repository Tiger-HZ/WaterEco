Bioeroding sponges are recognised pests in shellfish fisheries, as their boring activity can reduce market value, contribute to oyster mortality and cause economic losses. In New Zealand, bioeroding sponges in the shell‐burrowing alpha form are frequently observed in shells of the flat oyster Ostrea chilensis , a historically and culturally important species supporting several fisheries, including those in Foveaux Strait. Although sponge infestation has been noted for decades and linked to reduced meat quality, quantitative data on its distribution and abundance in oyster populations remain limited.
