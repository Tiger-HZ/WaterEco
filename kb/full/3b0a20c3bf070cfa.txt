Abstract
Source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) aims to adapt a source model pre-trained on a 
labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain, under the constraint that the original 
source data is no longer accessible. While some existing approaches achieve promising 
adaptation performance by approximating certain target samples as source-like samples 
to construct a pseudo-source domain and reduce domain discrepancies, they typically 
rely solely on the source model for this construction. This overlooks a critical issue: the 
source model may suffer from illusions when identifying pseudo-source samples, due to 
its inherited spurious correlations between domain-specific features (e.g., environment) 
and domain-invariant features (e.g., class identity) learned from the source domain. As a 
result, the reliability of the generated pseudo-source domain is compromised. In this work, 
we propose a novel Staying Rational and Resisting Illusions (SRRI) method for SFDA to 
resolve the two fundamental challenges: confirmation bias and domain shift. In particular, 
we innovatively leverage the CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) model as a 
source of external knowledge to mitigate feature confounding. SRRI employs knowledge 
distillation to guide the source model in disentangling class-discriminative causal fea -
tures from domain-specific spurious features, building upon this, we design a CLIP-guided 
dual-model validation and class balancing strategy to ensure the reliability and richness 
of the pseudo-source domain samples even when poor distillation occurs in some tasks. 
Furthermore, we propose a dynamic pseudo-source domain optimization mechanism that 
continuously fine-tunes the task-specific prompts of the CLIP during adaptation to further 
correct the bias of the target model on hard samples, while periodically reconstructing and 
refining the pseudo-source domain. Ultimately, we apply robust supervised learning and 
distribution alignment to these respective domains. Extensive experiments show that SRRI 
outperforms the state-of-the-art methods.

1 Introduction
As a subfield of transfer learning, Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) relies on access 
to labeled source domain data and unlabeled target domain data (Pan & Yang, 2010). How-
ever, in certain scenarios, accessing source data may be infeasible due to concerns such 
as privacy and security (Lao et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2019). To address this limitation, 
Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) (Kim et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2020) has emerged 
as a widely studied alternative. Its objective is to enable knowledge transfer using only a 
pre-trained source model and unlabeled target domain samples, without requiring access to 
the original source data.
Current SFDA approaches primarily focus on generating or extracting auxiliary informa-
tion to facilitate unsupervised adaptation. These methods can be broadly categorized into 
two types. The first seeks to uncover additional supervisory signals from the source model 
(Huang et al., 2021; Lao et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022) and target data (Tang et al., 2022; 
Yang et al., 2021, 2022a), while the second aims to construct a pseudo-source domain (Cui 
et al., 2023; Du et al., 2024) to simulate a conventional UDA setting. This work centers on 
the latter approach, exploring how to effectively build a pseudo-source domain to enhance 
adaptation performance.
As previously mentioned, the source model in SFDA is trained on labeled data from 
the source domain. However, in most cases, individual samples consist of both domain-
invariant features (e.g., category identity) and domain-specific features (e.g., background or 
environment). When spurious correlations (Li et al., 2025; Yue et al., 2023) exist between 
these two types of features in the source domain, they can severely impair the generaliza-
tion ability of the model. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the source model, inheriting knowledge 
from the source domain, may form misleading associations, mistaking domain-specific fea-
tures for indicators of certain categories. This illusion causes the model to associate target 
domain samples with incorrect class identities, leading to the construction of a low-quality 
pseudo-source domain. To address this issue, we propose a novel Staying Rational and

We first distill the rich semantic understanding of the CLIP into the source model to help 
it overcome misleading correlations. Then, using the updated source model, we generate 
a pseudo-source domain with the assistance of CLIP-based validation and class balancing 
strategy. Finally, adaptation is performed using a UDA framework composed of three key 
modules. Importantly, during adaptation, we keep the CLIP model used for validation fro-

target model. This decouples pseudo-source domain validation from knowledge guidance 
and enables the periodic refinement of the pseudo-source domain. Our contributions can be 
summarized as follows: 
1. An innovative Staying Rational and Resisting Illusions (SRRI) approach is proposed that 
distills the deconfounded knowledge of the CLIP model to mitigate spurious correlations 
learned by the source model and effectively addresses the unreliability of pseudo-source 
domains due to confirmation bias in Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA).
2. The CLIP-guided dual-model validation and class balancing strategy in our SRRI 
approach ensures that high-quality pseudo-source domains can be obtained even in the 
presence of poor distillation, thus providing absolutely excellent domain environments 
for subsequent distribution alignment to cope with the challenge of domain bias.
3. We propose a dynamic pseudo-source domain optimization mechanism that fine-tunes 
the task-specific prompts of a CLIP to provide a more robust teacher signal, thereby 
improving the model perception of hard samples and periodically refining the quality of 
the pseudo-source domain.
4. Extensive experiments conducted on standard SFDA benchmarks demonstrate that SRRI 
substantially improves the quality of the pseudo-source domain and significantly outper-
forms previous state-of-the-art methods.

2 Related Work
In this section, we review the recent source-free domain adaptation methods related to our 
approach as well as methods for multimodal foundation models.
2.1 Source-Free Domain Adaptation
Existing Source-free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) approaches fall into two distinct cate-
gories. The first category comprises pseudo-labeling based self-training methods. These 
approaches generate pseudo-labels for target domain samples using the source model and 
leverage them as supervisory signals to guide adaptation. SHOT (Liang et al., 2020) is a 
pioneering work in this direction, optimizing the model through information maximization

222 Page 4 of 27
Machine Learning (2026) 115:222
and feature clustering. Many subsequent studies have focused on improving pseudo-labels 
quality by incorporating techniques such as nearest-neighbor clustering (Liang et al., 2022; 
Yang et al., 2021), active learning strategies (Li et al., 2022b), or designing more robust 
clustering objectives (Qu et al., 2022). In addition, to further enhance the robustness of 
pseudo-labeling, some researchers (Tian et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2025) have begun to explore 
the structured information between samples. The second category involves source informa-
tion generation or reconstruction to facilitate adaptation. Some methods employ generative 
adversarial networks (GANs) to synthesize “pseudo-source" data (Du et al., 2024; Kim 
et al., 2021; Tian et al., 2022), while others transfer statistical information, such as batch 
normalization parameters, encoded in the source model to align feature distributions across 
domains (Ishii & Sugiyama, 2021; Yang et al., 2022b). However, a fundamental limitation 
shared by these methods is their complete reliance on the biased source model for predic -
tions. Specifically, when there exists a significant distribution shift or spurious correlation 
between the source and target domains, the source model, distorted by these illusions, can 
easily fall into a vicious cycle of confirmation bias, leading to cumulative prediction errors 
(Arazo et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2021a). This paper focuses on a detailed investigation of 
the pseudo-source domain quality issue within the second category of approaches.
2.2 Multimodal Foundation Model
The core idea behind the multimodal foundation models is to align representations across dif-
ferent modalities, enabling the model to capture deep semantic associations between vision 
and language. For instance, text-to-image models such as DALL E (Ramesh et al., 2021) 
and Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) can generate highly realistic images based on 
complex textual descriptions. Other works, like ALIGN (Jia et al., 2021) and FLAVA (Singh 
et al., 2022), aim to learn a unified multimodal representation space that supports a wide 
range of vision-language tasks. Among these foundational multimodal models, CLIP (Con-
trastive Language-Image Pre-training) (Radford et al., 2021) has made a profound impact 
on the field of computer vision, thanks to its remarkable zero-shot recognition capability 
and strong domain robustness. A growing body of research has shown that the zero-shot 
ability of the CLIP allows it to outperform supervised ImageNet-pretrained models when 
handling out-of-distribution data, stylistic variations, and various types of data corruption 
(Yuan et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2021b). Motivated by these strengths, our proposed SRRI 
leverages the out-of-domain knowledge of the CLIP to correct the in-domain illusions of the 
source model. Compared to prior methods that rely solely on the source model to construct 
pseudo-source domains, our approach effectively mitigates knowledge shift and spurious 
correlations, thereby enabling the construction of a more reliable and higher-quality pseudo-
source domain.

3.4.2 Class Balancing Strategy
Through the above steps, we obtain an initial set of high-quality pseudo-source domain 
samples. Prior research (Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz, 2020; Kumar et al., 2018) suggests that 
larger source domains generally lead to better transfer performance. However, our obser-
vations indicate that when the consistency score is low and the acceptance threshold for 
pseudo-source samples is raised accordingly, it may lead to class imbalance-some classes 
may contain very few or even no samples in the pseudo-source domain. This negatively 
impacts performance and even reduces the overall number of pseudo-source samples. To 
mitigate this issue, we first count the number of samples per class within the current pseudo-
source index set 𝐼𝐼𝑃𝑃𝑃𝑃. We then identify the underrepresented classes and, while ensuring that 
the total number of pseudo-source samples remains sufficiently large, we supplement these 
classes by selecting additional samples from the candidate pool based on entropy ranking-
specifically, we choose the lowest-entropy samples for each deficient class. This process 
results in an updated pseudo-source index set 𝐼𝐼′ 𝑃𝑃𝑃𝑃. We define the final pseudo-source domain 
and the remaining target domain as follows:

4 Experiments
In this section, we first introduce the datasets and experimental setup. Then, we take the 
state-of-the-art methods that are currently available in the field of SFDA that can be used 
as comparators to perform comparative experiments on 4 domain adaptation benchmark 
datasets. And we purposely compare the current representative pseudo-source domain based

effectiveness of each element of the proposed methodology as well as the elimination of 
spurious correlations.
4.1 Experimental Setup
4.1.1 Datasets
We evaluate SRRI on four widely used domain adaptation benchmark datasets. (1) Office-31 
(Saenko et al., 2010) is a small-scale dataset comprising three distinct domains and 31 cat -
egories of office-related objects. (2) Office-Home (Venkateswara et al., 2017) is a widely 
recognized medium-scale dataset that includes four diverse domains and 65 object catego-
ries. (3) VisDA (Peng et al., 2017) is a challenging benchmark with 12 categories, where the 
source domain consists of synthetic images and the target domain comprises real images. (4) 
DomainNet-126 (Peng et al., 2019) is a large-scale dataset covering four visually diverse 
domains and a total of 126 categories.

and the CLIP Zero-shot (ViT-B/32) (Radford et al., 2021). (2) The second group consists 
of six representative UDA methods: BNM (Cui et al., 2020), CDAN (Long et al., 2018), 
SFAN (Xu et al., 2019), BCDM (Li et al., 2021), MCD (Saito et al., 2018), and DAPL (Ge 
et al., 2023). (3) The third group comprises 18 state-of-the-art SFDA methods, including 
SHOT (Liang et al., 2020), NRC (Yang et al., 2021), GKD (Tang et al., 2021), PS (Du et 
al., 2024), U-SFAN+ (Roy et al., 2022), AdaCon (Chen et al., 2022), CoWA (Lee et al., 
2022), SCLM (Tang et al., 2022), AAA (Li et al., 2022a), VDM (Tian et al., 2022), DIPE 
(Wang et al., 2022), PLUE (Litrico et al., 2023), GAP (Chhabra et al., 2023), SFADA (He 
et al., 2024), ASOGE (Cui et al., 2023), DPLS (Ma et al., 2024), CPD (Zhou et al., 2024), 
and TPDS (Tang et al., 2024a). (4) The fourth group comprises 4 CLIP-based SFDA meth-
ods, including DIFO (Tang et al., 2024b), ViLAaD++ (Tarashima et al., 2025), SHOT w/
Co-learn++ (Zhang et al., 2025), and NRC w/Co-learn++ (Zhang et al., 2025). DIFO and 
ViLAaD++ employ the ViT-B/32 variant of CLIP, whereas SHOT w/Co-learn++ and NRC 
w/Co-learn++ adopt the ViT-L/14@336 variant. Notably, ViT-L/14@336 exhibits superior 
baseline generalization performance compared with ViT-B/32. Among them, PS is the cur-
rent representative SFDA method based on pseudo-source domain.
4.1.3 Implementation Details
For all experiments, we conduct in PyTorch using a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 
GPU. We first pretrain the model on the labeled source domain to simulate the “source 
model” provided by the user in a typical SFDA scenario. The model checkpoint that achieves 
the best performance on the source-domain validation set is saved as the starting point 
for subsequent adaptation. We adopt ResNet-50 or ResNet-101 as the backbone feature 
extractor, followed by a bottleneck layer and a classifier. The bottleneck layer is set to have 
a dimensionality of 256. For the teacher model in our approach, we utilize the ViT-B/32 
variant of CLIP, with its parameters kept frozen throughout all stages. All experiments are 
conducted using the PyTorch framework. Optimization is performed using stochastic gradi-
ent descent (SGD) with Nesterov momentum. The momentum is set to 0.9, and the weight 
decay is 5e-4. The learning rates for the domain discriminator and classifier are set to be 10 
times that of the feature extractor. Specifically, the learning rate is 5e-4 for DomainNet-126 
and 1e-3 for all other datasets. The number of training epochs is set to 200 for Office-31, 100 
for Office-Home, 15 for VisDA, and 40 for DomainNet-126. For the cold-start distillation 
stage, the number of epochs is set to 20 for Office-31 and Office-Home, 3 for VisDA, and 
5 for DomainNet-126. For the fixed epoch interval, it is set to 20 for Office-31 and Office-
Home, 3 for VisDA, and 8 for DomainNet-126. Regarding hyperparameters, the adversarial 
loss weight 𝜆𝜆𝑎𝑎𝑎𝑎𝑎𝑎 is set to 0.1, and the batch size is fixed at 128 for all datasets.

We visualize the GradCAM of PS and SRRI trained on VisDA to show the ability of SRRI

the source-domain samples. After cold-start knowledge distillation, we then use the target 
model to generate the Grad-CAM visualizations of the pseudo-source domain samples from

on source domain samples, both are able to exploit their significant appearance differences

appearance of knives in the source domain sample, causing it to focus excessively on cer -
tain long and straight parts of the planes in the target domain sample instead of the overall 
fuselage, and thus incorrectly predicting them as knives. SRRI corrects this error by focus-
ing instead on the overall fuselage, resulting in correct predictions. Furthermore, the third 
row of the figure demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed dynamic pseudo-source 
domain optimization module on the challenging car and truck categories. Specifically, we 
treat the pseudo-source domain constructed immediately after cold-start distillation as the 
initial pseudo-source domain, and the one obtained after the final reconstruction as the final 
pseudo-source domain. Although spurious correlations lead to incorrect predictions on sev-
eral hard samples in the initial pseudo-source domain, these errors are effectively corrected 
in the final pseudo-source domain through task-specific prompt tuning and subsequent 
knowledge distillation.

5 Conclusion
In this paper, we analyzed a critical issue in existing SFDA methods that constructed pseudo-
source domains: the confirmation bias of the source model, induced by spurious inter-domain 
correlations, which compromised the reliability of the generated pseudo-source domain. We 
proposed a novel Staying Rational and Resisting Illusions (SRRI) method to rectify the 
misclassification tendencies of the source model on pseudo-source samples by distilling 
the powerful prior knowledge from the CLIP model. Next, we designed a pseudo-source 
domain generation strategy that integrated CLIP-guided dual-model validation and class 
balancing strategy. This ensured the creation of a superior pseudo-source domain even in 
scenarios with sub-optimal distillation performance. Furthermore, we proposed a dynamic 
pseudo-source domain optimization mechanism that continuously fine-tuned task-specific 
prompts of the CLIP during adaptation to provide an updated teacher signal for guiding 
the target model on hard samples, thereby periodically reconstructing the pseudo-source 
domain. Extensive experiments on four datasets demonstrate that our method not only sig -
nificantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches but also generates pseudo-source 
domains of substantially higher-quality. We believe our work offers an effective and promis-
ing solution to the challenges posed by the unavailability of source data in SFDA. For future 
work, we plan to extend this framework to multi-source free domain adaptation scenarios 
and explore more lightweight distillation strategies to improve adaptation efficiency.
