The biogenic synthesis of metal and metal oxide nanoparticles using algae and cyanobacteria has emerged as a candidate alternative to physical and chemical fabrication routes, which typically depend on hazardous reagents, high energy inputs, and toxic by-products. This critical narrative review synthesises the accessible peer-reviewed literature on algal and cyanobacterial nanoparticle biosynthesis, with particular attention to the biochemical mechanisms underlying metal ion reduction and capping, the analytical techniques used to confirm nanoparticle formation, and the evidence supporting applications in sustainable agriculture and environmental remediation.
