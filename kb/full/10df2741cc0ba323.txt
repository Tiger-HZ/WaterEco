Energy conversion in living organisms relies on biological membranes that facilitate electron transfer between oxidoreductases. In mitochondria, this process is mediated by the electron transport chain embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM). Under various physiological and genetic conditions, mitochondrial matrix Na+ levels increase, reducing IMM fluidity through the formation of ternary coordination adducts between Na+ and phospholipids. These adducts impair ubiquinone (CoQ) transfer between respiratory complexes, thereby promoting mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS) production and activating the hypoxic adaptive pathway.
