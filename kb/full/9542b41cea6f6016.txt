Summary The prospect of increasing drought intensity in many river basins under climate change threatens the persistence of vulnerable freshwater species. Understanding how the traits of each species affect its resistance and resilience to drought may help to identify those species at most risk and elucidate the mechanisms by which impacts occur. I analysed macroinvertebrate monitoring data collected from rivers across Australia's Murray–Darling Basin (&gt;10 6 km 2 ) during the middle and later stages of the recent decade‐long Millennium Drought and the initial post‐drought period.
