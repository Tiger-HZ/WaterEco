Anthropogenic nutrient enrichment is a recognised threat to coral reef ecosystems, yet the impacts of small, inhabited islands lacking modern wastewater treatment remain poorly quantified. We used a paired Control–Impact design in Faafu Atoll (Maldives) to test whether a modestly inhabited island (~700 residents) generates detectable isotopic and stoichiometric fingerprints in nearshore benthic organisms. Nearshore surface water nutrients were measured concurrently with δ¹³C, δ¹⁵N, and C/N ratios in three functional groups: the calcareous green alga Halimeda spp., a mixed group of non-calcareous macroalgae, and the giant clam Tridacna spp. Linear mixed-effects models revealed limited evidence of anthropogenic enrichment.
