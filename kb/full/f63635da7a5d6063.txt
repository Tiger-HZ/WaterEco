Spatio-temporal evaluation of land use and cover 
change and prediction of habitat quality in Estuarine 
Delta National Parks: a case study of the Yellow River 
Estuary National Park

Abstract
As a critical component of China’s national park system, the Yellow River Estuary National Park exhibits a 
unique habitat evolution mechanism shaped by the interplay between anthropogenic disturbances and ecologi -
cal restoration within a land–sea interaction zone. This study simulates land use and land cover change and 
assesses habitat quality dynamics from 2003 to 2023, with scenario projections for 2033, by integrating the 
InVEST and PLUS models. The results reveal a dual-process mechanism underlying habitat change: urbaniza -
tion drives the expansion of construction land, which encroaches upon cropland and barren land, leading to 
widespread habitat degradation and fragmentation; concurrently, wetland restoration and water body expansion, 
driven by ecological policies, partially offset these losses by locally enhancing habitat quality in coastal zones. 
This coexistence of degradation and restoration shapes a spatially heterogeneous pattern, where low-quality 
habitat expands and consolidates around urban agglomerations, while high-quality habitat becomes increasingly 
fragmented and confined to remnant wetland patches. Scenario projections indicate a continuation of these 
trends, with further expansion of low-quality habitat and sustained fragmentation of high-quality areas, suggest-
ing limited ecological resilience under current land use trajectories. By uncovering the process-based linkages 
between land use transformation and habitat quality response, this study provides a mechanistic understanding 
of estuarine habitat evolution and offers a scientific foundation for spatial planning and ecological governance 
in integrated land–sea national parks. These insights contribute to broader discourses on land quality and eco -
system service sustainability in deltaic social-ecological systems.

Introduction
The establishment of a nature reserve system with national parks as the main body in China is an important insti-
tutional innovation in the construction of global ecological civilization construction and a key practice to realize 
the harmonious coexistence between man and nature1. At present, China has established a national park system to 
protect the authenticity and integrity of the natural ecosystem and build a national ecological security barrier 2,3. 
Yellow River Estuary National Park is the first Marine land integrated national park in China. It undertakes the 
important mission of ecological protection and high-quality development in the Yellow River Basin 4. It is an 
important practice to fully implement the concept that “lucid waters and lush mountains are invaluable assets”, 
adhere to harmonious coexistence between man and nature, and promote all-round, all-round, and all-round eco-
logical protection5,6. As a key indicator of ecosystem health, habitat quality directly reflects the capacity of land-
scapes to provide essential ecosystem services, such as biodiversity maintenance, water purification, and climate 
regulation. Moreover, land use changes not only alter habitat patterns but also affect land quality which in turn 
influences the delivery of these services. Therefore, assessing habitat quality dynamics within the framework of 
land quality and ecosystem services is critical for guiding national park management and regional sustainability. 
In 2021, the Chinese government formulated the outline of the Yellow River Basin ecological protection and 
high-quality development plan, indicating that the high-quality ecological protection and high-quality develop -
ment of Yellow River Estuary National Park have been incorporated into the national strategic plan.
Although previous studies have extensively examined the spatial and temporal variation of habitat quality 
under land-use change, most have emphasized changes in habitat quality patterns and future trends. Less atten -
tion has been paid to the relationship between specific land-use transitions and their corresponding habitat quality 
responses. In estuarine wetlands, where human activities and ecological restoration jointly influence land-use 
change, overall habitat quality trends may conceal spatially differentiated processes of degradation and improve-
ment. Clarifying this relationship is therefore important for understanding habitat quality evolution in estuarine 
protected areas.
Against this background, this study examines the spatio-temporal relationship between land-use change and 
habitat quality in the Yellow River Estuary National Park. The PLUS model is employed to characterize and 
simulate land-use dynamics, while the InVEST model is used to evaluate habitat quality under observed and 
projected land-use patterns. (Fig. 1). At the level of land use simulation, the multi-factor driven land use change 
simulation prediction is constructed based on the PLUS model to identify the leading driving mechanism of land 
use spatial evolution in the study area. The land use pattern in 2023 was simulated by using the land use data in 
2003 and 2013, and compared with the actual land use results in 2023 to evaluate the accuracy and applicability 
of the model. On the premise that the reliability of the model has been verified, further use the land use change 
from 2013 to 2023 to predict the Scenario prediction of land use pattern in 2033, providing a spatial basis for 
habitat quality analysis. Finally, based on the land use data at different stages, the simulation results of the PLUS 
model are used as an important input of the InVEST model to quantitatively evaluate the temporal and spatial 
distribution characteristics of habitat quality in the study area in 2003, 2013, 2023 and 2033. The system reveals 
the potential ecological risk of land use change on habitat quality, so as to realize the comprehensive analysis of 
the evolution process of habitat quality evolution in Yellow River Estuary National Park.
Accordingly, the central scientific question of this study is how different land-use transitions are associated 
with spatially differentiated changes in habitat quality within a dynamic estuarine landscape. The research results 
can not only provide a scientific basis for the space control, ecological restoration and adaptive management of 
Yellow River Estuary National Park, but also provide a theoretical basis and typical cases for the research and 
practice of similar land and sea integrated protected areas in the world.

Literature review
Habitat quality is a key indicator to measure ecosystem health and biodiversity sustainability, and its core theory 
has evolved over time 7,8. In the early stage, the definition of habitat quality mainly focused on providing basic 
living conditions such as food, water and breeding sites for species9. With the deepening of ecological research, 
its connotation extends to the comprehensive assessment of ecological integrity and ecosystem services 10. Eco-
logical integrity emphasizes the integrity and stability of ecosystem structure, function and processes. A healthy 
ecosystem can resist external interference and maintain its original state for a long time 11,12. Ecosystem service 
functions link habitat quality with human well-being, emphasizing the ability of habitat to provide support ser -
vices, supply services and cultural services 13,14. Based on the evolution of the above theoretical connotation, 
research on habitat quality has gradually formed a theoretical system with landscape ecology theory and Bio -
diversity Conservation Theory as the core. Landscape ecology emphasizes the impact of spatial pattern on eco -
logical processes, and believes that Land Use and Land Cover Change (LUCC) is a key factor affecting habitat 
quality15,16. Rapid urbanization and agricultural expansion will lead to habitat fragmentation and connectivity 
decline17,18. The theory of ecological diversity protection is the core driving force for the study of habitat qual -
ity, and habitat degradation is the main reason for the decline in biodiversity 19,20. In summary, habitat quality is 
understood as a dynamic, multi-scale, complex system affected by both natural and human factors21.
The habitat quality assessment method system covers the traditional field survey method and model evalua -
tion method. The traditional field survey method has the advantages of high data authenticity and rich details by 
directly obtaining the data on species distribution and habitat characteristics22. However, these methods are often 
expensive and difficult to operate on a large scale or long time series. The RSEI model is based on remote sensing 
data to rapidly assess the regional ecological environment quality by integrating indicators such as greenness, 
humidity, heat and rainfall23. The MaxEnt model is often used to predict species distribution and assess habitat 
suitability by associating species appearance data with environmental factors24. The CA-Markov model is mainly 
used to simulate land use change and indirectly assess habitat quality changes 25,26. These models have their 
own advantages, but the common challenge is how to accurately obtain high-quality input data, calibrate model 
parameters and deal with spatial heterogeneity27. The InVEST model is widely used because it can quantify the 
degree of habitat threatened and assess habitat quality28,29. The habitat quality index is calculated by the sensitiv-
ity of land cover types to threat sources and the impact range of threat sources, which includes multiple factors 
such as economy, nature, society, and land use that jointly affect habitat quality30. This method breaks through the 
inherent limitations of traditional field survey and provides the feasibility for high-precision and dynamic habi -
tat quality assessment. Among these approaches, the InVEST model’s habitat quality module has been widely 
applied because it links land use/cover patterns to ecosystem service outcomes. By quantifying the impacts of 
threats on habitat suitability, it provides a spatially explicit assessment of a key supporting service which under-
pins biodiversity and other regulating and cultural services. This makes the InVEST model particularly suitable 
for studies framed within the land quality–ecosystem services nexus. These methods differ in their data require -
ments and applicability: field surveys provide detailed ecological information but are difficult to apply over large 
areas, RSEI is suitable for regional environmental assessment, MaxEnt is more appropriate for species-specific 
habitat suitability analysis, whereas InVEST provides a spatially explicit framework for evaluating habitat qual-
ity responses to land-use change.
Land-use simulation has gradually evolved from traditional cellular automata approaches toward machine-
learning and deep-learning methods capable of capturing complex spatio-temporal patterns. These approaches 
differ in their data requirements, interpretability, and analytical purposes. Deep-learning methods offer advan -
tages in learning complex spatial and temporal dependencies from data-rich time series, whereas PLUS is well 
suited to identifying the driving factors of land expansion and reproducing spatially explicit land-use transi -
tions. Compared with purely prediction-oriented approaches, process-based land-use models such as PLUS place 
greater emphasis on identifying land expansion rules and their associated driving factors, thereby providing a

The particularity of estuarine wetland habitat quality is that its rivers and coastlines intersect each other, form-
ing a unique ecological environment. Hydrological connectivity, such as runoff and sediment transport, is crucial 
to the formation and maintenance of estuarine wetland habitats 31. Studies have shown that estuarine wetlands 
are important biodiversity hot-spots and are vulnerable to human activities and climate change32. Therefore, it is 
particularly important to evaluate and protect the habitat quality of estuarine wetlands. The existing research on 
the Yellow River Estuary covers wetland evolution in the Yellow River Delta, habitat suitability of rare species, 
and the impact of human activities 31–32. However, relatively limited attention has been paid to the integrated 
coupling of habitat quality evaluation, simulation, prediction, and the quantification of underlying driving mecha-
nisms34,35. Existing studies have tended to focus on assessments at a single time point or scale, with compara -
tively less emphasis on comprehensive analyses that integrate historical evolution, current conditions, and future 
projections36,34. Moreover, although previous studies have examined the effects of human activities, climate 
change, and other driving factors, how these factors interact and how different land-use transitions are associated 
with corresponding habitat quality responses in the Yellow River Estuary remain insufficiently understood32.
Therefore, the PLUS model and InVEST model are used to provide effective tools for land use simulation and 
habitat quality assessment, respectively. The PLUS model can accurately simulate the spatio-temporal evolution 
of land use pattern 37, while the InVEST model can quantitatively assess habitat quality based on land use pat -
tern38,39. The integration of PLUS and InVEST combines the spatial simulation capability of land-use change 
with habitat quality assessment, enabling historical land-use transitions, future scenarios, and corresponding 
habitat quality responses to be examined within a unified spatio-temporal framework40.
Overview of the study area and data sources
Overview of the study area
Yellow River Estuary National Park (37°25′02″―38°17′53″N, 118°13′55″―119°30′57″E) is the first land-sea 
integrated national park in China. This study defines its research area based on the scope of three county-level 
administrative divisions—Kenli District, Hekou District, and Lijin County—as delineated in official documents 
including “the Construction Plan of the Yellow River Estuary National Park” and “the Boundary and Manage-
ment Zoning Map of the Yellow River Estuary National Park” issued by the Shandong Provincial Department of 
Natural Resources (Shandong Provincial Forestry Bureau) (Fig. 2). The region is located in the monsoon climate 
zone of the warm zone, with four distinct seasons and obvious interannual variation of precipitation. The land -
form is dominated by a delta plain and tidal flat, and the dynamic change of coastline is significant. Yellow River 
Estuary National Park has the most complete estuarine wetland ecosystem in China’s warm zone and the most 
active land and sea changes. It is a typical representative of the estuarine wetland ecosystem in the world.

Future land use prediction based on PLUS model
PLUS model validation
To evaluate the predictive performance of the PLUS model, the land-use changes observed from 2003 to 2013 
were used to simulate the land-use pattern in 2023. The simulated 2023 land-use map was then compared with 
the observed 2023 land-use map to assess the agreement between predicted and actual spatial patterns (Fig. 5). 
Overall accuracy and the Kappa coefficient were used as validation metrics. The results showed an overall accu-
racy of 0.85 and a Kappa coefficient of 0.7567 (> 0.75), indicating an acceptable level of agreement between the 
simulated and observed land-use patterns 58,59. Based on this validation, the land-use transition characteristics 
observed during 2013–2023 were subsequently used for the 2033 scenario simulation.

The spatial distribution of cropland, grassland and barren showed a marginal gradient with the county govern-

of population and economic activities, the land use intensity around the administrative center has been continu -
ously improved, and a large number of agricultural and ecological land has been converted into construction 
land, resulting in the significant compression of cropland, grassland and barren in the core area of the town. With 
the increase of the distance from the town, the intensity of human activities has gradually weakened, and can be 
relatively concentrated in the peripheral area. This land use development pattern, dominated by the expansion of 
construction land, not only improves the carrying capacity of cities and towns, but also weakens the spatial integ-
rity of agricultural and ecological land to a certain extent. From the perspective of socioeconomic background, 
China’s urbanization process has long presented a rapid development mode characterized by spatial expansion 
and factor agglomeration, which not only promotes regional economic vitality, but also forms a continuous 
squeeze on agriculture and ecological space.
On the basis of administrative center radiation effect, traffic conditions further magnified the impact of urban-
ization and industrial activities on land use patterns. As an important infrastructure for animal husbandry and 
industrial development, the layout of the class II roads has significantly improved regional accessibility and 
efficiency of factor flow in production, directly supporting the belt and node expansion of construction land. By 
integrating the regional internal transportation system, it promotes the agglomeration and development of indus-
tries along the line that rely on traffic conditions, such as animal husbandry and salt industry, and thus has a far-
reaching impact on the spatial layout of water and related productive land. The population agglomeration effect 
of the administrative center and the industrial amplification effect of traffic conditions are superimposed, which 
jointly promote the transformation of the land use structure in the study area to artificialization and high-intensity 
utilization, and continue to squeeze the low-intensity use of cropland, grassland and barren.

Land use prediction of Yellow River Estuary National Park in 2033
The prediction results of land use pattern of Yellow River Estuary National Park in 2033 are shown in Fig. 7. 
The area of each land use type is 2498.36 km² of cropland, 3.48 km² of grassland, 1127.87 km² of water area, 
36.00 km² of barren and 1650.04 km² of construction land. Cropland is still the largest land use type in the study 
area, but its proportion has decreased to 46.99%, indicating that the trend of agricultural land shrinking will con-
tinue; The proportion of grassland and barren decreased synchronously, indicating that it will tend to be marginal-
ized in the future land cover pattern. In contrast, the water area will increase to 1127.87 km² (21.21%), reflecting 
the sustainable role of wetland restoration and water ecological protection measures in the future scenario; con -

Spatio-temporal evolution characteristics of habitat quality from 2003 to 2023
Based on the land use data of Yellow River Estuary National Park from 2003 to 2023, the Habitat Quality module 
of the InVEST model was used to calculate changes in habitat quality. According to the natural breaks method 
(Jenks) in ArcGIS 10.8 and referring to relevant literature60,61, habitat quality in the study area was classified into 
five levels: low [0, 0.2), moderate-low [0.2, 0.4), moderate [0.4, 0.6), moderate-high [0.6, 0.8), and high [0.8, 
1.0]. To avoid interval overlap, the half-open interval principle was adopted to ensure that each value has a unique 
attribution.
From the perspective of spatial pattern (Fig. 8), the habitat quality of Yellow River Estuary National Park from 
2003 to 2023 showed a significant differentiation characteristic of “high in coastal areas and low in inland areas”. 
Moderate-high and high habitat quality areas are mainly concentrated in the Yellow River Estuary and coastal 
wetland areas, which are dominated by natural water bodies and wetland ecosystems with relatively weak human 
disturbance; while low and moderate-low habitat quality areas are widely distributed in inland cropland areas and 
urban built-up area agglomeration areas, and highly coincide with high-intensity agricultural utilization, urban 
expansion, and transportation infrastructure layout, indicating that land use mode is the key factor driving the 
spatial differentiation of habitat quality.
From the perspective of structural change characteristics (Fig. 9), the composition of habitat quality levels has 
been significantly adjusted during the study period. The proportion of low habitat quality continued to increase, 
becoming the most dominant habitat quality type in the study area; moderate-low habitat quality decreased signif-
icantly, while moderate habitat quality increased in the late stage. Moderate-high and high habitat quality showed 
restorative growth after reaching a phased trough in 2013, but their spatial distribution tended to be fragmented, 
patch sizes were small, and overall connectivity remained weak. In general, the structure of habitat quality levels 
in the study area shows a complex pattern of low-quality expansion and high-quality local restoration.
In order to further reveal the internal mechanism of habitat quality evolution, the analysis is based on the tran-
sition matrix of habitat quality from 2003 to 2023 (Table 8). The results showed that habitat quality changes had

mainly from moderate-low to low habitat quality; from 2013 to 2023, the degradation trend was further intensified, 
and the transformation area from moderate-low to low habitat quality reached 702.45 km². At the same time, the 
transformation from high to moderate-low habitat quality existed in both stages, indicating that high-quality habi-
tat is still facing continuous human activity pressure. In terms of habitat quality improvement, the improvement 
process mainly occurred within moderate-low habitat quality. During 2013–2023, the transformation areas from

Discussion
The observed evolution of habitat quality has direct implications for land quality and ecosystem services in the 
Yellow River Estuary National Park62. The expansion of low-quality habitat associated with built-up land growth 
and intensive land use indicates a decline in the landscape’s capacity to sustain biodiversity and related regulat -
ing services. In contrast, the localized recovery of relatively high-quality habitat in wetland and water-dominated 
areas suggests that ecological restoration can improve habitat conditions in specific locations. However, these 
improvements remain spatially limited and are insufficient to fully offset the broader pressures generated by 
continued land development. The coexistence of degradation and localized recovery indicates that habitat quality 
evolution in the Yellow River Estuary should not be interpreted as a uniform process, but rather as the combined 
outcome of different land-use transitions and their spatially differentiated ecological consequences.
This interpretation is broadly consistent with previous studies showing that urban expansion, agricultural 
intensification, and transportation infrastructure development are important drivers of habitat degradation in 
estuarine regions63,64. However, the present study further indicates that the ecological consequences of land-use 
change vary with both the direction of land conversion and its spatial context. The expansion and concentration of 
built-up land intensify landscape fragmentation and reinforce the persistence of low-quality habitat, particularly 
in inland and peri-urban areas. By contrast, the expansion of water-related ecological space and the restoration 
of wetland areas contribute to localized improvements in habitat quality. This finding is consistent with previous 
evidence that ecological restoration can alleviate habitat degradation to some extent, while also suggesting that 
localized restoration alone is insufficient to offset the broader ecological pressures associated with continued 
land-use restructuring65. Accordingly, the observed pattern of relatively high habitat quality in coastal and estua-
rine areas and lower habitat quality in inland areas reflects not only differences in natural conditions, but also the 
spatial interaction among development pressure, land-use change, and ecological restoration.
These spatial differences have important implications for the management of the Yellow River Estuary National 
Park. Areas characterized by persistently low habitat quality and continued expansion of built-up land should pri-
oritize the control of additional development pressure and the prevention of further fragmentation of ecological 
space. Coastal wetlands and estuarine areas with relatively high habitat quality should focus on maintaining the 
integrity and continuity of existing habitat patches while limiting new disturbances. In areas where habitat quality 
has improved alongside the expansion or restoration of water-related ecological space, management should place 
greater emphasis on consolidating restoration outcomes and strengthening ecological connectivity with surround-
ing habitats. Therefore, ecological restoration should be differentiated according to the spatial characteristics of 
land-use change and habitat quality, rather than relying on uniform interventions across the entire national park. 
Such spatially targeted management can enhance the practical value of habitat quality assessment for conserva -
tion zoning, restoration priority setting, and adaptive ecological management.
Several limitations should nevertheless be acknowledged. First, the InVEST habitat quality model is sensitive 
to the weights, influence distances, habitat suitability values, and sensitivity coefficients assigned to different 
threat factors. Although these parameters were derived from previous studies and adjusted according to the char-
acteristics of the study area, they cannot fully capture the complexity of ecosystem responses to anthropogenic 
disturbance. Second, the PLUS-based projections primarily reflect the continuation of historical land-use tran -
sition patterns and do not fully account for uncertainties associated with extreme climate events, hydrological 
change, or major policy adjustments. Future studies should therefore integrate longer time-series data, field-based

Conclusion
Based on the InVEST-PLUS coupling model, this study constructed a spatiotemporal evolution assessment and 
simulation prediction system for habitat quality in Yellow River Estuary National Park from the perspective of 
land use change, and systematically revealed the differentiation characteristics and response mechanisms of habi-
tat quality patterns under different human activities. The results show that land use transformation and its spatial 
pattern characteristics have a decisive impact on habitat quality evolution.
(1) Habitat quality in Yellow River Estuary National Park showed significant spatial heterogeneity. Coastal wet-
lands and estuarine waters are dominated by continuous natural patches, which maintain high habitat qual -
ity and show strong ecological stability. However, low habitat quality in inland urban expansion areas and 
high-intensity agricultural utilization areas continued to aggregate, forming a pattern of habitat degradation 
dominated by human disturbance. This difference reflects the imbalance between natural ecological space 
and human land expansion in spatial competition.
(2) Different land use evolution paths shape different habitat response patterns. The centralized expansion of 
built-up area aggravates landscape fragmentation by compressing natural habitat space, and significantly 
reduces regional habitat quality. Ecological patches dominated by wetlands and water bodies have buffered 
human activity pressure to a certain extent by maintaining landscape connectivity, forming a low-interfer -
ence, high-quality habitat maintenance mechanism.
(3) Habitat quality evolution has obvious stage-based and nonlinear characteristics. In the early stage of the 
study, rapid urbanization advancement continued to suppress habitat quality. In the later stage, with the inter-
vention of ecological protection and restoration measures, local habitat quality was restored, but low habitat 
quality still showed an expanding trend, indicating that the cumulative effect of human activity intensity 
remains dominant.
(4) Scenario simulation reveals the potential risks of future habitat quality evolution. The prediction results 
show that continuous built-up area expansion in the future will strengthen the stability of low habitat quality. 
Although high habitat quality patches can maintain a certain scale, their spatial connectivity will decline, and 
overall ecosystem resilience will face the risk of weakening.
(5) The study highlights the critical role of habitat quality as a proxy for ecosystem services in the context of 
land use change. The observed patterns of degradation and recovery have direct consequences for land qual-
ity, suggesting that future research should incorporate soil and vegetation attributes to fully assess the multi-
faceted impacts of human activities on estuarine landscapes. This integrated approach will better support 
policy-making aimed at balancing development with ecological conservation in national parks and similar 
protected areas.
The research shows that habitat quality evolution in Yellow River Estuary National Park is essentially the result 
of the coupling effect of land use transformation and ecosystem response. The InVEST-PLUS coupling model 
can provide quantitative support and methodological reference for zoning management and control of national 
parks, identification of priority areas for wetland protection and restoration, and integrated land-sea ecological 
governance.
