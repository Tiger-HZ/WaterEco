The Brazilian Forest Code mandates the creation of legal reserve areas on private properties to protect biodiversity, water resources, and natural ecosystems. However, recent political efforts aim to reduce these reserves, as proposed in bills 3334/2023 and 337/2022. This study examines the biological consequences of reducing Legal Reserves in the Amazon. We reviewed peer-reviewed papers from the Web of Science (WoS) focusing on the effects of fragmentation or habitat loss on predatory beetles, dung beetles, aquatic biota, water quality, water pollution, and the impact of pesticides on anuran amphibians. Reducing Legal Reserves would lead to significant repercussions in three key areas: i) Ecological: increased agricultural pests, species extinctions (both aquatic and terrestrial), decreased biodiversity, water pollution, and disruptions to food chains; ii) Human health: unsafe drinking water, contaminated fish used for food, and exposure to pesticides and heavy metals; iii) Economic: rising costs in agriculture and livestock production, along with direct impacts on traditional communities that rely on fishing for sustenance. This study strongly advocates for rejecting these bills and emphasizes the urgent need to halt Amazon deforestation and implement effective biodiversity management plans.
