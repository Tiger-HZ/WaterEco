Abstract Mesoscale eddies create dynamically distinct environments where thermohaline fronts partition the biological and optical structure of the upper water column. How these fronts organize the vertical and lateral structure of spring blooms is a longstanding observational challenge, largely because the bloom environment evolves rapidly across multiple spatial and temporal scales that are difficult to capture with conventional ship‐based sampling. This study presents a novel in situ dataset, combined with model and satellite‐derived products, examining how the thermohaline front of an anticyclonic eddy in the central Labrador Sea controls the biophysical structure of a spring bloom.
