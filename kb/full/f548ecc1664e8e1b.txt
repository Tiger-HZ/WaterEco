Green roofs play an essential role in mitigating urban environmental impacts, but challenges related to nutrient leaching can compromise their agronomic efficiency and environmental performance. Biochar, a carbon-rich material obtained from the pyrolysis of organic residues, has emerged as a promising additive to improve the physical-chemical properties of green roof substrates. Its high porosity, surface area, and cation exchange capacity contribute to nutrient retention, pH buffering, water holding, and pollutant adsorption. This review aims to evaluate the scientific evidence on the use of biochar in green roof substrates, focusing on its effectiveness in retaining nutrients and reducing leaching losses.
