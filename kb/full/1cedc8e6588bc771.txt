Abstract
Purpose: This study examines the effects of Human Resource
Competence (HRC) and Top Management Support (TMS) on
Financial Reporting Quality (FRQ), with the moderating role of
Organizational Culture (OC) within the Regional Office of the
Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province, Indonesia.
Methodology: The study involved data collected through a
structured questionnaire distributed to 320 financial management
officers at the Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province. The
data were analyzed using PLS-SEM with SmartPLS software.
Results: Empirical evidence demonstrates that HRC and TMS
have positive and significant effects on FRQ. Furthermore, OC
significantly strengthened the relationship between HRC and
FRQ and between TMS and FRQ.
Conclusions: The results demonstrate that improving employee
competence and strengthening managerial commitment are
essential for enhancing the quality of financial reporting. A
supportive organizational culture further reinforces the
effectiveness of these factors in producing transparent, reliable,
and credible financial statements.
Limitations: This study is limited to the Religious Affairs
Ministry of Jambi Province and examines only two antecedents
of financial reporting quality, namely human resource
competence and top management support, with organizational
culture as the moderating variable.
Contributions: Theoretically, this study extends Institutional
Theory by positioning organizational culture as an institutional
mechanism that strengthens the effects of human resource
competence and top management support on financial reporting
quality. Practically, the findings suggest that the Religious Affairs
Ministry should integrate competency development and
managerial support with its five cultural work values to
strengthen the quality of financial reporting.
Keywords: Financial Reporting Quality, Human Resource
Competence, Organizational Culture, Public Sector, Top
Management Support

1. Introduction
The quality of financial reporting as financial information presented by government institutions serves
as the basis for stakeholders to evaluate the effectiveness of public resource management, assess
accountability in the utilization of state budgets, and formulate economic decisions and public policies
more objectively (Nuswantoro & Rahmawati, 2025). Financial statements within the public sector
perform a strategic function as a medium of accountability for the implementation of the State Budget
and are expected to present the government's financial position fairly in accordance with Government
Accounting Standards. Government Regulation of the Republic of Indonesia No. 71 of 2010
concerning Government Accounting Standards stipulates that the quality of financial reporting is
determined by the qualitative characteristics of relevance, faithfulness, comparability, and
understandability. Accordingly, financial information is expected to accurately represent the
underlying economic conditions, remain free from material misstatements, be readily understandable
to users, and effectively support decision-making processes (Janwarin, & Narsa, 2022; Mediaty,
Pontoh, Nagu, HS, & Aziz, 2025). Increasing public demands for accountability in state financial
management have further heightened expectations of the standard of public-sector financial reporting,
given that the performance of contemporary public governance is increasingly determined by
government institutions' capacity to produce financial information that is credible, timely, and
compliant with prevailing statutory and regulatory requirements (Hapsari, & Susilowati, 2023;
Mediaty et al., 2025).
The government's commitment to strengthening financial reporting quality is reflected in a series of
public financial management reforms accompanied by improvements in audit opinions issued by BPK
(2026) across ministries and government agencies, including the Religious Affairs Ministry, which has
consistently maintained an Unqualified Opinion (Wajar Tanpa Pengecualian/WTP) from 2020 to
2025, as presented in Table 1. This achievement indicates continuous improvements in financial
governance at the ministerial level, driven by regulatory reforms, strengthened internal control
systems, and enhanced capacity among financial management personnel (Yusuf, Mediaty, & Mas,
2025). Nevertheless, BPK audit opinions are issued based on consolidated ministerial financial
statements and, therefore, may not fully reflect the quality of financial management practices across
individual subordinate units. Variations in organizational characteristics, human resource capacity,
leadership quality, and organizational culture across work units may result in substantial differences in
the quality of financial reporting. Consequently, empirical investigations at the organizational level
are necessary to gain broader insights into the factors influencing financial reporting quality outcomes
within the Religious Affairs Ministry (Nurnasari, & Muhyarsyah, 2025; Nuswantoro and Rahmawati,
2025; Wardani, Diatmika, & Werastuti, 2023).

A considerable improvement was observed in 2025, when the number of work units experiencing
delays declined to between 4 and 15 per month. This improvement may reflect greater familiarity
among financial operators with application and reporting procedures, despite personnel changes.
However, the persistence of delays indicates that improvements in reporting timeliness have not fully
addressed underlying reporting problems. For example, several work units experienced reconciliation
discrepancies because PNBP had not been entered into the system, while others had not adequately
detailed asset and inventory records or had discrepancies in account classification and incomplete
asset or inventory details. Similar issues persisted in the following months. These recurring
operational deficiencies indicate that financial reporting quality depends on more than familiarity with
the reporting system; the competence of financial personnel and organizational support for financial
reporting processes also contribute to work units' ability to produce timely and reliable financial
information.
The persistence of delays in financial report submission indicates that financial reporting quality
continues to face challenges that cannot be adequately explained solely by favorable audit opinions at
the ministerial level. In practice, various administrative and technical issues remain, affecting the
accountability of financial information (Isnaeni, & Anggraita, 2025; Wardani et al., 2023).
Furthermore, the existence of unfinished construction-in-progress projects and unresolved short-term
liabilities at the reporting date reflects weaknesses in internal control systems and regulatory
compliance (Zahraini, Fithri, & Choiruddin, 2026). These issues demonstrate that the preparation of
government financial statements constitutes an organizational process involving cross-functional
coordination, compliance with established procedures, technical competence among financial
management personnel, effective supervision, and the consistent implementation of operational
mechanisms. Consequently, variations in financial reporting quality across work units are substantially

influenced by internal organizational conditions that shape both individual and collective behavior
throughout the financial management and reporting processes (Nuswantoro & Rahmawati, 2025).
The financial reporting quality in the public sector is fundamentally shaped by the capability of
human resources, as technical expertise, professional experience, and a thorough grasp of government
accounting requirements constitute the essential foundation for producing financial statements that are
reliable, transparent, and accountable (Mahsun, Junaidi, Sumiyana, Nurdiono, & Suparmono, 2025;
Sulastri, Noviarti, Siagian, & Stefhani, 2026). Financial management personnel who possess strong
analytical capabilities and adequate knowledge of Government Accounting Standards are better
positioned to interpret increasingly complex regulatory requirements, manage public financial
transactions accurately, and ensure compliance throughout the financial reporting process. The
effectiveness of these individual competencies is closely associated with the strategic commitment
demonstrated by top management support through the consistent provision of policy direction,
organizational support, resource allocation, and continuous supervision of financial management
activities (Sulastri et al., 2026). The approach of top management support, characterized by
transformational and prophetic values that emphasize integrity, ethical responsibility, vision, and
employee empowerment, creates organizational conditions that strengthen employees' commitment to
institutional objectives and encourage greater consistency in the implementation of financial reporting
responsibilities, particularly within government institutions operating under dynamic regulatory
environments and increasing demands for transparency and public accountability (Basir, 2026;
Nurdian, Josiah, & Ayu, 2026).
Despite the strategic importance of competent personnel and top management support, these
organizational resources do not automatically translate into high-quality financial reporting without
complementary governance mechanisms that reinforce regulatory compliance and organizational
discipline. Organizational culture in the Ministry of Religious Affairs is embodied in the Five Core
Work Culture Values, namely integrity, professionalism, innovation, responsibility, and exemplary
conduct, which serve as behavioral guidelines for all public officials in delivering public services and
managing state resources (Natardi, 2025). The internalization of these values is expected to foster a
working environment characterized by adherence to regulatory requirements, effective coordination
among organizational units, strong commitment to accountability, and discipline throughout every
stage of financial management and reporting (Janwarin and Narsa, 2022; Sasongko, & Kuang, 2024).
Nevertheless, the existence of these organizational values does not automatically guarantee the
achievement of optimal financial reporting quality unless they are consistently translated into day-to-
day organizational practices (Darmasetiawan, Rapina, & Setiawan, 2023). Organizations with
relatively similar levels of human resource competence and top management support may still
produce different levels of financial reporting outcomes, depending on how deeply organizational
culture has been effectively internalized by organizational members (Soesiawan, Lestari, &
Supratikta, 2024). Conversely, an organizational culture that promotes integrity, professionalism,
innovation, responsibility, and exemplary conduct is likely to strengthen the contributions of
employee competence and top management support in producing financial statements that satisfy the
qualitative characteristics prescribed by the Government Accounting Standards (Nugraha, Saring, &
Tubarad, 2026).
Several studies have found that human resource competence contributes positively to financial
reporting quality, although the findings remain context-dependent. Wardani et al.
(2023) and Nuswantoro and Rahmawati (2025) reported positive contributions of human resource
competence to financial reporting quality, whereas Alminanda and Marfuah (2018) found an
insignificant relationship in regional government entities. Similar inconsistencies are evident in
studies of top management support by various accounting researchers. Trisnawati, Ahyani, and Ulfiati
(2020) and Hapsari and Susilowati (2023) demonstrated that top management support enhances
financial reporting, while Hidayakh, Rahayu, and Gowon (2020) found no significant effect of top
management support on financial reporting quality. These divergent findings suggest that the effects

of employee competence and managerial support may depend on the organizational conditions that
facilitate their translation into effective financial reporting practices.
Organizational culture may represent one such condition because it shapes employees' behavior,
ethical standards, and adherence to organizational procedures. According to Janwarin and Narsa
(2022) and Rasul, Kudrun, and Salih (2026), organizational culture and the ethical climate represent
critical contextual factors capable of shaping employee behavior and managerial commitment.
Darmasetiawan et al. (2023) found that organizational culture contributes to financial report
presentation quality, while Soesiawan et al. (2024) demonstrated the relevance of organizational
factors to financial reporting accountability. However, empirical evidence on the moderating role of
organizational culture in the relationship between human resource competence and financial reporting
quality and between top management support and financial reporting quality remains limited. This gap
is particularly relevant in the Religious Affairs Ministry, where the institutionalization of the Five
Work Values may shape how employee competence and managerial support are translated into
financial-reporting practices.
Although the Religious Affairs Ministry has consistently received an Unqualified Opinion at the
consolidated level, operational evidence from MONSAKTI indicates that reporting delays and
reconciliation problems continue to occur across work units, suggesting that favorable ministerial
audit outcomes do not necessarily eliminate reporting challenges at the organizational level.
Therefore, this study positions organizational culture as a moderating mechanism and examines
whether it strengthens the effects of human resource competence and top management support on the
quality of financial reporting. Specifically, this study examines whether human resource competence
positively influences financial reporting quality, whether top management support positively
influences financial reporting quality, and whether organizational culture moderates the relationship
between human resource competence and financial reporting quality and top management support and
financial reporting quality.
This study presents two extensions of prior scholarly inquiries. First, it extends prior research using
institutional theory by integrating Human Resource Competence, Top Management Support, and
Organizational Culture, positioning Organizational Culture as a moderating variable to explain the
conditions under which managerial and human resource capabilities contribute more effectively to
Financial Reporting Quality in the public sector. Second, this study provides empirical evidence from
the Indonesian Ministry of Religious Affairs, a highly regulated public sector institution characterized
by distinct organizational values, standardized financial management systems, and complex
accountability requirements, thereby enriching the limited scholarly evidence that examines the
contextual function of organizational culture as a moderating variable in strengthening financial
reporting quality within government organizations.
2. Literature Review and Hypotheses Development
2.1 Institutional Theory
Institutional Theory posits that organizational survival depends on the ability to attain legitimacy by
conforming to the norms, values, rules, and expectations embedded within the institutional
environment, rather than solely pursuing economic efficiency (Powell & DiMaggio, 2023).
Organizations respond to institutional pressures through coercive mechanisms arising from
regulations, normative pressures derived from professional standards and education, and mimetic
pressures resulting from the adoption of practices perceived as successful by peer organizations,
thereby promoting organizational isomorphism and reducing uncertainty (Isnaeni & Anggraita, 2025;
Powell & DiMaggio, 2023). In the public sector, these institutional pressures encourage government
organizations to comply with Government Accounting Standards (SAP), strengthen governance
practices, and improve accountability in financial management.
Accordingly, this study employs Institutional Theory to explain that: human resource competence
reflects normative pressures through professional knowledge and technical capability; top
management support represents internal institutional commitment manifested through strategic

leadership and the provision of organizational resources; and organizational culture functions as an
internal institutional mechanism that reinforces shared values and behavioral norms. The interaction
among these institutional elements is expected to strengthen organizational compliance with
accounting regulations and ultimately enhance Financial reporting quality by producing financial
information that fulfills stakeholders' demands for transparency, responsibility, and institutional
legitimacy (Darmasetiawan et al., 2023; Mediaty et al., 2025).
2.2 Financial Reporting Quality
Financial reporting quality is a fundamental concept in public sector governance because it reflects a
government's ability to provide credible financial information that serves as the basis for
accountability to stakeholders and supports economic, social, and political decision-making
(Nuswantoro & Rahmawati, 2025). High-quality financial information is expected to faithfully
represent an organization's financial position and performance by ensuring that it is objective, neutral,
and free from material misstatements, thereby enabling stakeholders to effectively evaluate public
resource management (Nasution & Supriadi, 2024). Although previous studies consistently recognize
financial reporting quality as a key mechanism for enhancing public accountability, they emphasize
different dimensions.
Arif & Erna (2022) conceptualizes financial reporting quality as an indicator of stewardship and
transparency in public fiscal administration through the fair presentation of financial information,
whereas Nasution & Supriadi (2024) highlight the significance of complete, transparent, and unbiased
financial reporting for enhancing the usefulness of information value for assessing financial
performance and organizational accountability. Extending these perspectives, Mediaty et al.
(2025) and Zahraini et al. (2026) argue that public institutions' ability to produce reliable financial
information reflects their commitment to transparency, accountability, and good governance.
Collectively, these studies suggest that financial reporting quality extends beyond the technical
preparation of financial statements by encompassing the broader governance function of enhancing
accountability, public trust, and informed decision-making.
Within the Indonesian public sector accounting system, financial reporting quality is assessed based
on compliance with the Government Accounting Standards (Standar Akuntansi Pemerintahan/SAP),
as stipulated in Government Regulation No. 71 of 2010, which requires financial information to
possess the qualitative characteristics of relevance, faithful representation (reliability), comparability,
and understandability to satisfy users' information needs. Relevant information is expected to provide
predictive and confirmatory value while being reported promptly. Reliable information should
faithfully represent economic events, remain free of material misstatements, and be verifiable by
independent parties (Yusuf et al., 2025).
In practice, financial reporting quality is frequently reflected in audit opinions issued by the Supreme
Audit Board of Indonesia (Badan Pemeriksa Keuangan/BPK), where an Unqualified Opinion (Wajar
Tanpa Pengecualian/WTP) reflects that financial statements conform to relevant accounting standards
and present an entity's financial standing in all material respects (Hanly, Indahwati, & Surianti, 2023).
However, previous studies argue that compliance with accounting standards and the attainment of an
Unqualified Opinion do not necessarily guarantee high-quality financial reporting, as reporting quality
remains contingent upon robust internal control arrangements and the ethical climate within
organizations to minimize the risk of material misstatements (Janwarin & Narsa, 2022). Accordingly,
this study defines financial reporting quality as the capacity of government financial statements to
deliver relevant, credible, transparent, comparable, and understandable financial information in
accordance with Government Accounting Standards, thereby supporting public accountability and
high-quality decision-making.
2.3 Human Resource Competence
Human Resource Competence (HRC) refers to the capability of personnel to perform organizational
duties and responsibilities through the integration of knowledge, technical skills, and professional
attitudes that are aligned with job requirements and established professional standards, making it a

498
strategic organizational resource for enhancing the effectiveness of public sector governance
(Trisnawati et al., 2020). Human resource competence extends beyond technical proficiency and
encompasses the ability to interpret regulations, analyze organizational problems, make appropriate
decisions, and adapt professional expertise to changes in the organizational environment and public
administrative systems. Within the context of public financial management, competent personnel are
essential for ensuring the accuracy of accounting processes, as financial management officers are
expected to possess comprehensive knowledge of the Government Accounting Standards (Standar
Akuntansi Pemerintahan/SAP), budget administration procedures, and financial reporting obligations,
thereby ensuring the proper recording of each transaction, classified, and reported in accordance with
the applicable regulations. Studies by Nuswantoro & Rahmawati (2025) suggest that highly
competent public officials are better equipped to apply financial regulations consistently, complete
reporting processes in a timely manner, and produce financial information with greater reliability and
credibility than individuals with limited technical capabilities.
Empirical evidence generally supports the positive contribution of human resource competence to
financial reporting quality by improving the effectiveness of accounting processes and reducing the
risk of reporting errors. Nevertheless, previous studies have emphasized different mechanisms through
which this relationship occurs. Nasution & Supriadi (2024) argue that competent personnel enhance
the effectiveness of accounting information systems, thereby improving the credibility of financial
statements. In contrast, Mediaty et al. (2025) and Wardani et al. (2023) highlight that technical
knowledge, professional experience, and practical expertise directly improve the decision usefulness,
reliability, and consistency of financial information. Furthermore, the increasing digitalization of
public financial management has expanded the scope of human resource competence to include digital
literacy and the ability to operate government financial management information systems effectively.
In this regard, Yusuf et al. (2025) contend that the effectiveness of digital technologies ultimately
hinges on the competence of their users, rather than on the technology itself. Collectively, these
empirical studies demonstrate that human resource competence encompasses the integration of public-
sector accounting knowledge, technical proficiency in operating financial information systems, and
professional behavior that enables the production of dependable, timely, and regulation-compliant
financial statements.
H₁: Human resource competence positively influences financial reporting quality
2.4 Top Management Support
Top Management Support (TMS) constitutes a critical strategic determinant in the public sector by
ensuring the effective implementation of accounting policies, allocation of adequate organizational
resources, and establishment of robust oversight mechanisms that promote transparent and
accountable financial reporting. Organizational outcomes reflect the values, cognitive orientations,
and commitment of senior executives, as top managers establish the tone at the top that shapes
employees' behavior toward financial integrity, regulatory compliance, and organizational
accountability (Biloblovskyi, Haidaienko, Khrystenko, Demchuk, & Morozova, 2024). Empirical
evidence largely supports this theoretical perspective, although studies emphasize different
mechanisms through which managerial commitment enhances Financial Reporting Quality.
Trisnawati, Ahyani, and Ulfiati (2020) found that leadership commitment strengthens employee
motivation and improves the accuracy of financial statements, whereas Al-Hashimy, Yao, and Pitchay
(2025) demonstrated that active managerial support enables organizations to translate investments in
computerized accounting systems into higher-quality financial information. Similarly, Hapsari and
Susilowati (2023) argued that top management involvement in strategic planning, oversight, and
decision-making activities is critical to reducing the risk of material misstatements and ensuring that
financial management information systems operate effectively to satisfy stakeholder accountability
requirements. Collectively, these studies indicate that Top management support contributes to
financial reporting quality by fostering an organizational environment that facilitates effective
financial management, regulatory compliance, and reliable financial reporting.

Despite its theoretical significance, empirical evidence regarding the effectiveness of top management
support remains inconclusive. While many studies report a positive relationship between managerial
commitment and financial reporting quality, others have produced contradictory findings. Hidayakh,
Rahayu, and Gowon (2020) found that top management support had no significant effect on financial
reporting quality, suggesting that managerial commitment may function merely as a formal
compliance mechanism rather than a substantive driver of reporting reliability. Nurnasari and
Muhyarsyah (2025) found that conventional leadership practices do not necessarily improve financial
reporting quality in complex public sector organizations, where bureaucratic structures and
fragmented responsibilities may weaken the effectiveness of top management initiatives. These
inconsistencies suggest that the relationship between top management support and financial reporting
quality is unlikely to be universally direct and may instead depend on contextual organizational
factors. Accordingly, the contradictory findings in the existing literature underscore the importance of
examining Organizational culture as a moderating mechanism that strengthens or weakens the
influence of top management support on financial reporting quality.
H₂: Top management support positively influences financial reporting quality
2.5 Organizational Culture
Organizational Culture represents a system comprising shared values, beliefs, behavioral expectations,
and norms that shape how organizational members interpret accounting practices and perform
financial reporting responsibilities. Organizational Culture determines whether employees'
competencies and the strategic direction from top management can be effectively translated into high-
quality financial information. Studies by Janwarin and Narsa (2022); Wulandari, Khafid, and
Wahyuningrum (2020) generally support the view that a strong ethical culture reinforces the positive
effects of human resource competence and the effectiveness of internal controls on financial
accountability by fostering integrity, compliance, and organizational commitment. Collectively, these
findings indicate that organizational culture establishes the behavioral environment through which
institutional resources and managerial initiatives are transformed into reliable financial reporting
practices.
Despite this consensus, the literature presents inconsistent findings regarding the role of
organizational culture in improving financial reporting quality. Bhandari, Mammadov, Thevenot, and
Vakilzadeh (2022) argued that organizational cultures characterized by excessive collaboration and
strong interpersonal relationships may inadvertently weaken formal control mechanisms, thereby
increasing opportunities for earnings management and reducing reporting quality. These findings
challenge the conventional assumption that harmonious workplace environments enhance
organizational transparency. Similarly, Soesiawan, Lestari, and Supratikta (2024); Libuang, Sondakh,
Morasa, Budiarso, Tumiwa, and Nagy (2025) found that the moderating role of organizational values
is context-dependent and does not consistently strengthen the effects of technical capabilities or
managerial commitment across different public sector settings. These inconsistencies suggest that the
influence of organizational culture is contingent upon the organizational setting within which it is
embedded. Accordingly, further investigation is required to understand how context-specific
organizational values, such as the Five Core Work Values of the Indonesian Ministry of Religious
Affairs, interact with human resource competence and top management support to mitigate the risk of
material misstatements and strengthen transparent and accountable financial reporting in public sector
organizations, particularly within the Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province.

2.6 Research Framework
The research framework presented in Figure 1 illustrates the proposed relationships among human
resource competence, top management support, organizational culture, and financial reporting quality.
Human resource competence and top management support were positioned as independent variables
expected to directly influence financial reporting quality. Higher levels of human resource competence
are expected to enhance employees' ability to perform financial management and reporting tasks
accurately, while adequate top management support is expected to provide the direction, resources,
and organizational commitment necessary to prepare high-quality financial reports. Accordingly, these
relationships are formulated as H₁ and H₂, respectively.
Figure 1 incorporates organizational culture as a moderating variable. Organizational culture is
expected to influence the strength of the relationship between the two independent variables and
financial reporting quality. Specifically, a supportive organizational culture may strengthen the effect
of human resource competence on financial reporting quality by encouraging employees to
consistently apply their knowledge, skills, and abilities in financial reporting practices. Similarly, an
organizational culture that promotes accountability, collaboration, compliance, and commitment may
strengthen the influence of top management support on financial reporting quality by facilitating
effective implementation of managerial direction and organizational resources. These moderating
relationships are represented by H₃ and H₄. Thus, the proposed framework extends direct-effect
relationships by considering organizational culture as a contextual factor that may determine how
effectively human resource competence and top management support contribute to financial reporting
quality.
As illustrated in Figure 1, the proposed model assumes that financial reporting quality is influenced by
both individual capabilities and organizational support, while the organizational context may
determine the extent to which these factors translate into improved reporting outcomes. Therefore, the
model moves beyond examining the direct effects of human resource competence and top
management support by recognizing organizational culture as a contextual mechanism that may
strengthen or weaken these relationships. This framework provides the basis for testing whether a
supportive organizational culture enhances the contribution of competent financial personnel and top
managerial support to achieving higher financial reporting quality.
3. Research Methodology
A quantitative research design was adopted to investigate the associations between human resource
competence, top management support, organizational culture, and financial reporting quality. The
study was conducted by the Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province, Indonesia. The population
comprised financial management personnel working within the Regional Office, including Budget
User Authorities (KPA), Commitment-Making Officers (PPK), Payment Order Signing Officers

(PPSPM), Treasurers, Financial Management Staff, and Assistant Expenditure Treasurers (PPABP),
with a total population of 900 employees. Purposive sampling was employed because the study
required respondents with direct knowledge of and experience in financial management and reporting
activities. Thus, respondents were selected based on their direct involvement in preparing, managing,
reviewing, or supporting financial reporting processes, ensuring that data were obtained from
personnel with relevant responsibilities and experience. Following the recommendation of (Hair,
Hollingsworth, Randolph, & Chong, 2017), the minimum sample size for PLS-SEM analysis is
determined using a ratio of 10 respondents per indicator. The research model comprised 24
measurement indicators, with some indicators operationalized through more than one statement.
Accordingly, the minimum required sample size was 240 respondents (24 × 10 respondents). A total
of 320 valid responses were obtained and retained for the subsequent analysis.
This study utilized original data gathered through a standardized questionnaire distributed to the
respondents. Measurement instruments were derived from previously validated scales and adapted to
align with the context of public-sector financial management. Responses were measured using a
semantic differential scale ranging from 1 (never) to 7 (always), enabling participants to report the
frequency with which each statement reflected their organizational practices and experiences (Powell
& DiMaggio, 2023). The questionnaire measured four latent constructs: human resource competence,
top management support, organizational culture, and financial reporting quality.
The empirical data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-
SEM) through SmartPLS software. The analysis was conducted in two stages. The first stage was the
outer model, which involved the evaluation of the measurement model by assessing convergent
validity through factor loadings and Average Variance Extracted (AVE), discriminant validity using
the Fornell–Larcker criterion, and internal consistency reliability using Cronbach's Alpha and
Composite Reliability. The second stage is the inner model, which involves the evaluation of the
structural model through the coefficient of determination (R²), effect size (f²), model fit assessment
using the Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR) and Normed Fit Index (NFI), followed
by hypothesis testing and moderation analysis using the bootstrapping procedure to examine the
significance of the proposed direct and moderating relationships.

4.5 Discussion
4.5.1 The Influence of Human Resource Competence on Financial Reporting Quality
The empirical findings demonstrate that human resource competence has a positive and significant
effect on financial reporting quality, indicating that improvements in employees' knowledge, technical
expertise, professional experience, and understanding of government accounting standards
substantially enhance the ability of public sector organizations to produce financial statements
consistent with prevailing accounting requirements and satisfy the fundamental attributes of decision-
useful financial information (Nasution & Supriadi, 2024). Human resource competence represents a
strategic organizational capability that enables financial management personnel to accurately
recognize, measure, record, classify, and disclose financial transactions in accordance with
Government Accounting Standards while maintaining consistency with prevailing regulatory
requirements (Mediaty et al., 2025). Employees with adequate accounting knowledge, technical
proficiency, analytical ability, and professional judgment are more capable of identifying reporting
errors, responding appropriately to changes in financial regulations, and ensuring the continued
relevance, reliability, comparability, and timeliness of financial statements throughout the reporting
cycle, thereby strengthening organizational accountability and reducing the likelihood of material
misstatements in public sector financial reporting (Libuang et al., 2025).
The preparation of financial reports within the Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province involves
increasingly complex administrative and accounting processes following the implementation of
integrated government financial management systems, including SAKTI and MONSAKTI, which
require financial management personnel to continuously adapt to evolving regulatory provisions,
technological developments, and reporting procedures established by the central government. These
institutional demands require employees to demonstrate comprehensive competencies encompassing
government accounting, information technology, regulatory interpretation, internal control
implementation, and problem-solving capabilities to ensure accurate and consistent financial reporting
activities despite frequent revisions to financial policies and digital applications. In response to these
challenges, the Religious Affairs Ministry has continuously invested in competency development
through technical guidance programs (bimbingan teknis), professional education and training,
competency enhancement initiatives, and certification programs designed to strengthen employees'
technical capacity and professional expertise in public financial management, creating an
organizational environment in which financial reporting responsibilities can be executed more
effectively while supporting higher levels of transparency, accountability, and public trust in
government financial information.
These findings are consistent with Institutional Theory, which states that human resource competence
serves as an institutional capability that enables employees to internalize regulatory expectations and
transform formal accounting requirements into standardized organizational practices that are
consistently implemented throughout financial management processes (Mediaty et al., 2025). The
2026 | Annals of Human Resource Management Research / Vol 6 No 3, 493-512
506
competence of financial management personnel facilitates the interpretation of increasingly dynamic
government financial policies, supports appropriate professional judgment when addressing complex
accounting issues, and encourages greater adherence to institutional rules governing financial
accountability (Arif & Erna, 2022). This institutional perspective is particularly relevant within the
Religious Affairs Ministry, where the quality of financial reporting depends on the extent to which
employees can embed regulatory requirements into routine organizational practices while responding
effectively to institutional expectations regarding transparency, accountability, and good governance.
This allows financial reporting processes to remain consistent with the principles of public sector
financial management and reinforces the legitimacy of institutions in fulfilling their accountability
obligations.
4.5.2 The Influence of Top Management Support on Financial Reporting Quality
The empirical findings indicate that top management support has a positive and significant effect on
financial reporting quality, demonstrating that the support of organizational leaders plays a pivotal
role in establishing conditions conducive to the preparation of transparent, reliable, and accountable
financial reports. Top management support in the public sector extends beyond administrative
oversight and encompasses strategic leadership reflected in policy direction, resource allocation,
organizational coordination, supervision, and continuous monitoring of financial management
activities, all of which influence employees' ability to implement government accounting standards
consistently and effectively (Al-Hashimy et al., 2025). Strong top managerial support encourages the
establishment of organizational priorities that emphasize regulatory compliance, strengthens internal
communication among financial management units, facilitates the timely resolution of operational
constraints, and creates a work environment in which financial reporting responsibilities are
performed with greater accuracy and professional accountability, thereby supporting the production of
financial information that satisfies the qualitative characteristics required in the public sector (Isnaeni
& Anggraita, 2025; Nurnasari & Muhyarsyah, 2025).
Top management support in the Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province is reflected through the
active involvement of organizational leaders in implementing bureaucratic reform and strengthening
public financial governance in accordance with the strategic priorities of the ministry. Top
management support is demonstrated through continuous supervision of financial management
processes, provision of adequate budgetary and technological resources, implementation of merit-
based human resource development policies, and encouragement of the effective utilization of
integrated financial management applications such as SAKTI and MONSAKTI, which serve as the
primary platforms for budget execution, accounting, and financial reporting across government
institutions. These managerial initiatives are aligned with the Ministry's Five Work Values Integrity,
Professionalism, Innovation, Responsibility, and Exemplary Conduct which provide behavioral
guidance for employees in carrying out their financial management responsibilities while fostering
greater discipline, ethical awareness, collaboration, and adherence to government accounting
regulations (Natardi, 2025). Such organizational support enables financial management personnel to
adapt more effectively to evolving regulatory requirements, technological developments, and
increasingly complex reporting procedures while maintaining consistency in implementing public
sector financial management practices (Al-Hashimy et al., 2025).
From the Institutional Theory perspective, top management support represents an institutional
mechanism through which organizational leaders communicate legitimacy by reinforcing regulatory
expectations, promoting accountability, and embedding governance principles into routine
organizational activities (Isnaeni & Anggraita, 2025). This supportive leadership encourages
employees to consistently implement government accounting standards while aligning organizational
behavior with broader expectations of transparency, accountability, and public sector legitimacy (Arif
& Erna, 2022). Consequently, the synergy between strategic leadership and a supportive institutional
environment facilitates the production of high-quality financial reports that meet both regulatory
requirements and stakeholder expectations (Mediaty et al., 2025).

4.5.3 The Moderating Effect of Organizational Culture on the Relationship between Human Resource
Competence and Financial Reporting Quality
The empirical findings demonstrate that organizational culture positively and significantly moderates
the relationship between human resource competence and the quality of financial reporting. The
contribution of employees' competencies to financial reporting quality becomes stronger when
supported by an organizational environment characterized by shared values, ethical norms, and
collective commitment to accountability and regulatory compliance (Darmasetiawan et al., 2023).
Although technical competence equips financial management personnel with the knowledge and
professional capabilities required to implement Government Accounting Standards, operate integrated
financial management systems, and exercise sound professional judgment, the extent to which these
competencies are translated into high-quality financial reporting depends on the organizational culture
that shapes employees' attitudes and work behavior (Nugraha et al., 2026). Organizational culture
establishes behavioral expectations that encourage employees to consistently apply their knowledge,
collaborate across functional units, comply with standardized financial procedures, and maintain
professional responsibility throughout the financial reporting process, thereby creating institutional
conditions in which individual competence generates greater organizational value in producing
transparent, reliable, and accountable financial information (Abernethy, Li, Zhang, & Shi, 2023;
Bhandari et al., 2022).
The contribution of human resource competence effectiveness in the Religious Affairs Ministry of
Jambi Province is reinforced by the institutionalization of the Ministry's Five Work Values: integrity,
professionalism, innovation, responsibility, and exemplary conduct. These organizational values
function as behavioral standards that guide financial management personnel in carrying out
increasingly complex financial administration through integrated applications such as SAKTI and
MONSAKTI, while ensuring compliance with Government Accounting Standards (SAP) and public
financial management regulations (Wardani et al., 2023). Integrity encourages employees to maintain
honesty and objectivity in recording financial transactions; professionalism promotes continuous
competency development and adherence to technical standards; innovation facilitates employees'
adaptability to evolving financial regulations and digital systems; responsibility strengthens
commitment toward timely and accurate financial reporting; and exemplary conduct encourages
leaders and senior personnel to cultivate ethical work practices that influence organizational behavior
across all administrative levels (Nugraha et al., 2026). Integrating these cultural values into routine
financial management activities creates an organizational environment in which competent employees
can utilize their technical expertise effectively while maintaining consistency with institutional
expectations regarding transparency, accountability, and public service (Isnaeni & Anggraita, 2025;
Mediaty et al., 2025).
4.5.4 The Moderating Effect of Organizational Culture on the Relationship between Top Management
Support and Financial Reporting Quality
The findings indicate that organizational culture positively and significantly moderates the
relationship between top management support and financial reporting quality. This suggests that the
positive influence of top management support on financial reporting quality becomes stronger when
accompanied by a supportive organizational culture. While top management provides strategic
direction through policy formulation, resource allocation, supervision, and performance evaluation,
the effectiveness of these managerial actions depends on the organizational values that guide
employees' daily behavior (Al-Hashimy et al., 2025; Hanly et al., 2023). A culture that emphasizes
integrity, accountability, professionalism, collaboration, and compliance encourages employees to
respond more effectively to managerial directives, thereby enhancing the precision, reliability, and
timeliness of financial reporting (Janwarin & Narsa, 2022). Consequently, organizational culture
strengthens the capacity of top management to generate higher-quality financial reports (Mediaty et
al., 2025).
Within the Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province, financial reporting is a collaborative process
involving multiple functional units responsible for budgeting, accounting, treasury, and reporting. The
implementation of government financial systems, such as SAKTI and MONSAKTI, requires technical
2026 | Annals of Human Resource Management Research / Vol 6 No 3, 493-512
508
guidance from top management and collective adherence to standardized procedures and
organizational values. The Religious Affairs Ministry promotes Five Core Work Values: integrity,
professionalism, innovation, responsibility, and exemplary conduct, which establish behavioral
expectations for all employees (Natardi, 2025). When these values are consistently practiced,
managerial support is more effectively translated into coordinated financial management practices,
stronger inter-unit communication, and greater compliance with the government accounting standards.
Consequently, organizational culture reinforces the impact of top management support by creating an
environment where managerial decisions are implemented consistently across organizational levels,
ultimately enhancing the quality of financial reporting.
These findings are consistent with the tenets of Institutional Theory, which posits that organizational
effectiveness is influenced by formal governance structures and institutional norms, shared values,
and socially accepted organizational practices (Isnaeni & Anggraita, 2025). Institutional Theory
explains that leaders are more likely to influence organizational outcomes when their policies are
supported by institutionalized cultural values that encourage employees to internalize organizational
expectations (Mediaty et al., 2025). Top management support in the Ministry of Religious Affairs
derives its effectiveness from hierarchical authority and its alignment with organizational values that
legitimize accountability, transparency, and compliance as collective norms. Therefore, organizational
culture functions as an informal institutional mechanism that amplifies the effectiveness of managerial
commitment in improving the quality of financial reporting. The significant moderating effect
identified in this study confirms that the quality of financial reporting in public sector organizations
depends on managerial commitment and the institutional environment that enables managerial
policies to be consistently adopted and practiced throughout the organization.
5. Conclusions
5.1 Conclusion
This study found that human resource competence and top management support positively influence
financial reporting quality in the Ministry of Religious Affairs of Jambi Province. Organizational
culture also significantly strengthens both relationships, demonstrating that the organizational context
plays an important role in translating employee competence and managerial support into higher-
quality financial reporting. Theoretically, these findings extend Institutional Theory by positioning
organizational culture as an institutional mechanism that reinforces the effects of human resource
competence and top management support on financial reporting quality. The findings indicate that
improving financial reporting quality requires an integrated approach. Policymakers and Ministry
leadership should strengthen the competency development of financial management personnel and
ensure sustained managerial guidance, supervision, and organizational support. Financial management
units should translate the Five Work Values into routine reporting practices through consistent
responsibility, coordination, supervision, and compliance with financial reporting procedures.
Integrating these measures can strengthen the reliability, transparency, and accountability of financial
reports.
5.2 Research Limitations
The findings are limited to the Regional Office of the Religious Affairs Ministry of Jambi Province
and, therefore, may not be generalizable to other public sector organizations in Indonesia.
Furthermore, the research model included only human resource competence and top management
support as determinants of financial reporting quality, with organizational culture serving as the sole
moderating variable. Other organizational, technological, governance, and related factors that may
influence financial reporting quality were not considered in this study. Moreover, the adoption of a
cross-sectional research design restricts the extent to which temporal developments can be examined
adequately.
5.3 Suggestions and Directions for Future Research
Future research could broaden the empirical scope by encompassing a more diverse array of public-
sector institutions across multiple geographical settings, thereby enhancing the external validity and
generalizability of the findings. Subsequent studies may also integrate a wider spectrum of

509
organizational, technological, and governance-related determinants to develop a more nuanced
understanding of the factors that shape financial reporting quality. Moreover, the adoption of
longitudinal and mixed-method research designs could yield deeper insights into the temporal
dynamics and interrelationships among the investigated constructs, while elucidating the underlying
mechanisms through which these factors influence the financial reporting quality of public sector
organizations.
