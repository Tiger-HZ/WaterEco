Abstract This study aimed to evaluate lairage behavior, carcass lesions, blood biomarkers, and meat quality in pigs transported over two distances (28 ± 10 km and 112 ± 24 km) during two seasons (winter and summer). A total of 256 surgically castrated male pigs were evaluated under commercial slaughter conditions. Behavioral observations were conducted in 10 pens in winter and 8 pens in summer. Pigs transported during summer spent 20.4% more time drinking than those transported in winter (6.5% vs. 5.4% of lairage time). Regardless of distance or season, pigs spent more time standing during the first hour and lying down during the second and third hours (p &lt; 0.05), with a 45% increase in lying behavior from the first to the second hour.
