Abstract Chestnut (Castanea sativa Mill.) coppices are increasingly exposed to interacting biotic and climatic stressors that may affect stand health and timber quality. This study assessed the occurrence and the possible technological impact on wood quality in basal logs in a 37-year-old coppice stand in central Italy by integrating field surveys, sawmill assessments, dendrochronological analyses, phenological observations and multitemporal satellite imagery. Dark-brown wood discoloration and localized basal decay were detected in coppice shoots. The mean length of the discarded basal log in the sawmill was 0.87 m, with a total of 0.91 m³, corresponding to 2.4% of the processed stem volume.
