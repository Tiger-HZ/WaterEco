The increasing demand for water resources has made the protection of aquatic ecosystems and the monitoring of water quality increasingly imperative. In this context, cyanobacteria stand out as photosynthetic prokaryotic microorganisms. Cyanobacterial blooms triggered by eutrophication can produce toxins that negatively affect water quality. Cyanobacteria are morphologically divided into groups such as Chroococcales, Nostocales, Oscillatoriales, Pleurocapsales, and Stigonematales. Some of their species support plant growth by fixing nitrogen. With seasonal changes, algal blooms can occur, and these processes can disrupt ecosystem balance and reduce dissolved oxygen levels in the water. Cyanobacteria are also of economic and industrial importance, with potential use in the food, fuel, fertilizer, and pharmaceutical industries. For example, Spirulina is a popular food supplement, as it contains high levels of protein, vitamins, and minerals in its content. In this chapter, the effects of cyanobacteria on 36 water quality, their toxin types, biodiversity, and ecological roles, as well as their potential in different areas of application, are analyzed. The chapter argues that cyanobacteria pose a risk to ecosystem health, especially in terms of algal bloom, eutrophication, and cyanotoxin production, but can also be beneficial in various industrial and biotechnological applications.
