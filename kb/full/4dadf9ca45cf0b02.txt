Understanding the dissolution of alloy nanoparticles in corrosive environments is essential for designing stable electrocatalysts. In this study, we investigate the corrosion behavior of AuxPdy nanoparticles in halide-containing electrolytes using cyclic voltammetry combined with ICPMS quantification of dissolved metals. The results demonstrate that metal dissolution depends on the complexing strength of the halide ions. Alloying Pd with Au significantly suppresses Pd dissolution within the potential range where Au remains stable against dissolution. Conversely, increasing the Pd content shifts Au oxidation to lower potentials, suggesting that while Au stabilizes Pd, Pd reduces the electrochemical oxidation stability of Au.
