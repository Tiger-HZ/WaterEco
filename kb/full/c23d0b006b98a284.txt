There are direct and indirect drivers of biodiversity loss that contribute to species extinction and, consequently, to the loss of ecosystem services. Most of these drivers are of anthropogenic origin and, particularly in recent times, new consequences of industrial or economic activities have been identified that irreparably disrupt ecosystems. There is a race against time to establish conservation and management policies for biodiversity that can halt or mitigate these impacts. Additionally, species face threats related to habitat fragmentation and transformation. As habitat contamination increases, wildlife displacement and migration occur, while climate change promotes the expansion of vectors and pathogens.
