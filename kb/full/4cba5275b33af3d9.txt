Central European countries face lower environmental awareness, reflected in the limited number of studies on eco-innovation in this area. This study contributes to research on environmental innovation in the Czech Republic and Slovakia, which have been among the major European polluters in the past. In contrast to previous studies, we focus on indicators such as energy management, waste minimisation and recycling, and air pollution control measures. The results show that despite the geographical and historical proximity of the Czech Republic and Slovakia, environmental innovation is influenced by different determinants in different areas. In the Czech Republic, the presence of a manager responsible for the environment and climate change was confirmed as a key determinant of firms’ environmental initiatives. In Czech and Slovak firms, environmental regulations trigger environmental innovation, specifically those measures focused on reducing emissions, directly impacting public health and environmental protection. At the same time the Environmental and CO2 Emission goals are an incentive for eco-innovation in the Czech Republic and Slovakia. This paper serves several practical implications, both for policymakers and practitioners. First, we suggest introducing tax incentives or low-interest loan programmes that could encourage businesses to invest in renewable energy and improve energy efficiency within analysed countries. Companies should set measurable targets in energy and waste management, as clear goals and renewable adoption enhance efficiency and foster eco-innovation. JEL CODES: Q56, Q55
