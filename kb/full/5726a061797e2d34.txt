Ecological sustainability is often difficult for consumers to verify at the point of purchase. It therefore becomes purchase-relevant only when it is translated into credible, visible and category-compatible cues. This study examines how such cues shape stated preferences in a premiumised beverage category. An online survey of Swiss gin consumers ( N = 201) measured pro-environmental consumption values, purchase-cue priorities, life-cycle knowledge, stated willingness to pay, intended behavioural change and perceived barriers. Hierarchical clustering was used to develop an exploratory typology of the surveyed respondents, resulting in five cue-based consumer profiles. Across segments, origin-related and craft/small-producer cues structured premium value more strongly than ecological-sustainability cues alone. Stated willingness to pay for gin with an improved ecobalance was positive in most segments, but differences aligned more with purchase-cue priorities and perceived barriers than with life-cycle knowledge. Because the study relies on a non-probability online sample, self-reported measures and stated WTP, the segments should be interpreted as empirically grounded profiles of the realised sample rather than as representative market segments. Within this limitation, the findings suggest that responsible consumption in premiumised food and beverage markets depends not only on consumer attitudes or information provision, but also on whether ecological improvements are made trustworthy, easy to interpret and available in familiar purchasing channels. For producers and retailers, this implies that sustainability communication should be connected to concrete product cues, such as provenance, packaging, refill options and credible certification, rather than being presented as an abstract environmental claim.
