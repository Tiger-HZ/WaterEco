This article evaluates whether the Bar Council of India (BCI) converts its broad statutory powers into effective safeguards for professional competence, ethical practice, and the quality of legal education. It addresses a recurring gap between the formal design of the Advocates Act, 1961 and the observable capacity of the regulatory system to deliver timely, consistent, and transparent outcomes. The research uses a doctrinal and analytical method. It examines legislation, Bar Council rules, judicial decisions, official committee and Law Commission materials, and selected regulatory practices in the United Kingdom and the United States. The analysis finds that a unified national framework remains valuable, but its credibility is weakened by delayed disciplinary proceedings, uneven sanctions
