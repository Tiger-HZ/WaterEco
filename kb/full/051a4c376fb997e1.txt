ABSTRACT Cyanobacteria are abundant phototrophs and hold potential as a carbon-negative platform for bioengineering applications. However, these efforts have been hampered by limited mechanistic understanding of their gene expression, including transcription termination. Unlike most bacteria, cyanobacteria lack the transcription termination factor Rho, raising the speculation that all transcription ends with intrinsic terminators. Here, we show that most transcription units (TUs) in Synechococcus elongatus PCC 7942 are not terminated by known termination pathways. Although many TUs (52%) have unique, well-defined 3′ ends, only a small fraction have features that resemble canonical intrinsic terminators (22%).
