Open-water surfaces, including lakes and reservoirs, absorb solar radiation and can experience substantial evaporative water losses. Partial surface coverage with reflective floating elements may reduce both solar heat loading and evaporation, but coverage fraction, water temperature, evaporation, and boundary conditions are strongly coupled. This exploratory study evaluated water-temperature response and daytime water-level decline associated with partial coverage by silver-colored floating polyethylene discs under three reduced-scale outdoor scenarios in Rende, Italy. Three identical water containers (17 × 15 cm surface area; 10 cm water depth) were tested with 0%, 10%, and 25% nominal projected surface coverage (C0, C10, and C25, respectively).
