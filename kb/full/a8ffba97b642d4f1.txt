This study validates daily increment formation in otoliths of juvenile sharpsnout seabream (Diplodus puntazzo) from the Dardanelles using Alizarin Red S (ARS) marking at four concentrations (50, 100, 150, and 200 mg/L). Eighty wild-caught juveniles (20–76 mm total length) were subjected to 24-hour ARS immersion, followed by 10 days of captivity. Mortality varied among treatments, with cumulative rates of 5.9–12.5% across ARS groups and 6.7% in the control, with the highest mortality occurring in the 100 and 200 mg/L groups (12.5% each). ARS marks were successfully detected in 75.3% of examined otoliths, with mark quality increasing at higher concentrations.
