Potential use of termite soil as nutrient amendments in 
crop growth for local farmers 
 
Momah M 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.22271/reschem.2026.v7.i9a.387 
 
Abstract 
Termites are important ecologically, providing ecosystem services, playing critical environmental 
modifications that impact o n the physicochemical components of termite mound soils. This soil 
through termites’ activities of bioturbation, aggregation of so il organic matter provides and serves as 
potential soil enrichments that helps crop growths. The study assessed the potential use of termite soil 
in promoting crop growth. Termite mound soils from seven locations were assessed in relation with the 
surrounding soils. Parameters evaluated were the pH, total nitrogen (N) available phosphorus (P), soil 
organic matter and exchangeable cations (Ca, K and Mg). There were no significant variations in the 
pH of the termite mound soil and the surrounding soils. Also the values of N and P were low and no 
significant variations observed. While the significant variations were observed in the soil organic 
matter between the termite mound soil and surrounding soils. But the exchangeable cations level was 
low and the study showed no considerable variations between the termite mound and the surrounding 
soils. The study findings advised that it will be helpful for local and poor farmers to highness the 
inherent potentials in planting crops close to the termite mounds. 
 
Keywords: Termite mound soil, soil nutrients, nutrient amendments, crop growth, soil organic matter, 
soil fertility, exchangeable cations, local farmers 
 
Introduction 
Termites as social insects like the Macrotermes and Coptotermes build their elabor ate 
mounds by usually upward transportation of soil particles from depths and using materials 
and resources from their immediate surrounding soil. (Jouquet. P, et, al 2016) [17] Termite 
activities impacts significantly on soil properties leading to bio accumulation of necessary 
materials that are important to plant growth. This biological activities produce notable 
chemical and physical redistribution of soil at the subsoil and topsoil (Sembi et al, 2008). 
Termites mound building engineering promotes and enhances soil transformations 
(Dangerfield et al , 1998) [11]. Termites in buildin g their mound transfer materials from 
different soil depths and subsequently composite them on the mound, thereby impacting on 
the mound particle size, the pH content of the soil, nutrients and in the process impacts on 
the soil organic carbon in comparison with the adjacent soils unaffected by the termites 
activities (Blake, H and Okwakol, M (1997), Abiyat. L et al (2016). The mound engineering 
of termites, their various social activities and feeding habit contributes to mound soil nutrient 
content changes relative to adjacent soils (Momah et al, 2026) [23]. It has been reported that 
termite activities improves the organic matter content in the soil used for mound construction 
and changes the clay mineral composition of mound soils through integration of various 
materials used in its activities (Mahaney et al , 1999; Jouquet et al , 2002 [18, 22], Roose 
Amseleg et al , 2004) [27]. Termites are often regarded as soil nutrients composition 
enhancers, they improves the soil nutrients compositions through their behaviour and also 
within the surrounding soils (Abe et al, 2000; Aiki et al, 2025) [1, 5]. The physical and bio 
activities of the termite improves on crop growth and overall productivity through gradual 
breakdown of organic matters which are subsequently made available for crops. Termite 
mounds remains the major center and serves as a focal point for the aggregation o f soil 
nutrients and reallocation of within the environment (Adebajo et al 2021, Aiki et al 2024, 
Chisanga et al 2020) [4, 6, 9]. Food production has been a serious challenge in African 
continent, the sustainability are been challenged by various factors incl uding urbanization, 
climatic change, availability of funds and the needed eco -friendly fertilizers, this factors has 
also be compounded by depletion and degradation of available nutrients, and soil organic 
matters.

Materials and Methods 
The study was conducted from selected communities within 
Delta State Nigeria, the communities include, Warri, Agbor, 
Ogbehasi, Abraka and Oleh.

Soil Analysis 
Termite mound soils used for the analysis was collected 
from seven locations within the five communities. The soil 
analysis was conducted a t the Analytical Chemistry 
Laboratory, Department of Chemical Sciences, University 
of Delta, Agbor. 
 
Methods of soil analysis 
Nutrient Contents Analysis 
Total nitrogen (TN) determined by the Kjeldahl method and 
available phosphorus (P) determined by method outlined by 
Olisen et, al (1954). 
 
Determination of Soil pH 
The pH value of an aqueous solution is the negative 
logarithm of the hydro gen concentration. Ion activity was 
determined using a desktop pH meter. Soil pH were 
analysed in a 1:10 so il-to-water extract. Five grams (5.0 g) 
of each soil sample (in a sample cell) was added to 50 mL of 
distilled water. Th e sum of the soil was stirred to form a 
homogenous slurry, then the pH meter (Jenway 3015 model) 
and EC meter (Jenway 4010 model) probes were immersed 
respectively into the sample and allowed to s tabilize at 30 
°C, and the pH of the sample was recorded. 
Determination of Total Organic Matter (TOM) 
TOM was measured using a Muffle Furnace. 5.0g of soil 
sample was weighed into a crucible and placed in a furnace 
for 60 minut es at 500 °C to calcine, which burn off all 
organic matter. The weight of the calcined soil was 
calculated, and the difference before and after was then used 
to ascertain the total organic matter. 
 
Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC) 
Methylene blue spot tests were used to measure soil CEC. 
According to Holden et al . (2012) [15], the methylene blue 
test is simple and provides accurate results f or calcareous 
and clayey soils. Santamarina et al. (2002) [28] updated the 
technique. One gram of dirt p assing the #200 sieve was 
mixed with 50 mL of DDI water in a beaker with a magnetic 
stirrer. Titrated in 0.5 mL increments, a 0.005 N methylene 
blue solution was agitated. Each methylene blue addition 
was mixed for a minute. After mixing for one minute, a drop 
were placed on filter pa per. Repeat until a light blue halo 
develops around the drop of solution, indicati ng saturation. 
The droplet halo marks the end. To test this endpoint, five 
further samples were put onto filter paper at one -minute 
intervals. If the blue halo remained, the endpoint was 
verified. Methylene blue was also needed. This equation 
calculates CEC:

The present study showed low values of total nitrogen 
contents and available phosphorus in both termite mound 
and surrounding soils, but comparatively lower in 
surrounding soils that agreed with Eneji et al 2015 [14], that 
the aggregation of humus by soil feeding termites and 
accumulation of N₂ in termite bi omass and its subsequent 
redistribution to the soil ecosystem upon deaths played a 
considerable role in the higher values of N₂ and P in the 
termite mound so il. Termites that feeds on plants, litter and 
dead woods enhances the uptake of mineral components and 
humus formation (Sileshi et al 2010) [29]. It is important to 
know that N and P presence in termite soil are good 
indicators for plant growth. This study is i n agreement with 
Jose and Mayg 2020 [16] that observed high nitrogen in 
termite mound soil. Table 1 also showed that soil organic 
matter with high values in termite mound soil than the 
corresponding surrounding topsoil. The termite soil values 
ranged between 10 2.94 - 40.79 mg/g while the surrounding 
soil ranged between 62.29 - 8.28 mg/g. Higher organic 
matter has been attributed to termites activities, feeding 
habit that involves the incorporation of humus materials and 
fecal matters in mound construction (Chisa nga et al, 2020, 
Lou et al, 2022) [9, 21] Momah et al, 2018 [23] reported high 
organic matter content in soil, Clarke et al (2023) [10] also 
reported hig her soil organic matter. Dhembare (2013) [13] 
noted that termite’s uptake of soil organic matter and re -
distribution as its fe cal co mponent preserved organic 
biomass physically and chemically forming stable mass in 
soil. Ackerman et al (2007) [2] reported that feeding habit 
and materials engaged with during the mound construction 
by the termite added to the higher values of the soil organic 
matter. The exchangeable cations (CEC) of the termite 
mound soil and surrounding soil were generally low as 
reported in Table 1. Termite mound soil values ranged 
between 0.96 - 0.17 mg/g for K, 1.07 - 0.25 mg/g for Ca and 
4.00 - 1.00 mg/g for Mg an d surrounding soil values ranged 
between 0.43 - 0.10 mg/g for K, 1.33 - 0.25 mg/g for Ca and 
1.00 - 0.40 mg/g for Mg. This shows that the soil has low 
levels of dissolve salt and would not constitute limitation to 
crop growth. The level agreed with Oku et al 2021 [25] that is 
compatible with the nutrient condition of tropical soils.

The exchangeable K, Ca and Mg in the mound soils are not 
significantly different in contrast with the surrounding top 
soils. Jouquet et al (2004) [19] reported non-significant Ex Ca 
in termite soil in relation to surrounding soil. Which agrees 
with this study. The low exchangeable cations can also be 
attributed to the soil type. Termite either enhances or 
deplete clay’s chemically active surface area which affects 
the soil’s CEC composition. (Jouquet et al , 2016) [17]. 
Termite soils with low CEC can be improved with soils with 
higher CEC to support plant growth as it enhances soil 
nutrient availability and better soil fertility. 
 
Conclusion 
The study was conducted to assess the effectiveness of 
termite mound soil in supporting plant growth in relation to 
the surrounding topsoil. The study revealed that termite 
mound soils shows variations in contrast with the 
surrounding soil, this variations are attributed to changes in 
ecosystem brought about by termite activities, bioturbati on, 
soil formation and reformation, distribution and 
redistribution of organic matters, transportation activities of 
termites and soil amendments processes. Termite activities 
have effects on soil physico chemical properties and this 
properties enhances th e improvements of soil nutrients 
which facilitates crop growths. Local farmers can therefore 
tap into the rich nutrients available from termite soil for 
their local farming activities.
