Animal movements can transport materials across ecosystem boundaries, while simultaneously modifying habitats through physical engineering. We integrated GPS telemetry, camera trapping, sediment measurements and physicochemical sampling of ponds on Santa Cruz Island, Galápagos, to evaluate how giant tortoises connect and shape terrestrial and freshwater ecosystems. Telemetry revealed 2,254 visits by 21 tortoises to 12 ponds, with visits lasting up to 21.38 h and showing strong seasonal and diel variation, while camera traps showed peak pond use in January and during early morning and late afternoon.
