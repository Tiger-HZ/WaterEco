Ensemble machine learning has become the default modelling strategy in research on the prediction of student academic performance in higher education, and published comparisons routinely report that bagged, boosted and stacked combinations of base classifiers outperform single learners. The accumulated literature nevertheless remains difficult to interpret, because reported gains derive from heterogeneous institutional datasets, inconsistent evaluation protocols and outcome definitions that range from continuous grade estimation to binary at-risk flagging.
