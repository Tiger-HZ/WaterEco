Abstract Silicon carbide (SiC) power metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistors are increasingly operated under high-frequency pulse-width-modulated (PWM) gate drive, yet gate-oxide reliability is still qualified almost exclusively under direct-current (DC) stress. This work reports a controlled comparison of gate-oxide time-dependent dielectric breakdown (TDDB) under DC and pulsed-voltage stress for three commercial 1.2 kV device families (Wolfspeed C2M and C3M planar, Infineon trench), and interprets the observed frequency dependence in terms of interface-trap dynamics.
