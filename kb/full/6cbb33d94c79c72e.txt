ABSTRACT Compound dry and hot extremes (CDHEs) pose serious threats to rainfed agriculture, vegetation productivity and livelihoods in semi‐arid to humid central Indian regions, yet their spatial extent and ecological impacts remain insufficiently quantified. This study analyzes CDHE occurrence and vegetation response across districts of Madhya Pradesh using precipitation, temperature and normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) records from 2000 to 2024. Standardized precipitation (SPI), temperature (STI) and vegetation (SNDVI) indices were derived from ERA5, TerraClimate and MODIS datasets, and CDHEs were identified using three percentile thresholds (P30 &amp; T70, P20 &amp; T80 and P10 &amp; T90).
