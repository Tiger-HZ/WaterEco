Abstract
Irregular temporal sampling and limited data availability restrict the performance of remote sensing–based chlorophyll-
a prediction for water eutrophication assessment. Meanwhile, atmospheric pollutant deposition represents an important 
pathway for nutrient input into lakes, yet such information is rarely incorporated into data-driven prediction frameworks.
To address these challenges, this study proposes a multimodal fusion framework integrating atmospheric pollutant data 
and chlorophyll remote sensing imagery. First, a transfer learning-based generative adversarial network (TL-MCAS-CSG-
GAN) is developed to reconstruct missing chlorophyll remote sensing images and enhance temporal continuity. Second, a 
cross-attention Conv2D-LSTM GAN model is designed to fuse atmospheric six-parameter features with chlorophyll image 
sequences for eutrophication prediction.Experiments conducted on Taihu Lake datasets demonstrate that the proposed 
model improves SSIM, PSNR, and COSIN by 6.51% compared with baseline models without atmospheric feature fusion. 
The results indicate that atmospheric pollutant information provides complementary spatiotemporal signals for chlorophyll 
prediction and improves eutrophication assessment accuracy.The proposed framework offers a data-driven paradigm for 
multimodal environmental modeling and contributes to intelligent water quality monitoring and management.

Eutrophication prediction studies focus on applying math-
ematical models and remote sensing techniques to foresee 
its occurrence and development trends. These models are 
based on the physical and chemical characteristics of water 
bodies, biological indicators, and atmospheric conditions. 
Advances in remote sensing technology have improved the 
efficiency of large-scale water body monitoring and early 
warning(Laneve et al. 2024; Peppa et al. 2020). Predic -
tion methods can be divided into two categories based on 
whether they reveal the intrinsic mechanisms of water bod-
ies: Mechanism-driven prediction focuses on direct and 
indirect influencing factors, describing their relationship 
with eutrophication through mathematical equations. This 
approach has evolved from single-nutrient limitation mod -
els (Du et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2023) to incorporating tem-
perature variations (He et al. 2024) and suspended solids 
(Pan et al. 2023), and further to complex models integrating 
ecosystem dynamics and hydrodynamic processes (Koue 
2025). Such progression has deepened the multidimensional 
understanding of eutrophication and improved prediction 
accuracy.On the other hand, data-driven prediction models 
use big data and data mining techniques to analyze observa-
tional information, combining ground stations and remote 
sensing data. These models cover both specific water qual -
ity details at individual sites and broad water body char -
acteristics, revealing key influencing factors and patterns 
through data analysis and providing important decision-
making support for water environment management(Chen 
et al. 2021; Karimian et al. 2023; Li et al. 2021; Zhang et 
al. 2022). These two methods complement each other and 
jointly promote the scientific prediction and management of 
water eutrophication.
In addition to watershed nutrient inputs, atmospheric 
deposition has been increasingly recognized as an impor -
tant external nutrient source influencing eutrophication 
processes. In large shallow lakes such as Taihu Lake, atmo-
spheric nitrogen deposition has been shown to contribute 
significantly to nutrient loading and algal bloom dynam -
ics (Chen et al. 2018).Existing studies generally adopt 
two main approaches to incorporate atmospheric factors 
into eutrophication research. The first approach introduces 
atmospheric nitrogen deposition and related meteorologi -
cal variables into water quality models as external driving 
forces. Modeling studies have demonstrated that atmo -
spheric nitrogen deposition can measurably affect water 
quality dynamics and nutrient concentrations (Wang et al. 
2022).The second approach couples climate forcing with 
hydrodynamic–ecological models to simulate the influence 
of atmospheric processes on nutrient transport and eutro -
phication evolution. Recent research has shown that atmo -
spheric deposition and climate warming jointly alter water 
quality responses and ecosystem dynamics (Han et al. 
2023).These findings indicate that atmospheric processes 
are not merely background environmental variables but 
can act as external nutrient drivers influencing chlorophyll-
a variability. Therefore, integrating atmospheric pollutant 
information into chlorophyll-a prediction models is theo -
retically grounded and scientifically justified.In this study, 
the atmospheric dataset consists of daily PM2.5 images 
with 1 km spatial resolution, while chlorophyll-a data are 
derived from satellite inversion products with irregular 
temporal sampling. This difference in temporal resolution 
further motivates the need for temporal reconstruction and 
multimodal modeling.
Convolutional neural networks, as a classic deep learning 
algorithm, overcome the curse of dimensionality in high-
dimensional data analysis and are particularly suitable for 
image processing and recognition. Convolutional process -
ing makes feature extraction from images more efficient, 
providing a more streamlined input set for subsequent 
nonlinear transformations, thereby improving training and 
prediction effects(Archana and Jeevaraj 2024; Liang 2024; 
Liu et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2025). Generative adversarial 
networks (GANs), as excellent models for generating image 
data, consist of a generator and a discriminator. The genera-
tor learns to produce samples that approximate the distribu-
tion of real remote sensing images, while the discriminator 
distinguishes whether the images come from real remote 
sensing image samples or are generated by the generator. 
This allows the network model to effectively extract key 
features from water body remote sensing images and gener-
ate high-quality water body remote sensing image data(Gui 
et al. 2021; Jabbar et al. 2021; Pai et al. 2020).
To address the aforementioned challenges, this study 
develops a multimodal fusion framework integrating atmo -
spheric pollutant information and chlorophyll remote sens -
ing imagery for water eutrophication prediction. The main 
contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
(1) A transfer learning-based image reconstruction frame -
work (TL-MCAS-CSG-GAN) is proposed to address 
irregular temporal sampling and limited chlorophyll 
data availability, improving temporal continuity of 
remote sensing image sequences.
(2) A cross-attention multimodal fusion architecture is 
designed to integrate atmospheric six-parameter fea -
tures with chlorophyll image sequences, enabling joint 
spatiotemporal modeling across heterogeneous envi -
ronmental data sources.
(3) A structured ablation analysis is conducted to quantita -
tively evaluate the contribution of temporal modeling, 
attention mechanisms, and atmospheric feature fusion, 
demonstrating the effectiveness of multimodal integra -
tion for chlorophyll-a prediction.

Water body remote sensing image 
expansion method based on atmospheric 
pollutant knowledge transfer
Considering that chlorophyll is the main indicator for pre -
dicting water eutrophication, this paper uses chlorophyll 
remote sensing images as the dataset for modeling. How -
ever, the original chlorophyll remote sensing image data 
of the target water body has unequal sampling lengths 
and low sampling frequencies, resulting in unequal time 
intervals and a small volume of data samples, making 
the original data unsuitable for direct modeling and pre -
diction. Therefore, it is necessary to study a method for 
expanding the chlorophyll remote sensing image samples 
of the target water body. Additionally, since the volume 
of chlorophyll remote sensing images is small, expand -
ing these samples requires rich prior knowledge. Tra -
ditional methods based on single factors for expanding 
chlorophyll remote sensing image samples often produce 
results that differ significantly from real images. Consid -
ering the strong correlation between atmospheric pol -
lutants and chlorophyll concentration, it is necessary to 
study a method for expanding chlorophyll remote sensing 
image samples based on high-resolution atmospheric pol -
lutant image data as prior knowledge. In response to these 
issues, this chapter proposes a chlorophyll remote sens -
ing image expansion method based on transfer learning 
and multi-channel attention cascaded semantic selection 
networks.
The model for expanding water body remote sensing 
images based on atmospheric pollutant knowledge transfer 
consists of four modules, as shown in Fig. 1.
The first module is the cascaded semantic generation 
module, which overlays atmospheric pollutant images with 
the semantic maps of the target water body and uses them 
as inputs to the model. This module extracts features from 
atmospheric pollutant images and generates coarse-grained 
chlorophyll remote sensing images of the target water body. 
The second module is the multi-scale spatial pooling mod -
ule, which performs average pooling on the coarse-grained 
cascaded semantic features at different scales to expand 
the receptive field and obtain feature information from a 
larger area. The third module is the multi-channel attention 
selection module, which convolves multi-scale features and 
combines attention mechanisms to generate more accu -
rate results. The fourth module is the knowledge transfer 
module, which integrates the generated chlorophyll remote 
sensing images with the original chlorophyll remote sensing 
image data and adjusts the generated images using the origi-
nal data to achieve smooth fusion of the expanded image 
dataset.

Construction of the cascaded semantic generation 
module
The task of image translation and generation across different 
knowledge domains is a challenging endeavor, particularly 
when the domains represent different physical quantities. 
This study investigates how to leverage knowledge learned 
from atmospheric pollutant images of a target water body 
to generate reference chlorophyll remote sensing images. 
To enhance the accuracy of image translation, this paper 
employs semantic maps of the target water body as con -
ditional guidance. A U-Net-based semantic segmentation 
model is employed to extract semantic maps of the target 
water body. However, using semantic mappings solely in 
the reconstruction loss to guide the generation of chloro -
phyll remote sensing images provides only weak guidance 
for image generation. Therefore, this study not only incor -
porates the semantic maps of the target water body in the 
reconstruction loss but also uses them as input to the model.

Construction of the knowledge transfer module
The specific structure of the knowledge transfer module is 
illustrated in Fig. 6. First, a generative adversarial network 
(GAN) is used as the feature extractor. The B-domain image 
generator Gs1 learns the features of chlorophyll remote 
sensing images from the original target water body, while 
the B-domain image discriminator D completes adversar -
ial training to uncover the rules for generating chlorophyll 
remote sensing images. Additionally, the B-domain image 
generator Gs1 can eliminate redundant information. The 
original water quality remote sensing images are input into 
the feature extractor for learning. After learning the rules for 
generating chlorophyll remote sensing images, the network 
structure and parameters of the generator Gs1 are trans -
ferred and cascaded with an LSTM network, to form the 
knowledge transfer module. This module models the spa -
tial and temporal correlations of chlorophyll remote sensing 
image data.
The expanded water quality remote sensing image sequence, 
consisting of both generated and original observations arranged 
in chronological order, is fed into the LSTM network. Through 
its gating mechanism, the LSTM captures long-term temporal 
dependencies between consecutive time steps and mitigates 
abrupt variations potentially introduced by the generative pro-
cess. As a result, the module outputs a temporally smoothed 
sequence of chlorophyll remote sensing images.

Construction of the cross-modal feature encoder
The cross-modal feature encoder is composed of a bidi -
rectional cross-attention layer (Cross Attention), a self-
attention layer (Self Attention), and a feedforward network 
(Feed Forward), as shown in Fig. 10. Among them, the 
cross-attention layer is primarily used for information inter-
action between different modalities, allowing data from one 
modality to focus on and acquire feature information from 
another modality, thereby achieving feature complementar-
ity and enhancement across modalities(Li et al. 2022; Luo et 
al. 2024). The self-attention layer is mainly used to focus on 
information interaction within a modality, capturing internal 
dependencies within the modality and improving the overall 
representation capability of the encoder. The feedforward 
network performs nonlinear transformations on the fea -
tures output by the attention layers to extract higher-level 
abstract features. In this paper, the outputs F air_sa and 
F water_sa of the single-modal feature encoder are input 
into the cross-modal feature encoder, resulting in chloro -
phyll remote sensing image features F water_sa_air that 
incorporate atmospheric six-parameter factors.
Generation and discrimination module
The generation and discrimination module consists of a 
Markov discriminator and a feature decoder. The Markov 
discriminator is a special convolutional neural network struc-
ture composed of Conv2d convolutional layers, LeakyReLU 
activation function layers, and Sigmoid activation function 
layers, as shown in Fig. 11. The convolutional layers use a 
set of learnable filters to slide over the input image, perform-
ing weighted summation on local regions to obtain new fea-
ture representations, capturing local patterns and structural 
information in the image, and providing a basis for subse -
quent discrimination or generation tasks. The LeakyReLU 
activation function layer is used to increase the nonlinear 
capability of the neural network, helping to prevent gradi -
ent vanishing and improve the training effect of the model. 
The Sigmoid activation function layer maps the output of the 
mechanism to retain spatial information while reducing 
data dimensionality. Finally, the transformed feature vec -
tors are input into the LSTM layer to capture temporal 
dependencies in the sequence data. Through the gating 
mechanism (input gate, forget gate, and output gate), infor-
mation is selectively retained and transmitted, enabling the 
model to learn temporal features in the image sequence. 
In the example shown in Fig. 8, Each batch of input chlo -
rophyll concentration sequences has a temporal length of 
30, represented in a three-channel image format (256 × 256 
pixels) corresponding to the color-encoded concentration 
maps. After processing by the Att_Conv2D_LSTM model, 
spatiotemporal features F of the size D1 at 30 time points 
are obtained.
Construction of the single-modal feature encoder
The spatiotemporal features of atmospheric pollutant 
images and the spatiotemporal features of target water body 
remote sensing images are input into the single-modal fea -
ture encoder for feature dimension alignment.
The single-modal feature encoder consists of a self-
attention layer and a feedforward network layer, as shown 
in Fig. 9. Among them, the self-attention layer can auto -
matically calculate the weights of different positions in the 
input sequence, enabling the encoder to focus more on the 
features most relevant to the current task. This helps the 
model filter out the most useful information from a large 
amount of data(Hui et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024). The 
feedforward network layer can learn nonlinear transforma -
tions, further converting and extracting the feature repre -
sentations output by the self-attention module, aligning the 
feature dimensions of different modal data. In this paper, 
the atmospheric six-parameter and chlorophyll remote 
sensing image spatiotemporal features F air and F water 
obtained from the feature extraction module are input into 
the single-modal feature encoder, and the output is the 
atmospheric six-parameter feature F air_sa and chloro -
phyll remote sensing image feature F water_sa with the 
same dimensions.

The feature decoder consists of a ReflectionPad2d bound-
ary padding function, Conv2d convolutional layers, Layer 
linear layers, ReLU activation functions, and Tanh activation 
functions, as shown in Fig. 12. ReflectionPad2d is a two-
dimensional reflection padding layer in PyTorch that per -
forms reflection padding on the edges of the input feature map 
to protect edge information and avoid the loss of edge infor-
mation during convolution operations. The Conv2d convolu-
tional layer mainly performs weighted summation on local 
regions of the input image by sliding a convolution kernel to 
obtain new feature representations, effectively extracting local 
features and spatial relationships in the image, which is cru-
cial for image recognition and processing tasks. Additionally, 
convolutional operations have the characteristics of weight 
sharing and local perception, which can reduce the number of 
model parameters and improve computational efficiency. The 
Layer linear layer is typically used for linear transformation 
of input features, mapping the output of the previous layer 
to the input of the next layer. Each neuron in the linear layer 
network to a range of 0 to 1, providing the probability value 
of the correctness of the discrimination. Finally, the network 
outputs a feature map corresponding to the local regions of 
the input image, where each feature value reflects the prob-
ability that the region conforms to the real data distribution.

Experimental verification
To avoid circular validation, we strictly separate recon -
structed (generated) samples from the evaluation set. The 
reconstructed chlorophyll-a images are used only for train -
ing to enhance temporal continuity, while all quantitative 
evaluations are conducted exclusively on real chlorophyll-a 
observations. The dataset is split chronologically into train-
ing, validation, and test subsets to prevent future informa -
tion leakage. Metrics such as SSIM, PSNR, and COSIN are 
computed on the test set consisting only of real observa -
tions, and generated samples are excluded from testing.
Experimental data
Chlorophyll-a concentration is widely regarded as a key 
indicator for water eutrophication assessment. In this study, 
chlorophyll remote sensing images are used as the primary 
dataset for modeling. The chlorophyll-a data are provided 
as color-encoded inversion maps, where each RGB value 
corresponds to a specific chlorophyll concentration defined 
by the original data product. However, the original chloro -
phyll observations of the target water body are characterized 
by irregular temporal sampling and limited data volume, 
resulting in discontinuous time series that are not suitable 
for direct spatiotemporal prediction.
To address this issue, an image expansion strategy is 
introduced to enhance temporal continuity. Given the lim -
ited number of chlorophyll samples, auxiliary environmental 
information is incorporated to provide additional contextual 
support during reconstruction. Atmospheric pollutant data, 
which may be associated with nutrient deposition processes 
influencing eutrophication dynamics, are therefore used as 
complementary prior information.

Water eutrophication evaluation
Chlorophyll is the main parameter characterizing water 
eutrophication. The following is the Carlson evalua -
tion index formula for converting chlorophyll as a single 
water quality parameter indicator into water eutrophication 
degree:
TSI (Chl.a) = 10 (2.5+1 .086 ln (Chl.a)) (4.1)
In the formula, TSI represents the Carlson Trophic State 
Index, and Chl.a represents the chlorophyll concentration 
in the water body, with the unit: ug/L. According to the cor-
respondence between the TSI index and the trophic state 
of the water body, a series of continuous numbers from 0 to 
100 are used to divide the trophic state into five levels: oli -
gotrophic (TSI < 35), mesotrophic (35 ≤ TSI < 50), 
mildly eutrophic (50 ≤ TSI < 60), moderately eutrophic 
(60 ≤ TSI < 70), and hypereutrophic (TSI ≥ 70). The 
corresponding chlorophyll concentration indicator levels 
are shown in Fig. 36.

Conclusion
This study proposes a two-stage multimodal fusion frame -
work for water eutrophication prediction by integrating 
atmospheric pollutant information and chlorophyll remote 
sensing imagery.
First, a transfer learning-based chlorophyll image recon-
struction strategy (TL-MCAS-CSG-GAN) is developed to 
address irregular temporal sampling and limited data avail -
ability. By improving temporal continuity and enriching 
training sequences, the reconstruction module enhances 
the stability of subsequent spatiotemporal modeling. It is 
emphasized that reconstructed samples are used solely for 
training purposes, while all quantitative evaluations are 
conducted exclusively on real chlorophyll observations to 
ensure experimental validity.
Second, a cross-attention Conv2D-LSTM GAN model 
is designed to fuse atmospheric six-parameter features with 
chlorophyll image sequences for eutrophication prediction. 
Experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements 
in SSIM, PSNR, and COSIN compared with both GAN-
based variants and classical statistical temporal baselines. 
The ablation analysis further confirms that atmospheric pol-
lutant information provides complementary spatiotemporal 
signals that contribute to enhanced prediction accuracy.
From an environmental informatics perspective, incorpo-
rating atmospheric pollutant factors offers a data-driven rep-
resentation of external nutrient transport processes that may 
influence lake eutrophication dynamics. The proposed frame-
work demonstrates the potential of multimodal environmen-
tal data integration for intelligent water quality monitoring.
