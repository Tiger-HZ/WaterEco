RESEARCH ARTICLE 
Independent effects of phytase and β-mannanase on growth performance 
and nutrient utilization in broilers fed phosphorus-deficient diets without 
supplemental inorganic phosphorus 
Tanachot Pengsawad , Theerawit Poeikhampha , Chaiyapoom Bunchasak , Choawit Rakangthong and Sombat Prasongsook 
Department of Animal Science, Faculty of Agriculture, Kasetsart University, Bangkok 10900, Thailand. 
 
A B S T R AC T 
Background and Aim: Phytase and β-mannanase are widely used in poultry diets to improve nutrient utilization; however, 
their individual and interactive effects under phosphorus (P) restriction without supplemental inorganic P remain 
insufficiently characterized. This study ev aluated the effects of phytase and β -mannanase, alone and in combination, on 
growth performance, ileal digestible energy (IDE), and apparent ileal calcium (Ca) and P digestibility in broilers fed a P -
deficient diet without supplemental inorganic P. 
Materials and Methods: A total of 720 one -day-old Ross 308 male broilers were randomly allocated to four dietary 
treatments in a 2 × 2 factorial arrangement comprising phytase at 0 or 1,000 FTU/kg and β -mannanase at 0 or 800 
Thermostable mannanase unit/kg. Each treatment included 12 replicate pens of 15 birds. From days 8 to 24, birds received 
corn–soybean meal -based grower diets containing 0.40% total P, 0.14% non -phytate P, and 0.26% phytate P without 
supplemental inorganic P. Growth performance was evaluated, and ileal digesta were collected on day 24 to determine IDE 
and apparent ileal Ca and P digestibility. 
Results: Phytase supplementation increased final body weight by 14.4%, average daily gain by 17.8%, and feed intake by 
14.4% and improved feed conversion ratio by 2.9% (p < 0.01). Phytase also increased IDE from 2,641.84 to 2,686.30 kcal/kg, 
Ca digestibility from 5 7.82% to 58.83%, and P digestibility from 71.59% to 74.83% (p < 0.01). β -Mannanase increased IDE 
from 2,647.01 to 2,681.13 kcal/kg (p < 0.01) and Ca digestibility from 58.00% to 58.70% (p < 0.05) but did not significantly 
affect growth performance or P digestibility. No significant phytase × β -mannanase interactions were detected for gr owth 
performance, IDE, or mineral digestibility, although an interaction was observed for flock uniformity (p = 0.011). 
Conclusion: Under the P -restricted dietary conditions, phytase primarily improved growth performance and mineral 
utilization, whereas β -mannanase independently enhanced energy utilization and modestly improved Ca digestibility. The 
absence of synergistic effects indicates distinct enzyme responses under these dietary conditions. These findings support 
phytase as a nutritional strategy for substantially reducing supplemental inorganic P in carefully formulated broiler diets, 
although longer-term validation under commercial feeding conditions is warranted. 
Keywords: β-mannanase, broiler, calcium digestibility, ileal digestible energy, non -phytate phosphorus, nutrient utilization, 
phosphorus restriction, phytase. 
INTRODUCTION 
Poultry production plays an important role in meeting the growing demand for high -quality animal protein, 
while efficient nutrient utilization is essential for improving production efficiency and environmental 
sustainability. However, anti-nutritional factors can limit the nutritional value of plant -based feed ingredients by 
reducing nutrient availability in broilers [1]. Phytate, the principal storage form of phosphorus (P) in cereals and 
oilseed meals, is less available to poultry and can form complexes with proteins and minerals, particularly calcium 
(Ca), thereby reducing nutrient digestibility and utilization [2, 3, 4]. Exogenous phytase is therefore widely 
incorporated into poultry diets to hydrolyze phytate, release phytate -bound P, and improve P and Ca utilization 
[5]. Another relevant anti -nutritional component of plant -based diets is β -mannan, a non-starch polysaccharide 
present in soybean meal that may interfere with nutrient utilization and induce a feed-induced immune response

[6]. Dietary supplementation with β -mannanase hydrolyzes β -mannan and may alleviate these anti -nutritional 
effects, thereby improving nutrient and energy utilization in broilers [7, 8]. 
Phytase and β -mannanase act on different anti -nutritional components of plant -based diets and may 
therefore exert complementary effects on nutrient utilization. Phytase primarily improves the availability of 
phytate-bound P and associated minerals, whereas β -mannanase targets β -mannan-containing components of 
the feed matrix. Consequently, β -mannanase-mediated modification of plant cell -wall structures and digesta 
characteristics may improve nutrient accessibility and potentially influence the response to phytase 
supplementation. Understanding whether these enzymes exert independent, additive, or inter active effects is 
particularly relevant when diets are formulated under substantial nutrient restriction, where the identity and 
magnitude of the primary limiting nutrient may influence the response to exogenous enzymes. 
Despite the well -established individual effects of phytase and β -mannanase in broiler nutrition, their 
combined effects under P-restricted dietary conditions remain insufficiently characterized. Previous studies have 
commonly evaluated these enzymes in die ts containing supplemental inorganic P or as components of multi -
enzyme supplementation strategies, making it difficult to distinguish their individual contributions and determine 
whether β-mannanase modifies the nutritional response to phytase. In particular, limited information is available 
on the simultaneous use of phytase and β -mannanase in grower -phase Ross 308 broilers receiving diets 
completely devoid of supplemental inorganic P. Under these conditions, phytate -bound P constitutes an 
important dietary P source, while β -mannan remains a potential constraint on nutrient and energy utiliz ation. 
Therefore, evaluating both enzymes within a factorial experimental framework is necessary to distinguish their 
independent effects from their potential interactions on growth performance, energy utilization, and mineral 
digestibility. 
The present study addressed this knowledge gap using a deliberately P-restricted corn–soybean meal-based 
grower diet containing 0.40% total P, 0.26% phytate P, and 0.14% non-phytate P, without supplemental inorganic 
P. This nutritional model provides an opportunity to evaluate the intrinsic responses to phytase and β-mannanase 
under conditions in which dietary P availability is strongly constrained. Determining the individual and interactive 
responses to these enzymes may also have practical implications for optimizing enzyme supplementation, 
reducing dependence on inorganic P sources, improving utilization of dietary P, and potentially reducing P 
excretion associated with broiler production. 
This study aimed to evaluate the individual and interactive effects of dietary phytase and β -mannanase 
supplementation on growth performance, ileal digestible energy (IDE), and apparent ileal Ca and P digestibility in 
Ross 308 broilers fed a P -deficient diet without supplemental inorganic P. Specifically, the study evaluated 
whether phytase supplementation improved growth performance and mineral utilization, whether β-mannanase 
supplementation enhanced energy and nutrient utilization, and whether simultaneous supplementation with 
both enzymes produced interactive effects under P-restricted dietary conditions. It was hypothesized that phytase 
would primarily improve P and Ca util ization, whereas β-mannanase would primarily enhance energy utilization 
and nutrient accessibility, with combined supplementation potentially producing additive or synergistic 
responses. 
MATERIALS AND METHODS 
Ethical approval 
All procedures involving animals were reviewed and approved by the Kasetsart University Institutional 
Animal Care and Use Committee (Approval No. ACKU68 -AGR-006). Animal care, housing, experimental 
procedures, sample collection, and euthanasia were conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the National 
Research Council of Thailand. Throughout the experimental period, birds were maintained under environmentally 
controlled conditions, with temperature and lighting managed according to the Ross 308 Broiler Management 
Handbook. On day 24, birds selected for ileal digesta collectio n were euthanized by cervical dislocation before 
sample collection. 
Study period and location 
The experiment was conducted from February to March 2025 at the Department of Animal Science, Faculty 
of Agriculture, Kasetsart University, Bangkhen, Bangkok, Thailand. A total of 720 one -day-old Ross 308 male 
broiler chicks obtained from a commercial hatchery were reared for 24 days in an environmentally contr olled 
poultry house equipped with an evaporative cooling system. Temperature and lighting were managed according

to the Ross 308 Broiler Management Handbook. 
Study design 
A total of 720 broilers were randomly allocated to four dietary treatments in a completely randomized design 
with a 2 × 2 factorial arrangement. The two experimental factors were phytase supplementation at 0 or 1,000 
FTU/kg and β -mannanase supplementation at 0 or 800 Thermostable mannanase unit (TMU)/kg. The four 
treatments were T1, control diet without phytase or β-mannanase; T2, β-mannanase at 800 TMU/kg; T3, phytase 
at 1,000 FTU/kg; and T4, phytase at 1,000 FTU/kg plus β -mannanase at 800 TMU/kg. Each treatment consisted 
of 12 replicate pens, with 15 birds per pen and a stocking density of 12.5 birds/m². Thus, the experiment 
comprised 48 pens, with the pen serving as the experimental unit. The factorial design was used to evaluate the 
main effects of phytase and β-mannanase and their interaction. 
Diets and feeding management 
All birds received a common starter diet from days 1 to 7 that was formulated to meet the nutritional 
recommendations for Ross 308 broilers. From days 8 to 24, birds received experimental grower diets formulated 
primarily with corn and soybean meal, follow ing the recommendations of Leeson and Summers [9]. Diet 
formulation was based on the analyzed nutrient composition of the raw materials (Tables 1 and 2). 
The experimental grower diet was deliberately formulated without supplemental inorganic P to evaluate the 
responses to phytase and β -mannanase under P -restricted conditions. The diet contained 0.40% total P, 0.14% 
non-phytate P, and 0.26% phytate P. Phytas e and β -mannanase were incorporated at 1,000 FTU/kg and 800 
TMU/kg, respectively, according to treatment allocation. The four treatments therefore represented diets without 
either enzyme, with β-mannanase alone, with phytase alone, or with both enzymes. 
The phytase preparation was Natuphos E 10000 G derived from Aspergillus niger (BASF SE, Ludwigshafen am 
Rhein, Germany ), and the β -mannanase preparation was Natupulse TS derived from Thermothelomyces 
thermophilus (BASF SE, Ludwigshafen am Rhein, Germany). Phytase activities in the phytase-supplemented diets 
were 1,201 FTU/kg and 1,278 FTU/kg for treatments T3 and T4, respectively. The corresponding analyzed β -
mannanase activities were 853 TMU/kg and 858 TMU/kg for treatments T2 and T4, respectively. Phytase activity 
was determined according to ISO 30024:2024, whereas β-mannanase activity was determined using the BASF in-
house analytical method. 
Experimental diets were pelleted through a 3.0 -mm die at a conditioning temperature of 65°C –70°C. Feed 
and water were provided ad libitum throughout the experimental period using tube feeders and nipple drinkers.

Growth performance measurements 
Body weight (BW) was recorded on days 8 and 24, and the values were used to calculate average daily gain 
(ADG) during the grower period. Feed intake (FI) was determined as the difference between the amount of feed 
offered and the feed remaining. Feed conve rsion ratio (FCR) was calculated as FI divided by BW gain. Mortality 
was recorded daily and used to adjust growth performance calculations where appropriate. 
Ileal digesta collection and digestibility determination 
IDE and Apparent ileal digestibility (AID) of Ca and P were determined using Celite as an indigestible marker, 
which was added on top of the formulated diets at 1% (Celite 545, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). On day 24, 
one bird from each pen was selected and euthanized by cervical dislocation, resulting in 12 birds per treatment 
and 48 birds overall. Ileal digesta were collected from the intestinal segment extending from Meckel's 
diverticulum to approximately 40 mm anterior to the ileocecal junction following the sampling procedure 
described by Huang et al. [10]. 
Ileal digesta were gently expressed from the intestinal segment into collection containers and immediately 
stored at −20°C. The samples were subsequently transferred to −80°C until further processing. Before chemical 
analysis, digesta samples were freeze -dried under vacuum conditions using a VirTis freeze dryer (SP Scientific, 
USA). Diet and digesta samples were analyzed for gross energy using an adiabatic bomb calorimeter and for Ca 
and P according to Association of Official Analytical Chemists procedures [11]. Acid -insoluble ash (AIA) 
concentrations in the diet and ileal digesta were determined after ashing and treatment with boiling 4 M HCl, 
followed by ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometry. All laboratory analyses were performed in duplicate. 
AID was calculated using the standard marker-ratio method [12]: 
AID (%) = 100 − [100 × (AIA in diet / AIA in ileal digesta) × (Nutrient in ileal digesta / Nutrient in diet)]. 
IDE was calculated using the following equation: 
IDE (kcal/kg) = GE in diet − [GE in ileal digesta × (AIA in diet / AIA in ileal digesta)], 
where AIA represents acid-insoluble ash and GE represents gross energy.

All data were analyzed using SAS Studio (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). The pen was considered the 
experimental unit for all measurements. Data were analyzed as a completely randomized design with a 2 × 2 
factorial arrangement, including the main effe cts of β -mannanase and phytase and their interaction. The 
statistical model was as follows:

DISCUSSION 
The present study provides evidence that phytase and β -mannanase produced largely independent 
responses in broilers fed a P-deficient diet without supplemental inorganic P. Phytase markedly improved growth 
performance, IDE, and apparent ileal Ca and P digestibility, whereas β -mannanase primarily increased IDE and 
produced a modest improvement in apparent ileal Ca digestibility. No significant β -mannanase × phytase 
interactions were detected for growth performance, IDE, or mineral digestibility. This response pattern differs 
from some previous studies reporting additive or synergistic responses under less restrictive dietary conditions 
and suggests that di etary nutrient constraints may influence the magnitude and interaction of responses to 
exogenous enzymes. 
Growth performance 
Phytase supplementation significantly increased final BW, ADG, and FI and improved FCR, consistent with 
previous studies reporting improved growth performance and nutrient utilization following phytate degradation 
[14, 15]. In the present study, the grower diet contained no supplemental inorganic P and provided only 0.14% 
non-phytate P. Under these conditions, phytate-bound P represented an important dietary P source, providing a 
plausible explanation for the pronounced growth response to phytase supplementation. Hydrolysis of phytate by 
phytase can increase the availability of phytate -bound P and reduce the anti -nutritional effects associated with 
phytate–nutrient interactions, thereby improving nutrient utilization. 
In contrast, β-mannanase supplementation did not significantly affect final BW, ADG, FI, or FCR. Although β-
mannanase can improve nutrient utilization by hydrolyzing β-mannan in plant-based feed ingredients, the lack of 
a growth response in the present stu dy suggests that its effects on nutrient utilization were insufficient to 
translate into measurable performance improvements under the experimental conditions. The strong response 
to phytase relative to β -mannanase may indicate that P availability represented a more important nutritional 
constraint than the substrates targeted by β -mannanase. However, because the relative contributions of 
individual nutrient limitations were not directly quantified, interpret this explanation cautiously. 
A significant β-mannanase × phytase interaction was detected for flock uniformity, despite the absence of 
significant main effects of either enzyme. The interaction should be interpreted cautiously because no 
corresponding interactions were detected for final BW, ADG, FI, or FCR. Furthermore, the overall treatment 
comparison for flock uniformity was not significant. Thus, the observed interaction does not provide sufficient 
evidence of a consistent complementary effect of the two enzymes on growth performance. 
IDE 
Both β-mannanase and phytase independently increased IDE, indicating that the two enzymes influenced 
energy utilization despite their different primary substrates. The increase in IDE associated with β -mannanase 
supplementation is consistent with previous reports describing improved nutrient utilization following β-mannan 
hydrolysis [8, 16]. β-Mannanase has been proposed to modify β -mannan-containing cell-wall structures and the 
matrix surrounding protein storage vacuoles, potentially increasing nutrient accessibility within soybean meal 
[17]. These effects may improve energy utilization even when measurable improvements in growth performance 
are not observed.

Phytase also significantly increased IDE. In addition to binding P and other minerals, phytate can interact 
with dietary proteins and influence nutrient digestion. Hydrolysis of phytate may therefore alleviate some of these 
anti-nutritional effects and imp rove the utilization of dietary energy and other nutrients. This interpretation is 
consistent with the anti-nutritional effects of phytate described by Selle et al. [1]. However, the present study did 
not directly measure amino acid digestibility or endogenous nutrient losses; therefore, these mechanisms cannot 
be confirmed from the current findings. 
Importantly, no significant β -mannanase × phytase interaction was detected for IDE. Thus, although both 
enzymes significantly increased IDE, their simultaneous supplementation did not produce a statistically 
demonstrable synergistic response under the conditions evaluated. 
Calcium and P digestibility 
Phytase supplementation significantly increased apparent ileal Ca and P digestibility, supporting its 
established role in hydrolyzing phytate and increasing the availability of phytate-associated minerals [2, 5]. These 
findings are consistent with those of Venter et al. [18], who reported improved Ca and P digestibility following 
phytase supplementation across diets differing in phytate P content. The response observed in the present study 
is particularly relevant because the experimental diet contained no supplemental i norganic P, increasing the 
nutritional importance of P released from dietary phytate. 
β-Mannanase also produced a significant, although relatively modest, increase in apparent ileal Ca 
digestibility, from 58.00% to 58.70%. In contrast, β -mannanase did not significantly affect apparent ileal P 
digestibility. Modification of β-mannan-containing feed structures could potentially improve mineral accessibility 
within the gastrointestinal tract. However, the biological mechanism underlying the observed Ca response cannot 
be established from the present experiment. Digesta viscosity, mineral solubilization, endogenous Ca losses, and 
other potential mechanisms were not measured; therefore, mechanistic explanations for this finding remain 
speculative. 
Phytase increased apparent ileal P digestibility from 71.59% to 74.83%, representing an increase of 3.24 
percentage points. This response, together with improved growth performance, demonstrates that phytase was 
particularly effective under the P -restricted conditions evaluated. Nevertheless, AID represents nutrient 
disappearance before the terminal ileum and does not directly quantify whole -body P retention or bone 
mineralization. Consequently, the present findings should not be interpreted as evidence that phytase completely 
satisfied the birds' physiological P requirements. 
Interaction between phytase and β-mannanase 
No significant β-mannanase × phytase interactions were detected for final BW, ADG, FI, FCR, IDE, or apparent 
ileal Ca and P digestibility. This finding contrasts with previous studies reporting additive or synergistic responses 
to enzyme combinations in reduced -energy or reduced-protein diets [19, 20]. Differences in dietary formulation 
may partly explain these contrasting responses because enzyme efficacy depends on substrate availability, 
nutrient concentrations, and the nutritional constraints imposed by the basal diet. 
In the present study, complete omission of supplemental inorganic P created a dietary condition in which 
the availability of phytate-bound P was particularly important. The pronounced response to phytase suggests that 
alleviation of phytate -associated nutr ient constraints had a greater influence on growth performance than β -
mannan hydrolysis. Under these conditions, the scope for β -mannanase to further improve growth performance 
may have been limited. Nevertheless, describing this response as a “ceiling effect” would require additional 
experimental evidence across multiple dietary P concentrations. The present findings establish the absence o f 
statistically significant interactions under the conditions tested but do not demonstrate the mechanism 
responsible for that absence. 
Study limitations and future research 
This study had several limitations. First, the experiment was restricted to the grower period (days 8 to 24) 
and evaluated only one supplementation level of phytase and β -mannanase under a single P -deficient dietary 
condition. Consequently, dose -dependent responses and enzyme interactions across different dietary P 
concentrations could not be evaluated. Second, digesta viscosity was not measured, limiting direct assessment of 
a mechanism potentially associated with β -mannanase supplementation. Third, intestinal microbial composition 
was not evaluated; therefore, potential effects of enzyme supplementation on the intestinal microbiota could not 
be determined. 
Furthermore, tibia ash and other indices of bone mineralization were not evaluated. The findings regarding

skeletal mineralization. Although nutrient profiles of the raw materials were analyzed before diet formulation,

Future studies should evaluate multiple phytase and β -mannanase inclusion levels across graded dietary P 
concentrations and longer production phases. Incorporating measurements of bone mineralization, nutrient 
retention, digesta characteristics, and other mechanistic indicators would further clarify the nutritional 
consequences and potential interactions of these enzymes. 
Practical implications 
The present findings demonstrate that phytase has an important nutritional role in broiler grower diets 
formulated without supplemental inorganic P, as reflected by improvements in growth performance, IDE, and 
apparent ileal Ca and P digestibility. β -Mannanase provided additional, independent responses in IDE and 
apparent ileal Ca digestibility but did not significantly improve growth performance or P digestibility. These 
findings highlight the distinct nutritional responses associated with the two enzymes and indicate that their 
combined effects depend on the dietary conditions under which they are applied. 
From a practical perspective, the pronounced phytase response indicates the potential to substantially 
reduce reliance on supplemental inorganic P in carefully formulated broiler diets. Improved utilization of dietary 
P could potentially reduce P excretion and decrease dependence on inorganic phosphate sources. Similarly, the 
improvement in IDE associated with β -mannanase may provide opportunities for more efficient utilization of 
dietary energy. However, the present study did not directly evaluate feed cost, P excretion, nutrient retention, or 
environmental outcomes. Therefore, dedicated studies should confirm economic an d environmental benefits. In 
addition, longer-term and multi-phase studies under commercial production conditions, including assessment of 
bone mineralization and bird performance through market age, are required before complete elimination of 
supplemental inorganic P can be broadly recommended. 
CONCLUSION 
The present study demonstrated that phytase and β-mannanase produced distinct and largely independent 
nutritional responses in Ross 308 broilers fed a P-deficient grower diet without supplemental inorganic P. Phytase 
supplementation increased final BW from 1,093.11 to 1,251.03 g, ADG from 52.12 to 61.40 g/day, and FI from 
72.19 to 82.59 g/day and improved FCR from 1.39 to 1.35. Phytase also increased IDE from 2,641.84 to 2,686.30 
kcal/kg and apparent ileal Ca and P digestibility from 57.82% to 58.83% and from 71.59% to 74.83%, respectively. 
β-Mannanase did not significantly affect growth performance or apparent ileal P digestibility but increased IDE 
from 2,647.01 to 2,681.13 kcal/kg and apparent ileal Ca digestibility from 58.00% to 58.70%. No significant β -
mannanase × phytase interactions were detecte d for growth performance, IDE, or apparent ileal Ca and P 
digestibility, although a significant interaction was observed for flock uniformity. 
A major strength of this study was its controlled 2 × 2 factorial design , which allowed the individual effects 
of phytase and β -mannanase to be distinguished from their potential interaction. The experimental model was 
also characterized by the complete omission of supplemental inorganic P, with the grower diet containing 0.40% 
total P, 0.14% non -phytate P, and 0.26% phytate P. This design provided a stringent nutritional setting for 
evaluating enzyme responses when dietary P availability was substantially constrained. Furthermore, the study 
simultaneously evaluated growth performance, IDE, and apparent ileal Ca and P digestibility, allowing 
characterization of the two enzymes’ responses across complementary performance and nutrient -utilization 
outcomes. 
Overall, phytase was the principal enzyme associated with improvements in growth performance and 
mineral utilization under the P-restricted conditions evaluated, whereas β-mannanase primarily improved energy 
utilization and produced a modest increase in apparent ileal Ca digestibility. The lack of significant interactions for 
the major growth performance and nutrient utilization outcomes indicates that combining phytase and β -
mannanase did not produce a detectable synergistic response under the experimental conditions. Collectively, 
these findings show that nutritional responses to phytase and β -mannanase differ according to their respective 
functional roles and provide evidence of largely independent effects when broilers are fed a grower diet without 
supplemental inorganic P.
