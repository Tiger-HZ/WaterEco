Abstract The low-frequency shock and separation unsteadiness resulting from a three-dimensional transitional shock-wave/boundary-layer interaction is investigated for different freestream noise levels and incoming boundary-layer states in a quiet Mach-6 wind tunnel. In low-disturbance flow, the cone-slice-ramp geometry is studied with and without upstream boundary-layer trips, resulting in upstream-transitional and interaction-transitional SWBLI, respectively. When applied, the trips excite the second-mode instability, and the unsteady separation motion is studied for a range of second-mode magnitudes in the transitional upstream boundary layer. The effect of freestream noise is studied by operating the wind tunnel in both its low-disturbance and conventional-noise configurations. Unsteady
