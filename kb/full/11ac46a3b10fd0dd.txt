 Impact of Algal Bloom on Aquatic Ecosystems in the Bay of Bengal: A Case Study from the Bangladesh Coastal Region
 
 
 
 Dr. Ranojit Kumar Dutta 1 
 , Baishakhi Das 2 
 
 
 
 City University, Department of Science and Humanities (DSH), Birulia, Dhaka, 1340, Bangladesh 
 National University, Department of Political Science, Gazipur,1704, Dhaka, Bangladesh 
 
 
 
 
 Abstract 
 
 Harmful algal blooms (HABs) have emerged as a critical threat to marine biodiversity and ecosystem health, particularly in the Bay of Bengal along the Bangladesh coast. This study investigates the causes, dynamics, and ecological consequences of algal blooms in the region, emphasizing the degradation of aquatic ecosystems, fish mortality, oxygen depletion, and disruption of food webs. Rising sea surface temperatures, nutrient enrichment from agricultural runoff, and industrial pollution are identified as key drivers of bloom proliferation. Satellite remote sensing, water quality monitoring, and field surveys were employed to track bloom patterns and assess ecosystem responses.The findings reveal a significant increase in bloom frequency and intensity over the past decade, correlating strongly with anthropogenic nutrient loading and climate variability. Affected zones experienced drastic declines in dissolved oxygen (DO) levels, leading to hypoxic conditions and large-scale fish kills. Furthermore, indigenous fish and plankton populations showed altered community structures and reduced diversity due to prolonged exposure to bloom toxins.This research contributes to the understanding of bloom dynamics in a poorly studied tropical marine environment and proposes integrated mitigation strategies, including watershed management, 
pollution control, and early warning systems. The study underscores the urgent need for transboundary marine policy frameworks and community-based monitoring programs to safeguard the Bay of Bengal’s ecological integrity.
