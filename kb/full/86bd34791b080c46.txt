Impact of integrated nutrient management on growth 
attributes of soybean (Glycine max L.) 
 
AP Kale, VP Suryavanshi, MJ Patange, AN Puri, SB Suryawanshi and AK 
Ghotmukale 
 
DOI: https://www.DOI.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9m.6628 
 
Abstract 
A field trial was conducted during kharif season, 2025 -26 at the Experimental Farm, Department of 
Agronomy, College of Agriculture, Latur (Maharashtra), “Impact of Integrated Nutrient Management on 
Growth Attributes of Soybean (Glycine max L.)” The experiment was laid out in a Randomized Block Design 
(RBD) with seven treatments and three replications to study the effect of integrated nutrient management 
practices on growth attributes of soybean. The treatments consisted of the recommended dose of fertilizers 
(RDF) supplemented with farmyard manure (FYM), neem cake powder and multi -micronutrient Grade-I, 
either individually or in combination. The results revealed that the application of RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + 
neem cake powder @ 3 q ha⁻¹ + multi-micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₇) recorded superior growth and 
yield attributes of soybean compared to the other treatments. The integrated application of organic manure, 
neem cake powder and multi-micronutrients along with the recommended dose of fertilizers enhanced plant 
growth and yield performance. Treatment RDF + FYM @5t/ha -1 + Neem cake powder @ 3qt/ha -1 + Multi 
micronutrient Grade-I @25kg/ha-1(T₇) was followed by RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + multi-micronutrient Grade-
I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹(T₅) and RDF + neem cake powder @ 3 q ha⁻¹ + multi -micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ 
(T₆).

Introduction 
Soybean ( Glycine max L.) is an economically significant annual leguminous crop cultivated 
globally for its dual role as a pulse and an oilseed crop. Commonly known as the “Golden Bean,” 
it holds immense importance as a food legume owing to its high protein content (approximat ely 
40%) and oil content (around 20%), making it a vital source of nutrition for both humans and 
livestock. Taxonomically, soybean belongs to the family Fabaceae (Leguminosae), tribe 
Phaseoleae, which comprises several agronomically important legumes. The genus Glycine was 
first described by Linnaeus in the initial edition of Genera Plantarum and has since been classified 
into two subgenera Glycine and Soja based on morphological characteristics and molecular 
phylogenetic studies. 
Integrated Nutrient Manag ement (INM) is an eco -sustainable strategy that integrates organic 
manures, inorganic fertilizers, biofertilizers, and micronutrients in a balanced and complementary 
manner to maintain soil fertility and enhance crop productivity. In soybean cultivation, INM plays 
a crucial role in improving soil health, nutrient availability, and yield sustainability. Although 
soybean fixes atmospheric nitrogen through symbiotic association with Rhizobium japonicum, the 
crop still requires balanced nutrient supplementation to meet its high nutrient demand. The 
combined use of organic and inorganic sources improves nutrient use efficiency and reduces 
nutrient losses through leaching, volatilization, and fixation. 
The incorporation of organic amendments such as farmyard manur e (FYM) and neem cake 
enhances soil organic carbon, microbial activity, and cation exchange capacity, improving the 
physical and biological properties of soil (Mundhe et al ., 2023) [6]. FYM at 5 t ha⁻¹ with the 
recommended dose of fertilizers (RDF) has been found to increase the availability and uptake of 
N, P, and K, leading to higher yields. Neem cake also acts as a nitrification inhibitor and provides

secondary benefits such as pest suppression. The inclusion of 
multi-micronutrient mixtures (Zn, Fe, Mn, B, Cu) helps to correct 
hidden deficiencies, enhance photosynthetic activity, and 
improve seed quality parameters (Gupta et al ., 2024) [3]. 
Biofertilizers such as Rhizobium and phosphate solubilizing 
bacteria (PSB) further promote biological nitrogen fixation and 
phosphorus mobilization, improving overall soil fertility (Sahu et 
al., 2006) [10]. 
INM practices have also been shown to improve soybean 
productivity, protein and oil content, and economic returns while 
reducing dependence on costly chemical fertilizers. Long -term 
studies have demonstrated that the combined application of FYM 
and fertilizers enhances soil microbial populations and enzymatic 
activities, thereby sustaining soil fertility and yield stability 
(Yadav et al., 2021) [12]. Moreover, INM reduces environmental 
pollution, improves soil carbon sequestration, and contributes to 
climate-resilient and sustainable agriculture (Panda et al., 2020). 
Thus, INM not only ensures higher and stable yields of soybean 
but also maintains the ecological balance, soil fertility, and 
economic viability of farming systems under diverse agro -
ecological condition. 
 
Materials and Method 
An experiment was conducted during kharif season, 2025-2026 at 
the Experimental Farm of Agronomy, College of Agriculture, 
Latur, Geographically Latur district of Maharashtra state is 
located at 18°05° to 18° 75'North latitude and 77° 25' to77° 36' 
East latitude with the total geographical area is 7.37 million ha. 
Latur area comes under semi -arid and tropical region of the 
Maharashtra. The average annua l rainfall of the Latur district is 
795.3 mm. The mean maximum temperature during crop growth 
period was 26.54 °C while the mean minimum temperature was 
17.18°C. The mean humidity during morning was 1017.9 %. The 
soil of experimental plot was clayey in tex ture, low in available 
nitrogen (112.9 kg ha -1), medium in available phosphorus (7.46 
kg ha-1) and high in available potassium (1145.6 kg ha-1). The soil 
was moderately alkaline in reaction having pH 7.4. The 
experimental field was laid out in a Randomized Block Design 
(RBD) with seven treatments replicated three times. The 
treatments were T1: Control T2:100 % RDF T3:100% RDF + 
FYM @ 5t/ha-1 T4:RDF + Neem cake powder @3qt/ha-1 T5: RDF 
+ FYM@ 5t/ha -1+multimicronutrient grade - I @ 25kg/ha -1 T6: 
RDF + Neem cak e powder @3qt/ha -1 + multi micronutrient 
grade- I @25 kg /ha -1 T7: RDF + FYM @5t/ha -1+ Neem cake 
powder @3qt/ha -1+ multi micronutrient Grade-I @25kg/ha -1. 
Sowing was done by dibbling with seed rate of 65 kg/ha -1. 
Soybean variety MAUS 158 was sown with reco mmended 
fertilizer dose of 30:60:30 NPK kg/ha-1. Gross plot was of size 5.4 
m x 4.5 m and net plot of 4.5 m x 3.9 m. Soil application of 
nitrogen and phosphorous was applied through urea and SSP 
respectively at the time of sowing and FYM was applied at 15 
days before sowing. The recommended cultural practices and 
plant protection measures were taken. Five plants were randomly 
selected and tagged from each net plot to record biometric 
observations at various growth stages. These same plants were 
later harves ted individually for post -harvest analysis. The data 
collected from various observations were organized into tables 
and analyzed using analysis of variance (Panse and Sukhatme, 
1967) [8]. 
 
Result And Discussion 
Growth attributes 
Plant height(cm) 
The application of RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + Neem cake powder 
@ 3 qt ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Grade -I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₇) 
recorded significantly superior plant height at all the growth 
stages. However, it remained statistically at par with RDF + FYM 
@ 5 t ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₅) and 
RDF + Neem cake powder @ 3 qt ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient 
Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₆). The superiority of RDF + FYM @ 5 t 
ha⁻¹ + Neem cake powder @ 3 qt ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient 
Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₇) may be due to the synergistic effect of 
organic and inorganic nutrient sources. FYM and neem cake 
improved nutrient availability and soil microbial activity, while 
micronutrients supported key physiological processes. This 
balanced nutrient supply enhanced root gro wth, nutrient uptake 
and cell elongation, resulting in greater plant height. The present 
findings are in close agreement with those reported by Kumawat 
et al. (2019) [4], Patil et al. (2021) [9]. 
 
Number of branches plant⁻¹ 
The data revealed that the number of branches plant⁻¹ was 
significantly influenced by different integrated nutrient 
management treatments at all the growth stages viz., 30, 45, 60, 
75 DAS and at harvest. The treatment RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + 
Neem cake powder @ 3 qt ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Grade-I @ 
25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₇) recorded the highest number of branches plant⁻¹ at 
all the growth stages. However, it remained statistic ally at par 
with RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 
kg ha⁻¹ (T₅) and RDF + Neem cake powder @ 3 qt ha⁻¹ + Multi 
micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₆). As FYM and neem cake 
improved nutrient availability and soil microbial activity, t his 
resulted in better root growth, nutrient uptake and vegetative 
development, ultimately promoting greater branch formation. 
 
No. of functional leaves plant⁻¹ 
The application of RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + Neem cake powder 
@ 3 qt ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Gr ade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₇) 
recorded the highest number of functional leaves plant⁻¹ at all the 
growth stages, which was remained statistically at par with RDF 
+ FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Grade -I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ 
(T₅) at 45, 60 and 75 DAS. The increase in the number of 
functional leaves under integrated nutrient management 
treatments may be attributed to the balanced and sustained supply 
of nutrients through RDF, FYM, neem cake and multi 
micronutrients, which promoted vigorous vegetative growth, 
enhanced chlorophyll synthesis and delayed leaf senescence. The 
present findings are in close agreement with those reported by 
Verma et al. (2023) [11], Gupta et al. (2024) [3]. and Biswas et al. 
(2025) [2], who also reported a significantly higher number of 
functional leaves in soybean under integrated nutrient 
management practices. 
 
Leaf area plant⁻¹ (dm²) 
The data concluded that leaf area plant⁻¹ was significantly 
influenced by different integrated nutrient management 
treatments at all the growth stages viz., 30, 45, 60 and 75 DAS. 
The treatment RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + Neem cake powder @ 3 
qt ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₇) recorded 
the highest leaf area plant⁻¹ at all the growth stages, which was 
found statistically at par with RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ + Multi 
micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₅) and RDF + Neem cake 
powder @ 3 qt ha⁻¹ + Multi micronutrient Grade -I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ 
(T₆). The higher leaf area observed under integrated nutrient 
management treatments may be attributed to the balanced supply 
of macro- and micronutrients coupled with the beneficial effects 
of FYM and neem cake on soil fertility, nutrient availability and 
microbial activity. The present findings are in agreement with

Conclusion 
Based on the above results, it can be concluded that the 
application of RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹+ neem cake powder @ 3 q 
ha⁻¹+ multi-micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg ha⁻¹ (T₇) enhancing 
the growth and yield attributes, resulting in higher seed yield of 
soybean. This treatment was closely followed by RDF + neem 
cake powder @ 3 q ha⁻¹ + multi -micronutrient Grade-I @ 25 kg 
ha⁻¹ (T₆).
