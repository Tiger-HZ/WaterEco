ABSTRACT Human activities have intensified eco‐environmental problems, challenging harmonious human‐nature interactions. However, existing integrated protection and restoration (IPR) zoning approaches for mountains, rivers, forests, farmlands, lakes, and grasslands (MRFFLG) focus on a single scale and fail to incorporate the ecological network (EN) and social‐ecological coupling dynamics, limiting multi‐scale governance effectiveness. Taking the Three Gorges Reservoir Area (TGRA) as the research area, this study constructs a multi‐scale zoning framework from the social‐ecological systems (SES) perspective. By integrating MRFFLG systems and the social system (MRFFLG‐S systems) into an evaluation system, the coupling coordination relationship of MRFFLG‐S systems is assessed to delineate IPR zones, which are subsequently refined based on EN importance to enhance ecosystem connectivity and stability. Multi‐scale IPR pathways of MRFFLG are further explored. The results show that: (1) From 2010 to 2022, coupling coordination types of subsystems transition from imbalance to coordination across three scales, with pronounced changes at finer spatial scales. (2) The EN exhibits a spatial distribution pattern of “dense in the east and sparse in the west”. (3) A hierarchical governance system characterized by “macro‐scale guidance, meso‐scale regulation, and micro‐scale implementation” is established. Specifically, 11 zones are delineated at the county scale to identify overarching directions for IPR of MRFFLG, 16 differentiated strategies are formulated at the 10 km grid scale, and IPR project implementation is supported at the 1 km grid scale. This study promotes systemic protection and governance of MRFFLG and provides spatial guidance for precise zoning management to facilitate the harmonious development of human‐environment systems.
