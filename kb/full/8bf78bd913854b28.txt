Abstract： 14 
Water resources in coal mining areas face various environmental issues, especially eutrophication. In this 15 
context, the present study aims to explore the mechanisms underlying the seas onal effects of floating 16 
photovoltaic (FPV) coverage on water eutrophica tion in the coal mining subsidence area of Huainan 17 
City. In addition, the comprehensive trophic index method (TLI) was employed to evaluate the 18 
eutrophication levels of water quality in the study area. The major factors influencing the 19 
eutrophication process were further identified using principal component analysis (PCA) and 20 
Pearson correlation analysis. The risk of eutrophication in the water bodies of the study area was 21 
also evaluated under different FPV coverage based on the entropy-weighted water quality index 22 
(EWQI) model. The results showed that total nitrogen (TN) and total phosphorus (TP) reached the 23 
highest concentrations in summer, reaching 2.2 and 0.35 mg/L, respectively, and thus exceeding the 24 
Class IV water quality standard. These high concentrations were due to the promoting eff ects of 25 
increased water temperature on N and P cycling. The FPV coverage negatively influenced the light 26 
and heat conditions of the water bodies through shading, weakening the self-purification ability of 27 
the water bodies and, consequently, promoting nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) accumulation . 28 
According to the TLI results, the new-FPV (N), high-FPV (H), low-FPV (L), and control-FPV (CK) 29 
had anaerobic (TLI=29.55), mesotrophic (TLI=30.66), mildly eutrophic (TLI=51.66), and 30 
mesotrophic (TLI=39.42) conditions, respectively. The PCA results demonstrated the strong 31 
triggering effect of TN on the eutrophication of the water bodies. The EWQI model further revealed 32 
that the eutrophication risk level of L reached the early warning class (EWQI=18.1) due to the high 33 
TN concentrations, exceeding 1.25 mg/L. In contrast, the N and H zones were classified as safe and 34 
generally safe, respectively. The findings of this study demonstrated the mitigation effects of FPV 35 
3 
 
coverage on eutrophication, providing a theoretical basis for the management of water environments 36 
in coal mining subsidence areas. 37

1 Introduction 40 
Coal mining subsidence areas have been expanded in China due to the continuous 41 
exploitation of coal resources. Indeed, the coal mining-induced ground subsidence area 42 
in China has reached 8.67*109 km2. The coal mining subsidence area in Huainan City, 43 
Anhui Province, is located in the middle reaches of the Huaihe River, which is the core 44 
area of a major coal base in China. According to statistics, the total waterlogged area in 45 
the coal mining subsidence zone in Huainan City reached 9338 km 2 in 2022. The 46 
formation of these subsidence lakes has not only changed the hydrological landscape 47 
of the area but also strongly impacted the ecological environment(Chu L., 2025). These 48 
lakes are vulnerable to the accumulation of pollutants (e.g., nitrogen and phosphorus) 49 
due to the presence of closed depressions and poor water mobility, creating favorable 50 
conditions for eutrophication and algal blooms (Qing-jun et al., 2009). At present, the 51 
model which the installation of photovoltaic panel in coal mining subsidence areas has 52 
become one of the important approaches for ensuring effective management of water 53 
environments in coal mining subsidence areas. The impacts of photovoltaics on soil and 54 
vegetation have been explored in recent years. However, few studies have focused on 55 
the ecological impacts of photovoltaics on surface water bodies in coal mining 56 
subsidence zones, even though this technology may be promising for mitigating 57 
4 
 
eutrophication in these zones. The installation of floating photovoltaic (FPV) systems 58 
can effectively restrict algal growth, limit water evaporatio (Bax et al., 2023; Goh et al., 59 
2025), thereby improving water quality. Indeed, the quality of high PV-covered water 60 
areas is often better than that of low-covered or no-covered areas, exhibiting 61 
comparatively lower pollutant concentrations, such as total nitrogen ( TN) and total 62 
phosphorus (TP). This demonstrates the inhibiting effects of photovoltaic panel 63 
coverage on water body pollution through the reduction of light for algal blooms or 64 
blocking external pollution inputs (Sun et al., 2025). Moreover, these systems can be 65 
easily installed due to their highly modular nature, explaining their continuous 66 
applications in recent years. Water bodies in coal mining subsidence areas are mainly 67 
characterized by increased nutrient concentrations. Hence, it is crucial to assess 68 
eutrophication levels of surface water to ensure effective water quality management in 69 
coal mining subsidence areas (Qu et al., 2020). Eutrophication can trigger algal blooms, 70 
depleting dissolved oxygen (DO) in the water bodies, disrupting the balance of aquatic 71 
ecosystems, and even threatening the safety of drinking water (Chen Jiubin et al., 2025). 72 
With the promotion of photovoltaic power generation technology, multiple coal 73 
mining subsidence areas have been transformed into photovoltaic power stations. 74 
However, the increased coverage of photovoltaic panels may negatively affect the 75 
temperature of water bodies and light conditions, potentially influencing lake 76 
ecosystems (Sun et al., 2025). Most related studies have focused on water quality 77 
assessments in large-scale areas, such as lakes, oceans, and rivers. Whereas research on 78 
the water quality of small lakes under the presence of photovoltaic panels, especially 79 
5 
 
those in coal-mining subsidence zones, remains limited (Xu et al., 2009). Kang et al. 80 
(2025) and Shen Yilun et al. (2025) emphasized the importance of controlling 81 
phosphorus release from river sediments to effectively mitigate eutrophication in lakes. 82 
Li (2024) highlighted increased eutrophication risks with increasing TN and TP 83 
concentrations in water bodies. It is, therefore, crucial to implement early warning 84 
measures for eutrophication risks in coal mining subsidence areas under FPV systems 85 
to ensure water quality control. 86 
Most recent studies on water quality evaluation have employed the single-factor 87 
(An et al., 2023), Nemero pollution (Wang et al., 2024), and improved Nemero (Hu et 88 
al., 2023) index methods. However, these methods can only be used for analyzing single 89 
parameters, ignoring the overall quality of water bodies. In this context, the present 90 
study aims to assess the impact s of photovoltaic panels on seasonal changes in water 91 
quality, eutrophication levels, and associated pollution sources under different 92 
photovoltaic coverage in the Panji coal mining subsidence area of Huainan City, Anhui 93 
Province, using correlation analysis (LI et al., 2024.; Sahoo et al., 2024) and principal 94 
component analysis (Yin et al., 2023; Cai, 1997; Jingxiao et al., 2021). In particular, we 95 
established an early and specific warning system to assess the eutrophication levels in 96 
the different zones of the study area using the Entropy-weighted water quality index 97 
(EWQI), thereby ensuring effective water quality management. This study provides 98 
useful baseline data for understanding the dynamic patterns of water quality in the study 99 
area, as well as theoretical support for the rational use of water resources regions with 100 
similar features. 101

2 Materials and methods 102 
2.1 Study area 103 
The study is based on the Environmental Investigation and Evaluation of Water 104 
Resources in Coal Mining Subsidence Areas (GB/T37574-2019), taking into account 105 
four areas with different FPV coverage scenarios. The first scenario consisted of a new 106 
FPV coverage area in Dingji (N), adjacent to an agricultural irrigation ditch, with small-107 
scale sheep farming and a FPV coverage of 60.1%; the second scenario was related to 108 
a high coverage area (H), with a FPV coverage of 62.7%, connected to an irrigation 109 
system through a small water body and equipped with an automatic cleaning system for 110 
the FPV panels; the third scenario is a low coverage area (L) that increased from 33.8 111 
to 50.7% in September 2024, combining fishery and poultry farming with an automatic 112 
cleaning system for the FPV panels and implemented fish-catching techniques using 113 
bleaching powder ; the fourth scenario was considered as a control FPV zone (CK) 114 
without water quality management, making it prone to increased eutrophication. In 115 
addition, CK has been affected by pesticide runoff from neighboring farmland. The total 116 
area of the four zones is 5.47 km2 (Fig. 1). 117

2.3.2 Entropy-based water quality index (EWQI) 163 
The water quality index (WQI) method is a comprehensive method widely used to 164 
explore water quality based on multiple water quality indicators (Hu, et al.,2022; Cheng, 165 
et al.,2021). The WQI results can accurately identify the key factors affecting the 166 
regional water environment quality and provide a scientific basis for water environment 167 
management and governance. The modified EWQI can ensure optimized parameter 168 
weighting selection and quality assessment when compared with the traditional WQI 169 
method (Halder, 2025). 170 
In this study, the potential factors controlling eutrophication of surface bodies (e.g., 171 
SD, NH4+-N, Chl-a, NO₃--N, TN, and TP) were first screened using pearson correlation 172 
analysis and principal component analysis (PCA), and then employed to compute the 173 
EWQI. The measured values of each indicator are first normalized to improve the 174 
accuracy and representativeness of the water quality evaluation. The use of the EWQI 175

3 Results and discussion 203 
3.1 Seasonal water quality responses to the photovoltaic perturbations 204 
According to the results, the water quality in the study area showed significant 205 
seasonal changes under the different FPV coverage scenarios. As shown in Fig. 2, the 206 
highest TN and TP concentrations were observed in summer, reaching 2.2 and 0.35 207 
mg/L, respectively. These concentrations exceeded the Class IV water standard of 208 
Environmental Quality Standard for Surface Water (3838-2002). Besides 209 
photosynthesis, nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) cycles associated with aquatic plants 210 
are enhanced by increased water temperature in summer. On the other hand, decreased 211 
water temperature in autumn and winter can restrict photosynthetic activities of plants, 212 
decreasing N and P concentrations. In this study, the highest NH4+-N concentrations 213 
12 
 
were observed in autumn, reaching 0.82 mg/L. However, this concentration was in line 214 
with the Class III water standard (3838-2002 ). The water temperature exhibited a 215 
decreasing trend in winter, restricting the activities of living organisms in the water 216 
body. In addition, the transformation and removal rates of NH4+-N were slower in 217 
winter than in spring and summer, reducing it concentrations. NO₃-N and PO₄³⁻-P both 218 
comply with the limit values specified in the Standard: 10 mg/L and 0.6 mg/L 219 
respectively. On the other hand, Chl-a exhibited the highest concentrations in summer 220 
due to the effects of high temperatures and light intensities, promoting phytoplankton 221 
growth. In contrast, comparatively lower Chl- a concentrations were found in spring, 222 
autumn, and winter. The transparency of the water body showed a decreasing-223 
increasing trend, reaching the highest value in winter. This increasing trend 224 
demonstrated the reduction in the concentrations of suspended particles in the water 225 
body, leading to comparatively lower TP concentrations (Mao et al., 2025). 226 
From the photovoltaic construction time, the longer the time, the more components 227 
in the water body. The interactions between water indicators can negatively affect water 228 
quality. Indeed, water quality in the H zone was better than that in N. Water quality in 229 
the H zone was also better than that in N. The FPV coverage had indirect effects on the 230 
water quality by influencing water temperature, light intensities, and pollutant 231 
accumulation. The study area had numerous photovoltaic panels on the water body 232 
covered by FPV , potentially reducing the disturbance of the external environment on 233 
the water body to some extent. In fact, the FPV panels can reduce radiation heating of 234 
the water body, lower the surface water temperature, restrict the biodegradation of N/P, 235 
13 
 
and reduce Chl- a concentrations (Liu et al., 2023), thereby inhibiting phytoplankton 236 
photosynthesis and influencing the ecological balance of the water body. At the same 237 
time, the PV coverage might hinder the self-purification ability of the water body, 238 
thereby increasing the accumulation of N and organic pollutants. Bax et al. (2023) 239 
highlighted significant photosensitivity under three different FPV coverage (75, 100, 240 
and 75%), resulting in reduced light intensities by 73 to 100%. The light at the reference 241 
site was significantly higher than that in the FPV coverage area. Rahaman et al. (2025) 242 
showed a decrease in the Chl-a concentrations by 17 to 48% under a FPV coverage of 243 
10%. In addition, there was no decrease in the Chl-a concentrations in the absence of 244 
FPV coverage, further demonstrating the decreasing effect of this coverage on 245 
phytoplankton biomass. On the other hand, a previous related study showed 246 
significantly reduced concentrations of aquatic indicators under higher FPV coverage , 247 
effectively inhibiting excessive algal growth. A similar finding was revealed in a 248 
simulation study on the South- to-North Water Diversion Project (Ma et al., 2022). 249 
Water-based photovoltaic projects with 50% coverage can effectively control algal 250 
bloom outbreaks under reduced water flow rate conditions. The results of this study 251 
revealed changes in the concentrations of indicators in the same area, with FPV panels, 252 
as well as in distant areas. 253

3.3 Eutrophication risk assessment results based on the entropy-weighted water quality 271 
index(EWQI) 272 
The correlation and PCA results showed a significant positive correlation between 273 
eutrophication outbreaks and N concentrations. In other words, areas with high TN 274 
concentration had significant contributions to the eutrophication process. According to 275 
Tang et al. (2025), excessive N inputs, especially in the NH4+-N and NO3--N forms, can 276 
significantly promote algal overgrowth and, consequently, trigger the eutrophication of 277 
water bodies. On the other hand, Cao et al. (2024) indicated that low TN concentrations 278 
are conducive to improving water quality characteristics. Therefore, TN was considered 279 
a key environmental factor affecting the eutrophication level in the study area. 280 
This study considered the TN concentrations in the traditional WQI system. The 281 
sampling sites in the four regions were classified as eutrophic and non-eutrophic based 282 
on the collected monitoring data over the four seasons of 2024. According to Table 2 , 283 
the TN concentration threshold for Class IV was 1.25 mg/L. In other words, TN 284 
16 
 
concentrations exceeding this threshold can promote the eutrophication outbreak . 285 
Therefore, this concentration (1.25 mg/L) was considered a risk threshold of the 286 
eutrophication outbreak. Total nitrogen (TN) concentrations exceeding 1.25 mg/L can 287 
negatively affect the EWQI value. As shown in Fig 3, the N and H zones were safe , 288 
showing EWQI values of 80.5 and 65.4, respectively. On the other hand, the L zone 289 
exhibited an early warning class, while the CK zone was generally safe, with EWQI 290 
values of 18.1 and 41.7, respectively. These findings highlight the highest 291 
eutrophication risk in the L zone, suggesting the implementation of urgent and effective 292 
control measures. 293

3.5 Sources of eutrophication in the study area 317 
The results of this study showed strong and significant positive correlations of the 318 
Chl-a concentrations with the TLI in the N, H, L, and CK zones, with correlation 319 
coefficients (r) of 0.94, 0.87, 0.86, and 0.90, respectively (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5). This 320 
19 
 
finding further demonstrates the strong influence of Chl- a on the TLI, which is in line 321 
with the results of Singh and Yadav (2025), highlighting Chl- a as a key indicator of 322 
eutrophication. WT and SD had no significant correlations with Chl- a and TLI in this 323 
study. Zou et al. (2020) suggested the controlling effect of temperature before exploring 324 
eutrophication levels, as high water temperature can restrict algal growth and, 325 
consequently, influence the eutrophication process. Whereas SD can be used as an 326 
indicator for exploring eutrophication levels in the presence of algal biomass (Zhang et 327 
al., 2023; Zou et al., 2020), explaining the reason for focusing on the effect of Chl-a on 328 
the eutrophication levels of the water bodies in this study. NO₃--N is the product of 329 
NH4+-N nitrification, reflecting N cycling. NH4+-N can be derived from residual bait 330 
accumulation due to overfeeding, as well as from the continuous deposition of 331 
biological metabolic wastes. In fact, TN and TP can be mainly derived from livestock 332 
and poultry farming (Sang et al., 2025 ; Xu et al., 2024). These anthropogenic sources 333 
are the major factors controlling N and P changes in water bodies. Organic materials 334 
(i.e., livestock manure and residual feeds) are rich in protein components that can be 335 
decomposed to amino acids under the effects of microbial activities, and then gradually 336 
converted into NH4+ through ammonification, ultimately increasing NH4+-N 337 
concentrations in water bodies (Islam et al., 2024). These increased concentrations can 338 
subsequently increase the Chl-a concentrations, thereby aggravating eutrophication of 339 
water bodies. 340 
Surface water exploitation for irrigation purposes is common in the vicinity of N. 341 
Since the zone is characterized by the absence of fishery and poultry farming, therefore, 342 
20 
 
it is due to agricultural pollution (Huiyu et al., 2022). The H zone exhibited serious 343 
water pollution from livestock farming when compared with the other zones. Indeed , 344 
pollutants, such as dust and other organic pollutants, can enter the water body through 345 
rainfall-runoff. Similarly, water pollution in the L zone was from livestock and fishery 346 
farming products, including diatom species, sucrose, and bleaching powder. Livestock 347 
manure might also result in high N and P concentrations in the water body. The CK 348 
zone is characterized by the presence of fishery farming, agricultural irrigated land, and 349 
basic sanitation facilities. In addition, this area had a cyanobacterial bloom trend with 350 
dead fish floating on the shore, suggesting that P-containing fertilizers and manure were 351 
the main source of water pollution in the CK zone (Chenhu and Liu, 2019; Huiyu et al., 352 
2022; Fu et al., 2025). Increased rainfall in summer might also promote the migration 353 
of pollutants into the water body through watershed runoff and nutrient inputs, 354 
exacerbating eutrophication (Liu et al., 2025). 355

4 Conclusion 358 
The water quality in the all study area showed significant seasonal changes. In 359 
particular, the increased water temperature in summer and autumn resulted in high TN 360 
(2.2 mg/L) and TP (0.35 mg/L), exceeding the Class IV standard. Whereas the NH4+ 361 
concentration reached 0.82 mg/L (Class III standard) in autumn due to the low 362 
temperature-induced inhibition of nitrification. In contrast, the NO₃--N and PO₄³ --P 363 
concentrations met the standard over the study period. Photothermal changes 364 
influenced algal biomass in the study area. Indeed, Chl- a reached the highest 365 
concentrations in summer due to the high temperature and light intensities. In contrast, 366 
the lowest Chl-a concentrations were found in spring and winter. Photovoltaic coverage 367 
22 
 
significantly changed the water environment through its decreasing effects on surface 368 
water temperature, reducing N and P biodegradation rates, as well as weakening the 369 
self-purification ability of the water bodies. The nutrient status in the study area was 370 
different between the FPC coverage scenarios. The N, H, L, and CK zones exhibited 371 
TLI values of 29.55, 30.66, 51.66, and 39.42, corresponding to poor, medium, mildly, 372 
and medium eutrophic conditions, respectively. On the other hand, the obtained results 373 
revealed a strong contribution of TN accumulation to the trophic status, leading to the 374 
occurrence of eutrophication. The EWQI-based risk evaluation highlighted a high 375 
eutrophication risk in L, while the remaining area had acceptable eutrophication risks. 376 
The findings of this study suggest the optimization of FPV coverage in order to 377 
effectively inhibit algal proliferation and prevent TN accumulation in the water bodies. 378
