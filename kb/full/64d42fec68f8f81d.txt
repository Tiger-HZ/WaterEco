Streams formed in Glacier Bay, southeast Alaska, following glacial recession rapidly support populations of spawning salmon. Using both observational and experimental approaches, we examined the importance of pink salmon ( Oncorhynchus gorbuscha ) carcasses for benthic macroinvertebrate communities, approximately 10 years after salmon first colonized Wolf Point Creek, a first-order, lake-fed stream. Macroinvertebrate abundance on carcasses naturally accumulating in the stream channel was low, dominated by chironomids ( Eukiefferiella spp.). Macroinvertebrate drift was significantly lower from artificial experimental channels containing salmon flesh (P &lt; 0.001) compared with channels without.
