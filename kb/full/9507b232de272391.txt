Abstract Grassland science has traditionally treated water as a local constraint, assessed through precipitation, soil moisture, or evapotranspiration at site to regional scales. Emerging insights from Earth system science challenge this view, revealing freshwater cycling as a connected, planetary‐scale process shaped by land‐atmosphere interactions. Recent work on planetary boundaries has identified a transgression of the green water boundary, defined by deviations in root‐zone soil moisture from Holocene variability, with profound implications for terrestrial ecosystems. In this perspective, we argue that grasslands occupy a central yet underappreciated position within this emerging framework.
