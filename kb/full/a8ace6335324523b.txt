This study discusses the problem space of digital accessibility in socio-environmental studies based on Volunteered Geographic Information (VGI), emphasizing the challenges posed by the contrast between the complexity of geographic models and the high levels of cartographic illiteracy among the general population, particularly with regard to cartographic accessibility, community fatigue, and the difficulty of continuously updating data. VGI is addressed as a data visualization paradigm that confronts the dilemma between cartographic abstraction and visual realism. The chapter advances the hypothesis that increasing visual realism may reduce barriers to public access to VGI. To this end, it grounds the adoption of a Design Research approach, guided by Research Through Design (RtD), in which the development of a digital geographic model prototype serves as an instrument for exploring the problem space of accessibility in VGI. The methodology is structured around iterative cycles of development and critical analysis, in which design decisions are informed by the literature and evaluated based on the limitations and contradictions observed during the prototyping process. Finally, the chapter concludes that the primary barriers to VGI accessibility do not reside exclusively in technical limitations or visual perception, but rather in a structural tension between visual representation and cognitive interpretation, highlighting semantic mediation of spatial knowledge as a promising direction for future research.
