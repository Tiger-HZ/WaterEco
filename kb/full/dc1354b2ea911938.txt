Abstract
Chemical pollution in the Persian Gulf has become an important health concern in recent decades. This
investigation aimed to meta-analyses of potential toxic elements (PTEs) including lead (Pb), inorganic Arsenic
(As), Cadmium (Cd), Nickel (Ni) and Mercury (Hg) in the coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf. In this study, an
attempt was made to retrieve papers conducted on the concentration of PTEs in the coastal sediment of the
Persian Gulf by searching in international databases including Web of Science, Scopus, Embase and PubMed.
Meta-analysis concentration of PTEs in the coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf was conducted using random
effects model based on the country subgroup. Additionally, non-dietary risk assessment including non-
carcinogenic and carcinogenic risks from ingestion, inhalation and dermal contact routes and ecological risk
assessment was estimated. Seventy-eight papers with 81 data-reports (1650 sample size) were included in our
meta-analysis. The rank order of heavy metals in coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf based on pooled
concentration was Ni (65.44 mg/kg) > Pb (58.35 mg/kg) > As (23.78 mg/kg) > Cd (1.75 mg/kg) > Hg (0.77
mg/kg). The highest concentration of As, Cd, Pb, Ni and Hg was observed in the coastal sediment of Saudi
Arabia, Arab Emirates, Qatar, Iran and Saudi Arabia, respectively. Although the Igeo index in coastal sediment of
the Persian gulf was in Grade 1 (Uncontaminated) and grade 2 (slightly contaminated) but total target hazard
quotient (TTHQ) of adults due to As and Cd and Adolescent due to As, Cd, Pb and Niwas higher than 1 value In
Iran, In Saudi Arabic, TTHQ of adults due to As and Cd and adolescent due to As and Cd was higher than 1
value, In United Arab Emirates, TTHQ of adults due to Cd and Adolescents due to As, Cd and Pb were higher
than 1 value and In Qatar, TTHQ of adults age group due to Pb and adolescent age group due to Pb was higher
than 1 value. Total cancer risk (TCR) for adults and adolescent due to As was higher than 1E-6 in Iran, United
Arab Emirates and Qatar but TCR for adolescents due to As was higher than 1E-6 in Saudi Arabic. Therefore, it
is recommended to monitor the concentration of PTEs and implementation of programs to reduce the emission
of PTEs from resources in the Persian Gulf.
1. Introduction
Several inuential studies have paid signicant attention to potentially toxic elements (PTEs) because of their
ecotoxicity, bioaccumulation, ubiquity, and stability, leading to deleterious consequences on human health and
the environment (E Nour et al. 2022, Jeong et al. 2021, Neshat et al. 2023, Ş im ş ek et al. 2022). PTEs are dened
as natural elements with a relatively high density, at least 5 times higher than water, andtoxic or poisonous
effects at low concentrations (Witkowska et al. 2021). In addition to classic heavy metals such as copper,
nickel, cadmium, and zinc, heavy metals also comprise metalloids like arsenic, which can produce in-vivo
toxicity even at low exposure levels (Goutam Mukherjee et al. 2022). PTEs are ubiquitous because they occur
naturally in the earth's crust. However, there are several anthropogenic sources-including industries,
medications, and agricultural practices- in which high concentrations of PTEs can discharge into the
environment (Arfaeinia et al. 2019, Elsagh et al. 2021b).
It has been proved that exposure to environmental-related PTEs can endanger human health. For instance,
mercury is toxic to the nerves and kidneys and is mostly detected in marine and freshwater. Higher mercury
levels in the blood can lead to infertility (Díaz de León-Martínez et al. 2022). Lead affects the bone structure
and impairs nerve cell development and differentiation in children under six years old (Finkelstein et al. 1998,
Wong et al. 2015). One-third of the body's Cadmium (II) is stored in the liver and kidneys. Cadmium's toxicity
Page 3/25
means that even short-term contact can have serious effects on human health. Kidney damage, insomnia,
anemia, and cancer of the prostate and bladder are all results of long-term cadmium exposure (Garrett et al.
2013). All are aware that arsenic is toxic and causes cancer. It exists in the atmosphere, hydrosphere, and soil.
Claudia et al. proved that there is a relation between newborn birth weight and arsenic in drinking water, such
that arsenic-exposed kids lost 57 g of their weight (Hopenhayn et al. 2003, Yang et al. 2003).
Global monitoring of PTEs indicated their proliferation in aquatic ecosystems. The majority of such pollutants
are sunk into sediments, which are regarded as sensitive indicators for following them in aquatic ecosystems
(Jeong et al. 2021). PTEs can enter the trophic webs as they are resuspended, or biota in sediments release
them into the water column (Kahlon et al. 2018, Ş im ş ek et al. 2022). Additionally, substantial urbanization,
rapid industrialization, and economic development have signicantly polluted the coastal environments over
the past decades, such that releasing a considerable mass of anthropogenic PTEs into coastal environments
via a variety of approaches, including riverine intake and atmospheric deposition, has been spotted (Arfaeinia et
al. 2019, E Nour et al. 2022, Ş im ş ek et al. 2022). For example, Wang et al., (2020) study in coastal sediment of
East China sea, the concentration of PTEs (Cu, Zn, Ni, Pb, Cd) was measured coastal East China sea (one of the
most developed regions in China). The results shows that coastal sediment of East China sea is unpolluted to
moderately polluted. Also, the strong correlations and organic carbon isotope compositions data showed that
natural weathering detritus is the main source of pollution in coastal East of China sea(Wang et al. 2020).
In the present study, consideration was paid to the Persian Gulf’s sediments. This Gulf is a Mediterranean Sea
located in the Persian Gulf Basin, which is of Cenozoic origin, and linked to the Gulf of Oman by the Strait of
Hormuz. It is a biogeographic sub-province that can be found in the northern Indian Ocean (Elsagh et al.
2021b). In addition to being one of the world's most vital oil and gas transportation routes, the Persian Gulf is
also home to a thriving ecology, and its neighboring countries are rapidly acquiring new beach front
developments. The leading industries in the surrounding countries are oil and gas reneries; however, the area
also hosts several ancillary industries, including the manufacturing of plastic, cement, aluminum, and
polyethylene piping. Moreover, other routes can harm the sediments along the coasts, such as the discharge of
wastewater from sh breeding, animal husbandry, agriculture, and municipal solid waste management
(Arfaeinia et al. 2019, Elsagh et al. 2021b).
The concentration of PTEs in the sediments of the Persian Gulf with several lands uses, such as industrial,
urban, agricultural, and natural areas, were investigated (Arfaeinia et al. 2019). Compared to the natural and
urban sources, agricultural and industrial elds indicated extremely greater average PTEs content. It was also
discovered that the vast majority of HMs have strong interrelationships, indicating that their release into the
environment likely originated from the same sources (Arfaeinia et al. 2019).
In another work, Elsagh et al. just focused on the mean mercury concentration in the sediments of the Persian
Gulf. They proved that human activity was responsible for high mercury concentration in the sediment of six
sampling regions of the study (3.24 ± 0.28 µg/g) (Elsagh et al. 2021b). Anthropogenic sources such as
transporting crude oil and heavy trac, contamination from petroleum compounds, ships' emission of Ballast
water, mercury-relating mining, municipal wastewater, and agricultural process were associated with the
mercury contamination of sediments in the Persian Gulf. Additionally, this catastrophe was assumed to
become even worse by increasing erosion and, consequently, produced sediments containing urban
Page 4/25
contaminants. They claimed that anthropogenic sources and activities caused the movability of mercury in
polluted sediments (Elsagh et al. 2021b).
Despite numerous studies in the last two decades in the eld of pollution of Persian Gulf coasts with
PTEs(Alharbi &El-Sorogy 2017, Chaharlang et al. 2012, Einollahipeer et al. 2013, El-Sorogy et al. 2016, Elsagh et
al. 2021a, Janadeleh et al. 2018, Karbasdehi et al. 2016, Mahboob et al. 2022, Salimi et al. 2019, Sedigheh et al.
2021, Vaezi et al. 2015), no study was found in the eld of systematic review-meta-analysis and health risk
assessment. The main objectives of this study were meta-analysis concentration of PTEs in coastal sediment
of the Persian Gulf based on the subgroup of countries and estimation of non-dietary risk in adults and
adolescents age groups and ecological risk assessment.
2. Method And Material
2.1. The protocol and search strategy
This study was performed for assessing documents relating to the concentration of PTEs
(As, Cd, Pb, Ni and Hg) in coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf following PRISMA guidelines (Fig. 1) (Atabati et
al. 2020, Moher et al. 2011). All published investigations in the English language were searched in international
databases including Web of Science, Scopus, Embase, and PubMed as well as Google Scholar (grey literature)
from 1 January 2010 to 10 September 2022. Keywords were selected according to Medical Subject Headings
(MeSH) and were included
in international databases: ((ti/ab ("trace element") OR ti/ab ("toxic metals") OR ti/ab ("heavy metals") OR
ti/ab("metal(oid)s") OR ti/ab ("metals") OR ti/ab ("potential toxic metals") OR ti/ab ("potential hazard metals")
AND ti/ab ("sediment") OR ti/ab ("coastal sediment") OR ti/ab ("sea sediment") OR ti/ab ("soil") AND ti/ab
("Persian Gulf"). In addition, the list of references retrieved papers assessed by authors for the nd missed
papers.
2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria and extraction of data
In the current systematic review study, inclusion and exclusion criteria were assessed by two authors (YA.FA
and TR.MO) independently based on the title and abstract. Finally, the disagreement between the two authors
was resolved by a nal decision of the third author (I.IN). Our criteria for the selection of papers were including
(a) full-text available of articles, (b) publishing full text in the English language, (c) descriptive study, (d)
recording of average with a standard deviation of concentration of PTE and/or range the concentration of PTEs
in coastal sediment and (e) study conducted on coastal sediment of Persian Gulf. In this regard, interventional
studies, case reports, clinical trials, review articles, letters to editors, book chapters and all investigations that
were not conducted in the coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf were excluded from our study. Some studies
that not presented average concentration and/or range concentration of PTEs in coastal sediment were
excluded. The selected data of each study were including country, sample size, average and standard deviation
of PTEs, contamination factor (CF)and method of detection. The concentration of PTEs including ppb, ppm,
µg/kg and ng/g was converted to mg/kg.

3. Results And Discussion
3.1. Selection process of papers
The number of retrieved papers was equal to 1452, after removing duplicate papers and assessing the title,
abstract and full text of papers, 78 papers with 81 data-reports (1650 sample size) were included in our meta-
analysis (Fig. 1 and Appendix 4).

As observed from ndings, anthropogenic and agricultural activities such as transportation services, industrial
waste disposal, shing and oil exploration can be considered as possible sources of pollution and high
concentration of many heavy metals in different areas and increase the health risk in residents (Mirza et al.
2019).
3.4. Ecological risk assessment
According to studies, different indices have been applied to evaluate the contamination of ecosystems. One of
the most used indices is the Igeo index. Based on the degree of contamination, this index classies ecosystems
into seven groups (from 0 to 6), from uncontaminated to extremely contaminated. In current study, Igeo was
used to evaluate the contamination degree of As, Cd, Pb, Ni and Hg in the coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf.
Our results indicated in Iran, Igeo  ≤  0 was related to Ni and As (Grade 1: Uncontaminated) and Igeo 0 < Igeo  ≤  1
was related to Hg, Pb and Cd (Grade 2: Slightly contaminated). In Saudi Arabic, Igeo  ≤  0 was related to Ni and
Pb (Grade 1: Uncontaminated) and Igeo 0 < Igeo  ≤  1 was related Hg, As and Cd (Grade 2: Slightly
Page 9/25
contaminated) (Fig. 3 and Appendix 6). Consistent with our evaluation, Alahabadi et al suggested sediments of
the Tajan River were non-contaminated with respect to Fe, Ci, Ni and Zn based on the Igeo index. They reported
contamination of these areas could be affected by urban and domestic, industrial wastewater, and agricultural
activities (Alahabadi &Malvandi 2018). In a similar study, Kadhum et al listed the elements Ni, Cr, and Fe as low
contamination in the Bernam River of Malaysia (Kadhum et al. 2016). Liu et al (2021) reported contamination
degree of Cd and Pb in sediments of Haizhou Bay, China was all < 0 therefore the southern part of Haizhou Bay
was uncontaminated with these heavy metals (Liu et al. 2021). In another study conducted by Li et al (2015) it
was observed that the Igeo values of Pb in the sediments of Shuangtaizi estuary was categorized as class 0,
which indicates that the sediments in this region were uncontaminated by Pb metal (Li et al. 2015). Ogundele et
al showed that overall conditions of the potential ecological risks varied from low to considerable in coastal
sediments samples of Nigeria which can be related to anthropogenic activities and natural sources that could
discharge metals to sediments and contribute to the associated ecological risk (Ogundele &Ayeku 2020).
Finally, in the present meta-analysis study, although the ecological risk unlike the health risk, was acceptable,
but it should be mentioned that in the ecological risk, the effects of metals on the total environment evaluate,
while in the health risk, the effects of metals on human health are considered affected by various parameters,
therefore, it should be mentioned that ecological risk alone cannot determine the health conditions of metals in
the coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf.
4. Conclusion
In this study, a meta-analysis concentration of PTEs (As, Cd, Pb, Ni and Hg) in coastal sediment of the Persian
Gulf was conducted based on the country subgroup. Additionally, non-dietary risk assessment (non-
carcinogenic and carcinogenic risks) and ecological risk were estimated. The rank order of heavy metals in the
coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf based on pooled concentration was Ni > Pb > As > Cd > Hg. Although the
ecological risk assessment showed that the coastal sediment of the Persian Gulf is uncontaminated or Slightly
contaminate but TTHQ for both adults and adolescents due to As, Cd, Pb and Ni was higher than 1 value in
Iran, Saudi Arabic, United Arab Emirates and Qatar. In Iran, United Arab Emirates and Qatar, TCR for adults and
adolescents due to As was higher than 1E-6 but in Saudi Arabic,TCR for Adolescents due to As was higher than
1E-6. Therefore, exposed people are at considerable non-dietary health risk. Hence, it is recommended to carry
out continuous control programs for the concentration of PTEs as well as to identify the sources of PTEs of
coastal and try to reduce the emission of PTEs into the coastal sediments of the Persian Gulf.
