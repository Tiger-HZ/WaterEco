Background: Acute pain is one of the most common and clinically important problems in emergency medicine. Despite available pharmacological and non-pharmacological treatments, pain is frequently underestimated, undertreated, delayed, or insufficiently reassessed. This persistent phenomenon, known as oligoanalgesia, may adversely affect patient comfort, satisfaction, diagnostic cooperation, and the quality of emergency care. Aim: This narrative review evaluates current approaches to rapid analgesia in the emergency department, focusing on early pain assessment, reassessment, multimodal analgesia, opioid-sparing strategies, and organizational barriers to effective pain management.
