Low-pressure abrasive air jet cutting is a water-free and low heat nput technology with the potential for metallic component dismantling during nuclear facility decommissioning. Abrasive hardness plays a critical role in governing the material removal efficiency of ductile and high-strength metals. However, the manner in which the abrasive-to-target hardness ratio regulates abrasive fragmentation, effective material removal, and thermal response in low-pressure abrasive air jet cutting remains unclear, particularly for ductile metallic components under water-free cutting conditions.Unlike conventional abrasive water jet studies, this work investigates the effect of the abrasive-to-target hardness ratio on the cutting efficiency, abrasive fragmentation, and thermal response under water-free low-pressure abrasive air jet conditions, thereby providing a basis for abrasive selection in the dismantling of ductile metallic components in nuclear facilities.Cutting experiments were conducted on 304 stainless steel at a jet pressure of 2 MPa using five abrasives with different hardness values, including silicon carbide, brown-fused alumina, garnet, steel slag, and river sand. Each experimental condition was independently repeated three times, and the repeated tests showed good consistency in cutting performance and thermal response.The results showed that the cutting efficiency reached 43.3 mm3/s, confirming the capability of low-pressure abrasive air jets for efficient metallic plate cutting. With increasing abrasive Mohs hardness (Ha)/target hardness (Hb), the cutting efficiency exhibited a three-stage evolution, namely a slow increase, rapid enhancement, and subsequent stabilization. This variation was mainly governed by abrasive fragmentation.At low hardness ratios, severe abrasive breakage consumed a large fraction of the jet energy and limited effective material removal. When 1 < Ha/Hb < 1.5, abrasive fragmentation was markedly reduced, leading to a rapid increase in cutting efficiency. Beyond this range, further increases in the hardness ratio produced only limited improvement. The maximum cutting zone temperature remained below 113°C, demonstrating the low heat input characteristic of the process.
