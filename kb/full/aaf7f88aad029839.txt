Abstract To investigate the effect of surface hardness on the wear characteristics of magnetorheological fluid, transmission components with different surface hardness levels are fabricated using four surface treatments: untreated, nickel-plated, low-temperature nitrided, and titanium-coated. This study focuses on the mechanism by which variations in surface hardness affect the wear behavior and the power transmission performance of magnetorheological fluid. The variation in the shear yield stress of magnetorheological fluid under different surface treatments is measured using a self-built experimental apparatus dedicated to magnetorheological fluid power transmission and wear testing.
