Abstract
Coffee is a worldwide significant agricultural commodity, but nutritional deficits 
in coffee leaves impair plant health and output, necessitating early and correct 
diagnosis. Existing deep learning algorithms confront limitations such as common 
deficiency symptoms, limited datasets, and poor interpretability. This study presents 
an Explainable Vision Transformer (XViT)-based system for detecting coffee leaf 
nutrient deficiencies and making rule-based treatment recommendations. The 
approach improves data variety by preprocessing and class-wise augmentation, 
whereas XViT collects global and detailed visual patterns for classification. An expert-
defined rule-based module makes suitable treatment recommendations, while Grad-
CAM visualization enhances model clarity by emphasizing key symptom locations. 
Experimental results show that the proposed XViT model regularly outperforms 
baseline architectures such as ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B4, and ConvNeXt. On the 
initial dataset, XViT achieved 94% precision, 91% recall, 92% F1-score, and accuracy of 
94 ± 0.37%, whereas augmentation improved accuracy to 96 ± 0.29%. The rule-based 
recommendation module achieved 100% rule coverage, 100% rule consistency, a 
93.72% end-to-end recommendation success rate, and a Cohen’s Kappa agreement 
of 0.93 among agricultural specialists. Grad-CAM study indicated that the model 
concentrates on significant symptom regions linked with nutrient deficits, hence 
increasing transparency and reliability. Overall, the proposed framework for precision 
coffee nutrient management is accurate, explainable, and practical since it combines 
deep learning-based diagnosis with expert-guided treatment.

 1 Introduction
Coffee is one of the world’s most economically important crops, providing a significant 
income for millions of growers in tropical and subtropical countries. However, nutri -
tional deficits significantly impact coffee production by reducing plant health, crop 
yield, and bean quality [ 1, 2]. Nutrient deficiencies in nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium,

Adugna Discover Artificial Intelligence (2026) 6:1055 
magnesium, and iron are common apparent signs on coffee leaves. If not recognized 
promptly, these flaws can dramatically affect output and overall coffee quality [2, 3].
Traditional approaches for diagnosing nutrient deficiencies mainly rely on manual 
inspection, soil testing, and laboratory analysis [ 3, 4]. Although these methods are fre -
quently used, they are time-consuming, expensive, and require specialist knowledge. 
Furthermore, manual monitoring is difficult to implement successfully in large-scale 
coffee plantations, which limits its scalability and effectiveness [ 5, 6]. These limitations 
underscore the importance of automated, reliable nutrient-deficit detection systems for 
precision agriculture.
Recent advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning have greatly 
enhanced automated plant health monitoring. In particular, convolutional neural net -
works (CNNs) have demonstrated promising performance in agricultural image analy -
sis applications [7, 8]. However, CNN-based approaches frequently fail to capture global 
contextual information and subtle visual patterns for nutrient-deficit detection in com -
plex agricultural imagery [ 9]. Vision Transformers (ViTs) have recently emerged as an 
effective alternative due to their self-attention mechanisms and ability to model long-
range interactions in images [ 10–12]. These properties make transformer-based tech -
niques useful for detecting nutritional deficits in coffee leaves [13–16].
Despite recent progress, existing studies still have numerous limitations. The majority 
of past research has focused on detecting plant diseases rather than identifying nutri -
ent deficiencies. Furthermore, many current systems only provide categorization results, 
not treatment recommendations or interpretability analyses. Furthermore, transformer-
based learning techniques for detecting coffee nutrient deficiencies are underexplored.
To address these issues, this study provides an Explainable Vision Transformer (ViT) 
framework for automated nutrient deficiency identification and rule-based treatment 
recommendations in coffee leaves. The proposed architecture uses a Vision Transformer 
to gather global contextual information as well as subtle visual patterns in order to accu-
rately classify nutrient deficiencies. Based on the expected nutrient deficiency, an expert-
defined rule-based suggestion module automatically applies the appropriate agronomic 
treatment in accordance with accepted nutrition-treatment criteria. Grad-CAM is used 
to depict the discriminative regions that contribute to the model’s predictions, which 
improves transparency and helps with practical decision-making. The proposed frame -
work intends to support precision agriculture by offering an accurate, scalable, interpre -
table, and practical decision-support tool for managing coffee nutritional deficiencies.
The main contributions of this study are as follows:
1. An explainable vision transformer (ViT) framework is developed for automated 
detection of multiple nutrient deficiencies in coffee leaves by effectively capturing 
global contextual features and subtle visual symptoms.
2. An expert-defined rule-based treatment recommendation module is integrated to 
automatically generate appropriate agronomic treatment recommendations based on 
the predicted nutrient deficiency, enabling an end-to-end decision-support workflow.
3. A Grad-CAM-based explainable AI module is incorporated to improve model 
transparency by highlighting the discriminative regions of coffee leaf images that 
influence nutrient deficiency predictions.

1.1 Literature review
Automated plant disease diagnosis systems have been greatly enhanced by recent devel -
opments in computer vision, machine learning, and deep learning. Image-based dis -
ease detection techniques have drawn a lot of interest in coffee farming because they 
can facilitate early diagnosis, lower the cost of manual inspections, and increase crop 
management effectiveness. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), transfer learning 
techniques, hybrid deep learning architectures, and, more recently, transformer-based 
models are the main methods used in current research to classify coffee leaf diseases.
CNN-based architectures were widely used in early research on coffee leaf disease 
detection due to their powerful capacity to extract discriminative visual information 
from leaf images. Under controlled imaging settings, CNN models have been shown in 
multiple studies to accurately discern between healthy and sick coffee leaves. For exam -
ple, utilizing over 4000 images of coffee leaves, a CNN-based framework for identifying 
coffee wilt disease achieved a classification accuracy of 97.9%, demonstrating the effi -
cacy of deep feature extraction for disease recognition [ 17]. Similar to this, CNN models 
based on transfer learning, like MobileNet and ResNet50, were used to classify coffee 
rust, brown eye spot, wilt, and healthy leaves with an accuracy of more than 97% [ 18]. 
The effectiveness of CNN architectures in collecting local texture and color differences 
linked to coffee leaf diseases was validated by this research.
Many studies used transfer learning approaches with pretrained deep learning models 
to further enhance classification performance and solve the problem of limited labeled 
datasets. In coffee illness classification tasks, pretrained architectures such as ResNet50, 
InceptionV3, VGG16, Xception, and ResNet101 have demonstrated exceptional perfor -
mance [19, 20]. In fact, research on large-scale coffee leaf datasets reported classification 
accuracies of up to 99%, confirming transfer learning’s robustness for agricultural image 
analysis. These techniques’ success is largely due to their capacity to apply feature repre -
sentations learnt from huge benchmark datasets to domain-specific agricultural tasks. 
Despite their excellent classification accuracy, most extant transfer learning studies are 
exclusively concerned with disease identification and rarely address nutrient deficiency 
detection or treatment recommendations.
Beyond deep CNN structures, classic machine learning and ensemble learning tech -
niques have been investigated for coffee crop monitoring applications. Ensemble-based 
techniques, such as XGBoost, have been employed to detect Coffee Berry Disease early 
on, producing competitive results with accuracies of 98% [ 21]. These methods demon -
strated that handmade feature extraction mixed with ensemble classifiers can still deliver 
dependable performance in specific agricultural contexts. Nonetheless, traditional 
machine learning approaches can necessitate extensive feature engineering and may fail 
to generalize effectively under changing environmental conditions when compared to 
deep learning models.

Recent research has increasingly focused on hybrid and advanced deep learning archi -
tectures to improve coffee leaf disease detection. Hybrid models that incorporate archi -
tectures such as U-Net, SegNet, MobileNetV3, VGG16, and Swin Transformers have 
shown improved feature representation and classification performance [ 22, 23]. Trans-
former-based models, in particular, have received interest because of their self-attention 
processes and capacity to capture global contextual information, making them useful for 
detecting subtle illness indications in agricultural images.
Advanced techniques, such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), have also been used 
to monitor agricultural activities. Hybrid Vision Graph Neural Networks (HV-GNNs) 
excelled at detecting coffee pests and crop damage by accurately modeling spatial rela -
tionships in complex agricultural situations [24].
Beyond coffee crops, deep learning algorithms have been widely used to detect disease 
and nutrient deficiencies in crops such as rice, maize, wheat, and tomato [ 25]. Trans-
former-based models have recently demonstrated promising results in plant phenotyp -
ing and nutrient deficiency diagnosis, owing to their capacity to capture subtle visual 
fluctuations and long-range correlations within images [26].
Although previous research has yielded good classification accuracy for coffee leaf 
disease identification, a number of significant research gaps remain. First, most present 
research focuses on disease categorization rather than nutritional deficit identification, 
despite the fact that nutrient deficiencies have a major impact on coffee growth, produc-
tion, and quality. Second, many existing techniques conclude with nutrient deficiency 
classification but do not provide actionable treatment recommendations to help farm -
ers make timely management decisions. Third, traditional convolutional neural network 
(CNN) models frequently fail to capture the global contextual relationships and subtle 
visual patterns linked to nutrient insufficiency symptoms.
Furthermore, many contemporary systems do not use explainable artificial intelli -
gence (XAI) methodologies, which limits the transparency, interpretability, and practi -
cal application of their predictions in real-world agricultural contexts. Although Vision 
Transformers have shown high performance in a variety of computer vision tasks, their 
application to explainable coffee leaf nutrient deficit detection combined with expert-
guided treatment advice is mostly unexplored. Motivated by these constraints, this study 
provides an Explainable Vision Transformer (ViT) framework for detecting coffee leaf 
nutrient deficiencies and recommending rule-based treatments. The proposed system 
uses Vision Transformers’ global feature representation capacity to accurately detect 
nutrient shortages in coffee leaves. Based on the expected shortfall, an expert-defined 
rule-based suggestion module creates the appropriate agronomic solution using known 
nutrient management principles. In addition, a Grad-CAM-based explainability module 
is included to depict the image regions that contribute the most to the model’s predic -
tions, increasing transparency, interpretability, and user trust. The proposed framework 
for precision coffee agriculture combines accurate nutrient shortage diagnosis, expert-
guided treatment recommendations, and explainable artificial intelligence to create a 
practical and dependable decision-support system.

2 Methodology
2.1 Overview of the proposed framework
The proposed approach combines an Explainable Vision Transformer (XViT) for auto -
mated nutrient deficit detection and an expert-defined rule-based module for treat -
ment recommendations in coffee leaves. The framework functions as an end-to-end 
decision-support system, first analyzing coffee leaf images to diagnose nutrient deficien -
cies, and then converting the projected deficiency categories into appropriate treatment 
recommendations based on specified agronomic principles. The input images are pre -
processed and separated into fixed-size patches (16 × 16). These patches are embedded 
with positional encodings before being processed through several transformer encoder 
layers using multi-head self-attention methods. The obtained transformer represen -
tations allow for the efficient extraction of global contextual information and delicate 
visual patterns associated with nutrient insufficiency symptoms. Furthermore, a Grad-
CAM-based explainability module identifies the leaf regions that provide the greatest 
contribution to model predictions, boosting transparency and interpretation. By com -
bining precise diagnosis, expert-guided recommendations, and visual explanations, 
the proposed framework offers a realistic solution for smart agriculture and precision 
coffee nutrition management. For model development and evaluation, the proposed 
framework was assessed using a stratified five-fold cross-validation strategy. In each 
fold, approximately 861 images (72% of the complete dataset) were used for training, 96 
images (8% of the complete dataset; 10% of the training portion) were allocated for inter-
nal validation, and 239 images (20% of the complete dataset) were reserved as an inde -
pendent test set to ensure unbiased performance evaluation. Figure  1 depicts the overall 
design of the proposed framework.

2.2.1 Dataset collection
The main sources of photos of coffee leaves were two publicly accessible datasets. This 
study utilizes the CoLeaf dataset, a publicly available leaf image dataset designed for 
plant disease and nutrient deficiency analysis. In addition to the CoLeaf dataset, this 
study also employs the Plant Disease Classification Merged Dataset, a publicly available 
collection of leaf images compiled from multiple plant disease datasets. This merged 
dataset provides diverse disease categories and variations in illumination and back -
ground, which enhance the robustness of the proposed model. Images with unclear leaf 
conditions or several signs of nutritional stress were not included. As can be seen below, 
the dataset was finally divided into eight classes for nutrient deficiencies and a class for 
health in Table 1.
After merging data sources, the total number of samples became 1196 images, which 
were shuffled and split using a 5-fold cross-validation protocol.
2.2.2 Dataset preparation
First, every image was scaled to the 224 × 224 pixel fixed Vision Transformer input 
resolution. For uniform patch extraction and stable positional encoding in the ViT 
architecture, this resizing step guarantees that all input samples have the same spatial 
dimensions. A two-stage normalization procedure was then used to normalize the pixel 
values. First, each pixel’s intensity was divided by 255 to scale it to the interval [0,1]. Sec-
ond, to standardize feature distributions and speed up training convergence, standard 
ViT normalization based on the ImageNet mean (µ) and standard deviation (σ) was per -
formed per RGB channel.

ples of geometric transformations that mimic the variety of leaf orientation. In order 
to promote spatial invariance and enhance feature-learning under partial occlusion,

2.2.4 Dataset split
The dataset, which included 1,196 coffee leaf photos from two publicly accessible 
sources, was analyzed using a stratified 5-fold cross-validation technique to ensure accu-
rate performance estimates and decrease the danger of overfitting. The entire dataset 
was randomly divided into five about equal-sized folds while keeping the original class 
distribution for all nutrient deficiency categories and the healthy class.
During each cross-validation iteration, one fold was set aside as an independent test 
set, which was only used for the final evaluation of the trained model. The remaining 
four folds were utilized for model development. From the four training folds, 10% of the 
available training data were selected as an internal validation subset (approximately 96 
images, equivalent to 8% of the complete dataset) for hyperparameters optimization, 
early stopping, and model checkpoint selection.
For each fold, the dataset distribution was organized as follows:
 • Training set: Approximately 861 images (72% of the complete dataset).
 • Validation set: Approximately 96 images (8% of the complete dataset; 10% of the 
training portion).
 • Independent test set: Approximately 239 images (20% of the complete dataset).

2.3 Model architecture
The proposed system uses an Explainable Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture to 
detect nutrient deficiencies in coffee leaves, followed by a rule-based treatment recom -
mendation module. The model features a conventional Vision Transformer (ViT) back -
bone that extracts rich, hierarchical feature representations from input coffee leaf images 
using patch embedding and multi-head self-attention techniques. The collected feature 
representation is fed into a fully connected classification head, which predicts the nutri -
tional deficiency class using a Softmax activation function. To address class imbalance in 
the dataset, the classifier is trained using the cross-entropy loss function, with configu -
rable class weighting.
Once the nutrient shortage has been diagnosed, the projected class is passed to an 
expert-defined rule-based recommendation module, which automatically gets the 
appropriate agronomic treatment from a predefined nutrient-treatment mapping based 
on known agricultural norms. This modular design isolates the data-driven image classi -
fication process from the expert knowledge required to prescribe treatments, increasing 
transparency, maintainability, and scientific validity. Furthermore, a Grad-CAM-based 
explainability module is included to show the discriminative regions of coffee leaf pic -
tures that have the greatest influence on the model’s predictions, improving interpret -
ability and facilitating practical decision-making in precision agriculture. The technique

learning rates for each parameter to accelerate convergence and improve stability. 
A batch size of 16 was used to balance computational efficiency and gradient estima -
tion accuracy, and the network was trained for 100 epochs to ensure sufficient learning 
across all nutrient deficiency classes. All experiments were conducted on Google Colab 
using Python 3 and TensorFlow Keras. Data augmentation was applied to enhance data

2.5 Explainability
Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) was used on the proposed 
Explainable Vision Transformer (ViT) model to increase the interpretability of nutrient 
insufficiency predictions [27–29]. Grad-CAM gives visual explanations for classification 
results by highlighting the portions of the input image that contribute most strongly to 
the model’s decision [26].
In this approach, Grad-CAM is applied to the ViT model’s nutrient deficiency classifi -
cation output, resulting in attention heatmaps that identify the relevant regions of coffee 
leaf pictures associated with certain deficiencies. These highlighted locations could cor -
respond to visual characteristics like chlorosis, necrotic patches, discoloration patterns, 
or marginal scorching, all of which are key markers of nutrient deficits.
The resulting explanations improve transparency by helping researchers, agronomists, 
and farmers understand which leaf regions influenced the model’s forecast. Once the 
nutritional shortage has been diagnosed, the appropriate treatment advice is obtained 
using an expert-defined rule-based nutrient-treatment mapping. As a result, the explain-
ability module focuses on evaluating the visual evidence used to detect deficiencies,

2.6 Evaluation metrics
Standard classification metrics were utilized to evaluate the effectiveness of the pro -
posed Explainable Vision Transformer (ViT) framework in terms of nutritional insuf -
ficiency identification. Because treatment suggestions are generated after classification 
using an expert-defined rule-based mapping, no independent model assessment metrics 
are required. Instead, the accuracy and consistency of the suggestion module were tested 
using established nutrient-treatment relationships.
3 Deficiency detection
For the classification of nutrient deficiencies and treatment recommendations, standard 
metrics were computed:
 • Accuracy: the proportion of correctly predicted leaf deficiency classes among all 
samples.
 • Precision: the ratio of correctly predicted positive samples to all predicted positives, 
computed per class to evaluate class-wise reliability.
 • Recall: the ratio of correctly predicted positives to all actual positives, indicating the 
model’s sensitivity to each deficiency.
 • F1-score: the harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced measure 
of performance.
 • Confusion matrix: visualizes the distribution of true versus predicted classes, 
highlighting misclassification patterns and enabling detailed error analysis.
4 Treatment recommendation
The treatment suggestion module was evaluated based on how well the generated rec -
ommendations matched the expert-defined nutrient-treatment mapping. For each 
accurately detected nutrient shortage, the matching treatment was selected from the 
predetermined rule base, ensuring that the recommended intervention adhered to rec -
ognized agronomic standards.
This evaluation methodology assesses the proposed system comprehensively by mea -
suring nutrient deficiency detection accuracy and reliability, as well as confirming the 
rule-based treatment recommendation component’s practical utility. ViT classification 
performance, Grad-CAM-based interpretability, and expert-guided recommendations 
all illustrate the framework’s appropriateness for precision coffee nutrition management.

5.1.2 Treatment suggestions
The proposed rule-based treatment suggestion module was developed as a decision-sup-
port tool following the Explainable Vision Transformer (XViT) classification stage. The 
module does not participate in model training; rather, it receives the nutrient-deficiency 
class predicted by the XViT model and generates a suitable treatment recommendation 
based on specified agricultural criteria. The evaluation was designed to assess the rule-
based system’s capacity to turn nutrient deficiency forecasts into appropriate treatment 
suggestions. The evaluation centered on:
(1) Recommendation accuracy
(2) Rule coverage
(3) Rule consistency
(4) Error analysis
Unlike the earlier version of this work, treatment advice is not presented as a separate 
machine learning problem. The Explainable Vision Transformer (XViT) solely identifies 
deficiencies. The predicted nutrient shortage class is then passed to a transparent rule-
based inference engine, which pulls the appropriate treatment recommendation from a 
predetermined agricultural knowledge base.
The recommendation module uses an IF–THEN rule structure.
The general rule representation is: 
 IF Ci → THEN Ri (1)
Where:
 • Ci represents the detected nutrient deficiency class.
 • Ri represents the recommended treatment.
The recommendation criteria were created based on known coffee nutrient management 
recommendations and confirmed by agricultural specialists to verify that each treat -
ment suggestion corresponds to conventional agronomic practice for fixing the reported 
nutrient shortfall. The knowledge base includes one predefined suggestion for each 
Page 18 of 32
Adugna Discover Artificial Intelligence (2026) 6:1055 
nutrient class, providing explicit and consistent decision help. The developed knowledge 
base consists of nine treatment rules corresponding to the nine coffee leaf classes.
5.1.3 Rule knowledge bas
The rule knowledge base was created to establish the link between identified coffee leaf 
nutrient deficiencies and treatment recommendations. The knowledge base contains a 
set of preconfigured IF-THEN rules based on typical agricultural nutrient management 
strategies. Each rule offers expert knowledge that links a specific nutrient deficiency con-
dition indicated by the Explainable Vision Transformer (XViT) model to a suitable cor -
rective treatment solution. The goal of this rule-based method is to create a visible and 
interpretable recommendation process, allowing users to comprehend how a detected 
defect leads to a certain treatment solution.
The proposed system has nine rules that correlate to the nine classification catego -
ries: boron (B), calcium (Ca), healthy, iron (Fe), potassium (K), magnesium (Mg), manga-
nese (Mn), nitrogen (N), and phosphorus (P). When the XViT model predicts a nutrient 
deficiency class, the rule inference engine searches the knowledge base and applies the 
matching rule to provide the proposed treatment. For example, if the projected condi -
tion is nitrogen deficit, the rule engine will use the rule IF nutrient deficiency = Nitrogen 
THEN propose Urea. Similarly, potassium insufficiency triggers the rule that recom -
mends potassium sulfate, whereas a healthy leaf categorization yields no treatment 
prescription. This rule-based knowledge representation ensures that treatment recom -
mendations are consistent, understandable, and domain-specific, without the need for 
extra model training. The rule-based mapping depicted in Table 6.
The current knowledge base assigns one commonly recommended treatment to each 
nutrient deficiency based on expert consultation. The objective is to provide transpar -
ent and interpretable decision support rather than optimize treatment selection. Future 
work will extend the knowledge base by incorporating alternative treatment options, 
dosage recommendations, soil properties, coffee cultivars, and nutrient deficiency 
severity.

5.3.6 Evaluation of the rule-based treatment recommendation module
The contribution of the rule-based recommendation component was assessed indepen -
dently because it is a deterministic decision-support module rather than a trainable pre -
diction model. The XViT classifier’s expected nutrient shortage class was submitted to 
the IF-THEN rule inference engine, which created the appropriate treatment suggestion 
from the expert-defined knowledge base.
The rule-based module achieved:
 • 100% rule coverage, because all nine nutrient deficiency categories contained 
corresponding treatment rules.
 • 100% rule consistency, because identical inputs always generated identical treatment 
recommendations.
 • 93.72% end-to-end recommendation success rate, where incorrect recommendations 
resulted from XViT classification errors rather than rule inference failures.
 • Cohen’s Kappa = 0.93, indicating almost perfect agreement between agricultural 
experts and system-generated recommendations.
These results demonstrate that the rule-based recommendation module successfully 
converts model predictions into transparent and interpretable agricultural guidance.

statistically significant, paired statistical tests were performed using the accuracy values 
obtained from the five-fold cross-validation results.
The comparisons demonstrated statistically significant improvements:
 • XViT vs. ResNet-50: t = 20.99, p < 0.01.
 • XViT vs. EfficientNet-B4: t = 189.74, p < 0.01.
 • XViT vs. ConvNeXt: t = 48.72, p < 0.01.
The results confirm that the performance improvements achieved by the proposed XViT 
were statistically meaningful.

6 Discussion
The proposed XViT model routinely outperforms cutting-edge baselines like ResNet-50, 
EfficientNet-B4, and ConvNeXt in detecting nutrient deficits and suggesting practical 
treatment for coffee leaves. The proposed XViT successfully captures both local and 
global aspects of the input images for accurate nutrient deficiency classification. Subse -
quently, the classified deficiency is mapped to its related treatment using a deterministic 
rule-based engine. The vision transformer architecture contributes to enhanced perfor -
mance by modeling long-range dependencies across leaf images, which is particularly 
beneficial for complex patterns of nutrient stress that may not be captured by traditional 
CNN-based models.
Crucially, the interpretability of the predictions is made possible by the explainable 
nature of XViT, which highlights leaf regions most suggestive of nutritional deficits 
and is visualized by Grad-CAM and attention maps. This openness helps agronomists 
make better-informed and effective treatment decisions by validating the model’s deci -
sion-making process and offering useful advice. The baseline comparison study with 
ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B4, and ConvNeXt highlights the benefits of XViT in utilizing 
transformer-based global context.
Beyond quantitative superiority, XViT demonstrates promise in practical agricultural 
applications by bridging the gap between AI-driven predictions and field-level decision-
making through the combination of high accuracy and Explainability. Future research 
could concentrate on assessing how well the model applies to other coffee cultivars, 
environmental circumstances, and leaf developmental phases. Predictive power could be 
further increased by including multispectral imagery or temporal growth data. Overall, 
the findings show that XViT is an effective and comprehensible framework for manag -
ing the nutrients in coffee leaves, offering practical insights and a standard for upcoming 
models in precision agriculture.

6.1 Dataset size and overfitting considerations
Although the proposed model performs well, the dataset employed in this work is quite 
limited, with just 1,196 photos compared to larger benchmarks. Such minimal data may 
raise the likelihood of overfitting, in which the model learns dataset-specific patterns 
rather than generalizable characteristics.
To address this issue, numerous solutions were implemented. First, substantial data 
augmentation techniques such as rotation, flipping, scaling, and brightness manipula -
tion were used to artificially increase data diversity. Second, transfer learning was used, 
which enabled the model to use pretrained weights from large-scale datasets, enhanc -
ing generalization. Third, regularization techniques like dropout were used to keep the 
model from memorizing training data. To ensure robustness, the model’s performance 
was assessed using distinct training and validation splits.
Despite these efforts, the limited dataset size remains a constraint, and the model’s 
generalization capability may be further improved by incorporating larger and more 
diverse datasets collected under varying environmental conditions. Future work will 
focus on expanding the dataset and validating the model across different geographical 
regions.
6.2 Limitations
Despite its high performance, the proposed framework has a few restrictions. The data -
set’s quantity and diversity are restricted, which may impact generalization across dif -
ferent coffee types, geographies, and environmental conditions. The current model is 
based mostly on leaf images and does not take into account additional parameters such 
as soil conditions, climate, or plant growth stage, all of which influence nutrient deficits. 
Furthermore, the rule-based suggestion module is based on expert-defined knowledge 
and may require adaptation to diverse agricultural practices. Additional field validation 
Page 30 of 32
Adugna Discover Artificial Intelligence (2026) 6:1055 
with larger and more diverse datasets is required to increase robustness and real-world 
deployment capability.
6.3 Practical implications and deployment
The proposed XViT-based approach has the potential to improve precision coffee grow -
ing by delivering quick and interpretable nutrient shortage diagnosis and expert-guided 
fertilizer recommendations. In real-world applications, farmers or agricultural extension 
workers can use mobile devices or field cameras to collect photos of coffee leaves, which 
the model subsequently processes to indicate potential nutrient deficits. The projected 
deficiency class is then transformed into a suitable fertilizer recommendation using the 
rule-based knowledge module, allowing for timely and informed nutrient management 
decisions.
The incorporation of Grad-CAM-based explanations boosts user trust by showing the 
leaf regions that drive the model’s predictions, allowing agronomists to confirm whether 
the reported symptoms match meaningful deficiency patterns. This combination of auto-
mated diagnosis, transparent reasoning, and expert-based recommendations decreases 
reliance on manual inspection while promoting more efficient crop monitoring.
The proposed framework can be deployed as a lightweight mobile or cloud-based deci-
sion assistance application. A mobile-based system would allow for field-level diagno -
sis in areas with limited access to agricultural experts, whereas a cloud-based platform 
could provide centralized monitoring, model updates, and integration with additional 
agricultural data sources such as soil properties, environmental conditions, and farm 
management records. Future deployment studies should test the model in a variety of 
field situations, coffee types, and geographical regions to determine its robustness and 
scalability.
7 Conclusion and future work
7.1 Conclusion
This study proposed an explainable Vision Transformer (XViT)-based framework for 
automated coffee leaf nutrient deficiency classification and treatment recommen -
dation. The framework integrates class-wise data augmentation, an XViT classifier, 
Grad-CAM explainability, and a rule-based recommendation module to provide accu -
rate, transparent, and practical decision support for precision coffee farming. Experi -
mental results demonstrated that the proposed framework consistently outperformed 
ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B4, and ConvNeXt under identical experimental conditions. 
The proposed XViT achieved a classification accuracy of 94 ± 0.37% on the original 
dataset and 96 ± 0.29% on the augmented dataset, showing that data augmentation 
effectively improved class balance and enhanced the recognition of minority nutrient 
deficiency classes. Statistical analysis further confirmed that the performance improve -
ments over the baseline models were statistically significant ( p < 0.001). In addition, 
Grad-CAM visualizations demonstrated that the model focused on agronomical mean -
ingful symptom regions, improving the interpretability of its predictions. The rule-
based recommendation module also provided consistent and expert-aligned treatment 
recommendations, achieving 100% rule coverage, 100% rule consistency, and a 93.72% 
end-to-end recommendation success rate. Overall, the proposed framework offers an 
Page 31 of 32
Adugna Discover Artificial Intelligence (2026) 6:1055 
accurate, explainable, and reliable decision-support system with strong potential to sup -
port sustainable coffee nutrient management and precision agriculture.
7.2 Future work
Future studies will focus on enhancing the robustness and practical application of the 
proposed framework. A wider and more diverse coffee leaf dataset collected from dif -
ferent geographic regions and climatic conditions will be used to boost the model’s gen -
eralization capabilities. Additional nutrient deficiencies, plant diseases, and combined 
stress conditions can also be incorporated to develop a more comprehensive crop health 
diagnosis system.
Furthermore, the framework can be expanded to include environmental parameters 
such as soil nutrient information, meteorological conditions, and IoT sensor data, result-
ing in more context-aware treatment recommendations. Future research may look into 
advanced explainable AI techniques, such as SHAP and LIME, to supplement Grad-
CAM and improve model transparency. Finally, adopting the proposed framework as a 
lightweight mobile or cloud-based application would allow for real-time nutrient defi -
ciency diagnosis and treatment recommendations, making it more useful for precision 
coffee farming and sustainable agricultural management.
