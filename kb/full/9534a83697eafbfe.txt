Quantifying groundwater recharge in data-scarce volcanic islands is essential for sustainable water management, yet challenged by limited monitoring networks and the lack of perennial surface water bodies. This study introduces a potentially transferable grid-based SWAT+ framework, an innovative adaptation of semi-distributed hydrological modeling, to better capture spatiotemporal hydrogeological and climatic heterogeneity in such environments. The framework integrates satellite-derived evapotranspiration for physically consistent parameterization, employing Sobol global sensitivity analysis followed by a two-stage calibration (hydrological ratios and remote sensing-based). Demonstrated on El Hierro (Canary Islands) as a representative case, the model uncovers significant recharge variability influenced predominantly by land use, with secondary controls from soil and topography, yielding mean annual recharge of 58 mm·yr -1 (P05–P95: 31–89 mm·yr -1 ), substantially lower than prior local assessments. By leveraging remote sensing and advanced calibration, this approach enhances accuracy in semi-arid volcanic settings and is potentially applicable to other semi-arid volcanic contexts, enabling resilient water resource planning amid climate change uncertainties.
