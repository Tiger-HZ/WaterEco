Background: Prolonged recreational screen exposure adversely affects health-related quality of life (HRQoL), yet developmental shifts in gender-differentiated susceptibility across youth remain understudied. Objective: This study aimed to investigate both physical and psychosocial HRQoL in relation to daily recreational screen exposure among children and adolescents, asking in particular whether gender differences depend on developmental stage. Methods: In this cross-sectional study, data were obtained for 1,505 students using the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory 4.0: 678 children aged 7&amp;ndash;12 years were assessed by parent-proxy report, and 827 adolescents aged 13&amp;ndash;18 years completed the self-report version.
