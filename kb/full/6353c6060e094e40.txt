Ca(OH) 2 treatment has been recently used to remediate water quality problems associated with eutrophication in lakes on the Boreal Plain of western Canada. This study examines how Ca(OH) 2 treatment affects the survival of Hyalella azteca and larvae of Chironomus spp. in laboratory microcosms and the abundance and seasonal dynamics of macroinvertebrates in two eutrophic hardwater lakes. Macroinvertebrate data for three reference lakes sampled during the same time period are also presented. Additions of 225 and 295 g Ca(OH) 2 ·m -2 (74 and 107 mg·L -1 ) to two lakes resulted in lake water pH remaining within its natural range (&lt;10) and had no discernable effects on macroinvertebrate density or biomass up to 2 years after treatment.
