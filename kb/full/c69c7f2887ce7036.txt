Water is essential to all forms of life. For humans, it is used for agriculture, cooking, drinking, recreation, transportation, industry, power generation, and many other purposes. Therefore, there are increasing shortages of water due to the high demand, lack of precipitation, mismanagement of water resources, and river diversions. The UN indicates that more than 2.2 billion people lack access to safely managed drinking water, and 80% of wastewater is returned to the ecosystem without treatment or reuse. Therefore, there is an urgent need to change how water is managed globally to deliver safe water to billions. Climate change is adding new pressures on water resources. In particular, Goal 6 aims to achieve universal and equitable access to safe and affordable drinking water for all and to improve water quality by reducing pollution, increasing recycling and safe reuse. Sources of contamination must be managed and mitigated in a sustainable manner for the future. Clean water is essential to meet the increasing water demands. Therefore, there is an urgent need to ensure water quality that is accessible and sustainable in a changing world. This chapter focuses on the hydrologic cycle of water, pollutants found in water, and effects of climate change on water.
