Abstract The dissemination of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) in sewer systems poses a potential threat to public health. However, it is currently unclear how hydrogen sulfide (H2S) in anaerobic sewers affects plasmid-mediated ARG transfer. Here, we evaluated the effects of environmentally relevant concentrations (1–100 μmol·L–1) of H2S on ARG transfer across different sewer microbial communities. The results demonstrated that H2S significantly enhanced the transfer frequency of plasmid pKJK5 in sediment-derived (2.06–3.06-fold), sewage-derived (1.50–2.81-fold), and bioaerosol-derived (2.42–4.68-fold) recipient communities in standardized liquid anaerobic conjugation assays.
