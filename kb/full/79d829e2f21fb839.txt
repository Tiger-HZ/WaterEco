Abstract Water resource development and drought have altered river flow regimes, increasing average flood return intervals across floodplains in the Murray–Darling Basin, Australia, causing health declines in riparian river red gum ( Eucalyptus camaldulensis ) forests and woodlands. Environmental flow allocations helped to alleviate water stress during the recent Millennium Drought (1997–2010); however, quantification of the flood frequency required to support healthy E . camaldulensis communities is still needed. We quantified water requirements of E . camaldulensis for 2 years across a flood gradient (trees inundated at frequencies of 1:2, 1:5 and 1:10 years) at Yanga National Park, New South Wales, to help inform management decision‐making and design of environmental flows.
