Abstract Summer often brings harmful algal blooms to ponds, lakes, and even the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool. These blooms are caused by warm, shallow water with high levels of nutrients, especially phosphorus (P). Researchers from the USDA Agricultural Research Service developed phosphorus removal structures, called P‐Traps, to filter phosphorus from water before it fuels algae growth. Originally designed for agricultural drainage, this technology could also help clean urban ponds. Unlike current treatments that kill algae but leave nutrients behind, P‐Traps remove the phosphorus that causes blooms.
