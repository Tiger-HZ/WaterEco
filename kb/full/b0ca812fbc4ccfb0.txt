Abstract Various stressors impair growth in livestock, with heat stress (HS) being among the most extensively researched due to its detrimental effects on all aspects of animal performance. Beyond reductions in feed intake, HS independently disrupts numerous endocrine, physiological, metabolic, circulatory, and immune parameters. These alterations are interconnected over time, centering on impaired integrity of the gastrointestinal tract (GIT) barrier. Increased GIT permeability permits leakage of luminal antigens into circulation, triggering immune activation with both localized and widespread inflammation.
