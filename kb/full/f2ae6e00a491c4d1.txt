Cold-water corals are important for maintaining biodiversity and ecosystem functioning in the deep ocean, yet their response to oceanic sub-annual variation remains largely unknown. Here, radiocarbon-based calendar year and growth ring counts of organic nodes of bamboo corals (Family Keratoisididae) from the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans reveal growth ring formation rates of ~2-11 rings yr -1 , closely matching the periodicities of enhanced particulate organic carbon (POC) flux events (monthly POC flux exceeding median values) at corresponding water depths. Given the adaptation of these corals to the oligotrophic deep ocean, our results suggest that enhanced POC fluxes most likely play a key role in growth ring formation.
