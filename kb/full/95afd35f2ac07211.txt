This study evaluates the environmental and logistical feasibility of replacing calcined clay (CC) with sugarcane bagasse ash (SCBA) as a supplementary cementitious material (SCM) in Brazilian Portland-blended cement, using an integrated approach that combines life cycle assessment (LCA) and geospatial analysis. Two formulations of Portland-blended cement were assessed: one containing 14% CC as the pozzolanic material, and another in which this pozzolanic fraction was fully replaced by SCBA. LCA results were used to determine the maximum transport distance over which SCBA can be delivered to cement plants without compromising the environmental benefits achieved relative to CC. Subsequently, all cement plants and sugarcane mills in Brazil were mapped and spatially integrated with these transport limits. Replacing CC with SCBA reduced environmental impacts in every category evaluated, reflecting the advantages of using an agricultural waste in place of an energy-intensive pozzolan. SCBA can be transported up to 2665 km considering the global warming category and up to 669 km for fine particulate matter emissions while maintaining net environmental benefits over CC. Spatial analysis revealed that from an environmental perspective, most Brazilian cement plants (91%) could incorporate SCBA as an SCM, and 70% are located within 300 km of a sugarcane mill. Annual CO 2 equivalent emissions could be reduced by approximately 1 Mt if 70% of Brazilian cement plants incorporate SCBA. Overall, the wide availability of SCBA and its environmental advantages support its large-scale adoption in the Brazilian cement industry.
