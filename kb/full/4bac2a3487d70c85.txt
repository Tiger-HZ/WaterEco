Online surveys are increasingly the norm in public opinion research. Self-administered internet surveys present opportunities, but also specific risks for data quality. In search of solutions, scholars are returning to commitment as one way of encouraging participants to provide accurate responses. This article provides an extensive study of commitment as a potential method for improving data quality in the context of opt-in online surveys. Theoretically, I argue that the effectiveness of commitment in motivating good conduct depends on respondents’ material and immaterial cost-benefit calculations.
