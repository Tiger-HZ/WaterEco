Carnivorous plants such as Sarracenia, Drosera, and Utricularia are known to reduce investment in carnivorous traits when nutrients are supplied through their roots and shoots. We hypothesized that Cephalotus follicularis would respond similarly by reducing pitcher production when nutrients are available in the growth medium. To test this hypothesis, plants were grown for six months with varying amounts of Osmocote (14-14-14). We measured (1) number of pitchers, (2) number of leaves, and (3) crown size before and after treatment, and compared the differences among treatments. Contrary to our expectations, Osmocote application did not significantly affect pitcher production or crown size.
