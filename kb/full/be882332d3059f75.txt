The spatiotemporal resolution of rain-gauge networks fundamentally constrains the reliability of precipitation depth–duration–frequency estimates (DDFs), yet the uncertainty stemming from such restrictions remains unquantified in practice. We evaluated these constraints for short-duration extreme precipitation, using 1-min data from a high-density German network (277 stations) as the reference dataset. We constructed ensembles of lower-resolution networks by progressively reducing station density and aggregating rainfall to 15-min intervals, simulating typical operational networks. Uncertainty was propagated using hierarchical bootstrapping and empirical Bayesian mixture frameworks, closely aligning with U.S. NOAA’s Atlas 14 methods.
