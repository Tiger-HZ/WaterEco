Abstract 
Surface water quality is influenced by natural geochemical processes, urban activities, industrial discharges, 
agricultural runoff and microbiological contamination. The present study evaluates the physicochemical, heavy-metal, 
microbiological and organic-pollutant characteristics of four surface-water samples collected from the investigated 
study area. The analyzed parameters included pH, total dissolved solids (TDS), total hardness, chloride, sulphate, 
turbidity, colour, fluoride, iron, aluminium, lead, cadmium, manganese, total coliform and Escherichia coli. Total 
coliform was detected in all four samples, with Sample 3 recording the highest concentration of 80 MPN/100 mL, 
whereas E. coli was reported as <2 MPN/100 mL in all samples. Pesticides, PCBs, PAHs, DDT derivatives, endosulfan 
compounds, aldrin, dieldrin and related organic contaminants were reported below detection limits. The results 
indicate that the investigated surface-water samples are not suitable for direct drinking without appropriate treatment. 
The proposed treatment approach should be experimentally validated through analysis and post-treatment water-
quality assessment.

1. Introduction 
Surface-water resources are important for domestic, 
agricultural, industrial and ecological applications. 
However, their quality can deteriorate because of 
urbanization, industrial activities, agricultural runoff, 
wastewater discharge, erosion, mineral dissolution and 
microbiological contamination. Continuous 
monitoring of surface-water quality is therefore 
essential for determining its suitability for intended 
uses and identifying the major contaminants requiring 
treatment. Physicochemical parameters such as pH, 
TDS, hardness, chloride, sulphate, turbidity and colour 
provide important information about the general 
condition of surface water. High TDS and chloride 
concentrations indicate substantial dissolved ionic 
content, while elevated hardness reflects the presence 
of calcium and magnesium salts. Turbidity and colour 
are important indicators of suspended and dissolved 
materials and can affect the aesthetic and treatment 
characteristics of water. Heavy metals are of particular 
concern because some metals can persist in aquatic 
environments and accumulate in biological systems. 
Lead and cadmium are especially important 
contaminants because of their toxicity and potential 
human-health effects. Iron, manganese and aluminium 
may also deteriorate water quality when present at 
elevated concentrations. Microbiological 
contamination represents another important dimension 
of surface-water quality. The occurrence of total 
coliform organisms indicates possible contamination 
from environmental and anthropogenic sources. 
Although the absence of detectable E. coli provides 
useful information, it does not necessarily establish 
complete microbiological safety. 
Microbiological assessment shows presence of total 
coliforms in all the four sampling points of 
Kosasthalaiyar River under study with concentration 
varying between 6 and 80 MPN/100 mL. Sample S3 
showed the highest concentration (80 MPN/100 mL) 
and pointed out a hot spot of localized microbiological 
contamination. Total coliforms in surface water may 
be associated with sewage discharges, domestic 
wastewater, animal wastes, surface runoff and other 
environmental sources; however, total coliforms

should not be considered as conclusive evidence of 
fecal contamination alone as a number of members 
within the group may be found naturally occurring in 
aquatic environments (Edberg et al., 2000; World 
Health Organization, 2022). In the current study E. 
coli was reported as <2 MPN/100 mL in all four 
samples indicating that detectable E. coli 
contamination was not detected at the analytical 
reporting limit used. In 2020, an earlier environmental 
assessment of the Chennai Peripheral Ring Road 
recorded 23 MPN/100 mL total coliform and 12 
MPN/100 mL E. coli at a sampling point in the 
Kosasthalaiyar River (Chennai Peripheral Ring Road 
EIA, 2020). The present maximum concentration at S3 
(80 MPN/100 mL) surpasses the total coliform value 
reported earlier; however, the difference should not be 
interpreted as a temporal increase as the sampling 
locations, dates, seasons, hydrological conditions and 
analytical circumstances may differ. CPCB (2008) has 
set the limit of ≤50 MPN/100 mL for total coliform for 
Class A water and ≤500 MPN/100 mL for Class B 
water under the Designated Best Use criteria. S3 
therefore surpassed the Class A criterion, and S1, S2 
and S4 fell below the criterion. However, compliance 
with the total-coliform criterion alone does not 
indicate drinking-water suitability, which requires 
consideration of physicochemical, chemical and 
microbiological parameters together. Earlier studies 
on Indian river systems have also shown large spatial 
and seasonal variation in microbial contamination and 
associated high indicator bacteria with settlement, 
sewage related inputs and surface runoff (Pradhan et 
al., 2016). Therefore, the present findings are best 
interpreted as evidence of localized microbiological 
stress on water quality rather than definitive evidence 
of fecal pollution or a basin -wide temporal 
deterioration. However, the results support the need 
for continued spatial and seasonal monitoring and 
incorporation of an appropriate disinfection barrier 
into any treatment system intended for domestic or 
potable applications (World Health Organization, 
2022). 
Materials and Methods 
Surface water samples were collected from selected 
locations in the investigated study area to examine the 
overall physicochemical, heavy -metal and 
microbiological quality of the water. Four 
representative sampling locations were selected taking 
into account differences in land use, anthropogenic 
activity, drainage characteristics and potential sources 
of contamination. Sampling was carried out with clean 
sterilized sampling containers suitable for the 
respective analysis. 
Samples were collected in clean polyethylene bottles 
for physicochemical and chemical analysis and in 
sterile contamination free bottles for microbiological 
analysis. The sampling containers were labeled 
properly with the site of sampling, date and the 
identification number of the sample. Microbiological 
samples were collected aseptically, avoiding contact 
with the inner surface of the container or its cap. The 
collected samples were immediately transported to the 
laboratory under cooled conditions and anal yzed 
within the recommended holding period. Samples 
were checked for visual color, odor and suspended 
matter prior to laboratory analysis. Physicochemical 
analysis samples were stored under controlled 
laboratory conditions before analysis. Samples for 
heavy-metal determination were properly preserved to 
prevent adsorption, precipitation and other changes 
during storage. Microbiological samples were 
transported under refrigerated conditions and 
processed as soon as possible after collection. All 
sampling and glassware for microbiological analysis 
was autoclaved before use. All the reagents were of 
analytical grade and de-ionized water was used in the 
whole investigation. Standard laboratory procedures 
were followed for the preparation of reage nts, 
calibration of instruments and analysis of the selected 
water quality parameters. 
3.1 Surface-Water Sampling 
Four surface-water samples designated S1, S2, S3 and 
S4 were considered for the present investigation. 
Samples were collected from the selected surface -
water locations within the study area and subjected to 
physicochemical, chemical and microbiological 
analysis. 
The samples were analyzed for major water-quality 
parameters, heavy metals, microbiological indicators 
and selected organic contaminants.

E. coli was reported as <2 MPN/100 mL in all four 
surface-water samples. However, total coliform 
bacteria were detected in every sample. Sample 3 
recorded the highest total-coliform concentration of 80 
MPN/100 mL, substantially higher than the other 
sampling locations. This indicates a localized 
microbiological contamination hotspot. Although E. 
coli was not detected above the reported analytical 
threshold, the presence of total coliforms indicates that 
the surface water should not be considered 
microbiologically safe for direct consumption without 
appropriate treatment and disinfection. 
3.5 Organic Pollutants and Pesticides 
The investigated surface-water samples were analyzed 
for selected pesticides and organic pollutants, 
including PCBs, PAHs, DDT derivatives, endosulfan 
compounds, aldrin, dieldrin and related organic 
contaminants. All analyzed pesticides, PCBs, PAHs, 
DDT derivatives, endosulfan compounds, aldrin, 
dieldrin and related organic pollutants were reported 
as Below Detection Limit (BDL). 
Interpretation 
The absence of detectable concentrations of the 
analyzed organic pollutants indicates that these 
compounds were not detected at or above the 
analytical detection limits in the investigated surface-
water samples. Therefore, based on the available 
analytical results, detectable pesticide and persistent 
organic pollutant contamination was not a dominant 
component of the observed surfa ce-water quality 
deterioration. 
The BDL should be interpreted according to the 
analytical detection limit of each method. It does not 
necessarily indicate absolute absence of the 
compound.

The overall assessment demonstrates that the four 
samples exhibit different contamination profiles. 
Sample 1 is dominated by dissolved mineral 
contamination, Sample 2 by turbidity, colour and iron, 
Sample 3 by microbiological contamination and 
selected heavy metals, and Sample 4 by salinity, 
hardness and lead. 
5.7 Microbiological Contamination 
The total-coliform concentration ranged from 6 to 80 
MPN/100 mL. 
Sample 3 recorded the highest concentration of 80 
MPN/100 mL, which was substantially higher than 
the concentrations observed in S1, S2 and S4. The 
detection of total coliform in all four samples indicates 
microbiological contamination of the investigated 
surface-water system. Consequently, any proposed 
treatment system intended to produce potable water 
should incorporate an appropriate disinfection stage. 
5.8 Organic Pollutants 
All analyzed pesticides and selected persistent organic 
pollutants were reported as BDL. 
This indicates that detectable organic contamination 
was not a major component of the measured water-
quality deterioration. The dominant observed 
problems were instead associated with dissolved salts, 
hardness, turbidity, colour, selected heavy metals and 
microbiological contamination.

Pesticide and Organic Pollutants Contamination 
 
The analyzed surface-water samples were screened for 
a number of selected pesticides and persistent organic 
pollutants (POPs) such as polychlorinated biphenyls 
(PCBs), polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), 
DDT and its derivatives, endosulfan compounds, 
aldrin and dieldrin. all analyzed compounds were 
reported as below respective analytical detection limits 
(BDL) for tested samples.The BDL results indicate 
that none of the organic contaminants studied were 
detected at or above their respective analytical 
detection limits when using the analytical conditions 
described. Thus, within the scope and sensitivity of the 
analytical procedures used, detectable pesticide and 
selected persistent organic-pollutant contamination 
was not a major contributor to the observed decline in 
surface-water quality. This result is particularly 
significant when considered together with the 
physicochemical and inorganic contaminant results. 
The main water quality problems in the present study 
were associated with high dissolved solids, hardness, 
chloride, turbidity, color, some heavy metals and 
microbiological indicators. Hence, the observed 
degradation appears to be more associated with the 
dissolved inorganic constituents, suspended matter, 
metal contamination and microbial indicators rather 
than the organic pollutants selected and studied. 
 
 
The BDL should not be interpreted as evidence for the 
absolute absence of corresponding compounds. A 
BDL result signifies that the concentration of the 
specific compound was less than the analytical 
detection capability for the particular compound and 
method. Differences in extraction efficiency, matrix 
effects, analytical sensitivity and compound-specific 
detection limits may affect the reported result. Thus, 
the organic-pollutant results should be interpreted 
against the individual method detection limits 
(MDLs/LODs). Reporting the numerical detection 
limit for each analyte would be a further improvement 
of the analytical transparency of the study. 
 
Overall, the screening for organic contaminants gave 
valuable evidence that the selected pesticides and 
POPs were absent in the samples investigated in 
measurable quantities, while the more prominent 
water-quality issues were related to physicochemical, 
inorganic and microbiological parameters.

the river is now more contaminated as the sampling 
conditions and locations are not necessarily the same.

Microbiological assessment showed that total 
coliforms were present in all the Kosasthalaiyar River 
sampling sites studied and ranged from 6 to 80 
MPN/100 mL. Sample S3 showed a steep increase to 
80 MPN/100 mL which suggested a localized hot spot 
of microbiological contamination. Independent 
surface-water monitoring for the Chennai Peripheral 
Ring Road EIA reported total-coliform concentrations 
of 23 MPN/100 mL in the Kosasthalaiyar River in 
2020, corroborating the presence of microbial 
indicator organisms within this river system 
previously. (ES) The current S3 concentration is 
higher than this previously reported value, but the 
difference should not be interpreted as a temporal 
increase without an equivalent sampling location and 
seasonal observations. The CPCB designated-best-use 
criterion for Class A water is a total -coliform 
concentration of ≤50 MPN/100 mL. S3 is above the 
designated-best-use criterion for Class A Water while 
S1, S2 and S4 are below the designated -best-use 
criterion for Class A Water. (Central Pollution Control 
Board) However, total coliform alone cannot be used 
to assess microbiological quality and the occurrence of 
total coliforms should be interpreted along with E. 
coli, physicochemical parameters and other indicators 
of anthropogenic pollution.
