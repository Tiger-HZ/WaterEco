The systematic literature review on evapotranspiration in agricultural systems provides a comprehensive analysis of the hydrological cycle and water balance for water use in irrigated agriculture. This thesis aims to conduct a systematic literature review focused on the importance of evapotranspiration for irrigation use and management in various agricultural systems. The study identified key publications worldwide across different scientific platforms, grouping them based on each question established in the systematic literature review: (D1) How is the water balance and hydrological cycle structured for water use in irrigated agriculture? (D2) What are the methods for measuring evapotranspiration and their primary applications? (D3) What is the importance of evapotranspiration for irrigated and non-irrigated agricultural crops? A total of 9,380 articles were retrieved, of which 327 were selected for analysis. After reanalysis, an additional 26 articles were added through Snowballing techniques to complement the research. The study highlights the structured approach to understanding evapotranspiration, methods for measuring it, and its importance for both irrigated and non-irrigated crops. The chronological distribution of research works reflects a growing interest in evapotranspiration since the turn of the millennium, with contributions from various countries worldwide.
