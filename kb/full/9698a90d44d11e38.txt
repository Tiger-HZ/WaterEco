The Kura–Araz river system is central to Azerbaijan’s water security, irrigated agriculture, settlements, biodiversity and regional economic resilience. This study integrates two educational research projects that examined river pollution, declining water availability, biodiversity pressure and practical responses from complementary perspectives. The source materials were synthesized with peer-reviewed and institutional evidence and interpreted through water-security and green-economy frameworks. The combined evidence identifies a pressure chain that begins with transboundary and domestic wastewater, industrial and mining emissions, agricultural runoff, solid waste, flow regulation and climate-related water scarcity.
