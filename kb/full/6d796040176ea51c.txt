Abstract 
The dicarbonyls glyoxal (Gly) and methylglyoxal (Mgly) are key tracers for the oxidation of volatile organic 
compounds (VOCs) in the atmosphere , but their atmospheric chemistry in remote forest environment s are 
not well understood. A study was carried out during Jul. 31- Nov. 5 of 2016 at the summit of Mt. Tianjing 
(1,690m.a.s.l.), a remote mountaintop site in southern China , to measure the levels of Gly and Mgly and 
explore their sources and fate. During the study period, the average mixing ratios of Gly and Mgly were 
509±31 pptv and 340±32 pptv, respectively, with the Gly/Mgly ratios averaging 1.8±0.2. Both the 
dycarbonyl concentrations and the Gly/Mgly ratios were significantly higher than those observed in other 
background sites. Production yield calculations and meteorological data analysis indicate that high levels of 
Gly and Mgly observed at the study site were largely a combined result of rapid in -situ formation and 
regional transport by prevailing winds. On average, in-situ formation from precursors is estimated to account 
for 67% of the observed Mgly and about 9% of the observed Gly. There were significant changes in Gly and 
Mgly mixing ratios among different time periods when air masses from different source regions dominated, 
indicating contribution of regional transport to the observed dicarbo nyl mixing ratios at the study site . 
Biogenic emissions in eastern China and a nthropogenic emissions in the Pearl River Delta region were the 
two main sources responsible for the dicarbonyls observed at the site during most of the sampling period, 
but large-scale biomass burning in central China was also important in the late autumn, as supported by a 
backward trajectory analysi s of fire spot data and the identification of biomass burning tracers . This study 
provides insights into the background atmospheric chemistry and the impact of biogenic and anthropogenic 
sources on the dicarbonyls speciation. 
Keywords: glyoxal, methylglyoxal, high-elevation mountain, dicarbonyls, regional transport

1 Introduction 
Glyoxal (CHOCHO, Gly) and methylglyoxal ( CH3COCHO, Mgly), the two smallest bi -functional carbonyl 
organic compounds, are ubiquitous intermediates in the troposphere and p lay an important role in 
atmospheric chemistry (Fu et al., 2008 ). They are key tracers for the photochemical oxidations of many 
natural and anthropogenic volatile organic compounds (VOCs) , thus acting as novel indicators for active 
photochemistry on global scales (Spaulding et al., 2003 ). Gly and Mgly , being highly reactiv e in the 
atmosphere, contribute greatly to the formation of free radicals, ozone (O3), and peroxyacylnitrates (PANs) 
primarily through photolysis (62~71%) and photo-oxidation with OH and NO3 radicals (11~14%), affecting 
the oxidation capacity of the atmosphere (Fu et al., 2 008; Munger et al., 1995 ). In addition, several studies 
have suggested that gas/particle partitioning of dicarbonyls and their oxidation or oligomerization in 
aqueous aerosols and clouds could be a significant source of secondary organic aerosols ( SOA) due to their 
high water-solubility (Liggio et al., 2005; Lim et al., 2013; Ortiz-Montalvo et al., 2016; Schwier et al., 2010). 
The dicarbonyls are also important precursors for the formation of oxalic acid , the most abundant species 
found in organic aerosols (Kawamura and Bikkina, 2016 ; Mkoma and Kawamura, 2013 ). Therefore, 
knowledge about Gly and Mgly in the atmosphere is important for understanding atmospheric characteristics 
and processes such as VOC degradation, oxidative capacity, and SOA formation. 
The global inventories of Gly and Mgly are estimated to be ~ 45 Tg/yr and 140 Tg/yr, respectively (Fu et al., 
2008). Both Gly and Mgly are emitted directly from fossil fuel combustion (Hays et al., 2002 ), vehicle 
exhausts (Zhang et al., 2016 ), and biomass burning (Zarzana et al., 2017 ; Zarzana et al., 2018 ), but direct 
emissions are considered to be a minor source of Gly and Mgly compared to secondary formations 
(Myriokefalitakis et al., 2008 ). Although in many polluted regions anthropogenic acetylene, ethylene, and 
aromatics are considered as major precursors of dicarbonyls (Li et al., 2014 ; Liu et al., 2012 ; Miller et al., 
2016), on a global scale biogenic isoprene is the most important precursor of Gly and Mgly (Fu et al., 2008). 
Isoprene is the most abundant non-methane VOC in the atmosphere, contributing more than 40% of the total 
atmospheric VOC flux (Guenther et al., 2012 ). Acetone, which mainly comes from primary emissions of 
vegetation and secondary formation from oxidation of monoterpenes and methylbutenol, ranks as the second

most important precursor of Mgly (Jacob et al., 2002 ). However, recent satellite observations and modeling 
studies have shown that, in air pollution hot-spot regions such as southern and eastern China, there appear to 
be some missing sources for tropospheric Gly (Liu et al., 2012; Miller et al., 2016). This suggests a need for 
more ground-based measurements of the dicarbonyls and their precursors in order to establish a dicarbonyl 
precursor relationship in highly polluted regions. 
Most g round-based studies of ambient Gly and Mgly in recent years have been conducted in urban , 
suburban and rural atmospheres (Dai et al., 2012; Ho et al., 2014; Li et al., 2013b; Li et al., 2014; Lui et al., 
2017; Spaulding et al., 2003 ), and there is only limited knowledge about their concentrations and 
characteristics in the atmospheres of background areas such as high -altitude mountains (Kawamura et al., 
2013; Munger et al., 1995 ) and remote forests (Ieda et al., 2006 ; MacDonald et al., 2012 ; Matsunaga et al., 
2004). Recent studies have shown that in remote forests the atmospheric oxidative capacity and the 
degradation of isoprene would be amplified under the influence of anthropogenic emissions (Liu et al., 2018; 
Santos et al., 2018 ). As forests are the largest source of biogenic isoprene and high mountains frequently 
receive polluted airs through regional transport, the forested mountaintop sites offer a desirable locale for 
observing the interaction of anthropogenic and natural emission sources (Okamoto and Tanimoto, 2016) and 
obtaining valuable data and information for better understanding regional atmospheric oxidizing capacity 
and the formation of photochemical pollution (Li et al., 2013b; MacDonald et al., 2012). 
The Nanling Mountains in southern China is a major mountain range , which serves as the dividing line 
between subtropical and temperate zones (Siu et al., 2005 ). Influenced by the East Asian monsoons, the 
forest-covered mountain range is the key pathway for the long-range transport of air pollutants between 
southern and central -eastern China, making it a good location for background air quality monitoring in the 
region. To the south of the Nanling Mountains is the Pearl River Delta (PRD) , one of the most urbanize d 
regions in China. Over the last several decades, with rapid urbanization and industrialization the region has 
experienced serious air pollution problems (Chan and Yao, 2008 ). A number of studies have reported 
worsening photochemical pollution with strong atmospheric oxidative capacity (Hofzumahaus et al., 2009 ) 
and frequent occurrence of surface O3 pollution (Wang et al., 2009 ) throughout the region . In particular,

recent satellite observations have suggested that the PRD is one of the Gly “hot-spots” (Miller et al., 2016 ) 
in the world, likely due to the large photochemical turnover and aromatics emission in the region. Although 
a few ground-based studies have detected high levels of dicarbonyls in the PRD (Li et al., 2013b; Lui et al., 
2017), there have been no surface observations of these compounds in this photochemically active region. 
Direct measurement of dicarbonyls in representative areas, such as the Nanling Mountains is important for 
understanding the complex photochemical processes and reducing the severe air pollution in the PRD 
region. 
To improve our understanding of dicarbonyls in the background atmosphere of southern China and their 
sources and fate, we carried out an observational study for the first time at a forested mountaintop site in the 
Nangling Mountains. In this paper, we first discuss the meteorological conditions of the Nanling area and 
present the characteristics of measured mixing rat ios of Gly and Mgly . We then show the results of 
calculations of in-situ Gly and Mgly formation from the observed VOC precursors . Finally, we discuss the 
impact of regional transport of air pollutants from biogenic and anthropogenic source s on the 
photochemistry of the Nanling atmosphere. 
2 Experiments and methods 
2.1 Sampling site and sampling periods 
The sampling site (24°41′56″N, 112°53′56″E, 1,690 m a.s.l.) is located on the top of Mt . Tianjing of the 
Nanling Mountains in southern China and is about 200 km north of the PRD . It is one of the sixteen 
National Ambient Air Background Stations in China (Zhang et al., 2018) . This mountaintop site, usually in 
the free troposphere or upper planetary boundary layer , is surrounded by pristine subtropical forests with a 
vegetation coverage > 95%. Influenced by the East Asian monsoon, th e site receives warm humid air flows 
from the South China Sea during the summer and cold dry air parcels from central-eastern China during the 
winter. A detailed description of the site can be found in Gong et al. (2018). 
As the East Asian monsoon is divided into the warm and wet summer monsoon (Apr. to Sept.) and cold and 
dry winter monsoon (Oct. to Mar.), the sampling of the study was conducted during both the wet season (Jul.

31  Aug. 5) and dry season (Oct. 9  Oct. 15, Oct. 25  Nov. 5), for a total of 22 days , in 2016. During

09:0012:00, 12:00 15:00, 15:00 18:00, 18:00 21:00, 22:00 01:00 LT). The sunrise and sunset were 
around 06:00 and 19:00 in the wet season and 06:30 and 18:00 in the dry season, respectively. 
2.2 Sampling and analytical methods 
2.2.1 Dicarbonyls 
Dicarbonyls were collected with pentafluorophenyl hydrazine (PFPH) cartridges and analyzed by Gas 
Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS) (Ho and Yu, 2004 ; Pang et al., 2011 ). The sampler was 
placed inside a two-story building, with the sampling tube inlet located 1.5 m above the rooftop. Ambient air 
was drawn at a flow rate of 100 m l/min through a 2 -m perfluoroalkoxy tube (OD 1/4 inch). An air sample 
was collected with a sampling pump ( FCG-5, China) into a sampling tube that had been filled with Tenax 
TA (60–80 mesh, ANPEL Corporation, China ) and impregnated with PFPH ( GC grade >98.0%, ANPEL 
Corporation, China). The C=O groups of the target carbonyls reacted with PFPH to form therm ally stable 
derivatives. In order to avoid the interference of O 3, a 1 -m long helical copper tube (ID=0.25 cm) coated 
with saturated potassium iodide (KI) was placed in front of each sampling tube. A total of 209 samples were 
collected during the field campaign. The sampled tubes were stored at the ambient temperatures (~25 °C) for 
3 days and returned to the laboratory for analysis in one week. 
The sampled tubes were eluted with 4 .0-mL of hexane , and the hexane extracts were then concentrated to 
1.0 mL with a Termovap Sample Concentrator (DC-12, ANPEL Corporation, China ). Analysis of PFPH 
derivatives was performed on a gas chromatography -mass spectrometry detection system (7820A GC / 
5977E MSD, Agilent Technologies Inc., USA ) equipped with an HP-5MSI column (30 m × 250 μm × 0.25 
μm, Agilent Technologies Inc., USA). The initial temperature of the GC oven was set at 72 °C and 
maintained for 1 min ; the temperature was again increased to 110 °C at a rate of 8 °C min -1and then to 
175 °C at a rate of 4.5 °C min-1; after the temperature was kept at 175 °C for 2 min, it was then increased to 
200 °C at a rate of 2.5 °C min-1 and held for 8 min. The inlet temperature was 275 °C and the GC/MS

transfer line temperature was 290 °C. The memory effect of the dicarbonyl compounds and their 
decomposition in the injector were negligible. Solvent delay was set at 5 min for MS detector to avoid 
damage to MS detector due to the high level of PFPH in solvent. The mass spectrometer was operated in a 
scan mode with a mass range of 50450 Da. 
2.2.2 VOC Precursors 
VOC precursors were collected with a double-channel cryogenic pre -concentrator (TH-PKU 300B, Wuhan 
Tianhong Instrument Co. Ltd., China) and analyzed using a gas chromatography-mass spectrometry/flame 
ionization detection system (7820A GC, 5977E MSD, Agilent Technologies Inc., USA) . A PLOT 
(Al2O3/KCl) column (20 m × 320 μm × 3.0 μm, Dikma Technologies Inc. , USA) connected to an FID was 
used to quantify C 2–C5 hydrocarbons. A DB–624 column (60 m × 250 μm × 1.4 μm, Agilent Technologies 
Inc., USA ) connected to an MS was used to analyze other VOC species (e.g. C 5–C12 hydrocarbons, 
oxygenated VOCs, and halocarbons ). The carrier gas es for the two columns were ultra-pure helium 
(>99.999%). The initial temperature of the GC oven was set at 40 °C and maintained for 5 min, and the 
temperature was then increased to 180 °C at a rate of 6 °C min-1 and held at the final temperature of 180 °C 
for 4 min. The system was calibrated at 7 concentration levels in the range of 1002000 pptv, and R2 values 
for calibration curves were >0.99 for all species. The method detection limits (MDL) for target VOCs 
ranged from 2 to 50 pptv with a precision of <5% for all compounds. 
2.2.3 Measurement of other relevant data 
O3 was monitored with a commercial UV photometric instrument ( Model 49i, Thermo Scientific, Inc.) . 
PM2.5 was measured by tapered element oscillating microbalance (TEOM 1405, Thermo Fisher Scientific 
Inc., MA, US). Nitrogen dioxide (NO2) was monitored with a chemiluminescence analyzer equipped with an 
internal MoO catalytic converter (Model 42i-TL, Thermo Scientific, Inc. ). It should be noted that the NO 2 
measured with MoO conversion may significantly overestimate NO2 and the observed NO 2 actually 
represented a major fraction of NO y (Xu et al., 2013 ). Therefore, the observed NO 2 in this study was 
expressed as NO2*, which includes NO2 and other oxidized nitrogen compounds. Meteorological parameters,

2.2.4 Quality assurance and quality control (QA/QC) 
Before use, sampling tubes were pre-baked at 450 °C for 4 h, and Tenax TA was placed in a muffle furnace 
at 275 °C for 8 h to remove impurities. To avoid the interference from the ubiquitous carbo nyls in the 
laboratory air, the sample pretreatment was carried out in a glove box filled with ultra -pure nitrogen 
(>99.999%). To avoid the contamination during transport, the sampling tubes were stored in a clean 
refrigerator at < 4 °C. The flow rates of air samplers were calibrated before and after each sample collection 
using a flow meter. During the field campaign, breakthrough tests were conducted by connecting two tubes 
in series to collect ambient samples. At a flow rate of 100 mL min -1, collection efficiencies >82% and >92% 
were achieved for Gly and Mgly, respectively. Two parallel samples were collected daily, and the relative 
standard deviation (RSD) of the parallel samples are within 10%. One field blank and one laboratory blank 
were collected every two days during the field sampling , and t he concentrations of Gly and Mgly in the 
blank samples were either not detected or below the MDL. Recovery tests by using standard addition of the 
target compounds were also conducted, and the recovery percentages were in the range of 80%-92%. 
Calibration curves were established by analyzing the PFPH -dicarbonyl derivatives prepared in hexane 
solutions with known concentrations. The mixtures of the standard dicarbonyls (40 wt. % solution in H 2O, 
Sigma- Aldrich) and PFPH in hexane were allowed to stand at the room temperatures for at least 2 h to 
ensure complete derivatization. Concentrations of individual standard dicarbonyl ranged from 0.01 to 5.0 μg 
mL-1. Calibration curves were obtained by first running the calibration standards with the GC-MS system the 
same way as the hexane extracts and then linearly correlating responses (peak areas) with the amounts of 
dicarbonyls (R2 > 0.995). MDLs of dicarbonyls, which were determined by using seven replicate analyses of

3 Results and discussion 
3.1 Back trajectories and meteorological conditions 
To characterize the transport of air masses that arrived at the Nanling site during the field campaign, 72-hr 
back trajectories were calculated using the Hybrid Single Particle Lagrangian Integrated Trajectory 
(HYSPLIT) model (Stein et al., 2015) for each of the days in the sampling period (Figure 1). An elevation of 
1,800 m a.s.l. was chosen as the endpoint in the model (Lin et al., 2017). The end time was set to 05:00 LT, 
which was the start ti me of dicarbonyl sampling. Overall, the Nanling site was mainly affected by three 
sources of air masses during the sampling period : easterly, southerly and northerly . The easterly, which 
occurred between Jul 31 – Aug 2 and Oct 9–14, originated from the ocean, traversed through eastern China 
at a lower height, and arrived at the site from the northeast. The southerly, which occurred between Aug 3–5, 
Oct 15, and Oct 25–31, originated from the South China Sea, traversed through the Pearl River Delta region, 
and arrived at the site from the south. The northerly, which occurred during Nov 1–5, originated from central 
China, traversed at a higher height, and arrived at the site from the northwest. During the field campaign, the 
mean temperature, relative humidity , wind speed , and cloud cover were 14.9 ± 0.3 °C (mean ± 95% 
confidence interval, the same below), 89.0 ± 1.3 %, 4.2 ± 0.2 m s–1 and 58.0 ± 4.8%, respectively (Figure 2). 
The average mixing ratios of O 3, NO2* and CO were 56.2 ± 1.2, 3.2 ± 0.1 and 407.0 ± 16.1 ppbv during the 
sampling period. 
3.2 Data overview and comparison with other studies 
3.2.1 Concentrations and variations of dicarbonyls and related compounds 
Figure 2 presents the time series for Gly and Mgly and the key precursors (i.e. isoprene, acetone, acetylene, 
ethene, benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, xylenes), trace gases (O 3, NO 2*, CO , CH 3Cl, ethane ), and 
meteorological parameters (temperature, relative humidity, UVa, cloud cover, wind speed) for the sampling 
period. The mixing ratio of Gly ranged from 215 to 992 pptv with an average of 509±31 pptv , and the 
mixing ratio of Mgly ranged from 121 to 891 pptv with an average of 340±32 pptv. 
The mixing ratios of dicarbonyls and related gases were markedly different in the air masses of different

precursors to the Nanling site . Finally, w hen the northerly winds were prevailing in November, the air

biofuel combustion tracers ( e.g. CH 3Cl, acetylene, and CO). Overall, these observations reveal different 
characteristics of pollutants in the air masses transported to the site and indicate impact of both biogenic and 
anthropogenic sources on the concentrations and characteristics of dicarbonyls observed in this study.

High mixing ratio s of dicarbonyls found at forested mountain s ites may suggest strong accumulation 
processes caused by either more rapid production from the oxidation of local biogenic precursors or strong 
regional transport from anthropogenic influenced source regions or both . Many recent studies have 
attributed high levels of Gly mixing ratios observed in forested or urban regions to active photochemistry (Li 
et al., 2013b ; MacDonald et al., 2012 ; Miller et al., 2016 ; Miller et al., 2017 ). Kawamura et al. (2013 ) 
concluded that regional transport of biomass burning emissions significantly contributed to the observed 
high level dicarbonyls at Mt. Tai during intensive field burning of agricultural wastes in the North Chin a 
Plain. Although a number of studies have reported that the polymerization process es of dicarbonyls in 
particulate matter (PM) play an important role in the formation of SOA (Liggio et al., 2005; Ortiz-Montalvo 
et al., 2 016; Schwier et al., 2010 ), the relatively low PM2.5 concentrations in Nanling site (averaging 11.1 
μg/m3) suggest that its dicarbonyl concentrations may not be significantly affected by their heterogeneous 
reaction in PM. Based on the above , we hypothesized that rapid in-situ formation and strong regional 
transport could be the two main contributors for the observed high mixing ratios of Gly and Mgly at the 
Nanling site. Further discussion will be presented in Sections 3.3 and 3.4. 
3.3 In-situ formations of Gly and Mgly 
To understand the contribution of in-situ formation to the observed high levels of dicarbonyl mixing ratios, 
the Gly and Mgly production from the oxidation of measured precursors were estimated using a 
parameterized approach. 
3.3.1 Dicarbonyl production rate from precursors

sampling periods. The total production rate of Mgly (201 ± 46 pptv h−1) was significantly higher than that of 
Gly (74 ± 13 pptv h −1), with Mgly showing a stronger association with biogenic precursors when compared 
to Gly. The reactions of isoprene and OH dominated the dicarbonyl production, accounting for 58% of Gly 
and 86% of Mgly production, respectively . The OH-initiated oxidation of aromatics was the second 
important pathway for the Gly and Mgly production , accounting for 18% of Gly and 9% of Mgly , 
respectively. The oxidations of isoprene by NO 3, which mainly occurred at night, also contributed 
significantly to the dicarbonyl production (11% of Gly and 4% of Mgly). Some longer-lived precursors such 
as benzene, acetone and >C3 alkanes, which can travel a long distance before being oxidized, had only minor 
contributions to the in-situ dicarbonyl formation. 
Both Gly and Mgly production rates display a clear diurnal cycle with rapid rising at 09:00 LT and peaking 
at 12:00 LT (Figure 4), similar to the patterns of solar radiation and OH radical concentrations (Figure S1). 
The reaction of NO3 with isoprene dominated the dicarbonyl production at night. 
The dicarbonyl producti on also varied with the different air masses arriving at the site from different 
directions (Figure 4). The production of Gly and Mgly was the highest in the air masses with the easterly 
origin, followed by the southerly and northerly air masses . For the Mgly production, the oxidation of 
isoprene was the dominant contributor in all the air masses, with aromatics contributed slightly during the 
times dominated by the southerly winds. Unlike Mgly, the oxidation of isoprene was the most important in 
the Gly production during the times of the easterly winds, but the oxidation of aromatics and acetylene 
contributed substantially to the Gly production during the times when the southerly and northerly winds 
dominated, implying enhanced regional transport of anthropogenic precursors. 
Overall, the calculated dicarbonyl production indicates the important role of biogenic (e.g. isoprene) and 
anthropogenic (e.g. aromatics and acetylene) precursors in the secondary formation of Gly and Mgly, an 
observation consistent with previous studies in southern China by Li et al. (2014 and Miller et al. (2016).

3.3.2 Linking expected and observed dicarbonyls 
The expected mixing ratios of Gly and Mgly were calculated using the mixing ratios of precursors measured 
in the same samples for Gly and Mgly measurements (Figure 5). Overall, the average mixing ratios of the 
Gly and Mgly formed in-situ are 49 ± 18 and 222 ± 77 pptv, respectively, and account for 9 ± 3% and 67 ± 
21% of the observed Gly and Mgly, respecti vely. The expected mixing ratios of Gly and Mgly calculated 
using the production rates in Table 2 are higher for the times of easterly winds than those for the times of the 
southerly and northerly winds. The observed Mgly mixing ratio s can be adequately accounted for by the 
in-situ oxidation of observed precursors, among which isoprene was the dominant species. For Gly, however, 
a large difference exists between the observed and the expected mixing ratios, suggesting there might be an 
unaccounted-for sources for this dicarbonyl species. 
Our calculations show that on average only 9% of the observed Gly can be explained by the in-situ oxidation 
of the precursors. The large discrepancy between expected and observed Gly mixing ratios at the site could 
be due to (1) other unaccounted -for mechanisms for Gly production ; (2) formation of Gly from other 
biogenic VOCs (BVOCs) that were not measured ; or (3) long-range transport from additional primary or 
secondary Gly sources. First, one likely Gly production mechani sm is the oxidation of organic aerosol 
(Kwan et al., 2006 ), but this is not expected to be significant because of the much greater Gly production 
from gas phase oxidation of ubiquitous precursors in the lower troposphere (Fu et al., 2008 ). Second, 
potential contributions to the Gly production from the other unmeasured BVOCs including several 
monoterpenes (e.g. α-pinene, △3-carene, geraniol, and citral) and methylbutenol (MBO) are expected to be 
minor, as our calculations include all major BVOC precursors. Fu et al. (2008 ) reported that the mean 
overall molar yield of Gly and Mgly from oxidation of monoterpenes is 2.8% and 4.2%, respectively, which 
were 15~18 times lower than the contribution of isoprene. These authors also reporte the contribution of 
methylbutenol to the Gly production to be small (yielding ~ 0.3% of Gly) . Thus, a large portion of the 
observed Gly was likely transported to the Nanling site from either primary anthropogenic emissions or 
secondary sources.

On a regional scale, the lifetimes of Gly (2.9 h) in the atmosphere is generally longer than that of Mgly (1.6 
h) because of the more rapid photolysis and oxidation of Mgly (Fu et al., 2008). Thus it is expected that Gly 
would be more slowly removed out of the air and more easily transported to the Nanling sit e with aged air 
masses from the upwind areas. However, the equilibrium of dicarbonyls between production and loss is 
sensitive to relative humidity due to their high water solubility (Fick et al., 2003; Liggio et al., 2005) . It has 
also been found that wet deposition and uptake on clouds and aerosols are likely to be significant loss 
processes at night (Fu et al., 2008) . Therefore, in a local environment with high humidity such as th e 
forested mountains, the in -situ lifetime of Gly may be shorter than that of Mgly due to the much higher 
water solubility of Gly (Kroll et al., 2005). For example, our calculation shows that Gly has a shorter in -situ 
lifetime than Mgly at the Nanling site (Figure S1). Therefore, it is possible that compared to Mgly, Gly was 
more readily to be transported from the distant sources but was more quickly removed from the air when air 
masses passing through the humid forests surrounding the site. 
Although globally Mgly has a shorter atmospheric lifetime, regionally its ambient mixing ratio s can be 
slightly higher than that of Gly due to its much higher production yield from biogenic precursors (e.g. 
isoprene and monoterpenes) and ar omatics as well as precursors from urban regions (e.g., >C 2 alkenes 
and >C 3 alkanes) (Fu et al., 2008 ). This was observed in several studies conducted in remote or rural 
atmospheres, where the G ly/Mgly ratios were found to be less than 1 (Table 1). In this study, with the 
expected mixing ratio of Mgly significantly higher than that of Gly , the estimated ratio of the expected 
Gly/Mgly ratio is about 0.5 (Figure S2). However, the observed Gly/Mgly ratio (1.8 ± 0.2) was significantly 
higher than the expected ratio, suggesting that there were additional sources supplying Gly to the Nanling 
site. Thus, primary or secondary Gly transported from distant sources cannot be ruled out as a significant 
factor affecting the Gly mixing ratios in the areas around the Nanling site. 
3.4 Impact of regional transport 
The earlier discussions indicate that Gly could be transported over long ranges to the Nanling site from 
potential “hot-spot” regions such as forests, metr opolitan areas, and biomass burning areas. To assess the

impact of long -range transport of dicarbonyl precursors, polarplots of Gly and Mgly mixing ratio s were 
constructed and are shown in Figure 6. The plots indicate that high mixing ratios of Gly and Mgly were 
associated with winds from the south and, to a less extend, winds from east with speed >6 m s-1. There were 
active biogenic and anthropogenic emission sources to the east (Li et al., 2013a) and south (Fang et al., 2016) 
of our site, and the oxidized plumes from these sources could have contributed to the high mixing ratio 
levels of dicarbonyls. The observed mixing ratio s of dicarbonyls were mostly at high levels (>400 pptv) 
when the southly and eastly winds were strong (>4 m/s), suggesting that distance transport of dicarbonyls by 
winds might be a primary source of dicarbonyls during those times. 
3.4.1 Biogenic sources from eastern China in the easterly air masses 
High mixing ratios of secondary Gly have been observed over areas with high biogenic VOCs emissions 
(MacDonald et al., 2012 ; Vrekoussis et al., 2009 ). The easterly air masses passing through the areas with 
abundant vegetation (Li et al., 2013a) had the highest mixing ratios of BVOCs (including isoprene, α-pinene, 
and acetone). That a large fraction of Gly cannot be explained by the in -situ formation from the observed 
precursors in the easterly air masses strongly suggests the influence of biogenic sources from eastern of the 
Nanling site. After emitted from the sources and/or during the transport of air masses from the upwind areas, 
biogenic precursors with higher reactivity (e.g . isoprene and pinenes ) will decay more rapidly, with the 
survival of less reactive species (e.g. acetone) and the enrichment of oxidation products (i.e., Gly and Mgly) 
in the aged air masses. Therefore, these air masses can bring considerable amount of Gly to the site via 
long-distance transport under the favorable wind conditions. 
3.4.2 Anthropogenic source in southerly air masses from PRD region 
During the periods of the southerly winds, our calculation shows that only 2–16% of the observed Gly can 
be attributed to in -situ formation from precursors (Figure 5), and thus there is a large discrepancy between 
the observed and the expected mixing ratios of Gly (Figure 7). In the south erly air masses that had passed 
over the PRD, a region with high levels of Gly and its precursor aromatics based on both satellite 
observations (Miller et al., 2016; Vrekoussis et al., 2009) and ground-based measurements (Li et al., 2013b),

the mixing ratio s of the aromatics were very high. Due to their relatively long lifetimes, aromatics can 
greatly enhance the dicarbonyl mixing ratio levels in the outflow of anthropogenic emission regions (Fu et 
al., 2008). At the Nanling site diurnal variations of decreased daytime mixing ratios and increased nighttime 
mixing ratios were observed for aromatics (Figure 3), which was opposite to the diurnal variations of Gly 
mixing ratios with higher daytime mixing ratios (541 ± 175 pptv) and slightly lower nighttime mixing ratios 
(502 ± 170 pptv). The two different diurnal variation patterns indicate that unlike aromatics, Gly was not 
directly transported from the PRD but had more secondary production from the anthropogenic precursors 
during the regional transport. These results suggest the transport of air masses from the PRD region to the 
Nanling site as an additional source for secondary formation of Gly , especially under prevailing southerly 
wind conditions. 
3.4.3 Biomass burning emissions in northerly air masses from central China 
Although in the late autumn when the northerly winds were prevailing the dicarbonyl mixing ratios at the 
Nanling site showed a decline likely due to the lower temperature and the weaker UV radiation (Figure 2), 
they were still higher than the background levels of many other sites (Table 1). High concentrations of Gly 
and Mgly in biomass burning seasons are often attributed to the biomass burning or biofuel combustion 
(Kawamura et al., 2013 ; Zarzana et al., 2017 ; Zarzana et al., 2018 ). Direct emissions from biomass burning 
and biofule use have been estimated to contribute 38% of the Gly budget and 8% of the Mgly budget, lower 
than formation from biogenic precursors but higher than other anthropogenic emissions (Fu et al., 2008 ). 
Thus, during the late autumn, the dicarbonyls at the Nanling site were likely affected by the active biomass 
burning. CH3Cl, CO, and acetylene , because of their high emissions from biomass burning, have been 
widely used as representative tracers for air masses perturbed by biomass burning emissions (De Gouw et al., 
2004; Liu et al., 2008 ; Yurganov et al., 2008 ). Indeed, in our study the mixing ratios of CH 3Cl, acetylene, 
and benzene showed good to excellent correlations with the CO mixing ratio during November 1 -5, as 
shown in Figure 8. To further validate the potential influence of biomass burning on the dicarbonyl 
chemistry of the Nanling site, backward trajectories were analyzed along with the fire spot data provided by 
MODIS (Moderate-resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer). Backward trajectory patterns calculated using

episode occurred near the site during this period, and at the same time the CH3Cl mixing ratio reached the 
highest level at the site. Also, the ratio of CH 3Cl to CO was 0.31 (r = 0.60), which is within the suggested 
range of 0.2–0.83 for biomass burning or biofuel combustion (Andreae and Merlet, 2001 ).Based on the

4 Conclusions 
A field campaign to study ambient Gly and Mgly were conducted during Jul 31-Nov 5 of 2016 at a remote 
mountaintop site in southern China. During the sampling period, the average mixing ratios of Gly and Mgly 
were 509±31 and 340±32 pptv, respectively, with the average Gly/Mgly ratio equal to 1.8± 0.2. Both the 
concentrations and the ratio are significantly higher than those found in a number of background sites 
around the world . We calculated the production of Gly and Mgly using observed precursors and analyzed 
the influences of air masses with different origins t o examine the hypothesis that rapid in -situ formation 
combined with strong regional transport were the two main factors causing the high mixing ratio s of 
dicarbonyls observed at the site. On average, 67% of the observed Mgly can be explained by the in -situ 
formation from the precursors, but only 9% of the Gly can be accounted for by this pathway, suggesting that 
Mgly was mainly derived from the in -situ formation but Gly might still have unidentified sources. There 
were large variations in dicarbonyl mixing ratio s in the air masses originated from different regions , 
indicating the important role of regional transport of pollutants in the atmospheric chemistry of dicarbonyls 
at the Nanling site and the surrounding areas. Correlation analysis and model calculations indicate that the 
biogenic sources in eastern China and anthropoge nic sources in the PRD region were the two primary 
contributors to the dicarbonyls in the Nanling area. Furthermore, the backward trajectory analysis coupled 
with fire spot data shows that in the late autumn time of the sampling period, biomass burning als o had a 
significant impact on the dicarbonyls at the site. This study shows that atmospheric concetrations of Gly and 
Mgly in southern China are dominated by biogenic and anthropogenic sources and highlight s the role of 
biomass burning emission in late aut umn. Our results indicate that regional transport of anthropogenic

Highlights 
 Extremely high levels of Gly, Mgly and Gly/Mgly were observed at a remote mountaintop site in 
southern China; 
 Strong atmospheric oxidation capacity in this region can explain about 67% of the measured MGly but 
only 9% for Gly; 
 Regional transport of biogenic emissions from eastern China and anthropogenic emissions from the PRD 
region heavily influenced the Gly concentration and the Gly/MGly ratio at the national background 
station.
