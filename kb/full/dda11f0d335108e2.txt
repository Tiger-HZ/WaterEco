Background: Allergic rhinitis (AR) is a pervasive condition among competitive athletes, especially in endurance and aquatic disciplines. IgE-mediated Th2-type inflammation causes mucosal edema and increased upper airway resistance, forcing nocturnal mouth-breathing mechanics. This instability triggers frequent micro-arousals and disrupts sleep architecture, leading to documented reductions in restorative slow-wave and rapid eye movement sleep. Aim: To synthesize evidence on how AR-mediated sleep fragmentation degrades neurocognitive function, motor control, and physical output in athletes, while evaluating the safety and efficacy of contemporary pharmacotherapy.
