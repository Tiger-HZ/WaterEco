Stable isotope analysis of diet switching by fishes often is hampered by slow turnover rates of the tissues analyzed (usually muscle or fins). We examined epidermal mucus as a potentially faster turnover “tissue” that might provide a more rapid assessment of diet switching. In a controlled hatchery experiment, we switched the diet of juvenile steelhead (sea-run rainbow trout, Oncorhynchus mykiss ) from a plant-based feed with low δ 13 C and δ 15 N to a fish-meal-based diet with higher delta values. We found mucus to provide a significantly more rapid response to diet switching (half-life = 36 days for δ 15 N, 30 days for δ 13 C) than muscle tissue (half-life = 94 days for δ 15 N, 136 days for δ 13 C), even for growing juvenile fish.
