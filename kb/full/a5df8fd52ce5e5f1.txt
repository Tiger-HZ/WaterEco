Cover crops are increasingly promoted to improve soil health, reduce nutrient losses, and enhance water quality, yet their large-scale performance across diverse climatic and soil conditions remains insufficiently quantified. We hypothesized that cereal rye cover crop adoption would reduce sediment and nutrient losses, with potential impacts on cash crop yields, and that these effects would vary with precipitation regime and soil vulnerability. Using Conservation Effects Assessment Project data and Agricultural Policy/Environmental eXtender simulations across 4317 U.S. corn and soybean sites over 45 years, we evaluated two scenarios: current management (Base) and Base plus Cover Crop (BCC).
