Abstract Treated greywater offers a practical alternative to potable water for non-food landscape irrigation when treatment and monitoring are matched to source composition and exposure. Because the reviewed studies differed in design, scale, climate and monitoring, performance was synthesized as reported ranges rather than pooled averages. Bathroom-only greywater can often be managed through source control, filtration and restricted subsurface irrigation. Laundry-inclusive streams additionally require sodium, boron and surfactant control, whereas kitchen-inclusive or mixed streams need grease interception, stronger pretreatment and biological or physicochemical barriers. Public-access landscapes require validated disinfection, process verification and cross-connection protection. Membrane
