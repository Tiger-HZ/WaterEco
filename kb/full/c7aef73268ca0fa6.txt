Dihydroanatoxins are emerging contaminants produced by cyanobacteria that proliferate in continental surface waters. Recent studies suggest that the oral toxicity of dihydroanatoxin-a may surpass that of anatoxin‑a, a congener of the same family of homotropane alkaloids. Currently, climate change and anthropogenic contamination of continental water reservoirs contribute to more frequent and extensive cyanobacterial blooms, which release these hazardous secondary metabolites. This underscores the need for sensitive and reliable analytical methods capable of detecting dihydroanatoxins at trace levels. In this study, novel bioconjugates based on dihydroanatoxin-a haptens were synthesized and thoroughly characterized, enabling the generation of a panel of high-affinity monoclonal antibodies. Several antibodies exhibited IC50 values below 1 nM for both dihydroanatoxins, dihydroanatoxin-a and dihydrohomoanatoxin-a. Using these immunoreagents, we developed, for the first time, a highly sensitive monoclonal antibody-based direct competitive ELISA with a limit of detection of 0.016 ng/mL for dihydroanatoxin-a. The optimized immunoassay showed excellent precision and accuracy in a variety of fortified freshwater samples (lake, river, water well, and irrigation pond) across concentrations from 0.5 to 200 ng/mL, with recoveries between 79% and 112%, and coefficients of variation below 20%. Validation using naturally contaminated samples revealed good statistical agreement with a reference chromatographic method. Additionally, a lateral-flow immunochromatographic assay was developed for the on-site analysis of both dihydroanatoxins, providing a visual limit of detection of 3 ng/mL. Overall, the developed immunochemical methods offer rapid, reliable, and cost-effective approaches for monitoring dihydroanatoxins in environmental water samples.
