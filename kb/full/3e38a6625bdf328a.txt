Abstract The year 2025 marked a critical transition for indoor air quality policy, evolving from a neglected domain to a cornerstone of climate and health resilience. This perspectives piece traces the trajectory of this shift, beginning with Australia's domestic reckoning—catalysed by catastrophic bushfires, mounting evidence of air pollution inequalities and the energy efficiency paradox of increasingly airtight buildings—to expand to the global stage through the landmark 'Global Pledge for Healthy Indoor Air' at the United Nations. We synthesise emerging evidence on climate-amplified wildfire risks, economic productivity impacts and health burdens to argue that voluntary commitments must now evolve into enforceable, harmonised regulatory standards.
