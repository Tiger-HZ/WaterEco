Insufficient spatial detail in low-resolution (LR) remote sensing data tends to mask tiny water patches, thin river corridors, and intricate shoreline transitions, which severely restricts the precision of water body extraction tasks using aerial and satellite imagery. Our study proposes a two-stage cascaded framework that combines the Top-k Token Selective Transformer (TTST) for image super resolution (SR) with MSRAG-UNet for binary water-body segmentation. TTST reconstructs LR images at 2×, 4×, and 8× SR factors, after which the reconstructed images are processed by U-Net, ResUNet, and the MSRAG-UNet. Experiments were conducted on the S1S2-Water and LoveDA datasets to evaluate reconstruction fidelity, segmentation performance, boundary preservation, and computational complexity. The 2× setting achieved the highest reconstruction quality. Under this setting, the MSRAG-UNet obtained Dice scores of 0.8122 and 0.8358 on the two datasets, respectively, outperforming U-Net and ResUNet. Increasing the SR factor progressively reduced reconstruction and segmentation accuracy because severe downsampling removed fine spatial information that could not be reliably recovered. Accuracy–complexity analysis showed that the 2× configuration provided the highest accuracy, whereas the 4× configuration reduced computational cost by approximately 73% while retaining competitive segmentation performance. These results demonstrate that SR-assisted segmentation is governed by a trade-off among detail recovery, reconstruction artefacts, and computational cost, and that the proposed framework provides effective configurations for both accuracy-oriented and resource-constrained water-body mapping.
