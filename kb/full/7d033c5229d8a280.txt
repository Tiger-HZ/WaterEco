Sweet cherry (Prunus avium L.) production in mountainous Mediterranean regions is characterized by strong altitudinal gradients, heterogeneous soils, and short phenological cycles, which complicate nutrient management and the interpretation of conventional foliar analyses. This study aimed to evaluate the seasonal dynamics of macro- and micronutrient concentrations in flowers and leaves of sweet cherry orchards located in the Jerte Valley (Spain) in order to refine nutritional diagnosis under mountain conditions. Flower samples at full bloom and leaf samples at 30, 60, 90, and 120–150 days after full bloom (DAFB) were collected from early, mid-season, and late-maturing cultivars during two consecutive seasons.
