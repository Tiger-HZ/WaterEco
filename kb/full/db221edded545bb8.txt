Abstract. The phytoplankton spring bloom constitutes the majority of annual Arctic primary production. In the Eurasian Arctic it is usually composed of various diatoms and the haptophyte Phaeocystis; recently, Phaeocystis-dominated blooms seem to be expanding. Low silicic acid (Si) availability has been hypothesised to promote Phaeocystis blooms, though the key drivers of Phaeocystis and diatom competition are not yet well understood. To investigate how Si availability controls bloom succession and biogeochemical fluxes we conducted a 3-week mesocosm experiment in Kongsfjorden, Svalbard in May 2024 on the spring phytoplankton community.
