Lentic ecosystems support remarkably diverse and specialized biodiversity but remain understudied and increasingly threatened by environmental change. Environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding offers a rapid and comprehensive approach for ecological characterization in such systems. Here, we assess the efficacy of eDNA in detecting biodiversity patterns across multiple lentic systems within the Mt (Mount) Gambier Limestone Basin, South Australia. Multi-assay metabarcoding identified 1,753 operational taxonomic units (OTUs) belonging to the aquatic ecosystem, and 283 terrestrial OTUs representing natural allochthonous input due to ecological connectivity or DNA run-off into the sites. Even with limited eDNA sampling, more than 80% of the unique aquatic biodiversity across all our study sites were detected when compared to traditional records on the Atlas of Living Australia. Traditional records contrastingly were more effective at capturing highly endemic stygofauna and facultatively aquatic vertebrates, thus reflecting broader regional signals, and emphasizing the complementary nature of these sampling techniques. Diversity patterns from eDNA-characterized ecosystems revealed strong intra- and inter-site structuring of the ecosystem due to hydrogeological factors, particularly site type, pH, temperature and turbidity. Given the ongoing declines in water levels, increasing eutrophication, human land-use and groundwater pollution afflicting submerged cave systems at Mt Gambier and worldwide, these findings highlight the value of eDNA metabarcoding in providing the rapid, high-resolution biodiversity assessment required to inform effective conservation policies for these ecosystems.
