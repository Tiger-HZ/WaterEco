ABSTRACT Male parental care is a prominent and sexually selected trait in giant water bugs (Belostomatidae), where females preferentially mate with egg‐carrying males. However, females frequently encounter males that are not carrying eggs, and the criteria underlying mate choice under such conditions remain poorly understood. Here, we examined female mate choice in two closely related species, Appasus major and A. japonicus , by experimentally restricting mate‐choice assays to males that were not carrying eggs. We quantified male courtship behaviour and analysed female acceptance using a common statistical framework in both species. Female mate choice differed strikingly between the species.
