This study examined the water–sediment feedback mechanisms of fine sediment (0.1–0.3 mm) in the upper reaches of the Tarim River under low-flow velocities (0.12–0.32 m/s) through a combination of flume experiments and Eulerian two-phase flow simulations in Fluent. The standard k-ε turbulence model was employed to systematically evaluate nine conditions representing three sediment sizes and three flow velocities. The results show that (1) when the particle size is less than 0.2 mm and flow velocity is 0.12 m/s, the sediment remains under uniform settling condition; when the particle size exceeds 0.2 mm, deposition accumulates; and when the flow velocity exceeds 0.12 m/s, deposit thickness decreases.
