Sorghum is an important food crop worldwide, and its yield is significantly affected by nutrient distribution. However, few studies have examined how elevation influences the spatial distribution of soil nutrients and sorghum yield. This study explored these effects in the Maotai base. Soil samples were collected from 340 gridded sites, and 324 valid samples were retained after field verification at a 0–20 cm depth, and soil pH, soil organic matter (SOM), total nitrogen (TN), total phosphorus (TP), total potassium (TK), alkaline hydrolyzable nitrogen (AN), available phosphorus (AP), and available potassium (AK) were measured alongside sorghum yield.
