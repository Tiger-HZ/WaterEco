Abstract Cocopeat has become a widely favored soilless medium for producing potato minitubers, largely because conventional field soils retain water poorly and readily harbour soilborne pathogens. Its high price and irregular supply, however, create a strong incentive to identify blended media that match the performance of pure cocopeat at lower cost. Accordingly, this study assessed how varying cocopeat-to-pumice (C/P) ratios influence the physico-chemical characteristics of the growing medium, nutrient uptake, and tuber yield of Solanum tuberosum L. grown under greenhouse conditions.
