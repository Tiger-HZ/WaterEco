Optimizing nano urea for enhancing economic of rice, 
nutrient acquisition and nitrogen use efficiency in the 
Chhattisgarh plains 
 
Abdul Sajid Musani , Gourav Kumar Jatav , Anamika Verma, Swati Behra 
and Deepak Patel 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9c.6482 
 
Abstract 
The experiment was conducted during Kharif -2025 at the Instructional Cum Research Farm of Indira Gandhi 
Krishi Vishwavidyalaya , Raipur to optimize the nano urea for enhancing rice productivity , nutrient 
acquisition and nitrogen use efficiency in the Chhattisgarh plains. The experiment was laid out in a 
Randomized Block Design (RBD) with three replications. Different treatments al so had a significant effect 
on nitrogen , phosphorus and potassium uptake by the rice crop. The highest nitrogen , phosphorus and 
potassium uptake were recorded under 100% NPK +2 spray of Nano Urea Plus (@0.4%) at 45-50 DAT and 
60-65 DAT ( T12), while the low est in the control. Nitrogen use efficiency was highest (49.62%) in the 
treatment with 75% N, 100% P and K +2 foliar sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@0.4%) at 45–50 and 60–65 DAT 
(T10). Agronomical efficiency (AE) ranged from 28.53 to 43.76 kg kg⁻¹, physiological efficiency (PE) from 
142.18 to 198.68 kg kg⁻¹, agro-physiological efficiency (APE) from 65.06 to 90.19 kg kg⁻¹, apparent recovery 
efficiency (ARE) from 38.66 to 49.63% , and utilization efficiency (EU) from 61.80 to 96.41 kg kg⁻¹. The 
treatment comprising 50% N and 100% P and K + two foliar sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@0.4%) at 45 –50 
and 60–65 DAT (T6) recorded the highest AE (43.76 kg kg⁻¹), PE (198.68 kg kg⁻¹), APE (90.19 kg kg⁻¹) and 
EU (96.41 kg kg⁻¹). The highest ARE (49.63%) was recorded with 75% N and 100% P and K + two sprays 
of Nano Urea Plus (@0.4%) at 45 –50 and 60 –65 DAT ( T10), followed by T6 and T8 (50% N/67% N and 
100% P & K + two Nano Urea Plus sprays). The lowest AE (28.53 kg kg⁻¹) and ARE (38.66%) were observed 
under 100% N, P and K (T5), while the lowest PE and APE were recorded under 50% N and 100% P and K 
(T2) and the lowest EU (61.80 kg kg⁻¹) under 100% N , P and K with one Nano Urea Plus spray at 60 –65 
DAT (T11). The maximum benefit -cost ratio was achieved with 100% NPK + 1 spray of Nano Urea Plus 
(@0.4%) at 60 –65 DAT ( T11), which found almost similar to 75% NPK + 1 or 2 spray of nano urea plus 
(@0.4%) at 45-50 DAT and 60-65 DAT, whereas lowest under control (T1). 
 
Keywords: Nano urea, rice, productivity, nutrient use efficiency, economic 
 
Introduction 
Rice yield mostly relies on soil conditions and further more on the supply of the accessible 
nutrients like nitrogen , phosphorus, potassium, sulphur and zinc [18]. Rice plants require large 
amounts of mineral nutrients including nitrogen for their growth , development and grain 
production [17]. After carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, nitrogen is one of the important elements in 
plants because of its key part in chlorophyll production , which is basic for the photosynthesis 
process. Also, nitrogen is part of different enzymatic proteins that catalyze and regulate plant 
development processes [28]. 
Nitrogen plays a vital role in plant nutrition , being a key element of chlorophyll , which gives 
plants their green colour, and also an essential part of plant proteins. Over the past five decades , 
the global increase in crop yields has largely been driven by the extensive use of nitrogen fertilizers 
combined with improved agricultural practices [30]. As cereal crops require more nitrogen than 
other crops for optimal growth, development and grain production. Despite this need, rice growing 
soils are often nitrogen deficient [26]. The high productivity of irrigated rice today is mainly due to 
the heavy application of nitrogen fertilizers [4]. Nitrogen is considered the most limiting nutrient 
in irrigated rice systems worldwide [27]. In lowland rice, nitrogen comes from soil reserves,

biological nitrogen fixation and fertilizer input. However , a 
significant portion of soil nitrogen is lost through processes like 
plant uptake , denitrification, leaching and volatilization. These 
losses not only reduce nitrogen availability to crops but also pose 
environmental concerns. T herefore, enhancing nitrogen use 
efficiency remains a major challenge in sustainable rice 
production. 
Recent advancements in agriculture, such as the use of nano urea, 
offer promising improvements in rice cultivation. Nano urea , 
composed of ultra -small ure a particles , enhances nitrogen 
absorption by plants, leading to better growth , higher yields and 
improved nitrogen use efficiency. This form of liquid fertilizer is 
considered a superior alternative to traditional urea for instance , 
just 500 ml of nano urea can replace a 45 kg bag of conventional 
urea. Its nano -sized particles (about 30 nm) possess a much 
greater surface area, enabling efficient nutrient delivery through 
foliar application during key growth stages. Research by Sharmila 
and Rahale has shown that nano - clay based formulations like 
zeolite and montmorillonite can release nitrogen for over 1000 
hours, in contrast to less than 500 hours with regular fertilizers. 
This slow -release mechanism ensures consistent nutrient 
availability, boosting import ant yield parameters such as the 
number of panicles , grain count, test weight and both grain and 
straw yields. Beyond productivity , nano urea also contributes to 
environmental benefits reducing nitrate leaching into 
groundwater, cutting greenhouse gas emis sions and supporting 
climate resilience and sustainable farming practices [3]. 
 
Materials and Methods 
The field experiment was conducted during the Kharif -2025 
season at the Instructional - cum-Research Farm, Indira Gandhi 
Krishi Vishwavidyalaya (IGKV) , Raipur, Chhattisgarh 
(21.232245° N to 81.700344° E). Raipur lies in a dry sub -humid 
agro- climatic zone and receives an annual rainfall of about 1200–
1400 mm , with nearly 88% occurring during the southwest 
monsoon (June –September). During the crop season , the 
experimental site received 741.2 mm of well-distributed rainfall, 
mainly from the third week of July to the second week of 
November. Maximum temperature ranged from 28.0 to 32.8 °C, 
minimum temperature from 10.7 to 25.1 °C, while relative 
humidity varied from 84–94% (RH-I) and 38–83% (RH-II). The 
experimental soil was deep black clay (Kanhar) with high water-
holding capacity. The experiment was laid out in a Randomized 
Complete Block Design (RBD) with three replications and twelve 
treatments. Seedlings were transplanted on 24 July 2025 at a 
spacing of 20 × 10 cm. The recommended fertilizer dose (RDF) 
of 120:60:40 N:P₂O₅:K₂O kg ha⁻¹ was followed , with nitrogen 
applied at 50% , 67%, 75% and 100% of the RDF according to 
treatment (Table 1). Full doses of phosphorus and potassium were 
applied as SSP and MOP at transplanting. Nitrogen was supplied 
through urea in three splits (30% basal , 40% at maximum 
tillering, and 30% at panicle initiation). Nano nitrogen was 
sprayed twice, at maximum tillering (9 September) and panicle 
initiation (19 September), as per the treatment schedule. The crop 
was harvested at full maturity on 13 November 2025 (113 days 
after transplanting). Border rows were removed before harvesting 
the net plot area. The harvested produce was sun -dried for seven 
days, manually threshed on 21 –22 November , winnowed and 
dried further to achieve a grain moisture content of 12–13%. 
 
Soil sampling and analysis 
Soil samples were collected from the 0 –0.15 m depth before 
sowing and after completion of the two cropping cycles. The 
collected samples were air -dried, finely ground , and sieved 
through a 2 mm (100 -mesh) sieve before analysis. Soil pH was 
determined in a 1:2 soil -to-water suspension using a glass 
electrode pH meter following the method of Potentio metric [24]. 
Electrical conductivity (EC) was measured in the soil –water 
suspension after 24 h of equilibration using a conductimetric 
method [7]. Soil organic carbon (OC) was estimated by the 
Walkley and Black method [32]. Available nitrogen 
(N) was determined using the alkaline permanganate method [30], 
while available phosphorus (P) was analyzed in 0.5 M sodium 
bicarbonate extract according to Olsen [21]. Available potassium 
(K) was extracted with 1.0 N neutral ammonium acetate 
extractant [25]. Available micronutrients, including Zn, Cu, Fe and 
Mn, were determined using the 0.005 M DTPA extraction method 
[16], whereas available boron (B) was estimated by the hot water 
extraction method [6]. The initial chemical characteristics of the 
experimental soil are presented in Table 2.

Element analysis 
Grain and straw samples collected at harvest of rice and wheat 
crops were analyzed for N , P and K content using the Kjeldahl 
distillation unit [8], vanado-molybdophosphoric acid color method 
[14] and flame photometric methods respectively [8].

Economics 
The economics of cultivation were worked out by considering 
various variable costs , including expenses incurred on field 
preparation, seeds, fertilizers and labour, based on the prevailing 
market rates and expressed in rupees per hectare (Rs ha⁻¹). Gross 
returns were calculated by multiplying the grain and straw yields 
by their respective current market prices, as suggested by Perin et 
al [23], and were expressed in rupees per hectare (Rs ha⁻¹). Net 
returns per hectare were determined by deducting the total cost of 
cultivation from the gross returns and expressed in rupees per 
hectare (Rs ha⁻¹). The net benefit:cost ratio was calculated as the 
ratio of gross returns to the cost of cultivation using the following 
formula: Net benefit:cost ratio = Net returns (Rs ha⁻¹) / Cost of 
cultivation (Rs ha⁻¹). 
 
Results and Discussion 
Nutrient uptake 
The data on nitrogen (N) , phosphorus (P) and potassium (K) 
uptake in grain and straw are presented in Figures 1. Application 
of Nano N significantly influenced the uptake of N , P and K by 
the rice crop. Total nitrogen uptake ranged from 42.94 to 96.28 
kg ha⁻¹, with the highest total N uptake (96.28 kg ha⁻¹) recorded 
under 100% NPK + 2 sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@ 0.4%) at 45–
50 DAT and 60–65 DAT (T12), followed by 100% NPK (T5) and 
75% N + 100% PK + 2 sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@ 0.4%) at 
45–50 DAT and 60–65 DAT (T10), which were found to be at par 
with each other, whereas the lowest N uptake (42.94 kg ha⁻¹) was 
observed under the control ( T1). Similarly , total phosphorus 
uptake ranged from 3.93 to 9.83 kg ha⁻¹, with the highest P uptake 
(9.83 kg ha⁻¹) recorded under 100% NPK + 2 sprays of Nano Urea 
Plus (@ 0.4%) at 45–50 DAT and 60–65 DAT (T12), followed by 
75% N + 100% PK + 2 sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@ 0.4%) at 
45–50 DAT and 60 –65 DAT ( T10) and 100% NPK (T5), which 
were found to be at par with each other; the lowest P uptake (3.93 
kg ha⁻¹) was recorded under the control ( T1). Total potassium 
uptake ranged from 49.53 to 130.09 kg ha⁻¹ , and the highest K 
uptake (130.09 kg ha⁻¹) was observed under 100% NPK + 2 
sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@ 0.4%) at 45–50 DAT and 60–65 
DAT (T12), followed by 100% NPK (T5) and 75% N + 100% PK 
+ 2 sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@ 0.4%) at 45–50 DAT and 60 –
65 DAT ( T10), which were found to be at par with each other , 
while the lowest K uptake (49.53 kg ha⁻¹) was recorded under the 
control (T1). The higher uptake of N, P, and K under the integrated 
application of soil -applied fertilizers and foliar Nano Urea Plus 
may be attributed to the corresponding improvement in grain and 
straw yield , adequate nutrient availability , and synergistic 
interactions among N, P and K. Moreover, nano fertilizers possess 
a larger surface area and nano -sized particles , which facilitate 
their penetration and absorption into plant tissues , thereby 
enhancing nutrient uptake. The recommended dose of potassium 
also ensured an adequate supply of K and maintained proper 
potassium concentration in the plant , while the combined and 
synergistic effects of N , P and K contributed to greater nutrient 
uptake compared with the control treatment [5, 22, 33].

Nutrient use efficiency in rice crop 
Nitrogen fertilizer doses and application methods significantly 
(table 3) influenced nitrogen use efficiency indices in rice ,

APE from 65.06 to 90.19 kg kg⁻¹ , ARE from 38.66 to 49.63% , 
and EU from 61.80 to 96.41 kg kg⁻¹. The treatment comprising 
50% N and 100% P and K + two foliar sprays of Nano Urea Plus 
(@0.4%) at 45–50 and 60– 65 DAT (T6) recorded the highest AE 
(43.76 kg kg⁻¹), PE (198.68 kg kg⁻¹), APE (90.19 kg kg⁻¹) and EU

The lowest AE (28.53 kg kg⁻¹) and ARE (38.66%) were observed

recorded under 50% N and 100% P and K (T2) and the lowest EU

spray at 60 –65 DAT ( T11). Overall , foliar app lication of Nano 
Urea Plus improved nitrogen use efficiency compared with 
conventional nitrogen application by enhancing nutrient 
absorption, reducing nitrogen losses and increasing rice grain 
yield [2, 28, 19, 32].

Conclusion 
The results indicate that Nano Urea application significantly 
influenced the uptake of major nutrients (N, P and K) in rice. The 
highest nitrogen and potassium uptake was recorded under 100% 
NPK + two sprays of Nano Urea Plus , while the highest 
phosphorus uptake was observed under 100% NPK. In contrast , 
the lowest nutrient uptake was recorded in the control treatment. 
Overall, the integration of Nano Urea with recommended 
fertilizer doses enhanced nutrient uptake in r ice, thereby 
improving nutrient availability and utilization by the crop. The 
results also demonstrate that nitrogen dose and application 
method significantly influenced nitrogen use efficiency in rice. 
Application of 50% N with 100% P and K along with two foliar 
sprays of Nano Urea Plus (@0.4%) at 45 –50 and 60 – 65 DAT 
(T6) achieved the highest agronomic efficiency , physiological 
efficiency, agro- physiological efficiency and utilization 
efficiency, while 75% N with 100% P and K plus two Nano Urea 
Plus sprays ( T10) recorded the highest apparent recovery 
efficiency. In contrast , the conventional application of 100% 
recommended N, P and K showed comparatively lower nitrogen 
use efficiency, indicating that partial substitution of conventional 
nitrogen with Nano Urea Plus foliar sprays can improve nitrogen 
use efficiency in rice. Economic analysis showed that Nano Urea 
combined with recommended fertilizers increased profitability. 
The results showed that the application of Nano Urea along with 
50% NPK, 67% NPK or 75% NPK through inorganic fertilizers 
resulted in higher gross returns , net returns and B:C ratios. 
Overall, the integration of Nano Urea was economically 
beneficial for rice cultivation.
