Health systems have been developed on the basis of vertical disease-based programmes, but with the spread of multimorbidity and cardiovascular disease (CVD) in low and middle income countries (LMICs), there is a great deal of challenge. The co-curricula of communicable and non-communicable diseases (NCDs) have led to the occurrence of complex multimorbidity patterns with communicable diseases, like tuberculosis and HIV and non-communicable diseases such as cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, chronic respiratory diseases, mental health disorders. This highlights the need to shift from episodic and reactive approaches to health care provision to a personalized, life course and sound health systems approach that can facilitate the care of chronic conditions. Strategies include improving primary health care services, decentralization, one-stop screening clinics, task shifting to nurse or community health workers and standardised cardiovascular risk assessment. The need to highlight essential medicines, digital health technologies, electronic medical records, telemedicine and universal health coverage to enhance continuity of care and minimize financial hurdles also should be stressed. Socio-economic factors in LMICs, such as food insecurity, environmental exposures, health literacy and poverty also affect disease burden and access to health care. The community-based models integrated with their respective countries’ primary health care programs and successfully implemented in case studies conducted in Brazil, Thailand, Rwanda, sub-Saharan Africa, and in the WHO HEARTS initiative are clearly effective in improving cardiovascular outcomes and chronic disease management, on a large scale. Last, political will, sustainable funding, multisectoral cooperation, and implementation science research will be needed if a health system is going to develop resilience and successfully tackle multimorbidity and premature cardiovascular death in resource-limited countries.
