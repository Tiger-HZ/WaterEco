Ecological role of foraminifera as bioindicators of eutrophication 
and heavy metal pollution: a case study of Estany dels Ponts Lagoon 
(Mallorca, Spain)
Anna Khokhlova1 · Ermelinda del Buono2 · Pere Ferriol1 · Stefania Squadrone3 · Alessandra Griglione3 · 
Lorenzo Casanova4 · Valentina Fonseca1 · Guillem Mateu‑Vicens1

Abstract
The Estany dels Ponts is a coastal lagoon in northeastern Mallorca, Spain, currently undergoing a restoration project 
focused on cataloging its biota and characterizing the ecological and physicochemical conditions of its water column 
and sediments. This study analyzes the total foraminiferal assemblage in 16 sediment samples collected throughout 
the lagoon and investigates its relationship with nutrient and element concentrations, including heavy metals and rare 
earth elements (REEs). Foraminifera, as key bioindicators, were examined (2900 individuals belonging to 140 species) 
to assess their distribution patterns and environmental interactions. Despite significant anthropogenic modifications 
since the mid-twentieth century, the lagoon maintains a relatively good conservation status, exhibiting meso-eutrophic 
to eutrophic conditions, likely driven by wastewater infiltration during peak tourist seasons. These conditions influ-
ence sediment chemistry through organic matter deposition, favoring the dominance of tolerant species. The sediment 
composition reveals spatial variability in nutrient and heavy metal concentrations, with urban wastewater identified as 
the primary pollution source. Although most metal concentrations remain within safe thresholds, nickel levels exceed 
ecological limits, posing potential risks, particularly in the inner lagoon. The foraminiferal community, dominated by 
Ammonia aberdoveyensis, reflects adaptability to fluctuating salinity and temperature. Species diversity is comparable 
to other Cymodocea nodosa-dominated habitats. Despite the high organic matter content and presence of heavy metals 
in sediments, no significant pollution-related effects were detected, as no foraminiferal test deformities were observed.

1 Introduction
The anthropic effect caused by uncontrolled tourist development can have significant impacts on the environment. A clear 
example is the alteration suffered by the coastal areas of the Balearic Islands since the 1960s. The impact of this change has 
not only affected the coastline but also the water quality and seagrass meadows, as reflected by the evolution of foraminiferal 
assemblages, which not only demonstrate the impact but also the positive effect of mitigation measures [1, 2].
Coastal lagoons are another ecosystem affected by uncontrolled tourism development. These habitats represent eco-
logically significant points due to their uniqueness and the fact that they host particularly adapted species. This ecological 
importance has been the reason for their protection within the framework of European legislation.
The inclusion of the Water Framework Directive [3] in European protective regulations, along with the subsequent devel-
opment of several directives and laws, as well as national and regional regulations based on it, has sparked increased inter-
est in studying the condition of natural water bodies and in seeking bioindicators for their monitoring [4]. The WFD aims to 
achieve a “good status” for all EU waters by setting clear objectives and deadlines for water protection and restoration. This 
directive emphasizes the use of ecological and chemical indicators to assess the health of aquatic ecosystems and ensures 
that water bodies are managed sustainably across Europe [5, 6].
In response to these regulatory developments, there is an increasing focus on identifying reliable bioindicators—organ-
isms or biological responses that can signal changes in the environment—to monitor the quality of water bodies. Bioindica-
tors are essential because they can provide early warnings of ecological stress, pollution, or other adverse conditions affecting 
aquatic ecosystems, or inform about some negative influence in the past [4].
One of these coastal lagoons is the so called Estany dels Ponts. This site, listed in the Balearic Inventory of Wetlands with 
the code MAZH06, features a coastal lagoon that evolved from a much more extensive lagoon complex, so called s’Albufera, 
before its transformation in the 19th century. In the 20th century, the Estany dels Ponts and much of the surrounding wetland 
underwent profound changes, drastically altering its functions and values as a wetland.
As part of a larger lagoon complex, the Estany dels Ponts is linked with other water bodies like the remnants of the old the 
Estany Gran and Cibollar Lagoon, as well as several smaller, less identifiable lagoons. While the Cibollar Lagoon, a deep coastal 
lagoon with meromictic characteristics, has been extensively studied due to its unique biological community [7–10], there is 
limited and often fragmented information on the biotic and abiotic components of the Estany dels Ponts [11–13]. The water 
body of the lagoon system receives inputs from torrents, primarily the Torrent de Sant Miquel, which form the watershed 
located west of the study area. These torrents collect and distribute rainfall, as well as a portion of the water originating from 
the Serra de Tramuntana. Rainwater infiltrates through an aquifer composed mainly of Quaternary silts and sands, along with 
Lias (Lower Jurassic) limestones and dolomites [14, 15].
Given its significant natural value and the ecosystem services it provides, recent studies of the Estany dels Ponts have 
become particularly timely due to the intense anthropogenic pressures affecting this system. Highlighting its importance, 
a recently approved restoration project for the wetland, led by the Alcúdia City Council in collaboration with WWF and the 
University of the Balearic Islands (UIB), and funded by NextGenerationEU (2023-2025), has been initiated. This project includes 
cataloging the aquatic biota present in the lagoon, mapping, and ecological characterization of the water body and its sedi-
ments in terms of physicochemical parameters.
Through this comprehensive evaluation of the Estany dels Ponts’ ecological status from 2019, new information is pro-
vided such a high-resolution bathymetric characterization of the lagoon, the distribution of plants and macroalgae and a 
preliminary environmental evaluation. However, a more detailed and consistent analysis is needed regarding the bioindicator 
organisms. Foraminifera are shelled protozoans that serve as valuable indicators of environmental conditions due to their 
sensitivity to changes in water quality and sediment composition [16–20]. Due to their well-documented ecologies and the 
known stress tolerances of several species, the analysis of foraminiferal assemblages has proven valuable for assessing and 
monitoring coastal and shelf environments [16, 19–26].
Thus, the present work aims to offer a comprehensive characterization of the foraminiferal association and its application, 
along with the analysis of physico-chemical parameters, to evaluate the ecological state of the Estany dels Ponts.

2 Materials and methods
2.1 Study area
The Estany dels Ponts is a coastal lagoon placed on the NE of Mallorca (Balearic Islands, Spain). It is located adjacent to 
the dried-up the Estany Gran, near the town of Alcúdia, UTM EE-097085 (Fig.  1). The lagoon is connected to the sea in 
the north, while in the south, it is linked to the Canal des Cibollar, which leads directly to s’Oberta (Gran Canal) and, via 
a small diversion, to the Estany des Cibollar [11].
The Estany dels Ponts has a maximum length and width of 1515 m and 340 m, respectively, covering an area of 
0.3911  km2.The lagoon is enclosed by dune strips and displayed parallelly to the coast [11, 27]. The lagoon’s morpho-
metry has remained relatively unchanged since the late 1980’s, maintaining a practically flat bottom with some very 
localized slopes (Fig.  2A). During a preliminary survey phase, the bathymetric characterization (Fig.  2A) of the lagoon

was carried out using a Lowrance HOOK Reveal 5 sonar system equipped with a 50/200 HDI transducer. This instrument, 
in addition to providing depth data, allows for an approximate estimation of bottom hardness (Fig. 2B) based on signal 
reflectivity [28– 30]. This approach establishes the association between hard bottoms and consolidated rock or coarse 
textures (gravels and coarse sands), and between soft bottoms and loose sediments with finer textures (medium to fine 
sands, silts, muds, etc.). Most of the lagoon is dominated by loose sediments, featuring considerable sand as well as silt 
and clay. These finer sediments, rich in silt and clay, characterize the two deepest zones, which exceed 2 meters, and are 
also present in various patches in the central part of the lake [31].
With an average depth of around 1 meter, the lagoon facilitates a significant exchange of gases with the atmosphere 
and supports the development of substantial benthic vegetation due to the high light exposure across most of the bot-
tom. Depths exceed two meters in only two areas where the low light intensity reaching the bottom prevents vegetation 
growth. In a northern section of the lagoon, depths reach 1.5 meters, allowing the growth of macrophytes, specifically a 
community dominated by the green algal species Caulerpa prolifera, due to greater marine influence and light penetra-
tion in this area.
Due to its shallow depth, the Estany dels Ponts has a submerged macrophyte coverage exceeding 60% of its total 
area, with two very characteristic communities standing out (Fig.  3). According to Ferriol et al. [31] the area closest to 
the marine water inlet, towards the north, is dominated by a community of photophilous algae, mainly the large-sized 
Caulerpa prolifera, which hosts a high population of the anemone Paranemonia cinerea. Conversely, as the distance from 
the marine inlet increases towards the southern part of the lagoon, the Caulerpa community transitions into an inter -
esting mixed community, composed of the marine seagrass Cymodocea nodosa along with the euryhaline submerged 
macrophyte Potamogeton pectinatus. This community exhibits very high densities, and in the case of C. nodosa, the length 
of the shoots is significantly greater than those found in coastal meadows.

2.3 Collection method and sample treatment
Representative sediment samples (one replicate per site) from various environments of the lagoon (Fig. 6) were collected 
using a "Riverside" auger sampler from a semi-rigid boat equipped with an electric motor and GPS plotter probe, during 
July–August 2022. Samples (~ 100 g) were manually collected from the upper 2-cm layer and placed in plastic bottles, 
which were then directly transported to the laboratory and stored in a refrigerator until further geochemical analysis.
In the analysis of foraminiferal associations, we utilized the total assemblage (living + dead) approach [37]. This strat-
egy was implemented to prevent damage to the Cymodocea seagrass habitat, which predominates the lagoon bottom 
and is protected under European legislation (EU Habitats Directive 92/43/EEC, Barcelona Convention SPA/BD Protocol, 
1995). Investigating live foraminifera with an epiphytic lifestyle requires the collection of a statistically significant sam-
ple, which entails the destructive harvesting of a considerable number of plant shoots for subsequent processing and 
isolation of live foraminifera.
In some environments, such as degraded seagrass meadows or locations of particular significance—like the site of 
this study—certain methodologies may inadvertently alter the habitat intended for protection. To address this issue, we

adopted the total assemblage approach, as recommended in previous studies [19, 20, 38], which has been shown to yield 
valid results while significantly minimizing ecological impact. This methodology also allows for sampling foraminiferal 
species living on the surface and in the uppermost part of the substrate. To ensure that the sample is as representative 
as possible of in situ conditions and to avoid incorporating allochthonous specimens, only live foraminifera or those with 
pristine shells and no signs of reworking were considered.
To perform the analysis a subsample of each sample was used. The subsamples were initially treated with hydrogen 
peroxide solution for 24 h to eliminate organic matter attached to the foraminiferal tests. The sediments were thoroughly 
washed with tap water over a 0.063 mm mesh sieve and left to dry. Foraminifera were separated using a camera-equipped 
Leica MZ16 binocular stereomicroscope (camera Leica DFC295). Around one hundred and fifty foraminifera were identi-
fied per replicate sample. Taxonomic identifications were based on [39– 45]. Nomenclature was updated according to 
the latest version of WoRMS [46].
2.4 Foraminiferal assemblage diversity (Shannon and Margalef) and bioindicator (Ep, FI’ , ILS, FSI) indices
Foraminiferal assemblage diversity was analyzed using the Shannon index (H’) and the Margalef index (Dmg). The key 
difference between these indices lies in their approach to measuring diversity: H’ considers both species richness (the 
number of species) and evenness (how individuals are distributed across species), while Dmg focuses primarily on spe -
cies richness in relation to the total number of individuals in a sample. These indices were calculated using the PAST 
(PAlaeontological STatistics) data analysis package.
Besides the taxonomic classification, foraminifera were analyzed according to their ecological preferences, particularly 
their epiphytic behaviour sensu Mateu-Vicens et al. [19] and their response to organic matter content [47]. To determine 
the abundance of epiphytic forms in the foraminiferal association, the Ep index (number of epiphytes relative to the total 
amount of individuals-[19]) has been calculated for each sample. Those taxa with epiphytic behavior have been grouped 
under the different morphotypes representing life span, feeding strategy and motility [17, 42] (Fig. 7). The bioindicator 
indices were calculated based on ecological classification. The indices used in this study include the modified FORAM 
(Foraminifera in Reef Assessment and Monitoring) Index (FI’), the “Long vs. Short Life Span” Index (ILS), calculated by 
Khokhlova [48] and Mateu-Vicens et al. [19] using only epiphytic species, and the Foram Stress Index (FSI), as defined by 
Dimiza et al. [47] for the total foraminiferal assemblage.

2.5 Sediment chemical analysis
Sediment samples were dried at 60 °C, homogenized, and divided into replicates. The organic carbon (OC) content 
of sediment samples was determined using a standardized calculation approach. Total carbon (TC) was measured via 
dry combustion, following the ISO 10694 method, in which sediment samples were combusted at high temperatures, 
and the resulting CO₂ was quantified using a thermal conductivity detector. Inorganic carbon (IC) was determined 
separately based on the ISO 10693 method, wherein sediment samples were treated with acid to release CO₂ from 
carbonates, and the carbonate content was calculated volumetrically. The organic carbon content was obtained by 
subtracting IC from TC. Additionally, sediment moisture was measured gravimetrically following ISO 11465, ensuring 
accurate sample preparation and weight correction. Total nitrogen (%) was analyzed using the persulfate digestion 
method [49] and quantified via the vanadium reduction colorimetric method [50]. Total phosphorus was measured 
following the method of Aspila et al. [51], after ignition at 550°C and treatment with 1N HCl, while inorganic phospho -
rus was determined using the same acid without ignition. Organic phosphorus was calculated as total phosphorus 
minus inorganic phosphorus. Samples were digested on an orbital shaker with HCl, centrifuged, and the supernatant 
was analyzed.
Subsequently, the C:N, N:Porganic, and C:Porganic ratios were calculated to determine the C:N:P relationship or Redfield 
ratio [52], which will help to discern the origin of the organic matter in the lagoon.
2.6 Determination of chemical elements in sediments
Geochemical analysis was carried out on the sediments to determine the concentrations of major elements (typically 
> 1 wt% in rocks), trace elements (typically < 0.1 wt%, measured in ppm), and rare earth elements (REEs) (a subset of 
trace elements). The major elements in this study are Al and Fe; the trace elements, including heavy metals, are As, Cd, 
Cr, Cu, Mn, Ni, Pb, Zn, V, Co, Rb, In, Tl, Sc, Y. Among these, the REEs are represented by La, Ce, Pr, Nd, Sm, Eu, Gd, Tb, Dy, 
Ho, Er, Tm, Yb, and Lu. To perform the analysis the rest of the sediment samples were subjected to microwave digestion 
utilizing an ultra-wave oven (ETHOS 1, Milestone,) with 7 mL of HNO3 (70% v/v) and 1.5 mL of H2O2 (30% v/v). All the ele-
ments were measured by Inductively Coupled Plasma-Mass Spectrometry (Agilent 7800), following a standard protocol 
[53]. The limit of quantification (LOQ) for trace elements was 0.010 mg.kg-1 and for REEs 0.010 mg⋅ kg-1. The analytical 
methods were validated according to UNI CEI EN ISO/IEC 17025 (General Requirements for the Competence of Testing 
and Calibration Laboratories).
2.7 Statistical analysis
For statistical analysis Paleontological Statistics (PAST) software package v. 4.14 [54] was applied. Prior to conducting the 
analysis, the data were transformed using the log (X+1) function to reduce magnitude differences between variables. 
Two-way hierarchical clustering using the Ward’s method and Euclidean distance measurement were also carried out on 
the datasets, in which Q-mode is used for clustering samples and R-mode for clustering variables (species) [55]. In this case 
R-mode refers to the clustering of species with abundances > 3% in at least on sample. To determine the robustness of 
the clusters, a cluster analysis with Bootstrap support (100 iterations) was performed separately in Q-mode and R-mode.
To determine if there is a correlation of the more abundant species from each sample, sediment parameters (organic 
carbon, total nitrogen, inorganic phosphorus, organic phosphorus), ecological (FI’ , ILS, FSI), and biodiversity indices (Shan-
non H, Margalef ) a Pearson analysis was performed. Principal Components Analysis (PCA) was calculated to determine 
the correlation between the spatial variation of the foraminiferal indices (FI’ , ILS, FSI), biodiversity indices (Shannon H, 
Margalef ), and sediment parameters (organic carbon, total nitrogen, inorganic phosphorus, organic phosphorus). The 
abovementioned diversity indices were also calculated using the PAST.

3 Results
3.1 Nutrient concentration in sediments
The concentrations of organic carbon, total nitrogen, and organic phosphorus exhibit a similar distribution pattern 
(Fig. 8A, B, D; Table  1), with higher values in the northern half of the lagoon, including the samples along the north-
south gradient (D1, B2, D3, C4, B4, C6, D7, and B7). However, particularly high concentrations of nitrogen and organic 
phosphorus stand out at C6 and D1, respectively. The far southern end of the lagoon, which includes sampling sites C14 
and C15, also shows notably high concentrations of these nutrients, especially at C15, which coincides with the inflow 
point of channels carrying water from the Albufera wetland. In the southern half, excluding C14 and C15, concentrations 
are intermediate to low.
Regarding inorganic phosphorus (Fig.  8C; Table 1), its concentrations are low throughout most of the lagoon, with 
slightly higher values at the site closest to the connection with the sea (D1), along the western margin, and at the south-
ern end (B12, C14, C15), particularly at D13. In the Redfield ratios calculated from the C:N, N:P org, and C:Porg ratios, the C 
values range between 521 and 241, except for sample D13, which shows an unusually low value of 5. The N values range 
from 16 to 42.
3.2 Sediment geochemical analysis
The highest values of the elements concentrations are reported in the innermost part of the lagoon around B7 (see Table 2 
and Fig. S1 in Supplementary information 1). Exceptions were found for Cu, with the highest concentration in the north 
around D1, and for Sc and Ti, with the highest values in the eastern part (D1, D7, D11, D13).

The average heavy-metal concentrations from the the Estany dels Ponts are within natural ranges or below, accord-
ing to the values of ERL (Effects Range-Low) of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), which is 
consistent with low risk levels for most metals (Table 3).
However, there are moderate to high-risk values for nickel (Ni) concentrations in specific areas, such as B7 (23.7 mg/
kg) and A4 (84.7 mg/kg), which exceed the ERM (Effects Range-Median) values, indicating a high likelihood of adverse 
effects on marine organisms in those regions. Arsenic (As), copper (Cu), manganese (Mn), and vanadium (V) present 
concentrations above the ERL but below the ERM, suggesting potentially moderate risks. For instance, arsenic values in 
B2 (8.7 mg/kg), B7 (15.0 mg/kg), and D3 (9.3 mg/kg) slightly exceed the reference for natural concentrations (8.2 mg/kg). 
Similarly, copper in D1 (36.2 mg/kg) and D3 (34.6 mg/kg), and for vanadium in B7 (61.31 mg/kg) slightly exceeded the 
reference concentrations for natural conditions. Manganese exhibited uniformly elevated values throughout the lagoon, 
ranging from 70 mg/kg to 472 mg/kg, with average moderately polluted sediment values ranging from 56 to 510 mg/kg. 
For the less common metals and rare earth elements, reference values are less established, and natural concentrations 
can vary significantly.
3.3 Foraminiferal assemblage analysis
Data collected from 16 points across different areas of the lagoon were analyzed to gather information about the spe -
cies composition of epiphytic foraminifera. A total of almost 2900 individuals from 140 foraminiferal species, belonging 
to 28 families (Dataset_1.xls in Supplementary information and Supplementary Figure. S2), were identified in the total 
assemblage. Seventeen species occur with abundances over 3% in at least one sample and count for total abundances 
ranging between 52.85% (sample D1) and 96.62% (sample C14) (Table  4).
Foraminiferal assemblage in the lagoon is largely dominated by Ammonia aberdoveyensis, followed, at a much lower 
abundance, by Lobatula lobatula, A. inflata, Cibicides refulgens, Rosalina obtusa, R. globularis, and R. floridana among 
the calcareous perforate forms (hyaline), and Quinqueloculina ungeriana , Triloculina marioni, T. trigonula, T. oblonga and 
Nubecularia lucifuga among the porcelaneous forms (Table 4). Ammonia aberdoveyensis stands out as the principal species 
in most of the samples, comprising over 50% in 11 out of 16 samples (Table 4). This high abundance of A. aberdoveyensis is 
indicative of variable salinity and eventual low-oxygen periods [56]. The next most abundant species, L. lobatula, belongs 
to the epiphytic morphotype B. Morphotype C is represented by keeled species of genus Elphidium, E. advena (up to 
4.72%) and E. macellum (up to 3.15%). Morphotype A, represented by genus Nubecularia, is the scarcest one, occurring 
only in 9 sites, and ranging, when present, up to 5.59%.

4 Discussion
This discussion synthesizes the nutrient and geochemical analysis in the sediment including heavy metal and REEs con-
centrations to provide a comprehensive understanding of environmental dynamics within the Estany dels Ponts lagoon 
and how it affects the composition and distribution of the foraminiferal assemblage.
4.1 Nutrient and elemental characteristics of sediments
The sediment composition of the Estany dels Ponts lagoon reveals significant spatial variability in nutrient and element 
concentrations. The spatial distribution of organic carbon (C), total nitrogen (N), and organic phosphorus (P) concen-
trations suggests a higher accumulation of organic matter in the northern half of the lagoon and in the southernmost 
sampling site (C15). This latter point receives water input from the complex network of channels and lagoons connected 
to the Albufera.

phytoplankton (106:16:1) but slightly lower than the values reported for seagrasses (550:30:1) [57]. However, the 
obtained values closely resemble those recorded in aquatic macrophyte detritus (383–315:16–13:1) [58] and in bacterial

Sampling site D13 represents a notable deviation from the observed general trend. The calculated Redfield ratio

Sediments serve as critical indicators of environmental contamination as they accumulate heavy metals over time, 
offering a historical record of pollution events [15]. While metals like Cr, Co, Cu, Fe, Mn, Mo, Ni, Se, and Zn are essential 
for biological functions and naturally occur in the environment, their excessive concentrations can become toxic [60]. 
Conversely, metals such as As, Sb, Cd, Pb, Hg, Ti, Ag, and Sn, which are not biologically essential, can signify anthropo -
genic contamination [61].
In the Estany dels Ponts lagoon, despite the absence of direct industrial discharges, heavy metal contamination has 
been largely influenced by decades of anthropogenic activities, such as urban wastewater and the discharge of ashes 
from power plants, as well as concrete and rubble [27]. Establishing baseline levels for these metals is critical for assess-
ing the degree of pollution and its ecological impact [62].
Reference values for heavy metals, such as those provided by NOAA, are essential for identifying harmful concentra-
tions. Sediment Quality Guidelines (SQGs), like TEL (Threshold Effect Level) and PEL (Probable Effect Level), help assess 
the likelihood of adverse effects on marine organisms [63]. The ERL (Effects Range-Low) and ERM (Effects Range-Median) 
values provide benchmarks to evaluate the potential ecological risks of metal concentrations. Heavy metal concentra-
tions in the Estany dels Ponts lagoon vary significantly across different locations. The highest overall concentrations were 
found in the innermost part of the lagoon (B7), except for certain metals like Cu, which peaked in the northern region 
(D1), and Sc and Ti, which were more concentrated in the eastern samples (D1, D7, D11, D13). Most metal concentrations 
fell within natural ranges or below NOAA’s ERL values, suggesting a generally low ecological risk. However, nickel (Ni) 
concentrations at stations B7 and A4 exceeded ERM values, indicating a high likelihood of adverse effects on marine life 
in these regions. Arsenic (As), copper (Cu), manganese (Mn), and vanadium (V) presented moderate risks, with concentra-
tions above ERL but below ERM. These findings highlight the need for continued monitoring and mitigation strategies 
to address metal pollution, particularly in areas with elevated concentrations.
4.2 Foraminiferal assemblage and environmental conditions
The R-mode cluster analysis highlights the pronounced dominance of the Ammonia aberdoveyensis in the study area, 
as it exclusively forms Cluster 1. This suggests that A. aberdoveyensis is a key taxon, being dominant under the local 
environmental conditions that are characterized by seasonal variability mostly in temperature and salinity. In contrast, 
Clusters 2 and 3 comprise taxa commonly associated with an epiphytic lifestyle [19, 64–66]. This correlation is consistent 
with the widespread presence of vegetation across the bottom of Estany dels Ponts, supporting a habitat for epiphytic 
foraminifera. In fact, although cluster 2 includes species typically considered sediment dwellers (Ammonia beccarii, A. 
inflata), they exhibit an epiphytic behavior, at least seasonally [67]. In contrast, in Q-mode, cluster B includes the sam-
ples with the greatest influence from the epiphytic community and the best environmental indicators (higher diversity 
values and FSI scores).
Coastal lagoon environments, like the Estany dels Ponts lagoon, present highly variable conditions for benthic 
foraminifera due to intense seasonal and diel (day-night) fluctuations in temperature, salinity, and water quality. Such vari-
ability can be stressful for the foraminiferal assemblages [17], challenging their physiological limits and significantly influ-
encing their distribution [19]. Despite these challenging conditions, the species richness (number of species) recorded in 
the Estany dels Ponts lagoon is comparable to that found in other seagrass habitats, such as Posidonia oceanica meadows 
in the Mediterranean [19, 68–71] and Posidonia species in Australia [38]. However, it is notably higher than that reported 
in Halophila stipulacea meadows in the Red Sea [72] and Cymodocea-dominated environments in the Mediterranean 
[73]. Despite variations in species richness across these studies, the diversity indices (H’ and Margalef ) from Estany dels 
Ponts are consistent with the ranges observed in similar environments. In contrast, lower diversity (H’ values below 2) is 
observed in Lago Varano (SE Italy), where Cymodocea nodosa and Nanozostera noltii dominate [74].

There is limited literature describing and analyzing foraminiferal assemblages associated with Mediterranean Cymo -
docea meadows, especially when compared to the extensive research focused on Posidonia oceanica. One of the most 
detailed studies on Cymodocea-associated foraminiferal assemblages is by Blanc-Vernet [68], which identified important 
compositional similarities with Posidonia oceanica meadows, while also highlighting notable differences that give this 
assemblage a distinct character. In southern France, Blanc-Vernet observed that miliolids dominate these assemblages, 
followed by cibicidids, rosalinids-discorbids, and elphidiids. Other groups such as rotaliids, peneroplids, planorbulinids, 
nubeculariids, and nonionids occur less frequently, always constituting less than 10% of the assemblage.
In contrast, in the Estany dels Ponts lagoon, A. aberdoveyensis dominate (Table 4 and Dataset_1.xls in Supplementary 
information), while hauerinids (which correspond to miliolids in Blanc-Vernet’s study) are less abundant. The abundance 
patterns of other taxa in the lagoon are consistent with Blanc-Vernet’s observations. Blanc-Vernet suggested that the 
presence of homotrematids, planorbulinids, and nubeculariids indicated the influence of nearby Posidonia oceanica 
meadows. In the Estany dels Ponts lagoon, the presence of N. lucifuga among the more abundant taxa, and the encrust-
ing species P . mediterranensis and Miniacina miniacea (Morphotype A*, [19]), further underscores the influence of marine 
conditions, despite the lagoon’s limited connection to the open sea. These species likely enter the lagoon through marine 
currents, high-energy events, or both.
The high salinities observed during the sampling period [31] in the Estany dels Ponts lagoon (41.0 PSU), linked to 
intense summer evaporation, can significantly influence foraminiferal distribution. This salinity measurement contrasts 
with previous reports from the same site, which included broader seasonal data with values as low as 24.12 PSU during 
winter and more humid months [75]. These salinity variability aligns with the dominance of the euryhaline species A. 
aberdoveyensis, known for their ability to tolerate wide salinity ranges [67, 76], and low occurrences of A. beccarii. This 
last species, although preferably associated with normal marine conditions, can also be found in habitats with salinities 
lower than 20 PSU [ 67]. The prevalence of A. beccarii has also been reported in other Cymodocea-dominated environ -
ments in the Mediterranean, with salinity levels reaching up to 39.1 PSU (Tunisia–[73]).
The dominance of A. aberdoveyensis points to its remarkable tolerance of environmental disturbances, including low 
oxygen [19, 56]. This prevalence suggests potential stress in parts of the lagoon, a conclusion supported by lower values 
of Ep, FSI and diversity indices. The negative correlation between this species and these indices highlights its ability to 
thrive in highly variable, stressful environments, where it outcompetes more sensitive, stenoic taxa.
The stressful conditions observed in the lagoon, characterized by high organic matter content and fluctuations in 
temperature and salinity, may be linked to both natural and anthropogenic factors. While human activities in the area 
cannot be disregarded, these conditions are also influenced by the natural organic matter inputs from its marine con-
nection and the Albufera system. The inflow of marine water, along with brackish water contributions, combined with 
seasonal variations in evaporation and precipitation, drives the marked salinity fluctuations that define the lagoon’s 
dynamic environmental conditions.
Interestingly, few specimens of mixotrophic species that host symbiotic microalgae, which are sensitive to excessive 
organic matter (morphotype SB, [19], were also observed distributted randomly along the lagoon. These taxa include 
Sorites orbiculus (D1), Peneroplis pertusus (B2, B4, B7, D1, D3), Peneroplis planatus (A4, D1), and Laevipeneroplis proteus (A4, 
B4, D13). Due to their relatively long lifespan (approximately 1 year–[64], these symbiont-bearing species require more 
stable conditions. Therefore, the observed shells might represent tests transported through the canal from Posidonia 
oceanica seagrasses in open marine conditions and subsequently redistributed throughout the lagoon by episodic high-
energy hydrodynamic events (e.g., storms or strong winds).
However, the foraminiferal assemblage does not appear to show any significant negative effects from metal pollution 
or other environmental stressors, as no deformed tests were recorded. Benthic foraminifera, including epiphytic species, 
are considered excellent bioindicators of heavy metal contamination because they can incorporate specific trace ele -
ments (such as Cd2⁺, Zn2⁺, and Cu2⁺) during calcification, which can result in various morphological abnormalities [ 17, 
20, 25, 77–80]. However, the role of heavy metals in causing foraminiferal test deformations remains unresolved [18, 81], 
as deformed tests naturally occur in low percentages (<1%) in populations [22, 82, 83]. Additionally, a variety of natural 
environmental stressors—including high temperatures [84], low dissolved oxygen levels, salinity fluctuations (especially 
hypersalinity), periodic acidification, and test reconstruction after strong hydrodynamic events—can also contribute to 
abnormal growth forms [82, 83, 85].

5 Conclusions
Despite the significant anthropogenic modifications that the Estany dels Ponts lagoon has undergone, especially since the 
second half of the 20th century, its conservation status is reasonably good. Nevertheless, it presents meso-eutrophic to 
eutrophic conditions, characterized by high primary productivity and elevated nutrient concentrations, likely influenced 
by human activities, such as the leakage of domestic wastewater during the high tourist season. While these conditions 
promote productivity, they can also cause diel and seasonal fluctuations in oxygen, favoring tolerant species in the 
foraminiferal communities and altering sediment chemistry due to the deposition of organic matter.
Furthermore, the sediment composition in Estany dels Ponts shows significant spatial variability in nutrient and heavy 
metal concentrations, with pollution largely influenced by anthropogenic activities such as urban wastewater. Although 
most metal concentrations fall within natural or safe ranges, certain metals, such as nickel, exceed levels that could be 
harmful to lagoon life, particularly in the innermost parts and certain areas to the north and east. Therefore, it is impor -
tant to establish baseline levels, monitor metal concentrations, and implement mitigation strategies to address potential 
ecological risks, especially in areas with elevated concentrations.
Foraminifera show a distribution influenced by a complex interaction of environmental factors. The Q-mode analysis 
indicates that samples associated with higher diversity indices and less stressful conditions are associated with higher 
abundances of epiphytic species. In contrast, the R-cluster analysis shows that the species Ammonia aberdoveyensis 
dominates the lagoon, suggesting that this species is key to the ecosystem due to its ability to adapt to environmental 
variability, especially in salinity and temperature.
Despite seasonal fluctuations, which favor tolerant species such as A. aberdoveyensis, species diversity in the lagoon 
is comparable to that in other habitats dominated by Cymodocea nodosa. Additionally, some mixotrophic foraminifera, 
typical of Posidonia meadows and requiring more stable conditions, are randomly distributed throughout the lagoon, 
suggesting that their shells may have been transported from nearby Posidonia oceanica areas through high-energy 
hydrodynamic events, such as storms.
As for the potential effects of metal pollution, no deformation was observed in the foraminifera, suggesting that there 
is no significant impact from metal pollution on the foraminiferal communities of the lagoon. Foraminifera are known 
bioindicators of metal pollution, but deformations in their shells can occur due to a variety of natural factors, such as 
fluctuations in temperature, dissolved oxygen, and salinity, complicating the direct link between heavy metals and shell 
anomalies. In summary, no significant negative effects from metal pollution seem to be present, and the observed spe -
cies show remarkable adaptability to the fluctuating environmental conditions in the lagoon.
