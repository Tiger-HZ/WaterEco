This UAV hyperspectral remote sensing dataset of Zhengzhou Yellow River National Wetland Park is independently collected, fully preprocessed and manually annotated by our research group. The study area is located at 113°39′37.38″ E, 34°55′3.97″, a composite tidal wetland in the middle and lower reaches of the Yellow River with intermingled natural and artificial land cover types, making it a typical testbed for wetland ecological remote sensing classification in the Yellow River Basin.Field data acquisition was carried out in October 2024. A DJI Matrice 300 RTK multi-rotor UAV equipped with a Cubert X20P airborne frame hyperspectral imaging system was adopted. The sensor covers a spectral range of 350–1000 nm with a spectral sampling interval of 4 nm, generating 164 raw spectral bands in total. The flight altitude was set to 400 m, and an S-shaped back-and-forth flight route was designed to achieve full coverage of the study area. Before each flight mission, uniform whiteboard correction and dark current correction were performed to eliminate illumination deviation and inherent sensor dark current noise, yielding a total of 314 raw hyperspectral frames.Refined radiometric preprocessing was implemented on each single raw hyperspectral frame via the official supporting software Cuvis Touch. First, the automatic integration time was locked to eliminate spectral bias induced by fluctuating ambient illumination. Radiometric calibration was conducted using pre-acquired whiteboard reference frames to convert raw sensor digital number (DN) values into surface radiance. Dark current baseline frames captured with the lens cap closed were used to subtract intrinsic circuit noise band by band. Standard frames with clear ground object textures were selected for distance correction to compensate spectral and spatial errors caused by lens optical attenuation and field-of-view distortion. After radiometric correction, Lanczos upsampling and PanRatio pan-sharpening fusion algorithms were applied to export separate single-frame hyperspectral reflectance imagery and panchromatic imagery. Band filtering was then performed to retain the experimental spectral range of 402–950 nm, producing standardized single-frame pure hyperspectral reflectance imagery with a pixel dimension of 330 × 330 and a spatial resolution of approximately 0.6 m.Photogrammetric stitching was further conducted for full-scene imagery. All single-frame panchromatic images were imported into Agisoft Metashape to complete aerial triangulation, dense point cloud reconstruction and DEM generation. The panchromatic frames were batch replaced with corresponding single-frame hyperspectral reflectance imagery for rigorous orthorectification and seamless mosaicking. A single full-scene GeoTIFF orthomosaic was generated and subsequently converted to standard ENVI format for professional spectral analysis.
