Accurate soil organic matter (SOM) monitoring is vital for agricultural sustainability. In Shandong Province, soil moisture heterogeneity and surface cover severely limit spectral inversion. To address this, we propose a ‘temporal moisture reduction - spatial unmixing’ strategy for high-resolution SOM mapping. This approach achieves dual spectral reconstruction through minimum soil moisture (MinSM) synthesis and a vegetation-crop residue reduction model. Using the Random Forest (RF) model, we achieved optimal inversion (mean R2=0.76±0.01, mean RMSE=2.25±0.03g/kg) and generated annual 10-m resolution SOM maps (2015–2025). Results reveal a spatial pattern with enrichment in northwestern plains (>16.50 g/kg) and depletion in southeastern areas (~15.00 g/kg), primarily driven by straw return and parent material. Temporally, SOM followed a trajectory from rapid policy-driven increases to high-level stabilization. This framework effectively overcomes complex surface interference, offering a generalizable technical paradigm for fine-scale soil monitoring in complex agricultural ecosystems.
