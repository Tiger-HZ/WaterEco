ABSTRACT Many countries are already contending with relative water shortage where water demand structurally exceeds availability. Demographic and economic growth combined with deteriorating catchments and climate change will affect more countries and deepen this stress. To manage allocation effectively, institutional instruments are applied, comprising regulatory, economic and collective-action measures. In a longitudinal comparative analysis, based on a literature review and structured interviews, the development paths, instruments and performance attributes of emergent regulatory frames for institutional measures are studied in nine countries.
