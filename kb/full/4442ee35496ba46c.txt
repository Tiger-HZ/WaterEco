ABSTRACT Decentralised rainwater systems are well established in Thailand but remain poorly understood in coastal settlements with brackish groundwater and canals. This study examines how pottery-jar rainwater storage was configured, maintained, and adapted in Ban Khao Yi San, an estuarine village in Samut Songkhram Province, and how living heritage can inform water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) planning. A qualitative case study combined four field rounds in March and October 2025 and 2026 with observation, photography, document review, and contextual conversations with adult residents. Evidence covered environmental setting, built form, storage infrastructure, and changing water practices. Salinity, rainfall, and water quality were not measured.
