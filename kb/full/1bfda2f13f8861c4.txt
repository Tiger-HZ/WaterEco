In this paper investigations by the same authors on environmental issues concerning the
controlofthepollutionproducedbyhumanactivitieshavebeenextendedtoincludecostsrelated
to environmental interventions.
The proposed model consists of a spatially structured dynamic model which takes into
account the level of pollution induced by human activities and possible environmental inter-
ventions, including taxation. An optimal harvesting control problem has been discussed, with
an objective function composed of four terms, namely the intertemporal utility of the decision
maker, the space-time average of the level of pollution in the habitat, the disutility due to the
imposition of taxation and the cost of environmental interventions.
Aspecificnoveltyinthemodelproposedhereisthelocalizationofthepossibleinterventions
to a subregion of the whole habitat.
Computationalexperimentshavebeencarriedouttoexemplifytheoutcomesoftheproposed
model.
Key words:geographicaleconomics,environmentalpollution,reaction-diffusionsystems,non-
local interactions, optimal control, regional control, generalized production functions

6. Concluding remarks
Thestudypresentedaspatiallystructuredeconomicgrowthmodelthatincor-
porates pollution dynamics, environmental taxation, and localized intervention
strategies.Byformulatingandanalyzinganoptimalcontrolproblemwithmultiple
interacting objectives – maximizing intertemporal utility, minimizing pollution,
and accounting for both taxation disutility and intervention costs – we have ex-
tended previous frameworks in environmental economics and control theory to
includethespatiallocalizationofenvironmentalpolicies.Numericalsimulations
evidencethatrestrictingpollutioncontrolactionstooptimallychosensubregions
can effectively reduce pollution diffusion in the whole eco-system while main-
taining a satisfactory level of production. The results emphasize the importance
of spatial heterogeneity in designing efficient environmental policies: targeted
POLLUTION CONTROL FOR A SPATIALLY STRUCTURED ECONOMIC GROWTH SYSTEM609
interventions, rather than uniform controls, yield better trade-offs between eco-
nomic growth and ecological sustainability. Beyond its computational findings,
the proposed framework highlights the potential of regional control theory as a
valuable tool for eco-economic systems. Future research will explore more com-
plexspatialdomains,dynamicinterventioncosts,andstochasticfactorsaffecting
bothproductionandpollutiondynamics,tofurthersupportevidence-basedenvi-
ronmental management in geographically distributed economic systems.
