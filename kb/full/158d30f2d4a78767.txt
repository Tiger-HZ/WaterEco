Location of the Study Area 
The study area lies between longitudes 12 ° 36' E and 12 ° 40' E and latitudes 9 ° 48' N and 9 ° 52' N 
and falls in the northeastern Nigerian basement complex (Figure 1). It has hilly topography with 
structural ridges and inselbergs surrounding the alluvial plains. The area is characterized by 
tropical dry and wet seasons; the rainy season lasts from April to October, while the dry season 
from November to March; the mean annual rainfall ranges from 800 mm to 900 mm (Adebayo, 
et. al., 2020). 
Temperatures are high between March to June, when the daily maximum temperatures 
reaching up to 42 ° C, and also in October to November, immediately after the rainy season. 
During the months of December and January, temperatures drop to 20 ° C due to obstruction 
of sun rays by harmattan dusts, and also between July to September; due the influence of the 
humid maritime air mass. The relative humidity rises up to 90% in the months of August and 
September (Adebayo, et. al., 2020).

Geology of the Study area 
The study area is a basement complex terrain; with rocks which witnessed both pre-pan African 
and pan-African orogenies (Ekwok, et. al., 2021). The surface geology has been weathered to 
superficial deposits of gravels sands and clayey soils; with outcrops of the basement rocks still 
preserved in the hills. The underlying rocks consist of gneisses, older granites and tertiary 
volcanics (Figure 2). 
The gneisses are coarse -grained high-grade metamorphic rocks, with light and dark minerals 
segregated into visible bands; made up of biotite gneiss and banded gneiss. The biotite 
gneisses are fine -grained with strong foliation caused by the parallel arrangement of 
alternating dark and light minerals. The banded gneisses show alternating light -coloured and 
dark bands and exhibit intricate folding of their bands (Ekwok, et. al., 2021). These rocks exhibit 
fractures, veins, fissure, faults, joints and fractured zones due to tectonism. The older granites 
are coarse-grained igneous rocks made up of feldspar, mica, quartz, biotites, and microcline; 
occurring as large circular masses within the older gneiss complexes. The volcanic rocks occur 
as interbedded lavas among the basement complex and as post metamorphic extrusives and 
are composed largely of calcic plagioclases and ferromagnesian minerals (Ekwok, et. al., 2021). 
The most prominent are basalts; which and are characterized by small grain sizes and 
approximately equal proportion of calcium -rich plagioclase, feldspar and calcium rich 
pyroxenes. They posses’ vesicles and columnar jointing and occur as low r idges along stream 
channels of rivers as pumices, bombs and columnar joints (Ekwok, et. al., 2021).

One litre of water was collected from each borehole; which was pumped for about three to

The first container was acidified with two drops of concentrated nitric acid (HNO 3) for cations 
determination. The purpose of acidification is to stop bacterial growth, inhibit oxidation 
reactions and lead to precipitation of cations. The second plastic container was used for anion 
determination. The water samples were kept in cool boxe s to keep the temperature below 
20°C and later taken to the laboratory for analysis. The coordinates and elevation readings at 
borehole points were recorded using portable GARMIN Etrex 76 Global Positioning System 
GPS. 
 
Water Analysis 
The in situ physical parameters such as Temperature, Colour, Taste, Conductivity, and Total 
Dissolved Solid (TDS) were measured using the Ec/pH Multi parameter instrument; as the 
chemistry of groundwater is sensitive to environmental changes (Alsuhaimi, et . al., 2019). pH 
buffer 7, pH buffer 4/10 and distilled water were used to calibrate the pH meter at the first 
instance. 50ml of the water sample was poured into a beaker and the probe inserted into the 
water 10 minutes after the meter was switch on and th e pH was recorded when the reading 
became stable. The conductivity solution was used to calibrate the conductivity meter at 25°C; 
which was then switched on and inserted into the 50ml water sample. When the reading 
became stable, the electrical conductivity value was recorded. A standard solution of sulphuric 
acid (H2SO4) was used to determine alkalinity titrimetrically. The sample was titrated with the 
0.1N H2SO4 using phenolphtalein and methyl orange indicators. 100ml water was measured into 
a conical flask and 3 drops of phenolphthalein indicator added. When the sample remained 
colourless, it indicated in all cases that phenolphthalein alkalinity was zero. 
Standard methods prescribed by the American Public Health Association (2012) were used in 
analyzing the groundwater samples. Turbidimetric method was used to analyze sulphate. The 
method relies on the formation of Barium sulphate in the presence of acidified HCl and Barium 
chloride. 
 
Assessing Drinking water quality 
The two main yardsticks on which the standard for assessment of drinking water quality are 
the presence of substances with adverse health effects and the presence of objectionable 
taste, odours and colours (Khanoranga, 2019). The dissolved geochemical cons tituents in 
groundwater obtained in this study are correlated with the World Health Organization WHO 
(2021) standards.

The cations analyzed include; calcium ions (Ca 2+), magnesium ion (Mg 2+), ferric ions (Fe 2+), 
fluoride ions (F +), sodium ions (Na +), potassium ions (K +) and cupper ions (Cu 2+).The 
concentration of calcium ions (Ca2+) in the water samples ranges from 10.10 mg/L in well SO09 
to 51.25 mg/L in well SO18; with an average of 23.90 mg/L; all theCa 2+ values do not exceed the 
WHO (2021) standard for drinking water. Presence of Ca 2+ may be mainly due to geochemical 
processes involving Calcium dissolution in rocks. Calcium is a significant determinant of water 
hardness due its buffering qualities and functions as a pH stabilizer (Singh, et al., 2020). 
Magnesium ion (Mg2+) concentration varies from 11.96 mg/L in well SO20 to 49.12 mg/L in well 
SO19 with an average of 26.76 mg/L; which are within the WHO (2021) standard for drinking 
water. Both calcium and magnesium are found in groundwater that has come in contact with 
certain rocks and minerals. When these materials are dissolved, they release calcium and 
magnesium. The concentration of Mg 2+ in groundwater system in the study area can be is 
attributed to the dissolution of Mg ions in the groundwater system (El -Osta, et. al., M; 2022). 
The concentration ferric ions (Fe 2+) in the water samples ranges from 0.011 mg/L in well SO17 
to 4.65 mg/L in well SO18 with an average value of 0.86 mg/L.

Suitability for Irrigation Purpose 
The suitability of groundwater in the study area for irrigation purposes was examined and 
evaluated according to World Health Organization (WHO, 2021) standards. When using 
groundwater for irrigation, the accumulation of salts and alkalinity in the soil mu st be taken 
into consideration. Electrical conductivity (EC), total dissolved solids (TDS), total hardness 
(TH), sodium adsorption ratio (SAR),magnesium hazard (MH), Kelly’s ratio (KR), percentage 
sodium (%Na +) and permeability index (PI) are the irrigation water quality indices used to 
examine the possible risks. 
 
Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR) 
Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR) defines the agricultural water quality status and is used to 
study soil sodium hazards. It is an essential parameter for irrigation water quality assessment 
which involves the relationship between the ions of sodium versus cal cium and magnesium 
(Paternoster et al. 2021). The results in T able 1 were used to estimate the SAR as presented in 
Table 3. High SAR is an indication of low water quality for irrigated agriculture use. This study 
reveals that SAR concentration varies from 0.41 –2.78 with a mean of 1.47. According to 
Paternoster et al. 2021), SAR values that are less than 10 ( ˂ 10) indicate that all the water 
samples have excellent water quality for irrigation. 
 
Magnesium Hazard (MH) 
Magnesium is necessary for the formation of chlorophyll molecules in plant tissue and also in 
activation of specific enzymes. Its availability in groundwater depends on aquifer minerals, 
weathering and climate (Urom, et al. 2021). Magnesium Hazard (MH) is determined using the 
Ca2+ and Mg 2+ average concentrations. Prediction of MH parameter in irrigation water 
highlights its suitability for irrigation. In this study, the MH values ranged from 42.22 in well 
SO13 to 69.56 in well SO19 with a mean 51.19. Khodapanah et al. (2009) opined that MH ˃ 50 is 
not recommended for irrigation. Hence, water samples from wells SO01, SO02, SO03, SO04, 
SO09, SO10, SO11, SO13, SO17, SO19, SO27 and SO30; having values greater than 50 are 
consequently are not recommended for irrigation purposes. 
 
Kelly’s Ratio (KR) 
Kelly’s Ratio is obtained from the values of sodium against the sum of calcium and magnesium 
ions. The results in Table 3 show the values of KR vary from 0.0015 in well SO05 to 0.0874 in 
well SO15 with an average of 0.0300. KR ˃ 1 indicates an excess level of Na + in waters. Water 
having KR value above one is considered unsuitable water for irrigation since it implies a 
significant quantity of sodium ions, while the one with values below one is suitable for 
irrigation as it specifies sodium ions deficiency (Akakuru , et al. 2022). Water samples from all 
the wells have values less than 1 ( ˂1), thus are rated as suitable for irrigation purpose due to 
no alkali hazard.

Sodium affects the soil by closing up the pores and this leads to poor water infiltration and 
wetting of the soil and can cause permeability problems (Singh et al. 2020 and Paternoster et 
al. 2021). The %Na index is important in determining groundwater suitability for irrigation

Permeability index (PI) is calculated based on the values of Ca2+, Mg2+, Na+ and HCO3- dissolution 
in the groundwater system (Omorogieva, et. al., 2022). From Table 3, values vary from 14.9 in 
well SO16 to 57.8 in well SO20 with an average of 31.2. Values less than 25 ( <25.0) are

SO22, SO23, SO24, SO25 and SO29 are unsuitable. The remaining, making about 64% of the 
studied groundwater are good for use in irrigation. 
 
Electrical Conductivity (EC), Total Dissolved Solids and Total Hardness 
In this study, the Electrical Conductivity (EC) and Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) results show an 
appreciable level of acceptance for drinking purposes but continued dissolution of materials in 
the groundwater system can cause unusual level of EC and TDS respectively (Akakuru, et al. 
2022). EC indicates the total salt concentration, which is dependent on the charges from 
dissolved ions in water. It is a measure of s alinity hazard to crops; excess salinity reduces the 
osmotic activity of plant and thus interferes with the absorption of water and nutrients from 
the soil (Singh et al. 2020). A high EC of water indicates a high concentration of ions, 
representing the higher TDS in water. The EC values of the studied water samples are low and 
fall within the recommended standards for irrigation practice. TDS quantifies the materials 
such as sulfate, bicarbonate, sodium, carbonate, calcium, magnesium dissolved in water. When 
the TDS increase, it leads to reduces water clarity, giving the water a color and salinity behavior 
(Paternoster et al. 2021). The Total Hardness (TH) is an indicator of salt accumulation in the soil. 
Plant growth is directly affected by the salt content of irrigation water, and indirectly by the 
permeability of the soil structure and aeration. 
 
Water pH and temperature 
Water pH influences the volatility of the processes in flocculation and coagulation, hence 
affecting emitter clogging (Akakuru, et. al., 2022) . At a pH higher than 7.2, along with a high 
temperature, the magnesium and calcium carbonates precipitate in the lateral tubes and the 
drippers since the carbonates' solubility is inversely proportional to temperature. Most surface 
drip irrigation systems have tubes painted black and exposed to the surface; hence, they can 
be heated by the sun to high temperatures (Akakuru, et. al., 2022) . Consequently, a 
combination of heat from the sun and a pH higher than 7.2 precipitates calcium and magnesium 
carbonate in water; accumulating within the emitters. The groundwater under study recorded 
maximum temperature and pH of 21.52 °C and 6.3 respectively. Hence the studied groundwater 
water is free from risks related to flocculation and coagulation resulting in clogging of irrigation 
system.

Conclusions 
Groundwater in Song and environs is moderately acidic and unsuitable for direct drinking 
without treatment in some wells. Most physicochemical parameters meet WHO standards, but 
elevated nitrate and iron pose health concerns. For irrigation, most samples are suitable based 
on SAR, KR, and %Na⁺, but 40% are unsuitable due to high MH. Regular monitoring is 
recommended to prevent deterioration from anthropogenic activities.
