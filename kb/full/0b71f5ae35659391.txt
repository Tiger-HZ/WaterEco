Freshwater shortage impacts more than one billion people worldwide, while eutrophication persists to degrade aquatic ecosystems by encouraging excess growth of aquatic macrophyte biomass (AMB). Phytoremediation using free-floating plants like water hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes), duckweed (Lemna spp.), and water lettuce (Pistia stratiotes) has emerged as a viable, cost-efficient technique for removing nutrients, heavy metals, and organic contaminants from wastewater. The species are characterized by rapid growth and high biomass yield, making them suitable for large-scale remediation. However, the post-treatment accumulation of nutrient-rich APB causes significant disposal problems.
