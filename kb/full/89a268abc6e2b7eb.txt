Host–pathogen interactions represent a dynamic and complex biological interface that governs disease progression, immune modulation, and therapeutic outcomes in animal systems. Infectious diseases affecting livestock, poultry, aquaculture species, wildlife, and laboratory animals continue to impose substantial economic and ecological burdens worldwide. Conventional therapeutic approaches often suffer from poor bioavailability, systemic toxicity, non-specific tissue distribution, and the growing challenge of antimicrobial resistance. Consequently, targeted drug delivery systems have emerged as a promising alternative for improving therapeutic precision and efficacy. This review critically examines the mechanistic basis of host– pathogen interactions and their integration with advanced drug delivery strategies in animal systems. Particular emphasis is placed on nanocarrier-based delivery platforms, biologically derived carriers, and stimuli-responsive systems capable of exploiting infection-specific microenvironments. The review further explores receptor-mediated targeting, pathogen-specific biomarkers, tissue tropism, and intracellular targeting mechanisms that contribute to enhanced drug delivery efficiency. Applications in livestock, poultry, aquaculture, wildlife, and laboratory animal models are comprehensively discussed. Recent studies demonstrate that targeted delivery systems significantly improve drug stability, cellular uptake, sustained release, and therapeutic efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity and environmental contamination. However, several challenges remain, including biocompatibility concerns, regulatory limitations, production costs, and ecological risks associated with nanoparticle persistence. The integration of zoological sciences with nanotechnology, immunology, and pharmaceutical biotechnology provides transformative opportunities for improving disease management in animal systems.
