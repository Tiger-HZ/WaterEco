An open ligand generation module was developed to construct chemically valid ligand structures de novo. The framework is based on fragment assembly and enables the generation of ligands spanning coordination numbers from 6 to 11, followed by preliminary screening using multiple physicochemical criteria. The fragment library comprises seven predefined molecular units, each assigned a fixed coordination number defined by its number of connection points. Symmetric molecules are constructed through a half-structure strategy, in which one molecule is generated and subsequently mirrored to yield the full architecture. For cyclic structures, ring closure is introduced after linear assembly by randomly selecting two unused connection points, and the resulting ring size is validated using RDKit to ensure chemically reasonable macrocycles in the range of 9 to 19 members. Upon completion, all remaining connection points indicated as asterisks are capped with carboxyl groups (-COOH) to finalize the molecular structure. The resulting molecules are subjected to a suite of chemical filters. Generated ligands are required to exhibit a high degree of symmetry to ensure synthetic feasibility. Their logP values are computed using RDKit and constrained within a target range to balance hydrophilicity and hydrophobicity, thereby ensuring that free ligands remain water soluble while their metal complexes precipitate. In addition, ligand construction strictly follows predefined connectivity rules and coordination number constraints to guarantee that the total coordination number meets the design targets. Coordination polyhedron extraction module A dedicated module was developed to extract metal coordination information from computed complexes, apply geometric thresholds to identify coordinating atoms, and output standardized coordination geometries together with statistical reports. The workflow begins by reading an Excel file to obtain task identifiers and candidate coordination numbers, which may include multiple values. GJF files are then parsed to extract elemental identities and three-dimensional coordinates of all atoms. A reconstruction procedure is subsequently applied to recover bonding information by evaluating interatomic distances and inferring bond connectivity and bond order. During this step, atoms unsuitable for coordination, including nitrate nitrogen and aniline nitrogen, are identified, and excluded. Based on the reconstructed structure, all oxygen and nitrogen atoms are traversed and their distances to the metal center are calculated. The coordinating atom set is determined through distance-based ranking, and when multiple coordination numbers are possible, the optimal configuration is selected according to the uniformity of metal-ligand distances.
