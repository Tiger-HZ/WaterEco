The transition to lead-free soldering technology has driven significant research into advanced finishing layers with improved thermal stability and controlled interfacial reactivity. ENIG PCB finish is widely used in electronics, particularly in surface-mount technology for printed circuit boards. In the present work, previous studies on the reactivity of electroless Ni-P-Re finishing layers with varying phosphorus content were continued, this time using commercially available SAC305 solder (Sn-3.0Ag-0.5Cu). The addition of rhenium is expected to suppress or eliminate the massive detachment of intermetallic phases from the interface and their migration into the solder. To this end, three surface finish variants with different phosphorus content but constant rhenium content were deposited.
