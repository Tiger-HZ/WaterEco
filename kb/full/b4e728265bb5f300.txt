Abstract The accumulation of salts in the soil acts as a primary barrier to successful potato cultivation, directly hindering vital processes such as photosynthetic activity, overall development, and nutritional equilibrium. This research analyzed how the individual and synergetic application of zinc (Zn) and phosphorus (P) influences the physiological, morphological, molecular, and biochemical reactions of four distinct potato varieties (D6, KG911-91, D220, and KG913) grown in high-salinity environments. Findings confirmed that salt exposure consistently impaired root health, biomass production, and the density of photosynthetic pigments.
