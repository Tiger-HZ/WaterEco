Efficient management of water, energy, and nitrogen is essential for sustaining irrigated crop production in arid regions. However, under hydrologically buffered conditions, similar crop yields may conceal substantial differences in input demand and resource productivity. This study evaluated the integrated water-energy-nitrogen performance of tomato cultivated in clay loam soil under shallow groundwater influence and drip irrigation managed at a uniform soil moisture level. A two-season field experiment compared three N-rates: 431, 345, and 215 kg N ha -1 , corresponding to 100%, 80%, and 50% of the full N-rate, with and without biochar application.
