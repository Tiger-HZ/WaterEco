Biofilms are multicellular, structured communities of microbial cells formed on an abiotic or biotic surface that are cemented together with an extracellular matrix ( Ünal Turhan, 2019 ). The development of biofilms could be a defence mechanism or a survival strategy to withstand harsh environmental conditions through the biosynthesis of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) ( Shree et al., 2023 ). Biofilm could either be formed by a single species or by interactions between multiple species, and its composition is reliant on external environmental conditions ( Singh, 2020 ). Most biofilms are associated with detrimental effects such as elevated virulence, antimicrobial resistance, chronic infections in humans, or contamination in the food industry. However, biofilms possess significant potential to be harnessed for several beneficial applications in the fields of bioremediation, bioenergy, and several other fields of biotechnology ( Mukhi & Vishwanathan, 2022 ). Therefore, this chapter delves into the elucidation of the various positive applications of biofilms in fields of environmental sustainability. By understanding and harnessing the beneficial properties of biofilms, we can explore innovative ways to address environmental challenges and promote sustainability in various industries, such as soil remediation, wastewater treatment, microbial fuel cells (MFCs), and the production of bioenergy. Biofilm as a bioremediating agent is a cost-effective and efficient substitute for the detoxification of environmental contaminants.
