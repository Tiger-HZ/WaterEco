Nanomaterials provide a common materials platform for three major environmental engineering tasks: removal of gaseous pollutants from air, production of safe water, and treatment or reuse of wastewater. Their value arises from high specific surface area, adjustable surface chemistry, nanoscale porosity, catalytic activity, antimicrobial effects, and the ability to combine adsorption, separation, oxidation, reduction, and sensing within one material. This chapter reviews research published from 2000 to 2025 on titanium dioxide (TiO2), zinc oxide (ZnO), nanoscale zero-valent iron (nZVI), silver nanoparticles (AgNPs), carbon nanotubes (CNTs), graphene oxide (GO), metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), magnetic nanomaterials, and polymer nanocomposite membranes.
