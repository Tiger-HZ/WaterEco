The motivation for this study stems from the lack of evidence in the literature regarding the information content of sustainability-related financial disclosures. To enhance the usefulness of decisions, the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) issued International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) S1 and S2. IFRS S1 requires firms to disclose general material sustainability-related information, whereas IFRS S2 specifically addresses climate-related risks and opportunities by building on the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) framework. At present, firms worldwide are transitioning to IFRS S2. This study examines how climate-related financial disclosures affect firm value. Multiple regression analysis, along with the associated robustness analyses (propensity score matching analysis and difference-in-differences estimation), are employed to test the research hypotheses. Using monetary data from 1,784 Taiwanese listed firms (2023), the results show that the impact of climate disclosure is format-dependent. Traditional financial metrics remain dominant, whereas combined narrative-and-numerical disclosures are insignificant. This suggests that investors prioritize precise quantification over qualitative descriptions, highlighting the need for substantive financial reporting to enhance decision-making.
