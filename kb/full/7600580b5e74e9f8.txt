Cellulose is a highly abundant linear glucose polymer that exists in amorphous and fibrillar forms1-3. Primarily produced by vascular plants but also microbes and tunicates, cellulose predominantly performs architectural functions by stabilizing cell walls, tissues, and multicellular communities4-9. Owing to its association with other cell surface materials, physiological higher-order structures of cellulose have been difficult to obtain. Here, we describe a never-dried tunicate cellulose fibril structure by cryogenic electron microscopy, which resolves a fiber of more than 360 cellulose strands.
