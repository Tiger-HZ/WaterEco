Bottom-up (BU) inventories and top-down (TD) aerial measurements at midstream natural gas facilities frequently diverge, and this discrepancy remains a central challenge for methane accounting in the sector. Previous approaches typically reconcile this divergence by treating BU and TD data as independent estimates of a single true quantity to be adjusted toward agreement, rather than as evidence that each capture only part of a more complex underlying system. This complexity arises from methane emissions being strongly governed by equipment operational state and therefore instantaneous aerial “snapshots” translate poorly to annual inventories.
