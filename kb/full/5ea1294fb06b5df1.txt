Abstract The biological carbon pump exports phytoplankton‐derived organic carbon to the deep ocean, regulating long‐term carbon sequestration and climate. Yet how phytoplankton community composition controls the magnitude and efficiency of this process remains poorly understood. Using time‐series sediment trap samples, we analyze compound‐specific stable carbon isotopes of amino acids to trace taxon‐specific sinking particulate organic carbon (POC), revealing stark contrasts in sequestration efficiency between cyanobacteria and eukaryotic microalgae. Despite pronounced seasonal variations in surface productivity and community structure, bulk POC sequestration efficiency remains statistically invariant.
