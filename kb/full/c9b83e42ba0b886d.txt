Molybdenum complexes supported by phosphine–ligand–phosphine (PLP) pincers, combined with samarium diiodide and water, convert dinitrogen to ammonia under mild conditions with nitrogenase-like activities. Using explicitly correlated coupled-cluster calculations, spin–orbit corrections, and realistic concentration and speciation models, we elucidate the cleavage mechanism from N 2 -bound Mo(I) complexes to ammonia release. Dimerisation proceeds through pentacoordinate Mo(I)–N 2 complexes, and N 2 activation is controlled by terminal N 2 dissociation, which converts a [Mo]–N≡N–[Mo] unit into cleavage-ready [Mo]=N=N=[Mo] before spin crossover and bond scission.
