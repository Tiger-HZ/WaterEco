Abstract This study investigates how increasing drought pressure, groundwater decline, and rapid land use/land cover (LULC) change reshape the resilience and feedback dynamics of an agricultural socio-ecological system. An integrated framework combining hydroclimatic, vegetation, water, soil moisture, groundwater, and LULC indicators was used to assess long-term changes and critical system transitions. Despite intensifying drought and persistent groundwater decline, vegetation greenness increased, indicating that agricultural production has been sustained beyond natural hydroclimatic limits through human intervention, particularly irrigation.
