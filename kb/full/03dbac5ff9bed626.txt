Eutrophication accelerated the phylogenetic evolution of Daphnia 
sinensis in Lake Chaohu based on palaeogenetic records
Wenping Wang1,2  · Dongqing Yang1 · Lei Cao1 · Kun Zhang1 · Xiaoxue Xu1 · Yu Zhang1 · Daogui Deng1
Received: 14 May 2024 / Accepted: 16 December 2024 / Published online: 17 January 2025 
© The Author(s) 2025
Abstract
Increasing human activity and environmental changes have a complex impact on the evolutionary histories of most biomes. 
How environmental changes affect genetic evolution of wild populations has become a frequent research theme of biologi-
cal ecology. Molecular techniques, in conjunction with palaeogenetics, facilitate understanding of the relationship between 
the genetic structure of wild populations and environmental change. In this study, genes encoding 16S rRNA, cytochrome c 
oxidase subunit 1 (COI), internal transcribed spacer (ITS), and Na+/K+ ATPase were used as molecular markers to reconstruct 
changes in the temporal population genetic structure of Daphnia sinensis based on the analysis of resting eggs from the 
sediment layers in Lake Chaohu. The genetic variation of the 16S rRNA, COI, ITS, and Na+/K+ ATPase genes of D. sinensis 
comprised 0–0.7%, 0–2.5%, 0–9.7%, and 0–15.4%, respectively, and was significantly affected by the total nitrogen and total 
phosphorus contents. The evolutionary molecular clock analysis predicted that sequences in different layers diverged over the 
time of 1.3–1.6 million years, which greatly exceeded the real age of the sediment (60 years). Phylogenetic analysis showed 
that genetic variation of the 16S rRNA gene occurred the earliest, and the ITS gene had the highest genetic variation. Up 
to 32% of the Na+/K+ ATPase gene variation was explained by differences in the total phosphorus content. Moreover, we 
found evidence that high phosphorus levels may have triggered the directional selection of the Na+/K+ ATPase gene loci. 
Our results help to understand better the microevolution of cladoceran populations in eutrophic lakes.

Introduction
Over the last few centuries, the rapidly increasing anthro-
pogenic changes have posed severe evolutionary challenges, 
although natural populations are adapting to the environmen-
tal changes (IPCC 2014; Pedersen et al. 2022). Increasing Extended author information available on the last page of the article

human activity and environmental changes have profoundly 
affected the evolutionary histories of most biomes. Usually, 
aquatic environments are greatly affected (Schindler 1990; 
Chiaia-Hernandez et al. 2013), as nutrients such as nitrogen 
and phosphorus run off into rivers, lakes, and reservoirs. 
This alters biological populations and leaves behind distinc-
tive signatures in the sediments (Schindler 1990), which 
could then be deciphered by palaeolimnological analyses 
(Rippey and Anderson 1996; Meng et al. 2023). The genetic 
analyses of egg banks suggested that these environmental 
changes can occur rapidly and affect the evolutionary tra-
jectory of biological populations (Brede et al. 2009; Orsini 
et al. 2013a; Frisch et al. 2014). Daphnia resting egg banks 
preserved in lake sediments have been successfully used 
to reconstruct evolutionary processes (Decaestecker et al. 
2007; Brede et al. 2009; Most et al. 2015). Insights into how 
populations respond and adapt to their environments can 
be obtained by integrating ecology and molecular biology 
approaches (Orsini et al. 2013b; O’Grady et al. 2022).
Lake Chaohu (117°18′–117°50′ E, 31°25′–31°42′ N) is a 
subtropical eutrophic shallow lake, with mean depth of 3 m 
(Deng et al. 2008; Wang et al. 2016). Lake Chaohu is one of 
five largest freshwater lakes in China and has a total surface 
area of 780 km2. Because of severe eutrophication, cyano-
bacterial blooms have frequently occurred in Lake Chaohu 
since the 1970s (Tu et al. 1990; Deng et al. 2008; Wang 
2010). In 1982–2015, although total nitrogen (TN) and total 
phosphorus (TP) concentrations showed obvious decreas-
ing trends after 1995, they remained at high levels in Lake 
Chaohu (Zhang et al. 1997; Han 1998; Shang and Shang 
2007; Wang 2007). Zhang (2005) found that TN and TP 
concentrations in sediments first increased, then decreased, 
and remained stable again with an increase in the sediment 
depth. There are many P-rich rocks on the north shore of 
the lake, which might be one of the important reasons for 
the relatively high P concentrations in the sediments of the 
lake (Tu et al. 1990). Eutrophication and cyanobacterial 
bloom outbreaks significantly affect seasonal dynamics and 
community succession of crustacean zooplankton, includ-
ing Daphnia (Deng et al. 2008). During eutrophication, the 
genetic structure and evolutionary mechanisms within zoo-
plankton populations in Lake Chaohu may also be strongly 
affected. The 16S rRNA, CO I, and ITS genes have been 
widely used as molecular markers for the species identifica-
tion and phylogeny of cladocerans in recent years (Taylor 
et al. 1998; Von et al. 1999; Dewaard et al. 2006; Richter 
et al. 2007; Elias-Gutierrez et al. 2008; Kotov et al. 2009; 
Xu et al. 2009; Wang et al. 2016). The Na+/K+ ATPase gene 
has also often been used in the studies of genetic variation 
of various organisms (Talsma et al. 2014; White et al. 2014; 
Arystarkhova 2016; Fan et al. 2016), but not in cladocerans. 
Daphnia sinensis (Gu et al.  2013) is widely distributed in 
the eutrophic waters of the middle and lower reaches of the 
Yangtze River (Fig. 1) (Benzie 2005; Zhao 2005; Gu et al. 
2013). Previous studies have mainly focused on the physio-
logical and ecological aspects of D. sinensis biology (Xiang 
et al. 2010; Huang et al. 2015; Zhang et al. 2015; Jiang et al. 
2016), however, understanding of the genetic structure and 
evolutionary mechanisms shaping D. sinensis populations 
under environmental changes is insufficient, especially for 
wild populations in the natural state.
In this study, the genetic variation and phylogeny of D. 
sinensis hatched from resting eggs in the sediments of Lake 
Chaohu were analysed based on the sequences of the 16S 
rRNA, COI, ITS, and Na+/K+ ATPase genes. We expected to 
be able to test the following hypothesis: 1) the lake eutrophi-
cation would accelerate the genetic difference of D. sinensis; 
2) there may be significant difference in the responses of 
four genes to lake eutrophication; 3) the change of phospho-
rus content would promote the genetic mutations in Na+/K+ 
ATPase gene. Our study will help further to understand the

Materials and methods
Sampling site and sediment cores
The sampling site was located in the central zone of 
Lake Chaohu (31°33′ 28.74″ N, 117°32′ 11.41″ E) (Online 
resource, Fig. S1). Four sediment cores were collected 
using a columnar gravity corer 4.2 cm in diameter (Nanjing 
Institute of Geography & Limnology, Chinese Academy of 
Science) in August 2014 (Zhang et al. 2020). The physical 
and chemical characteristics of the lake water during the 
sampling period are shown in Table  1. Each sediment core 
was cut into 1-cm thick sections (samples) and stored in a 
refrigerator at 4 °C.
The samples of one sediment core were dried and 
weighed using an electronic scale. The samples were dried in 
an oven under the condition of 105 ± 5 °C. The drying time 
is set according to the quantity and humidity of the sample, 
which generally takes 16 to 24 h. Cooling and weighing: The 
dried sample is cooled to room temperature in the dryer. Dry 
samples were crushed and sieved through a 0.154-mm mesh. 
2–5 g powdered sample was weighed, and the age of the 
sediment depth was determined by a high purity germanium 
gamma spectrometer (Ametek-Amt Ortec C., China) at the 
State Key Laboratory of Lake Science and Environment, 
Nanjing Institute of Geography and Limnology, Chinese 
Academy of Sciences. According to the attenuation law of 
radioactive isotope, the radioactive dates were calculated 
using the constant rate of supply (CRS) 210Pb dating model 
(Chen et al. 2014). The ground samples were filtered using a 
0.074-mm mesh, and then, 50 mg of each 1-cm thick filtered 
sample was weighed to measure TN and TP contents by 
alkaline potassium persulfate digestion UV spectrophotom-
etry (Qian et al. 1990).
The remaining three sediment cores were used to collect 
the ephippia, i.e., resting eggs of D. sinensis (Fig.  1). Sedi-
ment samples (thick) were rinsed with aerated tap water and 
filtered through a plankton net (64 μm). The residual sedi-
ment was observed under a microscope (Olympus, Japan). 
D. sinensis ephippia and hatched individuals were identified 
according to the methods described by Benzie (2005).
Total DNA extraction
The resting eggs of D. sinensis from different sediment 
depths were incubated and hatched in an intelligent light 
incubator (Ningbo Saifu, China) at 25 °C. Each neonate 
was cultured at 25 °C, with the 12 h light:12 h darkness 
illumination cycle. Tetradesnus obliquus was used as food, 
and D. sinensis were kept in aerated tap water for 48 h 
(Wang et al. 2016).
The 102 young D. sinensis (Fig. 1) hatched from resting 
eggs were individually cultured to become adults at 25 °C. 
Genomic DNA of each adult D. sinensis was extracted 
using a TIANamp Micro DNA Kit (Wang et al. 2016). 
The integrity, concentration, and quality of the DNA 
were checked using a Nanodrop 2000 instrument (Thermo 
Fisher Scientific, USA). The concentration of DNA was 
85–120 ng/µL, and the A260/A280 ratio was 2.0–2.3.
PCR amplification and sequence length 
measurements
Mitochondrial 16S rRNA was amplified using the L2510 
and H3059 primers (Bouchon et al. 1994; Xu et al. 2014). 
The COI gene was amplified with the CO IF and CO IR 
primers (Xu et al. 2014; Wang et al. 2016). The ITS gene 
was amplified with the ITS F (5.8BF) (5′ -CCC TGA ACG 
GTG GAT CAC TA-3′) and ITS R (5.8BR) (5′ -TTA GAA 
GGA GTT TAC CTC CCG CTT AGG-3′) primers (Crease 
1999; Taylor et al. 2005). The Na+/K+ ATPase gene was 
amplified with the P1F (5′ -TAC ATG GAG CTT GGT GGA 
ATG-3′) and P1R (5′ -GCA AAA TCA TGT CTG CAG CTT-
3′) primers. The design of the Na+/K+ ATPase gene prim-
ers was done based on the D. sinensis transcriptome data 
(Zhang et al. 2016).
The 25 µL PCR mixture contained 1.0 µL of the genomic 
DNA, 2.5 µL of 10 × LA-Taq Buffer II, 0.25 μL of DNA pol-
ymerase TaKaRa-LA-Taq (5 U/µL) (Clontech, USA), 4.0 µL 
of dNTPs (2.5 mM) (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai), 1.0 µL of 
each primer (10 mM) (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai), 0.5 µL 
of Mg2+ (25 mM), and 14.75 µL of double-distilled H2O.
The conditions for 16S rRNA and COI gene amplification 
were described by Wang et al. (2016). For the ITS gene, an 
initial denaturing step of 2 min at 94 °C was followed by 
35 cycles of [1 min at 94 °C, 45 s at 53 °C, and 2 min at 
72 °C] and finished with the final extension step at 72 °C for 
10 min. For the Na+/K+ ATPase gene, an initial denaturing 
step of 3 min at 94 °C was followed by 35 cycles of [30 s at 
94 °C, 30 s at 58 °C, and 1 min at 72 °C], and finished with 
the final extension step at 72 °C for 10 min. PCR products 
were purified using a TIANGEN Biotech Kit (Tiangen, Bei-
jing) and their lengths were confirmed by electrophoresis in 
a 1% agarose gel.

Alignment and phylogenetic analysis
The PCR products were analysed by gel electrophore-
sis and sequenced (GenScript, Nanjing, China). The 16S 
rRNA (valid bases 508–523), CO I (valid bases 489–531), 
ITS (valid bases 1497–1524), and Na+/K+ ATPase (valid 
bases 734–736) genes were detected. The bidirectional 
nucleotide sequences were aligned and proofread using 
DNAStar (DNASTAR Inc., Madison, WI, USA) to remove 
unreliable bases based on the SeqMan peak. The homolo-
gous sequences used as outgroup for the analysis were down-
loaded from GenBank (Table 2).
Multiple sequence alignments were performed using 
ClustalX (Larking et al. 2007 ). The variable sites of D. 
sinensis sequences were analysed using DNAspV5 (Lib-
rado and Rozas 2009). The conversion/transversion ratios 
and genetic distances of D. sinensis at different sediment 
depths were calculated using MEGA 6.0 (Tamura et al. 
2013). Genetic distances were calculated using the Kimura 
two-parameter model with 1,000 bootstrap replicates. 
PhyML 3.0 (Guindon et al. 2010) was used for the maxi-
mum likelihood (ML) analysis utilising the GTR + G + I 
evolutionary model (Modeltest version 3.7, Nylander, 
2008). Phylogenetic trees were constructed using Bayes-
ian inference in MrBayes 3.1.2 (Ronquist et al. 2003). The 
first 25% of the trees were discarded as burn-in, and a 
50% majority-rule consensus tree was constructed from 
the remaining Bayesian trees based on 10,000,000 gen-
erations (Wang et al. 2016). Daphnia obtusa (Kurz, 1874) 
(FJ427466) (Adamowicz et al. 2009), D. pulex (Leydig, 
1860) (KF99371) (Xu et al. 2014), D. galeata (G. O. Sars, 
1863) (AB642075) (Ishida et al. 2011), and D. pulex 
(NC_000844) (Crease 1999) were used to construct the 
phylogenetic trees as outgroups of the 16S rRNA, CO I, 
ITS, and Na+/K+ ATPase genes of D. sinensis. Based on 
calibrations for other crustacean groups, divergence times 
were estimated using homologous 16S rRNA and CO I 
gene fragments for comparison.
Groups in the sediments
The resting eggs of D. sinensis in the 20 cm sediment 
depth of Lake Chaohu were divided into four groups at 
5-cm intervals to avoid mixed interference referencing 
the age of the sediment depth (i.e., A group: 1–5 cm ~ age 
of 2008–2014, B group: 5–10 cm ~ age of 1996–2008, 
C group: 10–15 cm ~ age of 1966–1996, and D group: 
15–20 cm ~ age of 1954–1966). Analysis of molecular var -
iance (AMOVA) was conducted to analyse the diversity of 
nucleic acid sequences of D. sinensis from different sedi-
ment layers of Lake Chaohu using Arlequin 3.5 (Excoffier 
and Lischer 2010), in which all individuals hatched from 
D. sinensis resting eggs obtained from the same 5-cm 
interval were regarded as belonging to the same group.
Relationships between nutrient factors and genetic 
variation of D. sinensis
Redundancy analysis (RDA) and variation partitioning 
analysis were employed to test the relationships between 
genetic variation of D. sinensis and TN/TP contents in 
the sediments using CANOCO for Windows ver. 4.5 (ter 
Braak and Smilauer  2002). In addition, one-way ANOVA 
was used to assess the relative impact of TN and TP on 
genetic variation of D. sinensis , using the “vegan” package 
(version 2.6-8) in R software (Oksanen et al. 2024).

Results
Age of Lake Chaohu sediments
210Pb dating showed that the 1–20 cm sediment depth of 
Lake Chaohu represented ~ 60 years, which is consist-
ent with the estimates of Zhang (2005) (Online resource, 
Fig. S2). TN and TP in the sediments were initially high, 
then low, and then remained stable with the increase in the 
sediment depth (Online resource, Fig. S3).
Molecular variation inferred from the sequences 
of the 16S rRNA, COI, ITS, and  Na+/K+ ATPase genes 
in D. sinensis resting eggs
The 102 sequences of the D. sinensis 16S rRNA gene con-
tained 507 identifiable base sites, including 498 conserved, 
nine variable, four single, and five parsimony-informative 
sites. The GenBank accession numbers for these sequences 
are MK922363–MK922464 (A1–A21, B1–B42, C1–C28, 
and D1–D11; Table 2). The average A, T/U, C, and G con-
tents were 34.4%, 34.4%, 18.2%, and 13.0%, respectively. 
The A + T content (68.8%) was significantly higher than the 
G + C content (31.2%). The overall transition/transversion 
ratio was 1.0.
The 102 sequences of the CO I gene contained 486 
identifiable base sites, including 465 conserved, 21 vari-
able, 2 single, and 19 parsimony-informative sites. The 
GenBank accession numbers for these sequences are 
MK941503–MK941604 (A1–A21, B1–B42, C1–C28, and 
D1–D11; Table 2). The average A, T/U, C, and G contents 
were 23.8%, 40.0%, 14.4%, and 21.8%, respectively. The 
A + T content (63.8%) was significantly higher than the 
G + C content (36.2%), with an overall transition/transver -
sion ratio of 2.0.
The 102 sequences of the ITS gene contained 1,468 
identifiable sites, including 1,116 conserved, 352 vari-
able, 134 single, and 218 parsimony-informative sites. 
The GenBank accession numbers for these sequences are 
MK940963–MK941064 (A1–A21, B1–B42, C1–C28, and 
D1–D11; Table 2). The average A, T/U, C, and G contents 
were 23.7%, 22.6%, 25.0%, and 28.8%, respectively. The 
A + T content (46.3%) was lower than the G + C content 
(53.7%), with an overall transition/transversion ratio of 0.5.
The 102 sequences of the Na+/K+ ATPase gene con-
tained 728 identifiable sites, including 558 conserved, 170 
variable, three single, and 167 parsimony-informative sites. 
The GenBank accession numbers for these sequences are 
MK952040–MK952141 (A1–A21, B1–B42, C1–C28, and 
D1–D11; Table 2). The average A, T/U, C, and G contents 
were 23.4, 29.3, 24.8, and 22.5%, respectively. The A + T 
content (51.7%) was slightly higher than that of G + C 
(47.3%), with an overall transition/transversion ratio of 0.67.
The sequence composition of the 16S rRNA gene (A + T 
content: 68.8%, G + C content: 31.2%, transition/transver -
sion ratio: 1.0) was similar to that of the CO I gene (A + T 
content: 63.8%, G + C content: 36.2%, transition/transver -
sion ratio: 2.0). The sequence composition of the ITS gene 
(A + T content: 46.3%; G + C content: 53.7%; transition/
transversion ratio: 0.5) was closer to that of the Na+/K+ 
ATPase gene (A + T content: 51.7%; G + C content: 47.3%; 
transition/transversion ratio: 0.67). ITS and Na+/K+ ATPase 
genes were more variable than 16S rRNA and COI genes.
In addition, AMOVA showed significant genetic variation 
of the 16S rRNA, COI, and ITS genes (p < 0.001) among and 
within populations of D. sinensis (Table 3). Moreover, there 
were significant genetic differences (p < 0.001) between the 
1–15 cm and 15–20 cm sediment layers for the 16S rRNA 
gene, between the 1–5 cm and 5–20 cm sediment layers for 
the COI gene, and between the 1–10 cm and 10–20 cm sedi-
ment layers for the ITS gene (Table 4).

Phyletic evolution of the 16S rRNA COI, ITS, 
and  Na+/K+ ATPase genes in D. sinensis resting eggs 
in different sediment layers
The genetic distances of the 16S rRNA gene ranged from 0 
to 0.007, based on the Kimura 2-parameter distance. Phy -
logenetic trees showed highly congruent tree topologies. 
The main divergences in the ML tree were in accordance 
with those in the MrBayes and NJ trees (Fig.  2). In the 16S 
rRNA phylogenetic tree, D. sinensis from different sediment 
depths evolved into two branches. One branch included D. 
sinensis from the 15–20 cm sediment layer (group D), and 
the other branch included D. sinensis from the 1–15 cm sedi-
ment layers (groups A, B, and C). In the 16S rRNA from the 
15–20 cm sediment layer, base No. 54 was T, whereas in 
other sediment layers, it was base A. In the sequences from 
the 15–20 cm sediment layer, base No. 58 was G, whereas 
there was no base in that position in the other sediment lay-
ers. These results suggest an obvious divergence of some 
D. sinensis 16S rRNA loci between the sequences isolated 
at a depth of 1–15 cm compared to those isolated at the 
15–20 cm sediment layer in Lake Chaohu.
The genetic distances of the CO I gene ranged from 
0 to 0.025. In the CO I phylogenetic trees (Fig.  3), group 
B (5–10 cm) showed greater genetic diversity than the 
other groups. However, 52% of the individuals in group A 
(1–5 cm) showed obvious divergence from those in groups C 
and D (10–20 cm). With regards to differences in sequences, 
base No. 7 in the 1–5 cm sediment layers was T, whereas 
it was C in the 10–15 cm sediment layers. Base No. 81 in 
the 1–5 cm sediment layers was T, whereas it was C in the 
5–20 cm sediment layers.

0–9.7%. The main divergences of the ITS ML phylogenetic 
tree were in accordance with those of the MrBayes and NJ 
trees (Fig. 4). The phylogenetic tree of the ITS gene didn’t 
show obvious regular variations; however, some base sites

Discussion
The A + T content in the 16S rRNA and COI gene sequences 
of D. sinensis from different sediment layers of Lake Chaohu 
(68.8% and 63.8%, respectively) was significantly higher than 
that of G + C (31.2% and 36.2%, respectively), which was 
consistent with the generally higher A + T content among 
invertebrates (Anderon 2000; Gao et al. 2000). However, for 
the ITS gene, the A + T content (46.3%) was lower than the 
G + C content (53.7%). The overall transition/transversion 
ratios of the ITS (0.5) and Na+/K+ ATPase genes (0.65) were 
significantly lower than those of the 16S rRNA (1) and COI 
genes (2). These results reflect the structural specificity of 
the ITS and Na+/K+ ATPase gene sequences in D. sinensis 
(Taylor et al. 2005; White et al. 2014; Arystarkhova 2016). 
There were small differences between different ages in the 
base percentage and overall transition/transversion ratios of 
16S rRNA, COI, ITS, and Na+/K+ ATPase genes.
The variability (1.8%) and genetic distance (0.7%) of D. 
sinensis 16S rRNA sequences from different sediment layers 
of Lake Chaohu were consistent with the highly conserved 
characteristics of this gene, in line with reports by Taylor 
et al. (1998) and Dai et al. (2010). For the D. sinensis COI 
gene sequences, variability and genetic distance estimates 
comprised 4.3% and 2.5%, respectively. Compared with the 
gene sequences of 16S rRNA and COI, the ITS and Na+/K+ 
ATPase genes had higher variability (24% and 23%, respec-
tively) and genetic distances (9.7% and 15.4%, respectively), 
which correlated with previously noted highly variable fea-
tures of these genes (Crease 1999; Taylor et al. 2005; Kotov 
and Taylor 2010; Ishida et al. 2011; Didehdar et al. 2015).
Owing to the development of industry and agriculture 
accompanied by elevated levels of human activity, Lake 
Chaohu has suffered from eutrophication since the 1970s 
(Tu et al. 1990; Zhang and Kong 2015). During eutrophi-
cation, the genetic structure and evolutionary mechanisms 
regulating zooplankton populations in Lake Chaohu were 
likely strongly affected. In this study, RDA indicated that 
TN and TP had significant effects on the genetic diversity of 
D. sinensis resting eggs based on the analysis of 16S rRNA, 
COI, ITS, and Na+/K+ ATPase gene sequences (p < 0.01) 
from different sediment layers of Lake Chaohu. Variations in 
TN and TP collectively explained > 50% of the genetic diver-
sity of D. sinensis resting eggs inferred from 16S rRNA,

accounting for 32% of its variation. Greater and more regular 
base variations in the Na+/K+ ATPase gene sequences of D.

observed compared to those in the 1–5 cm and 15–20 cm 
sediment layers, which was clearly reflected in the phyloge-
netic trees (Fig.  5). 210Pb dating showed that the 5–15 cm 
sediment layers of Lake Chaohu formed in 1976–1998, when 
TP was higher than at other sediment depths. Similarly, 
higher phosphorus concentrations in Lake Chaohu water 
were observed during 1976–1989 (Zhang et al. 1997; Han 
1998; Shang and Shang 2007; Wang 2007). Moreover, pre-
vious studies indicated that the population size of Daphnia 
increased gradually during this period (Deng et al. 2008). 
The relationship between the TP concentration and genetic 
variation of the Na+/K+-ATPase gene sequence was also sig-
nificant (r = 0.845, p < 0.01). The TP concentration explained 
32% of the genetic changes in the Na+/K+ ATPase gene. 
These results suggest that long-term changes in TP concen-
tration in the lake directly or indirectly impacted genetic 
variation of the D. sinensis Na+/K+ ATPase gene under natu-
ral selection, which is in accordance with the results of Ors-
ini et al. (2013b). In addition, differences in bases in some 
 Na+/K+ ATPase gene loci were obvious between D. sinensis 
resting eggs in the 5–15 cm layer compared to those in the 
1–5 cm and 15–20 cm sediment layers in Lake Chaohu. For 
example, base No. 135 site in the 5–15 cm sediment layer 
was C, whereas it was always T in the eggs from both the 
1–5 cm and 15–20 cm sediment layers. In addition, base No. 
162 in the 5–15 cm sediment layer was T, whereas it was A 
in resting eggs from both the 1–5 cm and 15–20 cm sediment 
layers. Moreover, the TP content in the 5–15 cm sediment 
layer was significantly higher than those in the 1–5 cm and 
15–20 cm sediment layers. Therefore, we deduced that high 
TP content may trigger the directional selection of bases in 
the Na+/K+ ATPase gene. This study has certain limitations 
due to research methodology. Only four molecular markers 
were utilized, which might not comprehensively represent 
the entire molecular profile of the subject under investiga-
tion. The limited number of markers could lead to an incom-
plete understanding of the genetic complexity and variation, 
potentially overlooking significant genetic factors.
In general, we conclude that the microevolution of D. 
sinensis in Lake Chaohu is a complex process and may be 
significantly affected by lake eutrophication. For future 
research, it is essential to expand the scope of molecular 
markers and add more environmental factors. Combine a 
broader range of markers with the influence of multiple 
environmental factors would provide a more detailed and 
accurate picture of the genetic landscape. This would help 
to overcome the current limitations and enhance the overall 
understanding and significance of the research.
