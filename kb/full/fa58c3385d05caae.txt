ABSTRACT Peatland rewetting can reduce CO 2 loss but increase CH 4 emissions. How vegetation shapes this trade‐off and how the water table modifies temperature sensitivity remain unclear. We analysed 14,643 observations from 285 peatland sites. CH 4 emissions increased more steeply with rising water‐table level (WTL) under graminoid dominance, while the pattern of declining ecosystem respiration differed between vegetation classes. Combined CO 2 ‐equivalent emissions (GHGE; respiration + CH 4 at GWP 100 = 28) declined by 73.9% as WTL rose from 60 cm below to 15 cm above the peat surface and were still declining at the wet end of the analysed range.
