Current physical research lacks a unified, fixed and universally reusable standardized deduction framework. The modeling of various physical phenomena is fragmented and empirical, making it difficult to implement the core principle of universal isomorphism—"One Principle Governs All Things". The core work of this paper is to establish the mandatory standardized deduction rules for the FT-MMT system, construct a nested parallel modeling architecture based on the unified spherically symmetric fundamental master equation, and standardize the irreversible five-step research procedure: axiom anchoring → physical image construction → hierarchical dimension reduction → pre-archiving of bare predictions → residual tracing and correction. At present, there is no complete standardized database for the material memory strength parameters exclusive to various substances. Therefore, a phased standardized transitional calibration process is designed for the system: first conduct bare calculations with the fundamental equation without introducing memory parameters, reversely calibrate the intrinsic memory constant \(M_c\) of the corresponding substance through residuals between results and benchmark calculations, then embed the parameters into parallel interference sub-equations to achieve high-precision simultaneous solution. This paper selects the critical flow height of water and oil in open channels as a complete calculation example to reproduce the entire set of mandatory deduction procedures, verifying that the unified fundamental equation architecture can independently fulfill quantitative physical calculations. Such post-hoc parameter calibration is only a transitional method in the early stage of system development. After batch calibration of various substances under multiple working conditions, a Catalog of Material Memory Strength Parameters can be compiled. In subsequent modeling, parameters can be directly looked up and retrieved to realize simultaneous one-step solution of fundamental terms and memory coupling terms. The fluid case only serves as a demonstration carrier for rule implementation, and the entire deduction specification is universal for expansion to the full physical fields including mechanics, acoustics, and phase transitions.
