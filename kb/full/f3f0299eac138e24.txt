Freshwater nutrient enrichment is one of the most pervasive drivers of ecological destabilization worldwide, triggering eutrophication, harmful algal blooms, hypoxia, and food‑web restructuring across lakes and rivers. This chapter synthesizes the biogeochemical, ecological, and physical processes through which nitrogen and phosphorus loading propagate through aquatic systems, emphasizing the coupled external and internal nutrient pathways that sustain high productivity even after external inputs decline. Drawing on contemporary research and long‑term monitoring records, the chapter integrates nutrient cycling, primary productivity, algal biomass dynamics, macrophyte loss, hypoxia formation, and multi‑stressor interactions into a unified framework describing how enrichment erodes resilience and increases the likelihood of regime shifts. Conceptual figures illustrate nutrient fluxes, eutrophication cascades, and trophic state transitions, providing a mechanistic foundation for subsequent chapters on early‑warning signals and cross‑system resilience diagnostics.
