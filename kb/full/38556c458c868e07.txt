Ensuring access to safe drinking water remains a major challenge in developing regions, where traditional water quality monitoring methods are often manual, time-consuming, and unable to provide real-time insights. These limitations delay detection of contamination and hinder timely intervention. The proposed model integrates low-cost sensors to measure pH, turbidity, temperature, and total dissolved solids (TDS), along with an embedded microcontroller for real-time data acquisition and processing. A four-layer architecture consisting of sensing, edge processing, GSM-based communication, and cloud visualisation was implemented.
