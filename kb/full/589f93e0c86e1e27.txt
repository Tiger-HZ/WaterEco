Abstract Patch dynamics provides a framework for understanding how spatial heterogeneity in patch quality and configuration influences biodiversity, yet their interactive effects remain poorly understood. We conducted a Before–After–Control–Impact (BACI) experiment testing how patch quality and configuration interact to influence stream benthic macroinvertebrate biodiversity. Patch quality was manipulated by releasing fine sediment into riffles at low (1,000 mg m⁻² h⁻¹) or high intensity (3,500 mg m⁻² h⁻¹), while patch configuration was manipulated by releasing sediment in adjacent patches to create clustered disturbances or non-adjacent patches to create dispersed disturbances. Macroinvertebrates were sampled before and after sediment releases and compared with unaltered control riffles.
