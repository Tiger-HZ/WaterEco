Abstract Forest disturbances are increasing in frequency and intensity worldwide, resulting in canopy opening across many wooded landscapes. Canopy opening increases exposure of below‐canopy organisms to external influences such as climate warming and nitrogen pollution. However, there is a lack of long‐term experimental data to test how forest understorey plant community responses to warming and nitrogen pollution are modulated by these novel light and thermal regimes, and to mechanistically quantify the effects of different drivers. Here we report dynamics of understorey plant biodiversity in a long‐term, full‐factorial experiment in ancient, temperate forest after 14 years of warming, nitrogen addition and simulated canopy opening with fluorescent tubes.
