Electroplating wastewater treatment is governed not only by total heavy-metal concentrations but also by metal speciation, organic complexation, salinity, intermittent discharge, and increasingly stringent requirements for water reuse and metal recovery. This review reframes recent advances from a removal-efficiency-centered perspective to a speciation-driven and resource-oriented framework. Treatment technologies are evaluated according to their dominant mechanisms, including precipitation and phase transfer, ligand destruction and redox conversion, selective separation, electrochemical recovery, biological transformation, and hybrid process integration.
