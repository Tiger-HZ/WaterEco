 Abstract 
 
 
 
 
 
 内容記述 
 Submerged nanofiltration (SNF) is an emerging approach for the direct treatment of surface water. Controlling membrane fouling during SNF is key to establishing a chemical-free system. This study evaluated intermittent ultrasonic (US) cleaning for controlling membrane fouling during SNF treatment using a polyamide nanofiltration (NF) membrane. Unlike previous studies that focused mainly on flux recovery, this study assessed US cleaning based on membrane integrity, effects of repeated SNF operations, and permeate stability. US exposure was applied at 26 kHz, 50 W, and a power density of 32.4 W/cm2. In the 2 cm probe-to-membrane distance configuration, MgSO4 rejection decreased slightly from 90% to 86% after 540 min of cumulative exposure. In contrast, US exposure at a 4 cm distance across another membrane did not affect MgSO4 rejection, suggesting a lower risk of membrane deterioration. Over 6 days of SNF treatment of dam water, the permeance of the NF membranes decreased by 24% due to membrane fouling. Cleaning with US for 5 min at a 4 cm probe-membrane distance fully restored membrane permeance. The effectiveness of US cleaning was further demonstrated over five SNF cycles for 30 days with a 5 min US cleaning duration, resulting in stable rejection of turbidity (93–97%) and organic matter, evaluated by DOC (77–88%) and UV254 (81–90%). Membrane characterization revealed that US cleaning removed major foulants, including proteinaceous organic matter and inorganic substances. These results suggest that periodic US cleaning is a promising strategy for stable operation without frequent chemical cleaning.
