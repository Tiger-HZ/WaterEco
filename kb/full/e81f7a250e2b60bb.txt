Menstrual dignity is a fundamental human right and an essential component of public health, encompassing not only access to menstrual hygiene products but also clean water, sanitation, health education, qualified healthcare services, and safe environments for menstrual management. This chapter discusses menstrual dignity as a human right, analyzes the impacts of menstrual poverty on health, education, and quality of life, presents the experience of the Sangrando Amor extension project, and explores the relationship between social vulnerability and violence against women. Initially, the chapter reviews the concepts of menstrual dignity and menstrual poverty, supported by scientific evidence and epidemiological data demonstrating their effects on health, school absenteeism, social exclusion, and gender inequality. It also examines international experiences and recent Brazilian public policies aimed at promoting menstrual health. Furthermore, the chapter describes the Sangrando Amor project, carried out in public schools in Sergipe, Brazil, which reached 2,584 students from 15 schools through health education activities, discussion groups, the distribution of 11,099 sanitary pads, and the installation of sanitary pad dispensers, producing positive outcomes in menstrual health education and menstrual dignity. Finally, the chapter addresses violence against women as a manifestation of gender inequality and highlights the need for intersectoral public policies integrating health, education, social assistance, and human rights. It concludes that promoting menstrual dignity is essential to reducing social inequities, strengthening citizenship, and ensuring healthier, safer, and more dignified lives.
