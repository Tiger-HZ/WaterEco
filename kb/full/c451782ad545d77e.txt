Abstract Wetlands, often referred to as the “Earth’s kidneys”, are essential ecosystems that filter pollutants and regulate biogeochemical processes. However, the mechanisms underlying their rapid self-purification are still in their infancy and lack deep understanding. Here, we propose that reactive oxygen species (ROS) constitute an intrinsic oxidative network that drives accelerated pollutant transformation in wetlands. We summarize that ROS are generated through coupled photochemical and redox-driven processes and are highly concentrated at environmental interfaces, including soil–water, fluctuating redox boundaries, and rhizosphere.
