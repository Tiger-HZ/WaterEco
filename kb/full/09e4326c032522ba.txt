Objective Petroleum-hydrocarbon plumes in groundwater are shaped by hydrological transport, redox conditions, and indigenous microbial activity. However, it remains unclear whether spatial differences among groundwater microbiomes primarily reflect physical separation among wells or ecological reorganization associated with distinct attenuation states within a plume. Methods We integrated hydrogeological context, petroleum-hydrocarbon concentrations, hydrochemical variables, and shotgun-metagenomic profiles from 15 groundwater monitoring wells at a petroleum-hydrocarbon-impacted site.
