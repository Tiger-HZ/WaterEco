Background. Xenoestrogens and microplastics are widespread environmental contaminants with potential endocrine-disrupting effects. Their exposure may interfere with hormonal signaling and contribute to disturbances in reproductive, metabolic, thyroid, and developmental processes. Aim. The aim of this review was to summarize current evidence on the sources, mechanisms, and health effects of xenoestrogens and microplastics, with particular emphasis on their relevance to physical activity. Materials and methods. A narrative literature review was conducted based on available human, animal, and in vitro studies addressing exposure to xenoestrogens, endocrine-disrupting chemicals, microplastics, and nanoplastics, as well as their potential endocrine and health effects.
