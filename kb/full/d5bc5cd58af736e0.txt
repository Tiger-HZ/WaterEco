Abstract Reactive transport modeling has become widely used to help improve understanding of hydrogeochemical processes from the pore scale to the watershed scale. In recent years, the scope of reactive transport applications has increased toward a higher level of complexity and process coupling. For example, the production and consumption of water as well as porosity evolution associated with the dissolution and precipitation of hydrated minerals can impact system evolution. Waste rock weathering, carbon sequestration, or the degradation of engineered barriers in radioactive waste repositories all constitute applications in which geochemistry and hydrodynamics can strongly influence each other.
