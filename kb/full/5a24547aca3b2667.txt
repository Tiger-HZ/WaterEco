Photon blockade produces antibunched light, but a source also needs a defined output port, nonzero extractable flux, and experimentally accessible controls. We analyze an atom-assisted three-wave-mixing cavity where the driven target mode is monitored directly as the output. A two-level emitter and an auxiliary pair-exchange channel provide coherent paths to the same target-mode Fock states, with their interference controlled by η = (Ω/ F a ) e i Δ ϕ . In the weak-drive limit, canceling the target two-photon amplitude gives high-purity single-photon blockade (1PB), whereas canceling the target three-photon amplitude gives pair-enhanced two-photon blockade (2PB).
