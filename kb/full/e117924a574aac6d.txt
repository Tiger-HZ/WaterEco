Abstract 
Two new taxa of the water mite genus Hygrobates Koch, 1837 are described from Cyprus: H. dorflingeri n. sp. 
and H. ponticus cyprioticus n. ssp . Both belong to the H. calliger complex, whose members are common 
components of water mite assemblages in low-, middle-, and high-order streams. Phylogenetic analysis and ASAP 
species delimitation based on COI barcoding support their recognition as distinct lineages. Their morphological 
and genetic similarity to close relatives from mainland Anatolia suggests potential dispersal pathways for ancestral 
lineages during the colonization of Cyprus. 
 
Key words new species, DNA barcoding, taxonomy, Mediterranean. 
 
 
Introduction 
 
Hygrobates Koch, 1837 is the second most species- rich genus in the family Hygrobatidae . In the 
Western Palearctic, the genus is represented by two subgenera: the polyacetabulate Rivobates , 
characterized by a genital field with more than three, often numerous, pairs of acetabula, and the 
nominate subgenus, which is distributed across all continents except Antarctica (Smit 2020). 
Traditionally, in water mite literature, Hygrobates specimens with a coarse ly lineated soft 
integument have been assigned to H. calliger Piersig, 1896. This species was considered a common 
inhabitant of low -, middle-, and high- order streams with a Palearctic distribution (Matsumoto et al. 
2005; Gerecke et al. 2016). Pešić et al. (2022) first questioned the taxonomic status of H. calliger using 
an integrative approach based on standardized sequences of the cytochrome c oxidase subunit I (COI) 
gene. Their DNA barcoding study demonstrated that H. calliger represents a species complex consisting 
of several distinct, previously overlooked lineages. Some of these lineages (e.g., H. calliger Clades 1, 2,

describe and introduce herein one new species and one new subspecies. 
 
 
Material and Methods 
 
Water mites were collected during a field trip to Cyprus in May 2025. The specimens examined in this 
study were sampled from two sites within the Kouris River catchment on the southern slopes of the 
Troodos Massif. Detailed descriptions of these sampling localities are provided by Pešić et al. (2026c). 
Additional specimens were obtained by the third author during a macroinvertebrate sampling campaign 
aimed at assessing the ecological quality of Cypriot streams. 
Specimens were collected using hand nets and immediately preserved in 96% ethanol for 
molecular analysis. Following non-destructive DNA extraction, specimen vouchers were stored in 96% 
ethanol for morphological examination. Selected vouchers were subsequen tly dissected and slide -
mounted in Faure’s medium, while the remaining specimens were transferred to Koenike’s fluid for 
long-term storage. The holotypes and paratypes of the new species are deposited in the Naturalis 
Biodiversity Center, Leiden, The Netherlands (RMNH). 
Morphological nomenclature follows Gerecke et al . (2016). All measurements are given in 
micrometers (µm). Genital acetabula are measured on both sides and their values are given as a range. 
The following abbreviations are used: Ac = acetabula; BS = bootstrap support; Cx-I = first coxae; dL = 
dorsal length; H = height; I-L-1-6 = first-sixth segment of first leg; L = length; mL = = median length; 
n = number of specimens examined; P- 1 to P-5 = palp segment 1 -5; RMNH = Naturalis Biodiversity 
Center, Leiden; W = width. 
 
DNA barcode analyses 
Molecular analyses were conducted at the Canadian Centre for DNA Barcoding (CCDB; ccdb.ca) in 
Guelph, Canada. At this institution, the barcode region of the COI gene was sequenced using standard 
invertebrate DNA extraction, amplification, and sequencing protocols. In total, DNA was extracted from 
five specimens of the Hygrobates calliger complex (Table 1). Relevant voucher information and 
consensus sequences were deposited in the Barcode of Life Data Systems (BOLD) under the project 
DS-CYPHYD "DNA Barcodes of water mites of Cyprus" (dx.doi.org/10.5883/DS-CYPHYD).

In addition to the sequences obtained in this study, additional COI sequence data of Hygrobates 
calliger complex were obtained from Pešić et al. (2022, 2023, 2026a) and downloaded from the BOLD 
Systems public database. Sequence alignments were performed using MUSCLE (Edgar 2004). Intra - 
and interspecific genetic distances were calculated based on the Kimura 2 -parameter model (K2P; 
Kimura 1980) using MEGA 11 software (Tamura et al. 2021). The latter was also utilized to construct 
Maximum Likelihood (ML) trees. The best -fit substitution model (TN93+G+I) was selected using the 
Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) implemented in MEGA 11. Tree construction incorporated an 
initial Neighbour-Joining (NJ) tree and utilized the Subtree -Pruning-Regrafting - Extensive heuristic 
search algorithm (SPR level 5). Branch support was calculated using the nonparametric bootstrap 
method (Felsenstein 1985) with 500 replicates, with values shown adjacent to the branches. Codon 
positions included were 1st+2nd+3rd+Noncoding. 
Additionally, the sequence data were analyzed using the Assemble Species by Automatic 
Partitioning (ASAP), a method designed to species partitioning using a hierarchical clustering algorithm 
based on the pairwise distance distribution ( Puillandre et al. 2021). We used the online ASAP version 
(https://spartexplorer.mnhn.fr/) with default settings and K2P distance model. 
 
 
Results 
 
Species delimitation using DNA-barcodes 
The final alignment for species delimitation using COI sequence data included 57 sequences from the 
Hygrobates calliger complex, with H. fluviatilis (Ström, 1768) (NLACA1046-17) from the Netherlands 
used as the outgroup. This final alignment consisted of 1,221 nucleotide positions. The resulting ML 
tree is presented in Figure 1. 
The COI phylogenetic analysis revealed nine highly supported, species-level clades within the 
studied H. calliger complex dataset. Six of these corresponded to the six previously known clades of the 
H. calliger complex identified by Pešić et al. (2022): 
• H. calliger Clade 1 sensu Pešić et al . 2022 ( BOLD:AEF4261, BOLD:AET1162, 
BOLD:AEO0848, BOLD:AFE0075), distributed from Portugal to Greece and northwards to 
Norway; 
• H. calliger Clade 2 sensu Pešić et al. 2022 (BOLD:AEL5782), known from Germany and Russia 
southwards to Montenegro and Bulgaria; 
• H. calliger Clade 3 sensu Pešić et al . 2022 ( BOLD:AEK4718, BOLD:AEK4719, 
BOLD:AEK4720) from Calabria (Italy); 
• H. calliger Clade 5 sensu Pešić et al . 2022 ( BOLD:AEO5296), known from Germany and 
Russia southwards to Croatia; 
• Hygrobates tyrrhenicus Pešić & Smit, 2022 (BOLD:AEO1422) from Corsica (France); and 
• H. ponticus Pešić, Esen & Mumladze, 2022, known from Türkiye and Georgia. 
Within the H. ponticus lineage, three subclades can be separated. The first subclade includes 
three specimens from Cyprus representing a unique BIN ( BOLD:AHI8370), described herein as H. 
ponticus cyprioticus n. ssp. This subclade is recovered as sister to the lineage ( BOLD:AEO0649) that 
includes the nominotypical subspecies, H. p. ponticus , from eastern Türkiye and Georgia. The third 
subclade encompasses sequences of another subspecies, H. ponticus yildirimi Pešić, Zawal, Gülle, 
Cakmak & Saboori, 2023, which is known from two BINs: one from southwestern Türkiye 
(BOLD:AEY7516) and one from eastern Türkiye ( BOLD:AGO8960). The seventh lineage, which 
includes two specimens from Cyprus described here as H. dorflingeri n. sp., was revealed with a very 
strong support (BS = 99%) as the sister clade of H. turani Pešić, 2026 from eastern Türkiye. Finally, the 
ninth lineage encompasses sequences of H. anatolicus Esen & Pešić, 2013 from eastern Türkiye (Esen 
et al. 2013), represented by two BINs (BOLD:AEX2126 and BOLD:AEY8569). 
The average genetic distance between the clades recovered within the studied Hygrobates 
calliger complex dataset ranged from 12.28% (between H. dorflingeri n. sp. and H. turani) to 25.74% 
(between H. dorflingeri n. sp. and H. calliger Clade 1; Table 2). The average K2P distance between 
clades within Hygrobates ponticus varied from 8.64% (between H. p. cyprioticus n. ssp . and H. p. 
ponticus) to 10.25% (between H. p. cyprioticus n. ssp. and H. p. yildirimi ). The ASAP delimitation 
method identified molecular operational taxonomic units (MOTUs) that mostly matched the nine

Discussion — Based on COI sequence data, the new species from Cyprus appears to be most 
closely related to Hygrobates turani, a species described from a single male collected in a stream along 
the Turkish Black Sea coast (Pešić et al. 2026a). Males of H. turani can easily be distinguished from the 
new species by a more projecting posteromedial margin of Cx-I, an anterior margin of the genital plate 
bearing a distinct knob- shaped medial projection, and comparatively larger genital acetabula, L Ac- 2 
and -3 > 100 µm (see Pešić et al. 2026a). 
Distribution — Cyprus; known only from the type locality (see Figure 6A in Pešić et al. 2026c).

Etymology — The subspecific epithet cyprioticus refers to Cyprus, where the subspecies was 
discovered. The name is an adjective in the nominative singular. 
 
DNA Barcode Analysis and Species Delimitation — The COI sequences form a unique cluster (BIN: 
BOLD:AHI8370), with the nearest neighboring BIN being that of H. ponticus ponticus 
(BOLD:AEO0649). The genetic distance between these two lineages was estimated at 8.64% K2P 
divergence, while the distance to the other subspecies, H. ponticus yildirimi, was estimated at 10.25% 
K2P (Table 2). 
 
Discussion — Molecular analysis revealed that the examined specimens from Cyprus represent a unique 
and well -supported COI clade. This clade appears to be genetically closer to the nominotypical 
subspecies from eastern Türkiye and Georgia than to H. ponticus yildirimi , which was originally 
described from southwestern Türkiye (Pešić et al . 2023). Given the allopatric distribution of the 
Cypriotic taxon relative to the two mainland subspecies, alongside its substantial genetic divergence (8.6 
to 10.3% K2P), we consider its status as a distinct subspecies justified. 
Morphologically, the new subspecies can be separated from both mainland subspecies. Females 
of the new subspecies exhibit a comparatively shorter gonopore, whereas it is distinctly longer and 
exceeds the length of the genital plate in the other two subspecies (see Pešić et al. 2022, fig. 10A; Pešić 
et al. 2023, fig. 1A). In males, the posterior margin of the genital plate has a small central protrusion 
within the indentation that extends beyond the posterior margin of the genital field, whereas it does not 
exceed the posterior margin in the other two subspecies (see Pešić et al. 2022, fig. 10C; Pešić et al. 2023, 
fig. 1B). Furthermore, according to its original description, Hygrobates ponticus yildirimi tends to have 
a longer ventrodistal projection on P -2 than the other two subspecies (L/W ratio: 1.2 vs. 1.0 in H. p. 
ponticus and H. p. cyprioticus n. ssp.). 
Distribution — Cyprus. 
 
 
Biogeographical Remarks 
 
Although the number of known water mite species from Cyprus is relatively limited (see Pešić et al. 
2026c for an overview), several significant characteristics of the water mite community and its faunal 
similarities with neighboring regions can be highlighted. The proximity of the island to mainland 
Anatolia appears to have facilitated colonization by species originating from the latter region.

• Torrenticola baueri — Insular populations of this species belong to the same BIN that is widely 
distributed across mainland Anatolia and Iran (Pešić et al. 2025). Based on current knowledge, 
the Cypriotic populations represent the westernmost limit of the species' distribution. 
• Atractides nodipalpis complex — The A. nodipalpis-like populations from Cyprus belong to A. 
concavus Pešić & Smit, 2021, a species widely distributed across mainland Anatolia. This case 
represents a clear example of allopatric divergence at population level driven by geographic 
isolation. The Cyprus populations form a distinct Barcode Index Number (BIN: 
BOLD:AHI8403) with a 4.27% p-distance from the closest mainland BIN (BOLD:AEK3669), 
which includes individuals of A. concavus from southwestern Anatolia. While long-term insular 
isolation has resulted in a clear genetic break, evolutionary pressures likely have not yet caused 
these populations to diverge into a distinct species. 
• Hygrobates persicus — Recently, Pešić et al. (2026b) argued that colonization by H. persicus 
occurred significantly earlier than the final separation of Cyprus from the mainland (south-
central Anatolia), which took place during the Pleistocene –Holocene transition within the last 
2.6 million years (Yılmaz 2021). These authors suggested that the H. persicus populations on 
the island have likely not had sufficient evolutionary time to diverge into a distinct species. 
• Hygrobates calliger complex — High genetic distances indicate that both new taxa described 
in this study are the result of long-term genetic isolation. Both taxa have their closest congeneric 
relatives in mainland Türkiye (Anatolia), further supporting the hypothesis that ancestral 
lineages colonized the island well before its final separation from the mainland.
