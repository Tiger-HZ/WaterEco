Background. Dentoalveolar trauma sustained during contact sports presents severe clinical challenges and lifelong economic burdens. Intraoral mouthguards serve as the primary preventive mechanism, yet protective efficacy varies significantly across commercial designs (Type I stock, Type II boil-and-bite, and Type III custom-made). Aim. This systematic review evaluated the biomechanical shock absorption, upper airway physiological impacts, athlete compliance dynamics, and socio-economic cost-effectiveness of different intraoral mouthguard modalities. Material and methods: A structured literature search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, and CENTRAL databases (2001–2026) was conducted to identify trials, biomechanical impact analyses, and epidemiological cohorts comparing mouthguard types.
