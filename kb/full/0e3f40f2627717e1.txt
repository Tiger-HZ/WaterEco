Abstract The persistent accumulation of public debt in developing economies has renewed concerns regarding its implications for long-term economic growth, particularly in countries where institutional frameworks remain fragile. While public borrowing can stimulate economic activity by financing productive investments, weak institutional quality may undermine the efficient allocation and management of borrowed resources, thereby limiting the growth-enhancing effects of debt. Despite extensive empirical investigations, evidence on the joint role of institutional quality and public debt in shaping economic growth remains inconclusive, especially for Ghana.
