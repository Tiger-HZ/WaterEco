This contribution briefly reviews and discusses the evolution of forest inventories in Italy − a representative European country for biodiversity stock and landscape diversification. The role of forest monitoring in planning and sustainable management, especially in light of climate change, was specifically investigated. Given the increasing need to quantify the ecological value of landscapes and carbon stocks in agricultural and natural soils, the objectives, methodologies, and innovations of the main Italian forest inventories were described here and technical details discussed extensively. Starting from the first inventory (hereafter IFNI85) and moving to the second and the third survey (namely IFNC 2003 and INFC2015), the present work explores founding 74 criteria, sampling techniques, technologies used (e.g. GPS, dedicated software, geographic information systems), and the growing integration level of empirical data from remote sensing and ground surveys. The evolution and progressive alignment with international standards, in particular FAO statistics and CORINE Land Cover (CLC) mapping, emerged up to the present day, where forestry information is mainly disseminated via official platforms. The chapter finally highlights the growing importance of forest inventories for planning, ecosystem protection, and carbon assessment at both national and global levels.
