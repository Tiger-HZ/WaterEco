The European Union’s Battery Regulation, implemented in 2024, significantly transforms the European battery value chain by promoting circular economy approaches to meet the rapidly increasing demand for batteries. The objective of this study was to evaluate the dynamic changes in nickel and lithium material flows and their consequent impact on the environmental footprint of the value chain over the next 25 years. The analysis covers both primary and secondary raw material production. The primary raw material system encompasses the processes from mining to the production of nickel hydroxide or lithium carbonate. The secondary raw material system involves recycling end-of-life batteries using hydrometallurgical methods. The impacts were calculated per kg Li2CO3 and kg of Ni(OH)2 for six different electricity scenarios with varying shares of electricity from renewables, fossil fuels, and nuclear energy. The material flow is based on the demand for electric vehicle batteries in Finland and the EU for the years 2010 to 2050. The dynamic material flow showed the extent and the timeframe at which secondary raw materials have the potential to substitute the primary raw materials and the related reductions in the environmental impacts. The results of this study indicate that the circular transition of EV batteries will result in a reduction in environmental impacts, however, the reduction depends on the electricity mix used in the recycling process. For the climate change category, the reduction impact compared to scenarios relying solely on primary raw materials varied between approximately 40 to 70 % depending on the applied electricity mix.
