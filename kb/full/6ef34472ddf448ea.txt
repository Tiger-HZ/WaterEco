Enhancing nutrient uptake and crop yield in mustard 
(Brassica juncea L.) via integrated nutrient management 
approaches 
 
ILI Venkatesh, Krishna Chaitanya Anantha, B Raju and P Revathi 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9g.6549 
 
Abstract 
During the Rabi season of 2020-21, an experiment took place at College Farm, Agricultural College, Polasa, 
Jagtial. The objective of the study was to investigate the impact of integrated nutrient management on the 
yield and nutrient uptake of mustard ( Brassica juncea L.) crop in the Northern Telangana Zone. The 
experiment was conducted in sandy clay loam soil and followed a randomized block design with nine 
treatments, each replicated three times. The treatments include T1: 100% Recommended dose of Fertilizer 
(RDF), T2: 100% RDF + FYM, T3: 100% RDF + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium, T4: 75% RDF, T5: 75% 
RDF + FYM, T6: 75% RDF + FYM + Biofertilizer consort ium, T7: Soil Test Based NPK, T8: 75% STB 
NPK + FYM and T9: 75% STB (Soil Test Based) NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium. Among the 
treatments, the combination of 75% STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium resulted in the highest seed 
yield for mustard (1666 kg ha-1), statistically equivalent to STB NPK (1644 kg ha -1), 100% RDF + FYM + 
Biofertilizer consortium (1593 kg ha -1) and 75% STB NPK + FYM (1565 kg ha -1). This treatment also 
exhibited significantly greater uptake of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, sulphur and zinc, comparable to 
the effects observed with Soil Test Based NPK, 100% RDF + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium, and 75% 
STB NPK + FYM treatments. Further, it was observed that lower N, P, K, S and Zn uptakes in seed and 
stover and at different stages of crop were recorded in the treatment 75 % RDF. The positive effects of 
organics and biofertilizers contributed to increased nutrient availability and uptake which promoted the 
increase of mustard yield. 
 
Keywords: Indian mustard, biofertilizers, FYM, nutrient uptake, seed and Stover yield. 
 
Introduction 
Oilseeds rank as India's second largest agricultural commodity, trailing only cereals, with a 
production of 31.3 million tonnes across 24.65 million hectares (Agricultural Statistics at a Glance 
2018) [1]. Among these oilseeds, rapeseed and mustard hold the second most crucial position 
as Rabi oilseed crops, contributing nearly 26% to the country's total oilseed production (Ajnar and 
Namdeo, 2021) [2]. On a global scale, India stands as the third highest producer of rapeseed and 
mustard, following China and Canada. This crop constitutes approximately one-third of India's oil 
output, solidifying its significance in the nation's edible oil seed sector. In terms of worldwide 
figures, India accounts for 28.3% of cultivated area and 12.0% of production (Mhetre et al., 2019) 
[13]. Within India, it holds the status of being the third most vital edible oil crop, trailing behind 
soybean and groundnut, contributing 27.8% to the oil economy. However, in India, inadequate 
nutrient utilization and suboptimal management practices have led to an average rapeseed mustard 
yield of just 1,089 kg per hectare (Anonymous, 2014) [3]. The productivity of rapeseed mustard is 
constrained by factors like rainfed cultivation on marginal land and suboptimal fertilizer use. 
Moreover, mineral nutrition significantly impacts the quality of mustard oil and cake (Tripathi et 
al., 2010) [24]. Overuse of chemical fertilizers can harm the soil's biological vitality, a process that 
must be prevented, as soil microbial communities drive all nutrient transformations. Excessive 
chemical fertilizer use, unbalanced or insufficient application and restricted organic fertilizer 
utilization contribute not only to nutrient deficiency in soil but also to its deterioration. Integrated 
Nutrient Management (INM) emerges as a flexible approach that minimizes reliance on chemical 
nutrient sources while maximizing farmer profitability.

INM incorporates chemical fertilizers, organic manures, legumes, 
crop residues and biological fertilizers. Adopting integrated plant 
nutrient supply and management strategies holds crucial 
importance for enhancing soil quality, input efficiency and crop 
yield, thereby boosting food and nutrition security in Indian 
agriculture (Swarup, 2010) [22]. Integrated nutrient management is 
vital not only for sustained high yields over time (Verma et al., 
2010) [25] but also for advancing soil health and ensuring 
environmental safety. The choice of fertilizer sources 
significantly influences plant nutrient uptake. Hence, the present 
study was conducted to assess mustard yield and nutrient uptake 
under diverse integrated nutrient management scenarios. 
 
Material and methods 
A field experiment was carried out at College Farm of 
Agricultural College, Jagtial with mustard as a test crop during 
Rabi, 2020. The experimental farm was located at 18º 50' 58'' N 
latitude and 78º 56' 97'' E longitude at an altitude of 243.4 m above 
mean sea level (MSL). The weekly mean maximum temperature 
ranged from 33.5°C to 30.8°C during the crop growth period. The 
mean minimum temperature for the corresponding period ranged 
from 12.8 °C to 18.6 °C, while the average maxi mum and 
minimum temperatures during the same period were 31.4 °C and 
16.0 °C, respectively. The average relative humidity was 66.7 %. 
The weekly mean evaporation ranged from 1.8 mm to 4.3 mm 
during the crop growth period. The cultivar used for the study was 
NRCHB-101. The experiment was laid out in Randomized Block 
Design (RBD) with three replications and nine treatments which 
includes T1: 100% Recommended Dose of Fertilizer (RDF), T2: 
100% RDF + FYM, T3: 100% RDF + FYM + Biofertilizer 
consortium, T4: 75% RDF, T5: 75% RDF + FYM, T6: 75% RDF 
+ FYM + Biofertilizer consortium, T7: Soil Test Based (STB) 
NPK, T8: 75% STB NPK + FYM and T9: 75% STB NPK + FYM 
+ Biofertilizer consortium. Biofertilizer consortium includes 
Azotobacter + Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria + Potassium 
Solubilizing Bacteria + Zinc Solubilizing Bacteria. The 
treatments were allotted randomly in each replication. 
 
Site description 
The initial analysis of soil samples revealed that the experimental 
soil is sandy clay loam texture. It exhibited lo w availability of 
nitrogen (196 kg ha-1), while phosphorus content was notably high 
(26.6 kg ha -1), as was potassium content (360 kg ha -1) and 
available sulphur content (26.8 kg ha -1). The recommended 
fertilizer dosage for mustard comprised 60 kg ha-1 of nitrogen, 40 
kg ha -1 of phosphorus, and 40 kg ha -1 of potassium. These 
nutrients were applied in the form of urea (46% N), single super 
phosphate (16% P 2O5), and muriate of potash (60% K 2O), 
respectively. Initial applications incorporated phosphorus, 
potassium and half of the nitrogen during the crop's sowing stage, 
while the remaining half of the nitrogen was applied at the 
flowering phase of the mustard (55- 60 days after sowing). 
Biofertilizers and farm yard manure (FYM) corresponding to 
each treatment were added into the soil before sowing. 
 
Plant sampling and analysis 
Plant samples at 30 DAS, 60 DAS and at harvest were collected 
from each plot after eliminating the border rows and the samples 
were washed with tap water followed by 0.1 N HCl and distilled 
water. They were first dried under shade and then in hot air oven 
at 60 0C. Oven dried plant samples were ground in grinder and 
stored in butter paper covers and later analysed for N, P, K, S and 
Zn. Nitrogen content in plant sample was deter mined by micro 
kjeldahl distillation method using Kelplus equipment (Jackson, 
1973) [8], Phosphorous content in the extract was estimated by 
reacting the extract with vanadomolybdate forming yellow colour 
complex in HNO3 medium and the content of potassium in diacid 
extract was determined by using flame photometer (Jackson, 
1967) [7] and was expressed as percentage. Sulphur content in the 
extract was estimated by developing turbidity with supplementing 
of stabilizing agent, gum acacia and a pinch of barium chloride to 
the diacid extract and Zinc in diacid extract at 30, 60 DAS and at 
harvest in plants was determined by using ICP -AES as per the 
specifications mentioned by Lindsay and Norvell (1978) [12]. 
 
Results and discussion 
Seed and stover yield 
Different integrated nutrient management options significantly 
influenced the seed yield of mustard (Table 1). The results of the 
experiment revealed that the seed yield showed variation from 
1143 kg ha-1 to 1666 kg ha -1 with a mean seed yield of 1416 kg 
ha-1. Sign ificantly higher seed yield was recorded under the 
treatment 75% STB NPK+ FYM + Biofertilizer consortium (1666 
kg ha ha-1) which was on par with STB NPK (1644 kg ha-1), 100% 
RDF+FYM+ Biofertilizer consortium (1593 kg ha -1), 75% STB 
NPK + FYM (1565 kg ha-1) and the lower seed yield was recorded 
under the treatment 75 % RDF (1143 kg ha ha -1). INM and soil 
test based fertilizer treatments recorded 34.4, 32.6, 28.5 and 
26.2% higher seed yield under 75% STB NPK + FYM + 
Biofertilizer consortium, STB, 100% RDF+FYM+ Biofertilizer 
consortium, 75% STB + FYM respectively as compared to 100% 
RDF. Higher seed yields under STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer 
consortium treatment was due to the combined application of 
nutrients through inorganic and organic sources provided 
balanced nutrition to the crop and improved the physical, 
chemical and biological properties of soil and maintained the 
nutrient supply throughout the plant growth stages which 
promoted vegetative growth and ultimately increased seed yield 
(Parmar et al. 2020 a nd Singh et al. 2006) [17, 19-20] . The 
comprehensive application of chemical fertilizers, organic 
fertilizers and microbial agents provided sufficient nutrients 
throughout the growth period. These results are in conformity 
with the finding of Ajnar and Namdeo (2021) [2]. Similar to seed 
yield stover yield of mustard was recorded significantly higher 
under the treatment 75% STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer 
consortium (3546 kg ha -1) which was on par with STB (3475 kg 
ha-1), 100% RDF+FYM+ Biofertilizer consort ium (3359 kg ha-1) 
and 75% STB NPK + FYM (3307 kg ha-1). The lower stover yield 
was recorded under the treatment 75 % RDF (2675 kg ha-1). The 
increase in stover yield can be attributed to the general 
improvement in plant organs (plant height and branches) 
associated with faster and more uniform vegetative growth of 
crops under the effect of INM and soil test based fertilizer 
application treatments. Similar findings were also reported by 
Parmar et al. (2020) [17]. The higher straw yield was mainly due 
to higher dry matter accumulation and also more translocation of 
photosynthates towards sink which is observed in treatments 
receiving integration of RDF along with organics and 
biofertilizers (Kaparwan et al. 2020, Chand et al. 2004 and Arya 
et al. 2007) [9, 5, 4].

Nutrient uptake 
The percentage of nutrient content in the seed and the dry matter 
formulation influence nutrient uptake. N, P, K, S and Zn uptake 
was recorded significantly higher with the integrated application 
of 75% STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium which was 
on par with STB NPK, 100% RDF+FYM+ Biofertilizer 
consortium and 75% STB NPK + FYM at 30 DAS, 60 DAS, seed 
and in stover. Further observation of data revealed that lower 
uptake of these nutrients was recorded in treatment receiving 75% 
RDF. Nitrogen uptake in plants at 30 DAS was recorded 
significantly higher under 75% STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer 
consortium (8.90 kg ha-1) which was on par with STB NPK (8.58 
kg ha-1), 100% RDF+FYM+ Biofertilizer consortium (8.13 kg ha-
1) and 75% STB NPK + FYM (7.85 kg ha -1). Further it was 
observed that lower N uptake of 4.87 kg ha -1 was recorded in the 
treatment 75% RDF (Table 2). Similar to nitrogen, P, K, S and 
Zn followed the same trend at 30 DAS. Phosphorus, potassium, 
sulphur and zinc uptake ranged from 0.83 to 1.50 kg ha-1, 4.60 to 
8.99 kg ha -1, 0.95 to 1.86 kg ha -1 and 9.19 to 17.8 g ha -1, 
respectively. Nutrient uptake at 60 DAS also followed the similar 
trend as at 30 DAS. At 60 DAS after sowing nitrogen uptake 
ranged from 26.3 to 41.2 kg ha -1, phosphorus uptake varied from 
5.86 to 11.0 kg ha-1, potassium uptake varied from 24.9 to 8.99 kg 
ha-1, sulphur ranged from 5.86 to 11.2 kg ha -1 and zinc uptake 
varied from 47.5 to 86.4 g ha -1. Integrated application of 75% 
STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium recorded the higher 
nitrogen uptake in seed (48.6 kg ha-1) which was comparable with 
application of Soil Test Based NPK (47.3 kg ha -1), 100% RDF + 
FYM + Biofertilizer consortium (45.1 kg ha-1) and 75% STB NPK 
+ FYM (43.2 kg ha -1). Similar to nitrogen, the phosphorus, 
potassium, sulphur and zinc uptake was also recorded higher with 
the integrated application of 75% STB NPK + FYM + 
Biofertilizer consortium, Soil Test Based NPK, 100 % RDF + 
FYM + Biofertilizer consortium and 75% STB NPK + FYM. In 
seed, phosphorus uptake ranged from 5.33 to 10.9 kg ha -1, 
potassium uptake was 5.63 to 10.8 kg ha -1 and sulphur uptake 
ranged from 5.62 to 11.2 kg ha -1. Lower uptake of all these 
nutrients was recorded in the treatment 75% RDF. Combined 
application of organics, inorganics and biofertilizers significantly 
influenced the nitrogen uptake by mustard stover. Significantly 
higher N uptake of 23.4 kg ha-1 was recorded with the application 
of 75% STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium which was 
on par with Soil Test Based NPK (22.8 kg ha -1), 100% RDF + 
FYM + Biofertilizer consortium (21.5 kg ha-1) and 75% STB NPK 
+ FYM (20.5 kg ha-1). Further it was observed that lower N uptake 
of 12.9 kg ha-1 in stover was recorded in the treatment 75% RDF. 
Similar to nitrogen, the phosphorus, potassium, sulphur and zinc 
uptake in stover was also recorded higher with the integrated 
application of 75% STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium 
which was comparable to Soil Test Based NPK, 100 % RDF + 
FYM + Biofertilizer consortium and 75% STB NPK + FYM. In 
stover phosphorus, potassium, sulphur and zinc uptake ranged 
from 4.29 to 8.17 kg ha-1, 38.7 to 68.1 kg ha-1, 12.3 to 22.1 kg ha-
1 and 35.2 to 73.4 g ha-1, respectively. 
It is observed that integrated application of RDF along with FYM 
and biofertilizers showed significant increase in nutrient uptake 
of different macronutrients (N, P, K and S) and dry matter 
accumulation might be due to positive effect of inorganic 
fertilizer applied along with FYM and biofe rtilizers. Balanced 
supply of nutrients to the soil leads to well developed root system 
which results in better absorption of water and nutrients (Sahoo, 
2013 and Singh and Sinsinwar 2006) [18, 19-20]. FYM application 
might have created favourable physical and chemical 
environment for root to uptake nutrients (Ajnar and Namdeo 
2021). More over FYM and biofertilizer consortium might add 
additional amount of nutrients to soil and making them available 
for longer period which synchronies with plant needs (Das et al. 
2010 and Kumar et al. 2019) [6, 11]. 
Increased uptake of N by the crop with FYM application might 
be due to the favourable effect of FYM on N absorption by the 
plant (Singh, 2020 and Kumar and Singh, 2019) [10, 11]. Increased 
P availability by applied fertilizers and native soil P and combined 
effect of released organic acids and organic anions on 
decomposition of farmyard manure in soil increased P uptake. 
Similar findings were reported by Mohapatra and Dixit (2010) [15] 
and Parihar et al. (2012) [16]. The findings on the increase in 
nutrient content and uptake by application of chemical fertilizers 
with FYM and biofertilizers are in agreement with the 
observations made by Mitra and Mandal (2012) [14].

Conclusion 
In conclusion, the present study highlights the significant impact 
of nutrient content in seeds and the formulation of dry matter on 
nutrient uptake in mustard plants. The integrated application of 
75% STB NPK + FYM + Biofertilizer consortium exhibited 
noteworthy outcomes, leading to increased nitrogen (N), 
phosphorus (P), potassium (K), sulphur (S), and zinc (Zn) uptake 
across various growth stages and plant parts. At 30 DAS and 60 
DAS, this integrated approach yielded higher N uptake, with 75% 
STB NPK + F YM + Biofertilizer consortium demonstrating 
comparable results to STB NPK, 100% RDF + FYM + 
Biofertilizer consortium, and 75% STB NPK + FYM. Similar 
trends were observed for P, K, S, and Zn uptake. Furthermore, the 
seed nutrient content was positively infl uenced by the integrated 
approach, particularly with regards to N, P, K, and S uptake, 
indicating the potential for improved seed quality and yield. This 
enhanced nutrient uptake is attributed to the synergistic effects of 
balanced nutrient management, inc luding inorganic fertilizers, 
FYM, and biofertilizers. The combined application of these 
elements created a conducive soil environment, promoting robust 
root development and efficient nutrient absorption. The positive 
effects of FYM on N absorption and the release of organic acids 
and anions further contributed to nutrient availability and uptake. 
The findings underscore the importance of a comprehensive 
nutrient management strategy that incorporates both organic and 
inorganic sources, supported by bioferti lizers. This holistic 
approach not only enhances nutrient uptake and plant growth but 
also holds promise for sustainable and improved agricultural 
productivity. The results of this study align with previous 
research, reinforcing the value of integrated nutrient management 
practices for optimizing crop performance and nutrient 
utilization.
