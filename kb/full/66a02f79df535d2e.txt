Abstract In this work, cotton stalks ( Gossypium barbadense ) and date palm stones ( Phoenix dactylifera ) have been used as biosorbents to remove cadmium; Cd(II), lead; Pb(II), and zinc; Zn(II) from mono- and multi-solutions. Each biosorbent was characterized using SEM-EDX, and FT-IR. The findings showed that pH, dose, contact time, metal concentration, and particle size affect the treatment process. The adsorption pattern was Pb(II) &gt; Cd(II) &gt; Zn(II) for both biosorbents. The adsorption performance of cotton stalks was higher than that of date palm stones. The fitted maximum uptake capacities; q m of cotton stalks were higher than those of date palm stones.
