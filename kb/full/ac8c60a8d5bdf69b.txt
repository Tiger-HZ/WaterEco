Abstract Accurate localization and reliable obstacle avoidance remain challenging for underwater robots in shallow and unstructured environments, where optical sensing can be degraded by light attenuation and turbidity, while acoustic sensing may be affected by multipath propagation and environmental noise. Weakly electric fish exploit externally generated electric-field signals for passive electrosensing and electrocommunication, and actively emit and receive self-generated electric signals to perceive nearby objects in turbid and confined aquatic environments.
