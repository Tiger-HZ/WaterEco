Abstract Background Quality of life (QoL) plays a crucial role in dementia care; however, QoL and its dynamic, context-dependent nature can be difficult to capture among people living with dementia due to challenges in memory and communication, and limitations of self-reported QoL instruments. Observational tools such as the Maastricht Electronic Daily Life Observation (MEDLO) provide narrative descriptions of the daily life of people living with dementia in nursing homes. However, the MEDLO tool was not developed to assess QoL specifically, and it remains unclear to what extent its narrative descriptions reflect aspects of QoL.
