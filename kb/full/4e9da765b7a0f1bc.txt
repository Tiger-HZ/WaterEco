Abstract Minimizing climate model biases is essential to reduce uncertainties in future climate projections. Despite recent advances, improvements between generations of Earth System Models (ESMs) remain modest, largely due to continued reliance on subgrid-scale parametrizations. These are required because CMIP6 model resolutions are too coarse to explicitly simulate small-scale processes such as ocean mesoscale eddies and deep atmospheric convection, which strongly influence climate patterns. Recent gains in computational power have enabled higher-resolution ESMs that can resolve some of these processes, thereby reducing the need for parametrization.
