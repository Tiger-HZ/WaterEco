To offset losses of seagrasses from natural and anthropogenic impacts, resource managers have adopted approaches such as water clarity-based standards. We evaluated whether North Carolina’s (NC) proposed light threshold (22% surface irradiance [SI] up to depths ≤1.7 m) sufficiently supports Halodule wrightii. We conducted two 3 wk field experiments to test combined effects of seasonal temperature (optimal ≥25°C; cold stress &lt;15°C) and light (ambient; ambient reduced by 80%; ambient reduced by 90%) on structural (biomass, shoot density, leaf length) and physiological (C:N ratio, nonstructural carbohydrates) responses.
