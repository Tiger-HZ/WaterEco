Abstract To quantify the coupled effects of moisture state, pore-structure state, and applied vertical pressure on loess collapsibility, a series of laboratory collapse tests was conducted on remolded loess collected from the Ili region of Xinjiang, China. The water content ranged from 6.6% to 21.0%, the dry density ranged from 1.24 to 1.47 g/cm³, and the applied vertical pressure ranged from 50 to 2000 kPa. The relationships of the collapse coefficient with water content, degree of saturation, dry density, and applied vertical pressure were first examined. Dry density was converted to void ratio, while degree of saturation was calculated from water content and void ratio.
