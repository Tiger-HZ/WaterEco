Abstract Water crisis, driven by scarcity, pollution, and uneven distribution, threatens water security worldwide. Water reuse projects can contribute to sustainable water-management objectives by augmenting water supplies, reducing pressure on freshwater sources, and generating economic, environmental, and social co-benefits when implemented within appropriate hydrological, institutional, regulatory, and governance contexts. However, prevailing evaluation approaches often remain incomplete, emphasizing direct financial gains (e.g. avoided imports and cost savings) while undercounting broader benefits such as energy savings, greenhouse gas reductions, water quality improvements, and flood mitigation.
