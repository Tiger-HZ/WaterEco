The Pothwar Plateau, covering approximately 1.82 million hectares in northern Punjab, Pakistan, is one of South Asia's largest contiguous rainfed (barani) farming systems and receives no perennial river or canal supply across most of its cultivated area. Its undulating topography, erratic rainfall regime, fragile loess-derived soils, and centuries of subsistence cultivation have produced a degraded agro-ecological landscape in which about 20% of land is affected by accelerated water erosion and an estimated 4.2 billion cubic metres of water is lost annually as surface runoff. These stresses are amplified by documented climate change, manifesting as increased heat-wave frequency, flash floods, droughts and shifting monsoon patterns.
