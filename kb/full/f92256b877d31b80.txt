Deepwater sandstone reservoirs are important hydrocarbon-bearing media and are prone to pore structure reconfiguration and seepage path evolution under long-term high-rate development conditions, which can significantly affect reservoir performance. However, their dynamic response mechanisms remain insufficiently understood. To address this issue, this study investigates the responses of deepwater sandstone reservoirs with different initial pore throat structures to prolonged high-rate water injection. Reservoir samples are first classified into three classes based on pore throat characteristics and fractal parameters derived from mercury intrusion capillary pressure (MICP) data.
