[“Adaptations: Investigate Relationships Between Adaptations and Their Source Texts” describes how readers can make sense of adaptations of other works. It encourages readers to avoid discussing adaptations in terms of fidelity or faithfulness to the source text. Instead, readers should focus on what has been added, deleted, and modified, and then draw conclusions about the effects, purposes, or results of these transformations. The chapter illustrates how to apply the strategy and includes discussions of Rashid Johnson's Native Son, ekphrasis , “Cinderella,” and Percival Everett's James, a Novel . The chapter then offers readers advice on the kinds of questions one should ask when making sense of an adaptation.]
