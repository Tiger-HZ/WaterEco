Abstract Plasma-activated water (PAW) has attracted attention for agricultural applications because plasma treatment can generate reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS) and promote nitrate formation. However, increasing plasma power to enhance nitrate production also increases energy consumption, while the resulting nitrate increase may remain limited. In this study, plasma-activated coffee ground solutions (PAC) were prepared by introducing spent coffee grounds into deionized water treated at different plasma input powers. Nitrate production and the physicochemical properties of PAW and PAC were compared to evaluate the effects of plasma power and the presence of the organic species.
