Abstract Aquatic eutrophication is a significant impact category in Life Cycle Assessment in countries with high environmental heterogeneity, such as Argentina. However, the usual practice in these studies is to use global characterization factors (given the lack of regional factors), which leads to results that generally do not reflect local conditions. To address this issue, phosphorus-based regional characterization factors are developed at the midpoint level to improve the spatial representativeness of this category. The methodology adapts and improves an existing model by differentiating the impact between ecological regions and incorporating observed concentrations of total phosphorus into the calculation of the effect factor.
