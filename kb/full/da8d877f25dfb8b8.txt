Recognition of phages as key components in biological processes for water decontamination has broadened the conceptual framework of microbial ecology, highlighting their capacity to participate in carbon cycle, nutrient removal, and micropollutant degradation <i>via</i> host lysis and lysogenization. This review integrates recent advances in phage research methodologies, clarifies the effects of phages on microbial populations, interactions, and metabolism in wastewater treatment, and discusses the phage modification strategies for water decontamination. An artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted workflow for phage detection, classification, host linkage, and functional inference has been developed and applied in wastewater treatment systems. The effects of phage-mediated lysis in water decontamination is analyzed across biological scales, encompassing both population-level and community-level. Phage-encoded auxiliary metabolic genes (AMGs) in biological wastewater treatment are summarized, with a note that functional interpretations can be unreliable. Temperate phages are discussed from a balanced perspective, considering potential beneficial effects as well as neutral or costly impacts on host fitness and wastewater treatment performance. Strategies for phage modification aimed at improving functional stability and programmability in wastewater treatment are reviewed, followed by an assessment of key challenges constraining practical applications. Collectively, this work provides a coherent framework to advance phage research for water decontamination.
