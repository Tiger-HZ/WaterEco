Abstract This study aimed to investigate changes in heart rate variability (HRV) in patients undergoing cardiac surgery and examined the association between HRV and patient-reported quality of life (QoL) during postoperative recovery. Twenty-one patients who underwent cardiac surgery were prospectively enrolled. Short-term HRV was measured at three timepoints: preoperatively (measurement 1), early postoperatively (measurement 2), and at 1 month postoperatively (measurement 3). The HRV indices included standard deviation of all normal-to-normal intervals (SDNN), low-frequency (LF) power, high-frequency (HF) power, low-frequency percentage (LF%), and the ratio of low-to-high frequency (LF/HF).
