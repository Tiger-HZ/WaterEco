Heavy-metal contamination of water remains a major environmental challenge, requiring adsorbents capable of efficient removal under realistic operating conditions while maintaining rapid kinetics, selectivity, and reusability. Although metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) have emerged as promising adsorbents for water remediation, most reported systems rely on post-synthetic functionalization and are typically evaluated under simplified laboratory conditions. In contrast, anionic MOFs possessing intrinsic ion-exchange properties remain largely underexplored for heavy-metal removal. Herein, we investigate SU-102, a robust bio-derived zirconium-based anionic MOF, for the removal of Pb²⁺, Hg²⁺, and Cd²⁺ from aqueous media. The intrinsic anionic framework promotes rapid metal capture through electrostatically driven ion exchange, enabling ultrafast adsorption with equilibrium reached within approximately 1 min for Pb²⁺ and 5 min for Hg²⁺ and Cd²⁺.. Adsorption studies reveal a consistent uptake trend of Pb2+ &gt; Hg2+ &gt; Cd2+, maintained under competitive conditions. Notably, SU-102 achieves complete removal of all three metals at environmentally relevant concentrations (1 mg L⁻¹) in both distilled water and real groundwater. Furthermore, SU-102 can be readily regenerated using a simple NaCl solution, highlighting the reversible adsorption behavior and enabling efficient reuse without harsh chemical treatments. Density functional theory calculations further provide molecular-level evidence that the experimentally observed selectivity order arises from stronger thermodynamic stabilization, enhanced electronic coupling, and more favorable interactions with the oxygen-rich adsorption sites of the ellagic acid ligand. These results establish SU-102 as an efficient and regenerable adsorbent for heavy-metal removal, making it a promising platform for practical water-treatment applications.
