Abstract Kongsfjorden, an Arctic fjord on west Svalbard, is strongly influenced by the advection of warm, saline Atlantic Water (AW) and has undergone pronounced changes in primary productivity in recent decades. Summer‐averaged chlorophyll concentration from CTD observations revealed exceptionally high values in 2014, far above the typical summer maxima. Enhanced productivity during early summer was associated with increased AW inflow, which supplied warm, nutrient‐rich waters to the fjord. In contrast, a short‐lived but intense productivity peak occurred in late summer, spanning the entire water column.
