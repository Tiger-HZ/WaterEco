The tanning, textile and dye industries utilize significant quantities of water throughout their production processes, resulting in substantial volumes of wastewater. This research seeks to shed light on the phytoremediation potential of microalgae biofilms in treating wastewater from tanneries, textiles and dyeing operations. Algae are recognized as a viable and sustainable source of natural antioxidants, which have diverse applications across multiple industries. Various algal species exhibit differing levels of antioxidant content and activity. Algal azoreductase is the enzyme responsible for the breakdown of azo dyes into aromatic amines by cleaving the azo bond and rate of reduction seems to correlate with both the molecular structure of the dyes and the specific algal species employed. The current study outlines the dye degradation capabilities and antioxidant properties of the predominant algal taxa, specifically two Chlorophyceae algae, Pithophora oedogonia and Spirogyra fuellebornei, as well as two Cyanophyceae algae, Scytonema guyanense and Oscillatoria jenensis, sourced from the English bazaar block and Old Malda block in the Malda district respectively. The capacity of algae to degrade azo dyes was assessed, revealing that certain algal species can partially degrade a variety of azo dyes. These algae were extracted from textile effluent factories located near the Mahananda River. This study proposes that in stabilization ponds, algae can actively contribute to the degradation of azo dyes, rather than merely serving to supply oxygen for bacterial proliferation.
