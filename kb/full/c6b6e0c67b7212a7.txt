Background: Excess adiposity is associated with cardiometabolic disease, musculoskeletal dysfunction, and altered biomechanical efficiency¹⁻⁵.However, composition-specific relationships between adiposity and functional movement competency remain incompletely characterized in adult populations.Purpose: To determine whether DXA-derived body fat percentage independently predicts functional movement quality and to evaluate differential sensitivity across discrete movement tasks.Methods: Cross-sectional analysis of 265 adults aged 18-65 years.Body composition was assessed via dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry.Functional movement was evaluated using deep squat and posterior rocking assessments scored on a 1-3 ordinal scale.Pearson correlations and multivariable regression adjusting for age and sex were conducted.Results: Body fat percentage was inversely associated with composite movement score (r = -0.417,p < 0.001).Deep squat demonstrated stronger association (r = -0.391)than posterior rocking (r = -0.069).In adjusted regression, body fat percentage remained independently predictive (β = -0.0117,95% CI -0.0155 to -0.0080), explaining 0.208 of variance.
