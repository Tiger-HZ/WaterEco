Abstract
Microbial communities are crucial contributors to the cycling of elements within groundwater systems. However, limited 
research has been conducted on the composition and assembly mechanisms of microbial communities in groundwa -
ter within the intensively cultivated vegetable areas (IVCs) of the Huaibei Plain, as well as the relationships between 
microbial communities and hydrogeochemical parameters. Therefore, in this study, the structure and assembly process of 
prokaryotic microorganisms in the groundwater of IVCs and rural areas (RAs) in the Huaibei Plain of China were inves -
tigated, and the factors influencing the microbiota structure, function, and assembly process in IVC and RA groundwater 
were assessed based on hydrogeochemistry, co-occurrence network analysis, and null modeling analysis. The main find -
ings indicate that groundwater from IVCs significantly increase NO 3-N concentrations, compareed with RA groundwater. 
Despite considerable similarity in groundwater microbial community composition between the IVCs and RA regions, 
significant differences were identified in a subset of microbial taxa across phylogenetic levels from phylum to genus. 
However, the groundwater in the IVC had a higher abundance of microorganisms related to nitrification at the genus level. 
Co-occurrence network analysis demonstrated that the microbial communities in the RA groundwater exhibited more the 
percentage of positively correlated edges, and the key species in the microbial communities of IVC groundwater were 
related to nitrogen and sulfur cycling. The null model analysis results indicated that dispersal limitation in stochastic 
processes dominated the assembly of groundwater microbial communities in the IVCs and RAs. Multivariate statistical 
analysis identified that pH is the key environmental factor controlling the groundwater microbial community composition 
and assembly process in IVCs. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the influence of IVCs on ground -
water physicochemical properties and microbial community assembly mechanisms.

Introduction
Agricultural production is the primary industry in the Huai-
bei Plain, but long-term high-intensity agricultural cultiva -
tion activities have resulted in high levels of pollutants in 
the groundwater (Yu et al. 2023). Farming activities such 
as fertilization and irrigation significantly affect the carbon, 
nitrogen, and sulfur cycling processes in groundwater (Chen 
et al. 2024). In addition, it is believed that there is a strong 
link between the levels of groundwater pollutants (nitrate 
nitrogen and heavy metals) and human activities (Wei et al. 
2017). Excessive amounts of contaminants in groundwater 
can change the composition of the microbial community,

and ingesting contaminated groundwater can impact human 
health (Qiu et al. 2023a).
Microorganisms are critical drivers of biogeochemi -
cal cycles, including elemental transport, enrichment, and 
transformations associated with microbial metabolism (Hu 
et al. 2022; Korbel et al. 2022). Under extreme environmen-
tal pressure, the microbial diversity in groundwater is sig -
nificantly reduced, which may have profound implications 
for the elemental cycling within the groundwater ecosystem 
(Hemme et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2024). Geomorphology, 
hydrodynamic conditions, and hydrochemical conditions 
all determine the characteristics of groundwater microbial 
communities (Xiong et al. 2023).
Revealing the ecological model of microbial commu -
nity assembly in groundwater holds great significance (Ji et 
al. 2022b). It is widely acknowledged that the assembly of 
microbial communities is shaped by both deterministic fac-
tors and stochastic processes (Stegen et al. 2012). Microbial 
community assembly mainly comprises deterministic and 
stochastic processes, and the balance between these pro -
cesses is one of the central issues in community assembly 
(Qiao et al. 2023).Environmental selection is an important 
mechanism driving the assembly of groundwater microbial 
communities, with nutrients, contaminant types, and con -
centrations all playing pivotal roles in shaping microbial 
communities (Zhong et al., 2023). Species characteristics, 
species interactions, and the predictable filtering effects of 
environmental and biological conditions on various species 
trigger deterministic community processes. In contrast, 
stochastic community assembly (neutral theory) proceeds 
through essentially random processes of dispersal, birth, 
death, and drift (Ji et al. 2022b). The structure and succession 
of microbiota are controlled by four fundamental ecologi -
cal processes: selection (homogeneous and heterogeneous 
selection), dispersal (homogeneous and dispersal-limited 
dispersal), drift, and diversification. Therefore, determin -
istic and stochastic processes always coincide in microbial 
community assembly processes (Lv et al. 2023). The struc-
ture and assembly mechanisms of microbial communities 
under various environmental stresses have received exten -
sive attention in recent decades (Xiong et al. 2023).
Vegetable cultivation generates large amounts of nitro -
gen and nutrients in groundwater, leading to high nitrate 
nitrogen levels in groundwater (Yu et al. 2023). The sewage 
network facilities in the rural areas (RAs) of the Huaibei 
Plain are not well developed, and domestic sewage often 
cannot be treated in a timely manner, causing domestic sew-
age to directly affect the groundwater quality in the region 
(Feng and Yu 2024). The vegetable planting area–village 
system (Fig. 1b) is the most representative influencing fac -
tor affecting the shallow groundwater quality and microbial 
community in the Huaibei Plain. However, previous studies 
on groundwater in the Huaibei Plain have predominantly 
focused on the hydrogeochemical characteristics (Chen et 
al. 2022), health risk assessments (Qiu et al. 2023a), and 
water quality evaluations (Yu et al. 2020) of groundwater

et al. 2025). The study area, located in the Huabei Plain of

microorganisms in groundwater of IVCs is higher than that 
in RAs; and (3) environmental factors influence the assem -
bly mechanisms of microbial communities.
Materials and methods
Study region and sample collection
The study area is located in the hinterland of Huaibei Plain, a 
typical plain area. The area has an ambient temperate semi-
humid monsoon climate with spring, summer, autumn and 
winter, rain and heat in the summer, and abundant light. The 
annual precipitation in 2022 was 781.6 mm, and the yearly 
average temperature was 15.8 °C, with a maximum tem -
perature of 39.7 °C and a minimum temperature of − 9.2 °C. 
According to the 2023 Suzhou City Statistical Yearbook, the 
vegetable-growing area in Suzhou City amounted to 57,253 
hectares, the grain sowing area was 939,006 hectares, the 
amount of fertilizer applied reached 296,600 t, and the 
output of vegetables and edible mushrooms was 2,435,000 t. 
Based on their stratigraphic characteristics, the Quaternary 
unconsolidated sedimentary sequence in the study area can 
be subdivided into four distinct aquifers (aquifer Ⅰ, Ⅱ, Ⅲ, 
and Ⅳ)(Yu et al. 2020). In the study area, The irrigation and 
drinking water used in the vegetable planting area consists 
of water from the aquifer Ⅰ that can directly receive recharge 
via atmospheric precipitation, with a depth of about 30 m. 
The study area is located in the northern part of the urban 
area of Suzhou City, which is a famous vegetable planting 
base (Yu et al. 2023). As shown in Fig. 1a, a total of 23 
groundwater samples were collected, of which 16 samples 
were from IVCs and 7 samples were from RAs.
Determination of hydrogeochemical parameters 
and DNA extraction, amplification, and sequencing
On-site pH, electrical conductivity (EC), oxidation–reduc -
tion potential (ORP), and total dissolved solids (TDS) 
testing was conducted using a multiparameter equipment 
(ProQuatro, YSI, USA). In October 2022 (autumn), 23 
unconfined groundwater (aquifer Ⅰ) samples were collected 
from the study area, with sampling depths of groundwa -
ter range from 25 ~ 40 m. To ensure the collection of fresh 
groundwater, the pump was operated to purge the well for 
approximately 15 min prior to sample collection. The water 
samples (2.5 L) were delivered to the laboratory within 8 
h of collection and stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C for fur -
ther testing. Before testing, the water samples were filtered 
using a 0.45-µm polyethersulfone (PES) filter membrane. 
The major ions (Na +, K +, Ca 2+, Mg 2+, Cl −, SO 4
2−, NO 3
−, 
and F −) in water were detected using ion chromatogra -
phy (ICS-600–900, ThermoFisher Scientific, USA). The 
HCO3
− content was determined via acid-base neutralization 
titration. The error in the charge balance of major anions 
and cations was less than 5% for all samples (Chen et al. 
2022). The total nitrogen (TN) and dissolved organic carbon 
(DOC) were determined using a total organic carbon ana -
lyzer (TOC-Multi N/C 2100, ANALYTIKJENA, Germany). 
DNA was extracted from the water samples using 0.22-µm 
membranes, which were subsequently stored at −80 °C. The 
16 S rRNA gene was amplified using primers 338 F (5′-

Statistical analysis
Prior to statistical comparison, normality testing was per -
formed for each physicochemical index. Variables conform-
ing to a normal distribution were analyzed via one-way 
ANOV A, and non-normal data were subjected to the 
Kruskal–Wallis test. AQ•QA was used to design the Piper 
trilinear diagram and to delineate hydrochemical types. 
Redundancy analyses (RDA) was performed to investi -
gate the effects of hydrochemical parameters on the micro -
bial community composition at the operational taxonomic 
unit (OTU) level. Non-metric multidimensional scaling 
(NMDS) analysis was conducted to assess differences in the 
phylum-level microbial community composition of ground-
water between the RA and IVC regions. Linear discrimi -
nant analysis effect size (LEfSe) was employed to identify 
microbial taxa, ranging from the phylum to the genus level, 
that differed significantly between groundwater samples 
from the RA and IVC regions. Functional annotation of pro-
karyotic taxa (FAPROTAX) functional predictions RDA, 
NMDS and LEfSe were performed on the Majorbio Bio -
Cloud platform (www.majorbio.com). To ensure the robust-
ness of the co-occurrence network, OTUs with a relative 
abundance greater than 0.1% were retained, Microbial co-
occurrence networks were constructed based on Spearman’s 
rank correlations using the rcorr function in the Hmisc 
package in R, with correlations considered significant at |r| 
> 0.6 and a Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P value < 0.05. 
Co-occurrence network analysis was conducted in Rstudio 
and visualized using Gephi. iCAMP was used to analyze 
the microbial community assembly (Ning et al. 2020). In 
the iCAMP analysis process, OTUs were first classified into 
bins according to the phylogenetic tree. In the next step, 
null modeling analysis was performed based on the bins. 
The final visualization of bins was completed using ITOL 
(https://itol.embl.de/).
Results
Statistics and analysis of hydrochemical parameters
The statistical results of hydrochemical parameters in RA-
IVC are shown in Table 1. The pH of RA-IVC ranged 
between 7.38 and 8.35, and both RA and IVC were weakly 
alkaline. The average TDS contents of RA and IVC were 
246.72 mg/L and 262.63 mg/L, respectively, indicating that 
the IVC groundwater had a high soluble ion content.
A comparison of the major cation contents in RA and 
IVC groundwater revealed that the Ca 2+ content in IVC 
groundwater was higher than ( p < 0.05) that in RA ground -
water. The F− contents in RA groundwater ranged between 
0.72 and 2.11 mg/L, with a mean value of 1.29 mg/L, and 
71.43% of RA groundwater samples contained F − at levels 
above the drinking water quality standard (GB 5749 − 2022) 
(> 1.0 mg/L). The F − content of groundwater in the IVC 
ranged between 0.62 and 1.52 mg/L, with a mean value of 
0.92 mg/L, and 43.75% of the IVC groundwater samples 
contained F − at levels above the drinking water quality 
standard (GB 5749 − 2022). Piper trilinear diagrams are pri-
marily employed to explore the types of hydrochemicals in 
groundwater (Yu et al. 2020).
As shown in Fig. 2, the cations in most IVC and RA 
groundwater samples were dominated by Ca2+, while Mg2+ 
was the central cation in a small number of water samples. 
Regarding anions, the groundwater of the RA and IVC areas 
was primarily dominated by HCO3. The results indicate that 
the hydrochemical type of IVC groundwater is Ca-HCO 3, 
and 71.42% of RA groundwater is Ca-HCO 3, with a few 
samples belonging to the Mg-HCO3. The Piper trilinear dia-
gram indicated that, although no significant differences in 
hydrochemical facies were observed between the RA and 
IVC regions, groundwater samples from the two regions 
exhibited distinct distribution patterns within the diagram 
(Fig. 2).

Microbial community composition of groundwater 
in the IVC and RA
A total of 1,165,493 high-quality sequences were detected in 
the 23 groundwater samples collected from RA-IVC, with 
an average length of 419 bp. These high-quality sequences 
were clustered to obtain 5009 OTUs assigned to 52 phyla, 
163 classes, 395 orders, 604 families, and 1103 genera. 
Moreover, the coverage index of the bacterial community in 
the groundwater of the IVC and RA was greater than 0.98, 
indicating that the sequencing results could satisfy the fol -
low-up analysis (Ji et al. 2022a; Sha et al. 2023).Venn dia-
gram (Fig. 3d) showed that the number of common OTUs in 
RA and IVC groundwater was 471, while 16 and 19 OTUs 
were unique to groundwater in the RA and IVC, respec -
tively. As shown in Fig. S1, the NMDS analysis revealed 
no significant difference in phylum-level microbial commu-
nity composition between groundwater samples from the 
RA and IVC regions ( p > 0.05). Nevertheless, groundwater 
samples from the RA region were predominantly distributed 
in the lower-left portion of the ordination plot. This distribu-
tion pattern may suggest a certain degree of hydraulic con -
nectivity between groundwater in the RA and IVC regions.
As shown in Fig. 3, the IVC groundwater microbial 
community at the phylum level was dominated by Proteo-
bacteria (65.49–90.00%), followed by Actinobacteriota

on average). The IVC was dominated by Proteobacteria

Microbial community assembly processes and 
environmental drivers
To investigate the assembly process of microbial communi-
ties in the RA and IVC, the relative contributions of five 
ecological processes to the microbial assembly were quan -
titatively resolved using a null modeling analysis (iCAMP) 
(Zhao et al. 2023). In general, the microbial community 
in the RA groundwater was found to be controlled by four 
ecological processes, namely dispersal limitation (66.67%), 
heterogeneous selection (0.62%), homogeneous selection 
(7.16%), and drift (24.67%), which together explained 
99.12% of the community assembly (Fig. 6). The ground -
water microbial community in the IVC was mainly con -
trolled by the three ecological processes of dispersal 
limitation (59.97%), homogeneous selection (1.97%), and 
drift (36.12%), which explained a total of 98.06% of the 
community assembly (Fig. 6). This further implies that the 
stochastic processes of dispersal limitation and drift domi -
nate the microbial community of the RA and IVC ground -
water. Dispersal limitation was dominant in 1, 9 and 10 bins 
of RA and IVC (Fig. 7). Drift was dominant in 10 and 5 bins 
in IVC and RA (Fig. 7).
Correlation analysis was performed to explore the rela -
tionship between the ecological process of microbial com -
munity assembly and environmental factors. As shown in 
Fig. 8a, the pH in the groundwater of the IVC was signifi -
cantly positively correlated with the role of dispersal limi -
tation in microbial community assembly. In contrast, there

Discussion
The Huaibei Plain area is one of China’s major grain and 
vegetable production areas, and the RA-IVC groundwater 
system in the Huaibei Plain is the most concentrated area 
affected by human production and living activities. Previ -
ous studies have explored the hydrochemical characteris -
tics (Qiu et al. 2023b), heavy metal contents (Feng and Yu 
2024), and health risks of groundwater consumed by vil -
lage residents (Jiang et al. 2021). Therefore, it is essential 
to further investigate the major anions and cations, the com-
position of bacterial communities, and the assembly pro -
cess in the RA-IVC groundwater system. This will help to 
understand the impacts of vegetable cultivation and village 
residents’ activities on the groundwater quality and bacterial 
communities.
Compared with the established groundwater quality stan-
dards, the characteristic pollutants in RA groundwater and 
IVC groundwater are F − and NO 3
−, respectively (Table 1). 
F− is among the anions that have been reported to exceed 
groundwater quality standards in the Huaibei Plain (Qiu et 
al. 2023b). The long-term consumption of groundwater con-
taining high levels of F− can lead to diseases such as dental 
fluorosis and osteofluorosis (Yu et al. 2020). The distribution 
of Cl− content in the RA and IVC groundwater was strongly 
spatially heterogeneous, with maximum values located in 
the IVC groundwater. Cl − is an indicator used to charac -
terize interference due to anthropogenic activities, and the 
Cl− content in the IVC groundwater sample #F07 reached 
218.19 mg/L, which would be considered class IV water 
according to China’s groundwater quality standard (GB/T 
14848 − 2017). The average contents of both SO4
2 and NO3
− 
were higher (p < 0.05) in the groundwater in the IVC. Large 
amounts of fertilizers, such as potassium nitrate and ammo-
nium sulfate, are often applied during vegetable cultivation, 
making the SO 4
2 and NO 3
− contents in the groundwater in 
the IVC area relatively high (Yu et al. 2023).
As shown in Fig. 3(c), although the composition of the 
phylum was very similar in the RA and IVC, the abundance 
of Proteobacteria was relatively high in the RA ground -
water. Moreover, Proteobacteria contain most genera of 
denitrifying bacteria (Duan et al. 2023). The abundance 
of Arthrobacter in IVC groundwater was markedly higher 
than that in RA groundwater (Fig.S4). It has been verified 
by previous studies that Arthrobacter is capable of degrad -
ing various organic pollutants and has been confirmed as 
an in-situ remediation strain for plasticizers (Kong et al. 
2022). Arthrobacter is a non-autotrophic nitrifying bacte -
rium that converts ammonia nitrogen into nitrate nitrogen 
under anaerobic conditions (Qiao et al. 2023). Achromo-
bacter is capable of both non-autotrophic nitrification and 
anaerobic denitrification (Yang et al. 2019). Consequently, 
the suitability of IVC groundwater for the growth and repro-
duction of Arthrobacter and how to effectively utilize it for 
the remediation of groundwater contaminated by intensive 
agricultural practices are the key research focuses in the 
subsequent studies.
The pH regulates nutrient and energy sources for micro -
bial development and intercellular interactions through 
affecting the solubility, valence state, and form of existence 
of elements (Xiong et al. 2012). The quality of groundwater 
and the composition of microbial communities within the 
same region can vary due to differences in land use types 
(Ji et al. 2022a). Vegetable cultivation is one of the primary 
economic sectors for villagers in the Huaibei Plain, and to 
efficiently manage the growth of vegetables, vegetable cul -
tivation areas are mainly distributed around villages (Fig. 
1c). Moreover, the amount of fertilizer applied during veg -
etable cultivation is several times that applied during cereal 
agriculture (Shi et al. 2009). Thus, there are differences in 
the NO3
− and some microorganisms of groundwater in IVCs 
and RAs. The relative abundances of Vogesella, Sideroxy-
dans, Achromobacter, and Nitrospira in the RA were more 
significant than those in the IVC (Wilcox rank-sum test, 
p < 0.05) (Fig. S2). Nitrospira is a typical nitrifying bacte -
rium with the ability to convert ammonia nitrogen to nitrate 
nitrogen (Wang et al. 2024). The potential functional map -
ping of groundwater microbial communities was accom -
plished using FAPROTAX (Ke et al. 2022). In summary, 
the IVC has a high nitrification-responsive microbial com -
munity (Fig.S5). (Zhong et al. 2025) found that agricultural 
activities significantly enhance the intensity of nitrogen 
metabolism in groundwater.
As shown in Fig. 4, K+ was significantly negatively cor-
related with Bdellovibrionota ( p < 0.05), and Bdellovibri-
onota was found to promote the degradation of pollutants 
by functional microorganisms ( p < 0.05) (Li et al. 2024). 
Although the RA co-occurrence network had fewer nodes 
and edges (Fig. 5c), the groundwater microbial community 
in the RA had more the percentage of positively correlated 
edges than the groundwater microbial community in the

IVC (Guo et al. 2022). This finding means that the complex-
ity of the groundwater microbial co-occurrence network 
in the IVC groundwater is high. Within the co-occurrence 
network of groundwater in the IVC (Fig. 5b), the top eight 
modules accounted for 91.23% of the community, and there 
were significant differences in community composition 
among modules.
The identification of key species based on the analysis of 
symbiotic networks discovered that Bacteroidota and Sul-
furitalea are keystone genera (Fig. 5e). Bacteroidota pro -
motes the degradation of macromolecular compounds such 
as starch and polysaccharides under anaerobic conditions 
(Sha et al. 2023). Sulfuritalea oxidizes sulfide through nitro-
gen fixation (Ke et al. 2022). This suggests that keystone 
genera associated with nitrogen and sulfur cycling evolve in 
groundwater under the influence of the IVC. In this study, 
drift and dispersal limitation were found to dominate the 
stochastic processes (Fig. 6), suggesting different diffusion 
conditions for bacterial communities in RA-IVC ground -
water systems (Lv et al. 2022). More complex microbial 
community structures are more susceptible to homogeniz -
ing selection, while less complex communities are more 
susceptible to fragmentation (Ji et al. 2022b). There is a 
strong link between the assembly of groundwater microbial 
communities and environmental factors (Ning et al. 2024). 
As there are frequent groundwater irrigation activities in the 
IVC, there is a positive correlation between dispersal limi -
tation in the assembly of groundwater microbial communi -
ties in vegetable growing areas and pH (Jiao et al. 2020). 
Similarly, previous studies have shown that deterministic 
processes dominate microbial community assembly under 
extremely acidic conditions, whereas dispersal limitation 
gradually becomes the predominant assembly process with 
increasing pH (Palomo et al. 2025). Therefore, alleviation 
of acid stress may reduce the relative importance of deter -
ministic processes while increasing the contribution of dis -
persal limitation to microbial community assembly. The 
contribution of dispersal limitation to microbial community 
assembly primarily depends on the mobility of the environ-
mental medium (Zhang et al. 2022). Groundwater micro -
bial communities are further affected by physical filtering or 
trapping effects (Ning et al. 2024).
However, this study still has some limitations. It only 
explores the composition and assembly mechanism of bac -
terial communities, and does not consider the impact of pol-
lutants such as pesticides, heavy metals, and microplastics 
on the microbial community in the groundwater of typical 
IVCs. In future research, we will use metagenomic sequenc-
ing technology to further investigate the biological infor -
mation of microorganisms in the groundwater of IVCs in 
detail. By combining indicators such as pesticides, heavy 
metals, and microplastics, we will quantify the changes in 
the survival strategies and energy metabolism of functional 
microorganisms under the influence of intensive agriculture. 
Thus, this will provide valuable information for formulating 
groundwater management and control strategies for inten -
sive vegetable cultivation areas.
Conclusion
This study analyzed the hydrogeochemical parameters and 
microbial community composition, assembly mechanisms, 
and their influencing factors in the groundwater of the IVC 
and RA in the Huaibei Plain, China. The key conclusions of 
this work are as follows:
(1) RA-IVC groundwater is weakly alkaline, with high 
levels of NO3
−, SO4
2−, and TDS. The IVC groundwater 
hydrochemistry type is Ca-HCO 3, while the predomi -
nant RA groundwater hydrochemistry type is Ca-HCO3, 
with a small number of samples with Mg-HCO3 types.
(2) The relative abundance of microorganisms related to 
nitrification in the IVC microbial community was higher 
at the genus level. The results of co-occurrence network 
analysis revealed that the complexity of the microbial 
co-occurrence network in the IVC was higher.
(3) The relationships between the five ecological pro -
cesses of IVC and RA microbial community assembly 
and environmental factors differed considerably, with 
a strong association between stochastic processes and 
environmental factors in the IVC microbial assem -
bly and a positive correlation between deterministic 
processes (HeS) and environmental factors in the RA 
microbial community assembly.’
