In this chapter, energy requirements for the various aspects of water management are discussed. Energy consumption corresponded to approximately 4% of all electricity consumed worldwide in 2014. Another 50 million tons of oil and diesel are required for irrigation and desalination of plants. Energy requirements are expected to double by 2040. There is a requirement, therefore, to shift to less energy-intensive water. There are numerous opportunities to accomplish a reduction in energy consumption and increase efficiencies in the water sector, which will be discussed. An understanding of water–energy integration is needed to develop integrated practices. Energy requirements must be reduced, and sources of contamination must be eliminated to maintain water quality and supply. Water must be conserved and managed properly. Various remediation technologies have been developed to treat water once it has been contaminated. The selection of these techniques requires examination of energy requirements and emissions, wastes generated, and water quality requirements. Development of more energy-efficient technologies to clean contaminated water resources is necessary to meet water and energy resource requirements. The wastewater sector can benefit from energy savings by improving energy efficiency and benefiting from the embedded energy in water. Practicing energy recovery can lead to up to the generation of up to 55% of the electricity needed for the municipal wastewater treatment sector. Reducing water losses can save substantial amounts of energy.
