Effect of integrated nutrient management on nutrient
content and uptake of rice and mustard crops

A BSTRACT
A two-year field experiment was conducted during 2024–25 and 2025–26 at farmer field of village
Ramdaspur Patahana, Jaunpur Uttar Pradesh near V.B.S. Purvanchal University Jaunpur, Uttar Pradesh,
to evaluate the effect of integrated nutrient management (INM) on nutrient content and nutrient
uptake/accumulation in rice and mustard under a rice–mustard cropping system. The experiment was
conducted on an alluvial soil of the Eastern Plain Zone of Uttar Pradesh using a randomized block de-
sign with three replications. Twelve nutrient-management treatments were evaluated, ranging from an
unfertilized control to combinations of recommended NPKS, farmyard manure (FYM), poultry manure
(PM) and vesicular arbuscular mycorrhiza (VAM). The recommended fertilizer doses were
120:60:40:25 kg N:P2O5:K2O:S ha−1 for rice and 80:40:40:20 kg N:P2O5:K2O:S ha−1 for mustard.
The integrated treatment T11, comprising 100% NPKS + 5 t FYM ha−1 + 1 t poultry manure ha−1 +
5 kg VAM, consistently produced the highest nutrient concentration in both crops. In rice, T11 recorded
1.46 and 1.72% N in grain and 0.69 and 1.19% N in straw during 2024–25 and 2025–26, respectively.
Corresponding N uptake by grain was 87.9 and 88.16 kg ha−1, while straw uptake was 48.6 and
48.86 kg ha−1. Total N uptake reached 136.5 kg ha−1 in the first year. The treatment also recorded the
highest P concentration and uptake, with grain P concentration of 0.41 and 0.61%, straw P concentration
of 0.21 and 0.71%, and grain P uptake of 24.7 and 24.96 kg ha−1 in the two years, respectively.
The Similarly, rice under T11 recorded the highest K concentration of 0.48 and 0.74% in grain and 1.75
and 1.95% in straw, with K uptake of 28.9 and 29.4 kg ha−1 by grain and 123.2 and 123.46 kg ha−1 by
straw. Total K uptake reached 152.1 kg ha−1. In mustard, T11 recorded the highest seed N, P, K and S
concentrations of 3.97, 0.76, 1.24 and 1.35%, respectively, in 2024–25, which increased to 4.01, 0.78,
1.27 and 1.38% in 2025–26.
The improvement in nutrient concentration and uptake under INM was attributed to the complementary
effects of readily available nutrients from inorganic fertilizers, gradual nutrient release from FYM and
poultry manure, and enhanced nutrient mobilization and absorption associated with VAM. The results
demonstrate that balanced integration of inorganic fertilizers, organic manures and biofertilizers is an
effective strategy for improving nutrient acquisition, nutrient-use efficiency and productivity of the
rice–mustard cropping system.

1. Introduction
Rice (Oryza sativa L.) and mustard (Brassica juncea L.) constitute an important cereal–oilseed cropping se-
quence in the Indo-Gangetic Plains, particularly in Eastern Uttar Pradesh. Rice is generally grown during the
kharif season, followed by mustard during the rabi season, enabling efficient utilization of land, water and
climatic resources. The rice–mustard system is particularly important in the Eastern Plain Zone, where inten-
sive cultivation has increased pressure on soil nutrient reserves.
Continuous cultivation of high-yielding crops and excessive dependence on mineral fertilizers can result in
nutrient imbalance, declining soil organic matter and reduced nutrient-use efficiency. Nitrogen is particularly
vulnerable to losses through volatilization, denitrification and leaching, while phosphorus may become un-
available through fixation. Potassium is also progressively removed from soil through harvested biomass.
The problem becomes more pronounced in intensively cultivated rice-based systems.
Mustard has a relatively high requirement for balanced nutrition, particularly nitrogen, phosphorus and sul-
phur. Sulphur is especially important because it is closely associated with protein synthesis, oil formation
and quality of mustard seed. Continuous rice–mustard cultivation without adequate nutrient replenishment
may therefore lead to depletion of major and secondary nutrients.
Integrated nutrient management (INM) offers an alternative approach in which mineral fertilizers are com-
bined with organic manures and beneficial microorganisms. Mineral fertilizers supply nutrients in readily

2. Materials and Methods
2.1 Experimental site
The experiment was conducted during two consecutive cropping
years, 2024–25 and 2025–26, at a farmer’s field located in
Ramdaspur Patahana village, Jaunpur, Uttar Pradesh, near V.B.S.
Purvanchal University, Jaunpur. The experimental site falls under the
Eastern Plain Zone of Uttar Pradesh and experiences a subtropical cli-
mate, which is generally suitable for the cultivation of rice during the
kharif season and mustard during the rabi season. The soil of the ex-
perimental field was an alluvial Inceptisol of the Gangetic plains.
Before initiation of the experiment, representative soil samples were
collected from the experimental field and analyzed for their basic
physico-chemical and fertility characteristics. The initial soil pH
ranged from 7.5 to 8.0, while electrical conductivity (EC) was below
0.5 dS m−1. The soil contained 0.4–0.6% organic carbon, with avail-
able nitrogen ranging from 200–250 kg ha−1, available phosphorus
from 15–20 kg ha−1, and available potassium from 250–300 kg ha−1.
These initial soil characteristics provided the baseline fertility status
for evaluating the effects of different integrated nutrient management
treatments on crop growth, yield and nutrient uptake.
2.2 Experimental design
The experiment was laid out in a Randomized Block Design (RBD)
with three replications. The individual experimental plots measured 5
m × 4 m, providing an adequate area for evaluating the effects of dif-
ferent nutrient-management treatments under field conditions. The
cropping sequence consisted of rice during the kharif season followed
by mustard during the rabi season. The same treatment structure was
maintained throughout the experimental period to assess the cumula-
tive effects of integrated nutrient management on crop performance
and nutrient utilization in the rice–mustard cropping system.
2.3 Nutrient-management treatments
A total of twelve nutrient-management treatments (T0–T11) were
evaluated. The treatments consisted of different combinations of inor-
ganic fertilizers supplying nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium
(K) and sulphur (S), along with organic sources such as farmyard ma-
nure (FYM) and poultry manure, with or without vesicular arbuscular
mycorrhiza (VAM). The treatments were formulated to compare the
effects of complete inorganic fertilization with reduced levels of inor-
ganic fertilizers supplemented with organic manures and biofertilizer.
The details of the nutrient-management treatments imposed in the ex-
periment are presented in Table 2.1.

through organic and biological nutrient sources, whereas the 100%
NPKS treatments were used to evaluate the response under the full
recommended inorganic nutrient supply in combination with organic
and biological sources.
2.4 Plant sampling and nutrient analysis
Representative plant samples were collected from each experimental
plot at harvest for determination of nutrient concentration and uptake.
The collected plant material was properly cleaned to remove adhering
soil and other foreign materials and was subsequently oven dried to
constant weight. The dried samples were ground into a fine powder
and passed through a 20-mesh sieve to obtain a uniform sample for
chemical analysis.
The nitrogen concentration in plant samples was determined by the
modified micro-Kjeldahl method. Phosphorus was estimated by the
vanado-molybdate phosphoric acid method, while potassium concen-
tration was determined using a flame photometer. For mustard, the
nutrient analysis included nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium
(K) and sulphur (S) in seed samples. Particular emphasis was given to
sulphur because of its important role in oilseed production, seed de-
velopment and oil quality.
Nutrient uptake was calculated from the nutrient concentration in the
plant material and the corresponding yield. The uptake of each nutri-
ent was expressed in kg ha−1 using the following formula:

2.5 Statistical analysis
The experimental data obtained for different growth, yield and nutri-
ent-related parameters were subjected to analysis of variance
(ANOVA) appropriate for the Randomized Block Design. The treat-
ment means were compared to determine whether the observed differ-
ences among nutrient-management treatments were statistically
significant.
The standard error of mean (SEm±) was calculated to indicate the
precision of the treatment means, while the critical difference (CD) at
the 5% probability level was used for comparison of treatment means
when the analysis of variance indicated significant treatment effects.
The statistical significance of treatment effects was evaluated at the
5% level of probability. The results obtained from the statistical
analysis were subsequently interpreted to determine the effects of in-
tegrated nutrient management on crop growth, yield, nutrient concen-
tration and nutrient uptake in the rice–mustard cropping system.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Effect of INM on nitrogen content and uptake of rice
The data presented in Table 4.1 clearly indicate that integrated nutri-
ent management practices significantly influenced the nitrogen con-
tent and its uptake in both grain and straw of rice during both the
years of experimentation (2024-25 and 2025-26).
Under T11, grain N concentration increased from 1.46% in 2024–25
to 1.72% in 2025–26, while straw N increased from 0.69 to 1.19%. N
uptake by grain was 87.9 and 88.16 kg ha−1, while straw N uptake
was 48.6 and 48.86 kg ha−1, respectively. Total N uptake reached
136.5 kg ha−1 in 2024–25.
The higher N concentration and uptake under T ₁₁ may be attributed to
the complementary supply of mineral and organic nitrogen. Mineral
fertilizers supplied immediately available N, while FYM and poultry
manure underwent gradual mineralization, thereby extending N avail-
ability over the crop-growth period. VAM-mediated improvement in
root development and nutrient acquisition may have further contrib-
uted to greater N absorption. The improvement was particularly evi-
dent compared with the unfertilized control, where grain N concentra-
tion was only 1.08% in 2024–25. Total N uptake increased from
44.5 kg ha−1 under the control to 136.5 kg ha−1 under T11, represent-
ing a substantial improvement in crop N acquisition. These findings
agree with earlier studies cited in the thesis, including Kandeshwari
et al. (2016) [1], who reported improved nutrient uptake in rice
through integrated nutrient management.
3.2 Effect of INM on phosphorus content and uptake of rice
The data presented in Table 4.2 clearly indicate that integrated nutri-
ent management practices significantly influenced the Phosphorus
content and its uptake in both grain and straw of rice during both the
years of experimentation (2024-25 and 2025-26).
Grain P concentration under T11 was 0.41% in 2024–25 and 0.61% in
2025–26, whereas straw P concentration was 0.21 and 0.71%, respec-
tively. Grain P uptake reached 24.7 and 24.96 kg ha−1, while straw
uptake was 14.8 and 15.0 kg ha−1. The reported total P uptake was
39.5 kg ha−1 in 2024–25.
The improvement in P nutrition under integrated management can be
associated particularly with the presence of VAM. Organic manure
decomposition may produce organic acids that reduce P fixation and
increase the availability of otherwise sparingly soluble P. VAM can
also extend the effective root system and enhance the exploration of
soil volume, improving P acquisition. Thus, the combination of inor-
ganic P, organic nutrient sources and VAM appears particularly
promising for improving P-use efficiency in rice. Similar result found
as Puli, M.R., Prasad, P.R.K., Jayalakshmi, M. and Rao, B.S. (2017)
[2].
3.3 Effect of INM on potassium content and uptake of rice
The data presented in Table 4.3 clearly indicate that integrated nutri-
ent management practices significantly influenced the Potassium con-
tent and its uptake in both grain and straw of rice during both the
years of experimentation (2024-25 and 2025-26).
Rice grain K concentration under T11 increased from 0.48% in 2024–
25 to 0.74% in 2025–26, while straw K concentration increased from
1.75 to 1.95%. Grain K uptake was 28.9 and 29.4 kg ha−1, and straw
K uptake was 123.2 and 123.46 kg ha−1. The reported total K uptake
reached 152.1 kg ha−1.
The control treatment recorded only 50.6 kg ha−1 total K uptake,
showing that integrated nutrient management greatly enhanced the
crop's capacity to absorb and accumulate potassium.
The positive response can be explained by the direct contribution of K
from organic manures and the improvement of soil physical and bio-
logical conditions resulting from organic matter addition. Organic
amendments can improve soil aggregation and cation-exchange pro-
cesses, thereby reducing nutrient losses and supporting continued K
availability. This result are found as Tiwari, R., Upadhyay, A.K., Rai,
H.K. and Dey, P. (2020) [3].
4. Effect of INM on nutrient content of mustard
4.4 Nutrient content and uptake in mustard
4.4.1 Nitrogen content and nitrogen uptake by mustard
The data presented in Table 4.4 clearly indicate that integrated nutri-
ent management practices significantly influenced the nitrogen con-
tent and its uptake in both grain and straw of mustard during both the
years of experimentation (2024-25 and 2025-26).
The highest nitrogen content in grain (2.45% and 2.51%) and straw
(0.88% and 0.91%) was recorded under treatment T ₁₁ (100% NPKS +
5 t FYM ha ⁻ ¹ + 1 t Poultry Manure ha ⁻ ¹ + 5 kg Biofertilizer VAM)
during 2024-25 and 2025-26, respectively. This treatment was closely
followed by T ₁₀ . The lowest nitrogen content in grain (1.82% and
1.85%) and straw (0.52% and 0.55%) was observed in the absolute
control (T ₀ ).
A similar trend was noticed in nitrogen uptake. The maximum nitro-
gen uptake by grain (56.8 and 59.7 kg ha ⁻ ¹) and straw (29.2 and
30.4 kg ha ⁻ ¹) was obtained under T ₁₁ , which was significantly superior
to most of the other treatments. The control plot recorded the mini-
mum uptake values.
The superior performance of T ₁₁ may be attributed to the combined
application of inorganic fertilizers with organic manures (FYM and
poultry manure) along with biofertilizer (VAM). Organic manures im-
prove soil physical condition, enhance microbial activity and ensure
slow and continuous release of nutrients, while VAM improves nutri-
ent absorption efficiency, particularly nitrogen. The better root devel-
opment and higher dry matter accumulation under this treatment also
contributed to increased nutrient content and uptake. this type of re-
sult are found as same Sahoo et al.(2018) [4]
4.4.2 Phosphorus content and phosphorus uptake by mustard
The Phosphorus content and uptake by mustard were significantly af-
fected by different nutrient management treatments (Table 4.5).
Treatment T11 recorded the highest phosphorus content in grain
(0.58% and 0.61%) and straw (0.27% and 0.28%) during both the
years. The lowest values were observed under control (T0).
Phosphorus uptake followed the same pattern, with maximum uptake
by grain (15.0 and 15.9 kg ha−1) and straw (7.9 and 8.3 kg ha−1) un-
der T11.
The improvement in phosphorus content and uptake under integrated
treatments, especially those involving poultry manure, FYM and
VAM, can be explained by the better solubilization and mobilization
of native and applied phosphorus. Poultry manure is rich in phospho-
rus, and VAM is well known for increasing phosphorus availability
through hyphal network and production of organic acids. The bal-
anced supply of nutrients also promoted better plant growth, leading
to higher biomass and consequently greater phosphorus accumulation.
As same result found Pati, P. and Mahapatra, P.K. (2015) [5].

4.4.3 Potassium content and potassium uptake by mustard
The results presented in Table 4.6 reveal that potassium content and
its uptake were markedly influenced by the integrated nutrient man-
agement practices.
The maximum potassium content in grain (0.74% and 0.78%) and
straw (1.78% and 1.85%) was recorded under T ₁₁ during 2024-25 and
2025-26, respectively. Similarly, the highest potassium uptake by
grain (21.4 and 22.6 kg ha ⁻ ¹) and straw (83.1 and 86.5 kg ha ⁻ ¹) was
also observed in the same treatment. The control treatment consis-
tently registered the lowest values.
Higher potassium content and uptake under T ₁₁ may be due to the
synergistic effect of organic and inorganic sources of nutrients.
Organic manures improve the cation exchange capacity of soil and re-
duce potassium fixation, while the addition of recommended dose of
fertilizers ensures adequate supply of potassium. The enhanced root
system under biofertilizer-inoculated treatments further facilitated
better absorption of potassium from the soil solution. Those result are
found similar as Singh, S.K. et al. (2018).
4.4.4 Sulphur content and sulphur uptake by mustard
Sulphur content and uptake by mustard were significantly improved
due to integrated nutrient management (Table 4.7).
Treatment T ₁₁ recorded the highest sulphur content in grain (0.47%
and 0.49%) and straw (0.20% and 0.21%) as well as the maximum
sulphur uptake by grain (8.4 and 8.9 kg ha ⁻ ¹) and straw (5.2 and
5.4 kg ha ⁻ ¹) during both the years. The lowest sulphur content and up-
take were observed under the control treatment.
The superior performance of T11 with respect to sulphur nutrition can
be attributed to the combined application of sulphur-containing fertil-
izers (NPKS) along with organic manures. Organic manures release
sulphur slowly during decomposition and improve its availability in
soil. Better plant growth and higher dry matter production under inte-
grated nutrient management also resulted in greater sulphur accumu-
lation in both grain and straw. This result was similar found as Sahoo
et al. (2018) [4].

5. Conclusion
The two-year investigation demonstrated that integrated nutrient man-
agement substantially improved nutrient concentration and nutrient
uptake/accumulation in the rice–mustard cropping system. Among the
tested nutrient-management treatments, T11 (100% NPKS + 5 t FYM
ha−1 + 1 t poultry manure ha−1 + 5 kg VAM) consistently produced
the best nutrient status. In rice, T11 resulted in the highest N, P and K
concentration in grain and straw and the highest uptake of these nutri-
ents. N uptake reached 136.5 kg ha−1, P uptake reached 39.5 kg ha−1,
and K uptake reached 152.1 kg ha−1 in the reported first-year totals.
The two-year study (2024-25 and 2025-26) revealed that integrated
nutrient management practices had a significant positive effect on ni-
trogen, phosphorus, potassium and sulphur content and their uptake
by mustard.
Treatment T11 (100% NPKS + 5 t FYM ha−1 + 1 t Poultry Manure
ha−1 + 5 kg Biofertilizer VAM) recorded the highest nitrogen content
in grain (2.45% and 2.51%) and straw (0.88% and 0.91%) along with
maximum nitrogen uptake by grain (56.8 and 59.7 kg ha−1) and straw
(29.2 and 30.4 kg ha−1). The same treatment also registered the high-
est phosphorus content in grain (0.58% and 0.61%) and straw (0.27%
and 0.28%) with corresponding uptake values of 15.0 and 15.9 kg
ha−1 in grain and 7.9 and 8.3 kg ha−1 in straw.
