Abstract 21 
Eutrophication has emerged as a significant environmental problem for global 22 
lakes. As an essential carrier of nutrients, particulate organic matter (POM) plays a vital 23 
role in the eutrophication process of these aquatic systems. In this study, POM from 24 
seven lakes with different trophic states in the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze 25 
River (China) was characterized using carbon and nitrogen stable isotopes and Fourier 26 
transform ion cyclotron resonance mass spectrometry (FT -ICR MS). The aim was to 27 
elucidate the relationship between the source and molecular composition of POM 28 
during the eutrophication process of lakes. The results indicated that POM was mainly 29 
composed of autochthonous (62.7%) and allochthonous (37.3%) sources, with the 30 
contribution from autochthonous sources being more pronounced across the different 31 
sources. The POM formulas mainly consisted of the subclasses CHO, CHON, CHOP, 32 
CHOS, and CHONS. Notably, CHOP formulas had the highest proportion of labile 33 
formula compounds, according for 51.56%. The unsaturation, aromaticity, and 34 
oxidation of unique POM formulas gradually decreased with increasing trophic states. 35 
A significant positive correlation was observed between CHOP and the percentage of 36 
labile compounds (MLBL%) in unique POM formulas. The relative abundance of lipid 37 
and protein compounds of unique POM formulas showed a positive correlation with 38 
lake trophic states, which indicated th at with the increase of lake trophic states, the 39 
content of autochthonous POM gradually increased. Herein, we inferred that with the 40 
intensification of lake eutrophication, the autochthonous POM increased, which was 41 
accompanied by a further increase of lab ile P -containing compounds in POM, thus 42 
leading to the increasing eutrophication process of lakes in the form of positive 43 
feedback. Overall, this investigation of POM at the molecular level illustrates the deep-44 
rooted mechanism of frequent lake eutrophicat ion. This is of great significance in 45 
understanding the fate of POM and effectively controlling lake eutrophication. 46

MS; Trophic states 48

1. Introduction 49 
Particulate organic matter (POM) in aquatic ecosystems is a complex mixture of 50 
various organic matter, including phyto - and zooplankton, detritus, fecal pellsts, 51 
bacteria, etc. (Dzierzbicka-Głowacka et al., 2010 ). POM is an essential component of 52 
the biological elemental cycle in lakes and reservoirs and plays a vital role in regulating 53 
organic and inorganic nutrient pools (Chen et al., 2018). For example, it can affect the 54 
characteristics of nutrients such as carbon (C), nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and some 55 
metal elements through microbial and photochemical processes (Kumar et al., 2010 ; 56 
Lønborg et al., 2009; Méndez-Díaz et al., 2014). Therefore, aquatic POM plays a non-57 
negligible role in aquatic biogeoc hemical cycles and global elemental cycles. 58 
Regarding the spatial and temporal scales of lakes, the quality and quantity of POM 59 
vary considerably and may be influenced by lake trophic states (Wang et al., 2012; Wu 60 
et al., 2018). Therefore, changes in the source and composition of POM in lakes with 61 
different trophic states may significantly impact the elemental cycling, and must be 62 
further explored. 63 
POM is an organic molecular pool from both autochthonous and allochthonous 64 
sources (Rossi et al., 2010). Autochthonous POM is mainly derived from macrophytes, 65 
phytoplankton, algae, and microorganisms. In addition, sediment resuspension is also a 66 
potential important source of POM in shallow water ecosystems (Hu et al., 2022). For 67 
allochthonous POM, important contributors are terrestrial materials such as soil organic 68 
matter (SOM), decaying terrestrial plants, a nd anthropogenic pollutants (Moyer et al., 69 
2012; Wang et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2009). Wind waves and microbial disturbances 70 
within water bodies and anthropogenic disturbances can affect POM composition and 71 
biogeochemical processes, thereby affecting elemental cycling in aquatic ecosystems 72 
(Wang et al., 2021). For example, these disturbances can cause POM with small particle 73 
size in surface sediments to be released into the overlying water column, which in turn 74 
participates in processes such as photolysis and enzymatic degradation to release 75 
dissolved nutrients such as C, N, and P (Harfmann et al., 2021; Kong et al., 2021). On 76 
the other hand, due to lake eutrophication and cyanobacterial bloom occurrence, algal 77 
blooms and macrophyte apomixis increase the release and contribution of 78 
4 
 
autochthonous POM in lake ecosystems, which in turn grea tly affects the quality, 79 
composition, and reactivity of POM in lakes (Cheng et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2023 ). 80 
However, there is a lack of sufficient knowledge on the sources and molecular 81 
characterization of POM in lakes with different trophic states and its possible im pact 82 
on biogeochemical cycling processes in lake ecosystems. Therefore, more 83 
comprehensive studies are urgently needed to address this knowledge gap. 84 
The sources and composition of organic matter can generally be characterized by 85 
isotopic tracing, spectroscopic analysis, and mass spectrometry (Li et al., 2023; Xiang 86 
et al., 2023). Carbon and nitrogen stable isotope analysis is used for tracing the sources 87 
of organic matter in aquatic ecosystems such as lakes, reservoirs, and rivers (Liu et al., 88 
2019). The contribution rates of different sources can be quantitatively calculated by 89 
isotopic mixing models. Among them, the Bayesian mixing model is widely used due 90 
to its higher error structure an d more reliable statistical basis (Torres -Martínez et al., 91 
2020; Xiao et al., 2023). Fourier transform ion cyclotron resonance mass spectrometry 92 
(FT-ICR MS) has been widely used for the identification of organic matter in complex 93 
environments due to its ul tra-high resolution and accuracy. It has been successfully 94 
applied to analyze the composition and transformation of various natural dissolved 95 
organic matter (DOM) from soil (Chen et al., 2023), groundwater (McDonough et al., 96 
2022), river (Li et al., 2023 ), lake (Hu et al., 2023 ), and marine ecosystems (Seidel et 97 
al., 2022 ). Therefore, this technique will also provide more i nformation about the 98 
molecular composition changes of POM, which will further deepen our understanding 99 
of the sources and fate of POM in lakes with different trophic states. 100 
Although the water quality of lakes in China has improved, the problem of 101 
eutrophication in lakes has not yet been eradicated, and the risk of deterioration in water 102 
quality still exists (Huang et al., 2020; Tong et al., 2017). The middle and lower reaches 103 
of the Yangtze River are the most developed areas of shallow lakes, and are importan t 104 
water storage reservoirs in China (Zhang et al., 2022b). Still, at the same time, they are 105 
also the areas where eutrophication lakes are concentrated. With the continued focus on 106 
lake eutrophication and cyanobacte rial blooms, there is an urgent need to clarify the 107 
linkage between POM chemistry and lake eutrophication process. Therefore, we 108 
5 
 
collected POM samples from lakes with different trophic states, and investigated their 109 
sources and molecular composition using carbon and nitrogen stable isotopes and FT-110 
ICR MS. The objectives of this study were (1) to analyze the sources of POM in lakes 111 
with different trophic states, (2) to analyze the molecular composition of POM in lakes 112 
with different trophic states, and (3) to link the molecular compositional characteristics 113 
of POM to water quality parameters to obtain new insights into the sources and 114 
molecular composition of POM in lakes with different trophic states, which in turn 115 
would provide new perspectives on lake eutrophication processes. 116 
2. Materials and methods 117 
2.1. Sample collection and water quality assessment 118 
A total of 7 typical lakes (including Lake Bailian (BL), Lake Liangzi (LZ), Lake 119 
Yehu (YH), Lake Qingling (QL), Lake Nantaizi (NTZ), Lake Nanhu (NH), and Lak e 120 
Longyang (LY)) in the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River in China were 121 
selected to characterize POM. The detailed information on sample collection and water 122 
quality parameter determination was described in Text S1. 123 
 The trophic state of the lake water were evaluated by improved Carlson trophic 124 
states index (TSI) following the description of Z hang et al. (2018). According to this 125 
evaluation method, water quality was categorized into five trophic states: oligotrophic 126 
(OLI, TSI < 30), mesotrophic (MES, 30 ≤ TSI ≤ 50), light eutrophic (LIG, 50 < TSI ≤ 127 
60), moderate eutrophic (MOD, 60 < TSI ≤ 70), and severe eutrophic (SEV , TSI > 70). 128

2.3. POM extraction 158 
In order to maximize the extraction of organic matter from POM, we carried out 159 
the extraction of POM samples as described by He et al. (2016). In brief, the particles 160 
obtained by freeze -drying were extracted using 0.1 M sodium hydroxide solution at 161 
room temperature and in the dark for 24 h. Subsequently, the extracts were freeze -162 
centrifuged at 10,000 rpm, and then the supernatants were passed through 0.22 μm filter 163 
membranes (Jinteng Experimental Equipment Co., LTD, Tianjin, China). A fraction of 164 
the filtered extract was diluted and adjusted to a pH of about 7 for total organic carbon 165 
(TOC) and P determinations. TOC was analyzed using a total organic carbon analyzer. 166 
The P contents were determined as shown in Section 2.1. The other part of the extract 167 
7 
 
was stored at 4 ℃ for subsequent solid phase extraction (SPE). 168 
2.4. SPE and FT-ICR MS analysis 169 
Bond Elut PAX cartridges (Agilent Technologies, 12257506 (500 mg, 6 ml)) were 170 
used to extract POM formulas from different samples. The extraction efficiency of the 171 
PAX cartridges for organic matter from POM was obtained by calculating the organic 172 
matter in the samples before and after the cartridges, and ranged from 27.8% to 58.6%, 173 
with an average of 44.1%. The detailed procedure for SPE was described in Text S2. 174 
The mass spectrometry analysis was conducted on the electrospray ionization (ESI) 175 
FT-ICR MS instrument under negative ion mode. The mass spectrometer was initially 176 
calibrated using sodium formate and recalibrated using some known mass series 177 
containing relatively high abundance CHO formulas. Formulas containing the elements 178 
C, H, O, N, P, and S were assigned using in -house program software, and formu las 179 
containing δ13C, δ15N, δ18O, and δ34S isotopes were considered. The relative abundance 180 
of each molecular formula was obtained by using the intensity of each molecular 181 
formula divided by the total intensity. Modified aromaticity index (AI mod) and double 182 
bond equivalence (DBE) were obtained by previous descriptions (He et al., 2016; Li et 183 
al., 2022). H/Cwa, O/Cwa, AImod,wa, and DBEwa represented intensity-weighted averages 184 
of these molecular parameters. 185

3.1. Water quality and eutrophication 194 
The general water quality parameters of different lakes were shown in Table S1 195 
and Table S2. The concentration ranges of dissolved oxygen (DO) , Chlorophyll-a 196 
(Chla), total nitrogen (TN) , and total phosphorus (TP) were 9.70-16.70 mg/L, 8.90 -197 
8 
 
67.00 μg/L, 0.04-7.76 mg/L, and 0.04 -0.44 mg/L, respectively. The TSI ranged from 198 
46.93-76.30 and covered mesotrophic, light eutrophic, moderate eutrophic, and severe 199 
eutrophic lakes. Chla is one of the important indicators of eutrophication in water bodies. 200 
There was a positive correlation between Chla concentration and various water quality 201 
parameters (including TSI, POM, TP, inorganic phosphorus (IP) , organic phosphorus 202 
(OP), particulate phosphorus (PP), and TN), which indicated that the input of nutrients 203 
would acceler ate phytoplankton growth, thereby exacerbating the eutrophication 204 
process of lakes. It was worth noting that there was a significant positive correlation 205 
between the contents of Chla and TP with a correlation coefficient of 0.86 (p ≤ 0.05) 206 
(Fig. S1), which suggests that TP plays an important limiting role in eutrophic lakes. 207 
The non-negligible role of TP in the growth of Chla and algal blooms ha d been well 208 
documented. Through meta-analysis, Yu et al. (2022) found that TP plays a more critical 209 
role under the hyper-eutrophic Chla conditions than TN, with TN changes contributing 210 
to a 5.2% increase in Chla, whereas TP changes contributed 272.0% Chla increase. 211 
Schindler et al. (2016) also found that reducing P inputs is the main way to control algal 212 
blooms and other eutrophication symptoms. In contrast, phytoplankton blooms caused 213 
by increased nutrient levels could lead to a decrease in water transparency, as reflected 214 
by the significant correlation between SD on the one hand, and POM and PP on the 215 
other hand (r of -0.77 and -0.82, respectively, p ≤ 0.05) (Fig. S1). 216 
POM in aquatic ecosystems is constituted of organic compounds that exist in the 217 
form of small particles in the water column. They can provide energy and nutrients for 218 
microorganisms in the water column, thereby affecting the growth, metabolism, and 219 
reproduction of organisms (Hu et al., 2022). At the same time, as an essential carrier of 220 
nutrients, the widespread presence of POM may affect water environmental factors such 221 
as water clarity, water quality, and DO, which in turn affects the structure and function 222 
of the entire ecosystem. The positive correlation between POM content and TSI (Fig. 223 
S1) indicated that the higher the trophic states, the higher the POM content in the lakes. 224 
High POM content would provide a large amount of nutrients for the growth of aquatic 225 
organisms, which could lead to higher primary productivity of the lakes, and then 226 
accelerate eutrophication (Gao et al., 2021 ; Qin et al., 2013 ). Therefore, investigating 227 
9 
 
the source and composition of POM is of great significance for understanding the 228 
mechanisms of algal blooms and eutrophication. 229 
3.2. Identification of POM sources 230 
This study used carbon and nitrogen isotopes and Bayesian modeling to reveal the 231 
sources of POM and to quantify the contribution of each source. The values of 13C for 232 
POM ranged from -63.48‰ to -25.85‰ (Table S3). The POM of Lake Qingling had 233 
the lowest 13C value of -63.48‰. This might be due to the more vigorous growth of 234 
aquatic organisms in Lake Qingling, which utilized dissolved inorganic carbon for 235 
photosynthesis and caused isotopic fractionation. This result also corresponded to the 236 
higher Chla value of Lake Qingling (Table S2). The average 13C value of POM in other 237 
lakes was -29.52 ± 3.82‰, which was close to the global average 13C value of lakes (-238 
29.7‰) (Liu et al., 2012 ). The 15N values ranged from 5.59‰ to 13.3‰, which 239 
overlapped with the values of algae, aquatic macrophytes, and terrestrial POM in Lake 240 
Taihu (Li et al., 2020) (Fig. 1a). The C/N ratios can be used to determine the source of 241 
organic matter in the aquatic environment (Lamb et al., 2006). Typically, when C/N > 242 
15 indicates that OM is predominantly from terrestrial sources, and C/N < 10 indicates 243 
that OM is domi nantly from autochthonous sources (Meyers, 1994). In this study, the 244 
C/N ratios were all less than 15 (Table S3), indicating that the POM was mainly derived 245 
from autochthonous sources. POM from Lake Longyang exhibited the highest C/N ratio 246 
(13.08), and a possible explanation was the higher degradation of POM from Lake 247 
Longyang and/or its higher contribution from terrigenous materials (Thibault et al., 248 
2019). 249 
Based on typical 13C and 15N values, seven potential sources were considered to 250 
quantify the contribution of different sources to POM. The results of end -member 251 
analysis showed that POM in different lakes was driven by both autochthonous (53.9%-252 
80.9%, mean 62.7%) and allochthonous ( 19.1%-46.1%, mean 37.3%) sources, 253 
including autochthonous phytoplankton (30.8 ± 18.7%), algae (12.9 ± 6.86%), 254 
macrophytes (10.5 ± 2.7%), sediment (8.5 ± 4.3%), and allochthonous terrestrial plants 255 
(14.8 ± 6.7%), SOM (9.8 ± 3.5%), and sewage (12.6 ± 7.8%) (Fig. 1b). Autochthonous 256 
sources accounted for a much larger part of the POM sources. This may be due to the 257 
10 
 
effective utilization of terrestrial nutrients by phytoplankton through microbial 258 
decomposition in lake ecosystems, resulting in an increase in auto chthonous sources 259 
(Dalu et al., 2015 ). The dominant role of autochthonous sources in POM has been 260 
confirmed in many lakes (Chen et al., 2018; Kumar et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2012). Chen 261 
et al. (2018) found that autochthonous sources in Lake Fuxian accounted for 61% of the 262 
total POC sources. Zhang et al. (2023) also discovered that DOM in the water column 263 
and sediments of Lake Erhai was mainly influenced by endogenous sources (especially 264 
phytoplankton). 265 
In our study, the proportion of each source did not show a significant correlation 266 
with lake trophic levels. This may be due to differences in the physical conditions of 267 
the lakes (including lake volume, topography, wind, and discharge), the growth of 268 
phytoplankton and aquatic plants, and the degradation and deposition processes of 269 
organic matter. Li et al. (2020) explored the spatial heterogeneity of sedimentary 270 
organic matter in Lake Taihu through C and N isotope signals. The results showed that 271 
in estuaries and open waters, the contribution of terrestrial organic matter to sediment 272 
organic matter was greater, reaching 46.8% to 55.0%; hydrodynamic conditions were 273 
an important factor influencing the differences in spatial distribution of sediment 274 
organic matter sources (Li et al., 2020 ). Wang et al. (2022) investigated the vertical 275 
distribution of POC sources in Lake Poyang using carbon and nitrogen stable isotopes. 276 
The results suggested that the bottom layer POC in Lake Poyang was mainly composed 277 
of exogenous sources and the surface layer POC was mainly composed of endogenous 278 
sources; the distribution and composition of the mid -bottom layer POC were mainly 279 
affected by soil erosion, sediment deposition and resuspension, while the surface layer 280 
was mainly affected by the environmental factors related to photosynthesis and the 281 
lake’s nutrient levels. In addition, factors such as isotope fractionation, uncertainty in 282 
environmental variables, and other reaso ns will lead to errors in model simulation 283 
results. Therefore, to further improve the reliability of the results, more variables should 284 
be considered for inclusion in future studies. At the same time, the effects of other 285 
factors such as hydrodynamic condi tions, nutrient concentrations, and changes in 286 
spatial and temporal scales should also be considered. 287

3.3. Molecular characteristics of POM 288 
3.3.1. Molecular indicators and compositions 289 
A total of 5092 -5898 formulas were identified in the negative ion mode for the 290 
seven lake POM samples, and their distribution on the Van Krevelen diagrams was 291 
shown in Fig. 2. The spectra showed a normal distribution in the range of 200-750 m/z 292 
(Fig. S2). Observing the molecular weight (MW) distribution of POM formulas, it was 293 
found that the number of formulas in the range of 300 -400 Da was the largest, and the 294 
average MW was in the range of 378-395 Da (Fig. S3). Five major subcategories were 295 
detected, including CHO, CHON, CHOP, CHOS, and CHONS (Fig. 3). The percentage 296 
of CHO formulas was the highest, reaching 41.71% -49.34%, followed by CHON 297 
formulas (41.42%-45.13%) (Fig. 3). CHOP formulas accounted for approximately 5.63% 298 
(2.96%-8.33%) of all POM formulas. In addition, there were some CHOS and CHONS 299 
formulas, accounting for 3.43% and 2.17% of all POM formulas, respectively (Fig. 3). 300 
To compare the structural characteristics of POM between the different lakes, we 301 
calculated the weighted average (wa) values of FT-ICR MS parameters (Table S4). The 302 
MWwa ranged from 352.98 to 367.67. AImod,wa ranged from 0.14 to 0.22 with an average 303 
of 0.19 ± 0.02, close to that of macrophyte extracts (AI mod,wa: 0.20) (Liu et al., 2020). 304 
DBEwa ranged from 5.31 to 7.19 with an average of 6.40 ± 0.64, which was lower than 305 
that of the overlying water of Lake Taihu (with an average of 8.42), a typical shallow 306 
eutrophic lake reported by Liu et al. (2020), but overlapped with the values for Lake 307 
Taihu macrophytes (7.27) and sediments extracts (5.81-6.61). The ranges of O/Cwa and 308 
H/Cwa were 0.48-0.57 and 1.28-1.48, with mean values of 0.53 ± 0.03 and 1.36 ± 0.07, 309 
respectively. In addition, D'Andrilli et al. (2015) developed the molecular lability 310 
boundary (MLB) to define the degree of instability of compounds, i.e., labile 311 
compounds for H/C ≥ 1.5 (MLBL) and less labile compounds for H/C < 1.5. The 312 
percentage of MLBL (MLBL%) for each lake was 30.97±7.63%, with Lake Yehu having 313 
the lowest MLBL% of 21.79% and Lake Nantaizi having the highest of 42.38% (Table 314 
S4). Further analysis of the distribution of MLB L% in the five subcategories (Fig. 3) 315 
showed that CHOP formulas had th e highest MLB L% of 51.56%, CHOS followed by 316 
44.09%, and CHON formulas had the lowest MLB L% of 26.67%, indicating that P -317 
12 
 
containing and S -containing formulas have higher instability than N -containing 318 
formulas. Higher MLB L% indicated that P -containing formulas in POM have higher 319 
chemical reactivity and bioavailability, and therefore are also more easily involved in 320 
the material cycle. 321 
3.3.2. Unique formulas 322 
The unique formulas are the molecular pools that are unique to the sample and 323 
distinguish it from other samples. Unique POM formulas in each lake sample can be 324 
obtained by removing all intersecting POM formulas in the seven lakes. T he 325 
distribution of unique POM formulas on the Van Krevelen diagrams was shown in Fig. 326 
2. The results showed that Lake Liangzi had the least number of unique POM formulas 327 
(196), and Lake Longyang had the most (566) (F ig. S4). The number of unique POM 328 
formulas was positively correlated with lake trophic states, i.e., POM in lakes with 329 
higher trophic states may have a wider range of sources and more organic matter may 330 
be involved in their material cycling processes. The unique POM formulas in different 331 
samples were dominated by lignin/CRAM-like (43.40 ± 20.81%), followed by proteins 332 
(18.28 ± 16.84%), tannins (12.85 ± 14.11%), lipids ( 9.83 ± 13.66%), carbohydrates 333 
(6.93 ± 5.49%), CAS (3.79 ± 4.47%), and unsaturated hydrocarbons (3.42 ± 7.91%) 334 
(Fig. S5). In addition, 1.50% of the formulas were unassigned (Fig. S5). 335 
DBE is an important parameter to illustrate the unsaturation and aromatic ity of 336 
organic compounds. The DBE values of unique POM formulas were distributed 337 
between 0 and 18, and lakes with higher trophic states had more formulas distributed 338 
within low DBE values (Fig. S6), indicating low unsaturation and aromaticity. The 339 
(DBE-O)/C and nominal oxidation state of carbon (NOSC) can be used to reflect the 340 
unsaturation and redox states of compounds (Phungsai et al., 2018 ). The higher the 341 
positive value of (DBE-O)/C, the higher the degree of unsaturation of the compounds, 342 
and the higher the positive value of NOSC, the higher the oxidation state of the 343 
compounds. According to the values of (DBE -O)/C and NOSC, compounds can b e 344 
divided into four categories: unsaturated and oxidized compounds, unsaturated and 345 
reduced compounds, saturated and oxidized compounds, and saturated and reduced 346 
compounds (Phungsai et al., 2019 ). Among the unique POM formulas, most of them 347 
13 
 
(46.96%-82.05%) had negative (DBE-O)/C values (Fig. 4), indicating a low degree of 348 
unsaturation. The proportion of formulas with a negative NOSC value was 30.61% to 349 
82.86%, with an average of 57.02%, indicating that unique POM formulas had lower 350 
oxidation. 351 
3.4. Linking POM molecular properties to trophic states of lakes 352 
We conducted a correlation analysis between the molecular parameters of POM 353 
formulas identified by FT -ICR MS and lake trophic states. None of the correlations 354 
reached significant levels. These correlations improved when considering only the 355 
unique POM formulas. Among the unique POM molecular parameters, the unsaturation 356 
represented by DBEwa and H/Cwa decreased with increasing lake trophic levels, and the 357 
aromaticity index, AImod,wa, also decreased with higher lake trophic levels (Fig. 5). The 358 
negative correlation presented by O/C wa with TSI indicated that the higher the lake 359 
trophic states, the lower the oxidation of the unique POM formulas. This phenomenon 360 
could be due to the increase of autochthonous vs allochthonous POM. Our previous 361 
research showed that P -containing formulas with lower O/C are more susceptible to 362 
photodegradation (Guo et al., 2023 ). Combined with the results of the present study, 363 
this suggests that lakes with higher trophic states have a higher molecular activity and 364 
potential to undergo degradation, thus making them more likely to be involved in 365 
material cycling processes. 366 
There was a significant positive correlation between MLB L% and the percentage 367 
of CHOP in unique POM formulas. At the same time, there was no significant 368 
correlation with other subcategories (Fig. 5) . This might indicate that unique P -369 
containing formulas dominated the overall distribution of active formulas, although the 370 
proportion of unique P -containing formulas was not high among all active formulas. 371 
The percentage of unique CHOP formulas and TSI showed a consistent correlation with 372 
H/Cwa, DBE wa, and AI mod,wa (Fig. 5), suggesting that in unique POM formulas, P -373 
containing formulas dominated the properties of POM, thereby affecting the lake 374 
trophic states. Sannigrahi et al. (2006) showed that P was associated with carbohydrates, 375 
amino acids, and lipids in ultraf iltered POM, whereas no correlation between P and 376 
lipids was observed in ultrafiltered DOM. This finding provided new thinking about the 377 
14 
 
relationship between P and labile substances in POM. The relative abundances of 378 
aliphatic compounds such as lipids and proteins were significantly positively correlated 379 
with lake trophic states. In contrast, relative abundances of compounds such as CAS, 380 
lignin/CRAM-like, and tannins compounds were negatively correlated with lake 381 
trophic states (Fig. 5). Lipids and proteins are typically autochthonous substances, 382 
while lignin/CRAM-like, tannin, and CAS compounds are allochthonous substances 383 
(Brooker et al., 2018; Feng et al., 2016). The correlation indicated that the higher trophic 384 
states of lakes, the greater the autochthonous contribution and POM activity, while the 385 
allochthonous contribution was gradually weakened, which corresponded to the 386 
prominent autochthonous contribution of POM in the previous section. 387 
Li et al. (2023) found that phytoplankton growth would release lipid and protein -388 
like compounds. More labile lipid and protein-like compounds can be easily degraded 389 
and utilized by microorganisms (Kim et al., 2006). However, microorganisms can both 390 
produce and consume organic matter. While they utilize lipid compounds, they also 391 
convert a portion of the easily degradable substances into microbial residues, which are 392 
more difficult to degrade and thus accumulate in the particulate matter, leading to an 393 
accumulation of aliphatic compounds in the lakes with higher trophic states. Previous 394 
studies have suggested that lignin/CRAM-like compounds are resistant (Mesfioui et al., 395 
2012; Yuan et al., 2017 ). However, we previously showed that lignin/CRAM -like P-396 
containing compounds can undergo photodegradation (Guo et al., 2023 ). Feng et al. 397 
(2015; 2017) also found that lignin/CRAM -like compounds were the fastest turnover 398 
terrestrial organic carbon compon ent in the Arctic basin, with a higher turnover rate 399 
than lipids and black carbon. The relative abundance of allochthonous substances such 400 
as lignin/CRAM -like, tannins, and CAS compounds in unique POM formulas were 401 
negatively correlated with TSI in this st udy, i.e., the relative abundances of 402 
allochthonous POM formulas gradually decreased as the lake trophic levels increased. 403 
This result also supported the idea that lakes with higher trophic states were more 404 
affected by autochthonous sources. As the trophic level of lakes increases, the 405 
utilization of allochthonous organic matters by autochthonous organisms increases, thus 406 
accelerating their growth and leading to a rise in lake trophic levels and plankton 407 
15 
 
biomass. The positive correlation between the relative abundances of lipids and proteins 408 
and TSI also suggested that the accumulation of autochthonous organic matter caused 409 
by the rapid degradation of aquatic macrophytes and algae in lakes might be a 410 
significant contributing factor to lake eutrophication. All these results demonstrated that 411 
eutrophication accelerated the conversion of allochthonous POM to aquatic POM, 412 
leading to the accumulation of autochthonous POM. 413 
3.5 Environmental Implications 414 
In recent decades, due to the enrichment of nutrients in the aquatic environment 415 
caused by human activities and the massive growth of algae and phytoplankton, there 416 
have been continuous outbreaks of cyanobacterial blooms throughout China and even 417 
globally (Hou et al., 2022 ). The fragmen tation and decomposition of algae and 418 
macrophytes after apoptosis releases large amounts of POM, which plays an essential 419 
role in the supply of organic matter pools in lake ecosystems. The source and properties 420 
of POM determine its biogeochemical processes in aquatic ecosystems, and even play 421 
a non-negligible role in the global material cycle. In this study, we found that lakes with 422 
higher trophic states presented higher POM content. This indicated that more organic 423 
matter was involved in the material cycling process in eutrophic lakes. Furthermore, in 424 
highly trophic lakes, the contribution of autochthonous POM gradually increase d and 425 
that of allochthonous POM gradually decreased. This suggested that the eutrophication 426 
process of lakes could accelerate the transformation of allochthonous to autochthonous 427 
organic matter. Consequently, this process would lead to the obvious autochthonous 428 
characteristics and the weakening of allochthonous characteristics, thereby accelerating 429 
the migration and transformation of nutrients in different media. 430 
In addition, we found that lakes with higher trophic states were characterized by 431 
POM with lower unsaturation, aromaticity, and oxidability. Previous studies have 432 
shown that autochthonous organic matter, such as aliphatic compounds and proteins are 433 
usually biologically labile, while allochthonous organic matter such as aromatic 434 
compounds and phenols are biologically refractory to degradation (Kellerman et al., 435 
2018; Liu et al., 2022). Our previous work has shown that P -containing formulas with 436 
low oxidizability and low unsaturation are more susceptible to photodegradation (Guo 437 
16 
 
et al., 2023 ). Therefore, the properties (ie. lower unsaturation, aromaticity, and 438 
oxidability) of POM formulas in lakes with high trophic states may lead to the 439 
accumulation of more labile and readily decomposable POM form ulas in eutrophic 440 
lakes, which may in turn accelerates the mineralization of organic matter in eutrophic 441 
lakes. Therefore, we suggest that eutrophication processes and algal bloom outbreaks 442 
lead to changes in lake POM composition, which in turn affects the biogeochemical 443 
cycles of organic matter (e.g., degradation and deposition) and pollutant behavior (e.g., 444 
adsorption, transport, and transformation). In brief, autochthonous POM, as an essential 445 
reservoir of organic matter, should receive more attention in eutrophic environments. 446 
In the future, it is necessary to study the impact mechanism of autochthonous organic 447 
matter pools in eutrophic lakes on larger-scale ecosystems, as well as the environmental 448 
behavior of organic matter at the molecular scale. 449 
4. Conclusions 450 
This study investigated the sources and molecular composition of POM in lakes 451 
with different trophic states using carbon and nitrogen isotope s and FT-ICR MS. We 452 
showed that autochthonous sources accounted for a greater proportion of POM sources 453 
in different lakes. P-containing formulas showed the highest molecular activity among 454 
all POM formulas, indicating that P -containing formulas have higher bioavailability. 455 
As lake trophic levels increased, the unique POM formulas displayed low unsaturation, 456 
low aromaticity, and low oxidizability, corresponding to an increase in the relative 457 
abundance of autochthonous compounds such as lipids and proteins. P -containing 458 
formulas in unique POM formulas showed significant correlations with MLB L%, 459 
unsaturation, ar omaticity, the relative abundances of lipid and lignin/CRAM -like 460 
compounds, suggesting that the unique P -containing formulas may be a link for POM 461 
to promote the eutrophication process of lakes. These findings will provide new insights 462 
into the sources and fate of POM in lakes under different trophic states and may further 463 
deepen our understanding of algal blooms in shallow lakes. 464
