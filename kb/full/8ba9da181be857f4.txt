Ubiquitous computing technologies offer unprecedented opportunities for continuous, passive, and longitudinal health monitoring outside clinical environments. However, standardized neuropsychological assessments—such as the Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT) for sustained attention and Cambridge Cognition (CANTAB) batteries for spatial memory—are frequently monotonous, leading to high user attrition during extended deployments. This paper explores the design space of gamifying cognitive assessment tools through a multi-pronged engineering approach. First, we investigate computer vision (CV) pipelines to extract reaction metrics from existing commercial games (Subway Surfers), highlighting the confounders of visual anticipation. Second, we examine spatial working memory abstraction via custom Unity prototypes (Blinking Bowls). Finally, we evaluate varying levels of game-mechanic abstraction applied to core attentional probes via thematic Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT) variants (water, tree, and robot games), supported by a formal pilot study of $n=50$ participants. Our findings provide a unified framework for balancing game design engagement with clinical data validity in ubiquitous health monitoring. Keywords: Ubiquitous Computing (UbiComp), Computer Vision, Spatial Working Memory, Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT), Gamification, Cognitive Assessment.
