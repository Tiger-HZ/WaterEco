Although water is present in nearly 3/4 of the earth, nearly the entire ocean area is unexplored due to continuous technological drawback. The Internet of Underwater Things (IoUT) is a novel term, as devices in the water have been connected to monitor and observe the marine environment remotely. Underwater wireless sensor network (UWSN) is a sub-infrastructure within the core of IoUT enabling the acquisition and transmission of data beneath the surface. However, the nature of acoustic communication channel constraints, high energy demands, low-processing capacities, and fluid network structures owing to node mobility inevitably pose challenges to UWSNs. All these weaknesses in the system expose UWSNs to various forms of cybercrimes such as wormhole attacks, Sybil attacks, flooding attacks, jamming attacks, spoofing attacks, and denial-of-service attacks. The research paper is a compilation of academic literature on the network security architecture of UWSNs, the critical vulnerabilities of the architecture, and the proposed countermeasures. This discussion reveals that power supply constraint, insufficient communication protocol, and unforgiving underwater transmission environment are the most prevalent causes of insecurity. Scholars also agree that the security infrastructures employed traditionally in the terrestrial sensor networks would not be suitable in the aqua environment, and therefore, the aqua environment needs special security means. The chapter ends its future research directions with the statement that to provide the security of the new world of the IoUT and UWSNs, it is necessary to develop powerful, energy-conscious, and context-conscious security paradigms.
