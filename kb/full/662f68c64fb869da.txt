Presented within the context of Paesaggi Sonori, a festival dedicated to sound as an artistic and cultural medium, this soundwalk repositions the mountain environment itself as a living, evolving musical composition. Rather than separating music from landscape, the work proposes that the acoustic ecology of the mountains—wind, water, rock, wildlife, and traces of human presence—constitutes a form of expanded, site-specific music.Participants are invited to engage in a guided listening experience that unfolds along a mountain path, where environmental sounds are framed as compositional elements: rhythms emerging from footsteps and terrain, drones shaped by wind across ridgelines, and shifting textures defined by altitude and vegetation. Through subtle interventions—such as curated listening prompts and carefully integrated recordings—the soundwalk creates a dialogue between composed and found sound, blurring the boundary between concert and environment.In this sense, the work aligns with the festival’s exploration of sound as both artistic material and perceptual practice. It extends the notion of performance beyond traditional stages, transforming the act of walking into a form of collective listening and the landscape into an open-air auditorium. The audience becomes both listener and performer, co-creating the experience through movement and attention.By situating environmental listening within a music festival, Listening to Mountain Breath invites reflection on how we define music, where it takes place, and how it relates to ecological awareness. The piece highlights how environmental changes—such as shifting weather patterns or human impact—manifest as audible transformations, encouraging participants to perceive ecological processes as sonic phenomena.Ultimately, the soundwalk contributes to Paesaggi Sonori by expanding its curatorial scope: it offers an embodied, site-responsive work in which music is not only something we attend, but something we inhabit. Through this approach, the project fosters a deeper connection between artistic listening and environmental consciousness, positioning sound as a bridge between cultural experience and ecological care.
