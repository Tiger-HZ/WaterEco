Mitochondrial dynamics has long been interpreted primarily through fission and fusion, yet tubular mitochondria can also undergo rapid pearling, a phenomenon in which elongated mitochondria reorganize into a beads-on-a-string morphology while retaining a continuous imaged contour. The occurrence and biological relevance of mitochondrial pearling require careful study. The dimensionless tension–bending ratio used to organize these observations is a heuristic analogy to single-membrane tubes, not a validated quantitative model of the mitochondrial double membrane.
