Plasma-activated water (PAW) selectively kills glioblastoma cells while sparing normal brain cells, but whether this selectivity originates in membrane biophysics, channel-mediated transport, or intracellular clearance is unresolved. We dissect the question for U87-MG glioblastoma cells versus normal astrocytes by combining all-atom molecular dynamics of the two unoxidized plasma membranes with an experiment-anchored transfer-function model of H2O2 influx. Simulations of unoxidized bilayers demonstrate that physiological cholesterol enrichment in glioblastoma membranes marginally increases lipid packing and acyl-chain order, conferring no baseline passive permeability advantage on the malignant cell.
