Abstract In long-distance water diversion projects, channel bifurcation zones are prone to developing a three-dimensional flow structure characterized by a “blind-channel reach,” which consequently induces sediment deposition in the inlet region. Taking a diversion channel of the Middle Route of the South-to-North Water Diversion Project as a case study, a three-dimensional numerical model is established to systematically investigate the flow characteristics in the inlet region as well as the transport and deposition behavior of suspended particles. The results indicate that flow separation of the main current occurs in the inlet area.
