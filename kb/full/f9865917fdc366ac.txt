This study develops a reproducible computational framework for groundwater flow simulation and constrained abstraction planning. A conservative finite-volume scheme with harmonic interface transmissivity and backward Euler integration represents transient flow in a heterogeneous confined aquifer. Linear response coefficients connect the numerical model to a convex quadratic allocation problem. A controlled 90-day experiment distributes a demand of 1,200 m³/day among three abstraction zones. Compared with equal allocation, the nominal optimum reduces mean squared drawdown by 25.87% and maximum drawdown by 30.88%, while preserving the total supply.
