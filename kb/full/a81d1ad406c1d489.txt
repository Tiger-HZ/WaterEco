Abstract Background Essential tremor (ET) is a multidimensional disorder impairing quality of life (QoL) through motor and non-motor manifestations. Magnetic Resonance-guided Focused Ultrasound (MRgFUS) thalamotomy reduces tremor, but clinical dimensions associated with patient-perceived benefit remain unclear. Objective To characterize 1-year multidimensional outcomes post-unilateral MRgFUS thalamotomy in ET and examine how changes in treated-hand tremor, functional disability, and tremor-related embarrassment relate to QoL. Methods From a consecutive institutional cohort undergoing unilateral MRgFUS thalamotomy, 1-year outcome data from 33 ET patients were retrospectively analyzed.
