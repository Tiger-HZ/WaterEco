Cyanobacteria, ancient photosynthetic prokaryotes, acclimatize to diverse light conditions. Light is not just an energy source for these organisms but also a crucial environmental signal that governs their growth, physiology, and ecological spread. Cyanobacteria possess a sophisticated system for light detection and response, crucial for their central role in global carbon and nitrogen cycles. The present work covers the complex network of photoreceptors—including phytochrome-like proteins, i.e., cyanobacteriochromes, and rhodopsins—that allow cyanobacteria to sense light. We also explore how the photosynthetic electron transport chain acts as a redox-sensitive system to regulate photosystem activity in response to light quality and intensity.
