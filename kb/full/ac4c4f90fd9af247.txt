Annually, billions of fish are captured and released under the assumption that they will recover and survive, which may not occur. Fishing-induced stressors require subsequent recovery, characterized by increased energy expenditure, greater vulnerability to predation and disease, and lost opportunities for critical activities such as feeding and migration. This study examined the physiological responses of adult coho salmon (Oncorhynchus kisutch) to interactions with fishing gear in coastal British Columbia, Canada. Collaborating with recreational fishers, we used novel onboard respirometers to measure metabolic recovery for 1 h, alongside blood sampling to assess physiological status.
