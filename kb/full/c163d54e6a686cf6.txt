Abstract 
 
The experiment was carried out during the rabi season of 2019-20 at the Instructional-cum-Research Farm of Assam Agricultural 
University, Jorhat, to evaluate the effects of irrigation scheduling and sulphur fertilisation on growth parameters, crop growth rate 
(CGR), water use efficiency, yield, and oil content of Indian mustard ( Brassica juncea L.) under irrigated conditions in Assam. 
The experiment was laid out in a split -plot design with two factors, viz., irrigation scheduling and sulphur fertilisation, each at 
different levels, with three replications. The treatments comprised six irrigation schedules, viz., I0 - rainfed, I1 - irrigation at 25-30 
DAS, I2 - irrigation at flowering (F), I 3 - irrigation at 25-30 DAS + flowering, I 4 - irrigation at 25-30 DAS + siliquae formation

1. Introduction 
 
Oilseed crops play an important role in the Indian agricultural economy, with rapeseed and mustard being major oilseed crops 
widely cultivated across the country. In recent years, Indian mustard ( Brassica juncea L.) has gained considerable importance in 
Assam as an alternative rabi oilseed crop because of its adaptability. However, because it is predominantly grown under rainfed 
conditions during the rabi season, productivity remains relatively low because of moisture stress and uneven rainfall distribution. 
Water deficit limits crop growth and adversely affects several physiological processes (Mohan et al. , 2025). Indian mustard 
responds strongly to irrigation, particularly during critical growth phases (Singh et al., 2025). Timely irrigation has been reported to 
improve crop growth, yield attributes, and seed quality (Ray et al., 2014). Therefore, appropriate irrigation at critical crop growth 
stages is essential for better growth and yield. Recent literature on Indian mustard likewise identifies drought and moisture scarcity 
as important constraints to crop productivity, reinforcing the relevance of efficient water management under water -limited 
production conditions (Baug & Darvhankar, 2026). Recent field evidence further indicates that irrigation scheduling, when 
integrated with nutrient management involving sulphur, can influence crop performance and quality -related responses in Indian 
mustard (Kumar et al., 2025). 
 
In addition to water availability, optimisation of nutrient supply plays an important role in increasing the productivity of Indian 
mustard. Rapeseed and mustard have a higher sulphur demand than most oilseed crops (Hegde & Babu, 2009). Rapeseed and 
mustard are highly sensitive to sulphur deficiency, which influences seed chemical composition and oil synthesis. Adequate su lphur 
availability also promotes seed and oil yield in Brassica crops and is essential for the synthesis of chlorophyll, proteins, enzymes, 
and vitamins (Shah et al., 2022). As such, crop yield can be increased by up to 50% with an adequate supply of major nutrients, 
particularly sulphur. Sulphur application is therefore essential to meet the additional sulphur requirement of oilseed crops, 
particularly mustard, and to enhance oilseed production. Recent research has shown that sulphur -containing nutrient management 
can influence seed yield and associated crop responses in Indian mustard (Gupta et al., 2024). Similarly, field evaluation of varying 
sulphur levels and application methods has demonstrated measurable differences in mustard productivity, supporting the agrono mic 
importance of appropriate sulphur management (Mohapatra et al., 2024). 
 
In Assam, the rabi season (mid-October to February) is characterised by prolonged drought, with very low average rainfall of about 
50 mm and high crop evapotranspiration demand. Given limited irrigation resources, identifying irrigation schedules for criti cal 
growth stages is necessary to maximise water productivity. Although irrigation and sulphur management have each been studied in 
mustard, their combined effects under the specific irrigated rabi conditions of Assam, particularly on growth, crop growth rate, 
water use efficiency, yield, and oil content, require assessment. Accordingly, the present study was based on the hypothesis that 
combining critical-stage irrigation scheduling with balanced NPK and sulphur fertilisation improves growth parameters, water use 
efficiency, and overall yield of Indian mustard under Assam conditions. Therefore, the objective of the present study was to evaluate 
the effects of irrigation scheduling and sulphur fertilisation on growth parameters, crop growth rate, water use efficiency, yield, and 
oil content of Indian mustard under irrigated conditions in Assam. 
 
2. Materials and Methods 
 
The present field experiment was conducted during the rabi season of 2019 -20 at the Instructional -cum-Research Farm, Assam 
Agricultural University, Jorhat. The climate of Jorhat is subtropical and humid. The total rainfall during the crop growth pe riod in 
rabi 2019-2020 (13 November 2019 to 22 February 2020) was 36.4 mm, received in 10 spells, of which five occurred during the 
crop maturity stage. The total evaporation during the crop -growing period was 123.7 mm, creating a rainfall deficit of 87.3 mm 
relative to transpirational demand. The weekly mean maximum temperature ranged from 20.7 oC to 29.7 oC, and the minimum 
temperature ranged from 8.4 oC to 18.1 oC during the experimental period. The weekly average morning relative humidity ranged 
from 97.1% to 100%, and bright sunshine ranged from 2.1 to 8.2 hours day -1 during the experimental period. The average depth to 
the groundwater table was 88.35 cm and ranged from 58.6 to 121.2 cm below the soil surface. 
 
The Indian mustard variety 'NRCHB101' was used in the experiment. The field experiment was laid out in a split -plot design with 
three replications. The main plots comprised six irrigation schedules (I 0 to I5), while the subplots consisted of three sulphur levels, 
viz., S0 (0 kg ha-1), S1 (20 kg ha -1), and S2 (40 kg ha -1). Farmyard manure (FYM) @ 2 t ha -1 and the recommended dose of N -P2O5-

3. Results and Discussion 
 
3.1 Growth Parameters and Crop Growth Rate (CGR) 
 
Application of irrigation significantly influenced the various growth parameters, viz., plant height (Table 1), dry matter 
accumulation (Table 2), and branches plant -1 (Table 3); however, plant population at harvest was non -significant. Crop growth rate 
(Table 4) at different crop growth stages was also significantly influenced by irrigation. Plant height at harvest, dry matte r 
accumulation at 50 and 75 days after sowing (DAS), number of primary, secondary, and total branches plant -1, and crop growth rate 
during 25 -50 and 50 -75 DAS were highest with three irrigations at 25 -30 DAS, flowering, and siliquae formation stages (I 5), 
followed by two irrigations at 25 -30 DAS + flowering (I 3) and 25 -30 DAS + siliquae formation (I 4). These treatments were 
statistically at par and produced higher values than one irrigation (I 1 and I 2), whereas the lowest values were observed with no 
irrigation (I 0). Sharma et al. (2006) reported that increased irrigation might have contributed to cell turgidity and consequently 
enhanced the photosynthetic activity of the plant. This may be because increased irrigation enhanced nutrient uptake, translo cation, 
and assimilation, thereby increasing plant height, number of leaves, and dry matter accumulation. Similar responses to two 
irrigations at 25 and 53 DAS (Roy et al., 2017) and three irrigations at branching, flowering, and pod -filling stages (Yadav, 2005), 
or at 35, 60, and 90 DAS (Mishra et al., 2019), compared with lower irrigation regimes have also been reported.

3.2 Water Use and Water Use-efficiency 
 
In the present investigation, the data (Table 5) revealed that three irrigations at 25 -30 DAS + flowering + siliquae formation 
recorded the highest evapotranspiration and total water use by Indian mustard among the treatments. This was followed by two 
irrigations at 25 -30 DAS + flowering and 25 -30 DAS + siliquae formation, whereas the lowest evapotranspiration and total water 
use were recorded under rainfed conditions. However, crop and field water use efficiencies under irrigation schedules I 1 and I0 were 
close to each other and comparatively higher than under the remaining treatments, while the lowest values were recorded with I5. 
This may be because consumptive water use (CU) increases with irrigation frequency, whereas crop water use efficiency follows the 
opposite trend. Therefore, higher evapotranspiration and total water use under the higher irrigation regimes (two or three irrigations) 
were associated with comparatively lower crop and field water use efficiencies. Similar results in different crops have also been 
reported by Piri et al. (2011), Raut et al. (2000), and Sarma et al. (2007).

Application of different sulphur levels resulted in little variation in evapotranspiration and total water use (Table 5). How ever, crop 
and field water use efficiencies increased with increasing sulphur levels. The highest crop water use efficiency was obtained with 40 
kg S ha-1, followed by 20 kg S ha-1, both of which were considerably higher than the control. This may be due to the increase in seed

4. Conclusion 
 
The study demonstrated clear main effects of irrigation scheduling and sulphur fertilisation on growth, water use, and produc tivity 
of Indian mustard under the irrigated rabi conditions of Assam. Three irrigations at 25 –30 DAS, flowering, and siliquae formation 
produced the highest seed and oil yields, but required the greatest water use and resulted in the lowest crop and field water use 
efficiencies. Two irrigations, either at 25 –30 DAS + flowering or at 25 –30 DAS + siliquae formation, produced very similar seed 
yields (1291.55 and 1290.87 kg ha⁻¹, respectively) while using less water than the three -irrigation schedule. Sulphur at 40 kg ha⁻¹ 
produced the highest seed yield, oil content, and oil yield, although 20 kg ha⁻¹ was statistically at par with 40 kg ha⁻¹ for several 
growth traits. The reported irrigation × sulphur interactions were non -significant for the measured growth and yield variables. 
Overall, the findings indicate that critical -stage irrigation and adequate sulphur nutrition can improve Indian mustard productivity 
under the conditions of this study, with two irrigations providing a favourable balance between yield and water-use performance. 
 
5. Limitations 
 
The experiment was conducted at a single location in Jorhat during one rabi season (2019–20), using one Indian mustard variety and 
three replications. Consequently, the observed responses may be specific to the climatic and experimental conditions of that season 
and site. The irrigation treatments were evaluated only at the stated crop -growth stages and irrigation depth, so the findings apply to 
those management conditions. Multi -season and multi -location validation, including additional mustard genotypes, would 
strengthen the generalisability and confirm the stability of the observed yield and water-use responses.
