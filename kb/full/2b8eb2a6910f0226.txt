ABSTRACT Schematic illustration of the synthesis of CoFe₂O₄-modified biochar from durian shell using FeCl₃·6H₂O and CoCl₂·6H₂O, followed by PMS activation for carbamazepine (CBZ) degradation. Reactive oxygen species generated on the catalyst surface attack CBZ, leading to its oxidation and eventual mineralization into CO₂ and H₂O. Waste-derived biochar has emerged as a sustainable and low-cost catalyst for peroxymonosulfate (PMS) activation; however, its practical application is often hindered by insufficient active sites, limited catalytic efficiency, and poor recoverability. To address these challenges, a magnetic modified biochar (CFBC) was synthesized by anchoring CoFe2O4 nanoparticles onto durian shell-derived biochar and applied for carbamazepine (CBZ) degradation via PMS activation.
