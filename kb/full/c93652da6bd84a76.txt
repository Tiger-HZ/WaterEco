Background: Pit-and-fissure sealants are widely used to prevent occlusal caries, but daily brushing and repeated acid exposure may compromise their surface integrity. This in vitro study evaluated the effects of simulated long-term toothbrushing, with and without intermittent citric acid exposure, on the surface properties of six pit-and-fissure sealants. Methods: Disc specimens (n = 84; n = 14 per material; n = 7 per subgroup) prepared from five resin-based sealants and one resin-modified glass-ionomer were subjected to 60,000 brushing strokes under a 2 N load with an abrasive slurry, either alone or combined with intermittent immersion in citric acid (pH 2.5).
