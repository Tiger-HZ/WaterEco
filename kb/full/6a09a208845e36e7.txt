Abstract: Emission charge policy has recently switched from pollutant discharge fees to environmental taxes in China. 
Considering spatial heterogeneity, the effects of changes in emission charge policy may subject to different Chinese regions. 
In this study, environmental efficiencies of Chinese regions are evaluated through provincial environmentally extended 
input-output tables and a frontier-based optimization model. Driving factors of environmental productivity growth are 
identified through global Luenberger productivity decomposition approach. Moreover, spatial heterogeneity on the effects 
of change in emission charge policy on environment and economy are assessed. Results show that all regions experienced 
environmental productivity growth. Technology progress is the major driving factor in most regions with an average 
contribution of 90%, while technical efficiency regress slows environmental productivity growth in Southwest region. 
Switching from pollutant discharge fees to environmental taxes would decreases emission intensities by 1.42% on average, 
but it would have different negative impact on economic growth (-1.13%~-4.90% of regional GDP) due to spatially 
heterogeneous trade-offs between environmental protection and economic development. Addressing such spatial 
heterogeneity provide not only a basis for diversified tax rate determination but also a framework for other environmental 
policy assessment. 
Keywords: environmental efficiency; emission charge policy; productivity decomposition; IOA; DEA

1. Introduction 38 
Industrialization and urbanization caused by rapid economic development lead to 39 
over-energy consumption in China. Currently, China is the biggest country of energy consumption 40 
in the world, ranking the top of the growth in global energy consumption for 17 years ( BP, 2018). 41 
In 2017, China accounted for 23.2% and 33.6% in the global energy consumption and the growth 42 
in global energy consumption, respectively (BP, 2018). Due to the excessive energy consumption, 43 
environmental pollutions are becoming increasingly serious in China, especially air pollutions and 44 
water pollution s. During the 12th Five -Year period, China dominated approximately 30% of 45 
global sulfur dioxide (SO 2) emission and 20% of global nitrogen oxides (NO x) emission per 46 
annum ( Zhang et al. , 2018). Meanwhile, particulates emissions, including soot and dust (SD) 47 
(Liang et al., 201 6) and particulate matter (Song et al., 2017 ) are also at high levels in China. 48 
Because of the severe air pollutions, the number of heavy pollution days raise continually and 49 
about one third of Chinese cities struggle with fog and haze issues ( Xie et al. , 2018). Beside air 50 
pollutions, series of water pollutions intensify the problem of scarcity of drinking water supply. 51 
For instance, the Yangtze River, which is the source of drinking water for up to 800 million (M) 52 
people, undertakes the most proportion of national water polluted industrial activities (Chen et al., 53

Facing severe environmental and ecological problems and their effects on public health, 57

emission reduction targets for major pollutions since the 11th Five -Year Plan (Yang et al., 2018). 63 
And ecological protection targets are completed in the 13th Five-Year Plan for Eco-environmental 64 
Protection. Those policies did have positive effects on slowing down the rate of emission growth 65 
to some extent. In 2017, carbon emission increased 1.6% in China, which was the half of the 66 
average rate over the past decade ( BP, 2018). Other pollutions met the corresponding emission 67 
targets as well (Wang et al. , 2014). However, environmental policies associated with emission 68 
abatement and environmental protection will limit production and further reduce economic growth, 69 
in other words, there are trade-offs between environmental protection and economic development. 70 
It has been confirmed that strict environmental policies would have a negative impact on GDP in 71 
China (Ahmed & Ahmed, 2018). 72

have a more comprehensive estimation of policy effects. Environmental efficiency is one of the 74

reduction. 107

2. Methods and data 121 
2.1. Environmentally extended input-output analysis for efficiency measurement 122 
Environmentally extended input -output analysis (EEIOA) is widely applied to assess the 123 
environmental impacts related to energy consumption (Chen et al., 2018a), pollutant emission, e.g. 124 
CO2 ( Meng et al., 2018 ) and mercury emissions ( Li et al. , 2015), efficiency measurement 125 
(Aguilar-Hernandez et al. , 2018), and improved potential evaluation at regional level ( Mi et al., 126 
2015) or national level (Mi et al., 2017). The advantage of EEIOA is that the relationship between 127 
environment and economy is treated in one unitary and closed monetarily valued material flow. 128 
Besides, each sector’s characteristics can be captured through multi-sector input-output table. In 129 
addition, impacts of environmental policies include both the effects of policy itself and the effects 130 
of efficiency changes associated with policy reform . Thus, in order to evaluate the effects of 131 
environmental policy itself on environment and economy, the impacts of efficiency change 132 
required to be eliminated, so that different policy scenarios could be compared under the same 133 
environmental efficient benchmark. Therefore, frontier-based optimization model is developed by 134 
combing EEIOA and DEA to calculate environmental inefficiency score. The framework is 135 
illustrated as in Figure 1. 136

abatement cost and abatement benefit . Besides, inputs for productive activities and inputs for 163

inputs of emission abatement sectors are calculated through the balance of Leontief input -output 174

2.3. Data sources 252 
Chinese provincial input-output tables are issued every five years by Department of National 253 
Economic Accounting, National Bureau of Statistics of China. The last two issues of provincial 254 
input-output tables with 42 industrial sectors of 30 Chinese provinces in 2007 and 2012 are used 255 
as the basic economic datasets. The sector category is in line with that in 2012 input-output tables 256 
(see Table S2). Given that labour and capital are the main sources of extensible primary inputs, the 257 
social available resource z0 is calculated based on the weighted average of the extensible rates of 258 
labour and capital with the weight equaling to the proportion of each primary input to the total 259 
primary input. The extensible rates for the whole country and 30 regions are measured based on 260 
unemployed population and depreciation of original value of fixed asse ts, approximately ranging 261 
from 10% to 50%. For easier discussion, we assume the same extensible rate in all primary inputs 262 
and all provinces, thus we take 30% as the extensible rate of primary input and 130% as the social 263 
available resource z0. 264 
As for env ironmental data, we get the national emission loads of the sixteen pollutants in 265 
2007 and 2012 from Chinese Environmentally Extended Input‐ Output (CEEIO) Database (Liang 266 
et al. , 2017). Values of national emission abatement cost come from Department of Indu stry 267 
Statistics of National Bureau of Statistics of China and are distributed to each pollutant by their 268 
emission proportion. We allot the national emission loads and national emission abatement cost to 269 
provincial ones by the provincial energy consumption proportion. Total final use of emission 270 
abatement sector (environmental benefit associated with emission abatement ) is measured by the 271 
product of emission abatement load and the health costs per unit of emission. According to World 272 
Bank (2007), total health costs of air pollution and water pollution in China is 3.8 % of GDP and 273 
2.0% of GDP, respectively. 274

2.4. Pollutant equivalents transformation 276 
Given that each pollutant has different environmental effects, same emission loads of 277 
different pollutants will pose different degrees of negative impacts on environment. Thus, it is 278 
necessary to unify the negative effects of various pollutants on environment and public health. 279 
China’s Ministry of Environmental Protection published a list of taxable pollutants and the 280 
corresponding “pollutant equivalent”, which could be applied to transfer the physical units ( Ephy) 281 
of emission loads of different pollutants to the equivalent units ( Eequ) according to their 282 
environmental and health impacts (Zhang et al. 2018). The equivalent units (k) are listed in Table 283 
S3, serving as the coefficients to divide emission loads in physical units (Ephy): Eequ=Ephy/k. 284

From the above results we can see that, tightening emission charge policy would reduce 455

purpose of discussing this effect of different sectors in different regions, we measure the 460 
percentage change of carbon em ission associated with total output under pollutant discharge fees 461 
policy and environmental taxes policy (see Figure 5). In this study, we only consider the 462 
synergistic effects of carbon emission reduction due to the total output reduction, other factors 463 
such as abatement technical improvement or trade diversion are not included. Besides, we assume 464 
that carbon emission factor is fixed under different emission charge policies. As reflected from 465

Southwest region, respectively. Additionally, compared with other regions, carbon emission 469 
reduction in South coast region would be the most obvious , indicating that the emission charge 470 
policy would have more obvious effects on both environment and economy in South coast region, 471 
which, again, verifies the results in Section 4.2. 472

Nevertheless, there are several limitations of this study. In order to reflect the material flow 527 
among various industrial sectors and figure out the impacts of environmental policy on economic 528 
production, input-output tables are chosen to be the basic economic dataset. The last two issues of 529 
provincial input -output tables of 30 Chinese provinces are in 2007 and 2012, thus, we cho ose 530 
these two years for the analysis. Considering the rapid economic development and changes in 531 
production structure, the illustrations would be more accurate using the more recent values if the 532 
data is available. Additionally, data related with emission abatement at sector level is incomplete. 533

to inaccuracy. Furthermore, material flow of different regions would lead to emission leakage 535 
embodied in trade. Future study can be conducted with the consideration of the material flow and 536 
emission leakage among multi -regions to fi gure out the effects of emission charge policy on 537 
multi-regional emission leakage and analyze the regional unfairness of emission charge policy. 538
