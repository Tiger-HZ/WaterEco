Abstract
Accurate prediction of pedestrian crossing intention is critical for 
the safety of automated vehicles. Existing approaches mainly rely 
on visual appearance and scene context derived from raw images 
and semantic segmentation. We propose a geometry-aware frame-
work that incorporates surface normal maps encoding pixel-level 
3D surface orientation. To integrate heterogeneous information, 
we extend a multi-stream GRUśattention architecture with surface-
normal features. The visual branch combines surface normals, local 
context, and segmentation overlays, while the non-visual branch in-
cludes body pose, bounding boxes, and ego-vehicle speed. Extensive 
ablation studies on the PIE dataset demonstrate that surface normal 
maps provide consistent performance gains. When combined with 
contextual features, they improve F1 score and AUC by 3.85% and 
2.35%, respectively, and yield more stable predictions up to two 
seconds before crossing events. These results highlight the impor-
tance of geometry-informed features as an effective complement to 
conventional visual inputs.

1 Introduction
Pedestrian crossing prediction, also known as pedestrian intention
prediction (PIP) models, aim at forecasting whether a pedestrian
will cross the road in the near future. This automated driving func-
tion is a key element in the enhancement of intelligent and safe
autonomous driving systems [ 27]. In complex urban environments,
vehicles often encounter pedestrians whose actions must be accu-
rately anticipated to ensure safe and timely manoeuvres. As one of
the most vulnerable and unpredictable road users, pedestrians play
a significant role in traffic-related incidents. Reliable prediction
systems must therefore analyse and integrate pedestrian motion
cues, environmental context, and interactions with the ego-vehicle
to effectively infer crossing intentions.
Previous studies have utilised visual and non-visual cues such as
pedestrian bounding boxes, body pose keypoints, and vehicle dy-
namics to infer pedestrian crossing behaviour [ 1, 11, 26]. However,
many existing approaches rely heavily on raw frame features or se-
mantic segmentation inputs, both prone to degradation under chal-
lenging conditions such as poor lighting or visual ambiguity [24, 25].
Current approaches often lack an explicit representation of physical
scene elements, such as curbs, pavements, and road surfaces, which
are essential factors influencing pedestrian decision-making. While
appearance and semantic cues capture visual context, they provide
limited insight into the physical layout of the scene, which strongly
influences pedestrian crossing behaviour (e.g., the spatial separa-
tion between road and sidewalk). A geometry-aware representation
helps the model understand which parts of the scene are walkable
or suitable for crossing.
To address these limitations, we investigate the integration of 
surface normal maps as a complementary geometry-aware modal-
ity for pedestrian crossing prediction. Rather than proposing a 
new prediction architecture, our objective is to evaluate whether 
explicit geometric scene representations provide complementary 
information beyond conventional appearance- and semantics-based 
features. Surface normal maps represent the per-pixel 3D orienta-
tion of visible scene surfaces and thus encode scene structure rather 
than appearance. Although they are estimated from RGB frames 
using DSINE [2], they capture geometric cues that are relatively less 
sensitive to lighting, shadows, or texture variations [14]. Surface 
normals have been successfully employed in various tasks, includ-
ing 3D reconstruction and affordance prediction. However, their 
potential for pedestrian crossing prediction remains unexplored.

Unlike depth maps or Lidar-derived geometry, surface normals offer
a compact, camera-derived representation of surface orientation
that can be estimated directly from monocular videos.
Inspired by prior multi-stream designs [ 1, 11, 26], the proposed
pedestrian crossing prediction model processes six input modali-
ties: local context, semantic segmentation (via overlaid raw-frame-
segmentation maps), surface normal maps, bounding box, body
pose, and ego-vehicle speed. Each modality is temporally modelled
using a dedicated GRU-attention stream to capture its temporal
dynamics, and the resulting features are fused using a hybrid late-
stage strategy. Experiments on the PIE dataset [ 19], a widely used
benchmark dataset for pedestrian behaviour analysis, demonstrate
that incorporating surface normal maps consistently improves per-
formance across multiple metrics. Notably, surface normals enhance
the consistency of model predictions over different time-to-event
(TTE) horizons. We adopt Yang et al. [ 26] multi-stream design not
to propose a new architecture, but to systematically evaluate the in-
fluence of adding a geometry-informed modality under a controlled,
established baseline. Our key contributions are as follows:
• We introduce surface normal maps as a complementary 
geometry-aware representation for pedestrian crossing pre-
diction, representing the first application of this modality in 
this context.
• We demonstrate that integrating surface normal maps into 
an established multi-stream GRU-attention framework con-
sistently improves pedestrian crossing prediction perfor-
mance across Accuracy, F1 score, and AUC metrics.
• We conduct comprehensive ablation studies and temporal 
analyses showing that incorporating surface normals im-
proves model robustness across longer time-to-event inter-
vals.
By incorporating surface normal information into crossing pre-
diction, we introduce a complementary geometric cue that enhances
spatial understanding of urban scenes and supports more reliable
decision-making for autonomous systems.
2 Related Work
Early approaches to pedestrian crossing prediction primarily relied
on handcrafted features or convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
applied to RGB data [ 20, 21]. While effective in capturing visual
appearance, these methods often overlook non-visual signals such
as pedestrian dynamics or vehicle motion. Subsequent works ad-
dressed this limitation by incorporating additional modalities. For
instance, Kotseruba et al. [ 13] introduced the PCPA framework,
which fused image features with contextual and dynamic cues. Ca-
dena et al. proposed the Pedestrian Graph [ 4] and its enhanced ver-
sion [3], integrating pose keypoints, image context, and ego-vehicle
speed for richer scene understanding. Similarly, Liu et al. [ 15] mod-
elled interactions between pedestrians and other road users using
a graph convolutional network (GCN), capturing both spatial rela-
tionships and temporal dependencies in dynamic scenes.
With the advent of transformer-based architectures, several mod-
els have advanced the field by capturing long-range dependencies
through self-attention mechanisms. TrEP [ 28] models temporal
relations in pedestrian behaviour while accounting for uncertainty
via evidential learning, whereas TAMformer [ 17] employs a multi-
modal transformer to forecast future pedestrian actions across mul-
tiple anticipation horizons. PedFormer [ 18] introduces cross-modal
attention and gated multi-task learning for joint trajectory and
intention prediction.
Alongside these developments, multi-modal fusion techniques
have also been explored. Ham et al. [ 11] proposed MCIP, a multi-
stream network that processes heterogeneous features in parallel
to exploit their complementary properties. PIP-Net [ 1] integrates
spatial and kinematic inputs using recurrent units and temporal
attention, and introduces local motion flow and categorical depth
features for improved visual encoding.
Despite these advancements, most state-of-the-art models rely
primarily on RGB appearance or 2D semantic segmentation to rep-
resent scene context. However, such representations are sensitive
to adverse illumination, reflections, or low-texture regions [14], and
often lack explicit 3D geometric understanding.
Surface normal maps provide an explicit representation of scene
geometry by encoding the per-pixel orientation of visible surfaces.
These geometric cues capture structural properties of the envi-
ronment, such as surface layout, boundaries, and spatial config-
uration, that are directly relevant to pedestrian movement and
crossing behaviour. While surface normal estimation has been
widely studied in geometric scene understanding and reconstruc-
tion tasks [ 9, 10, 12, 22], its potential role in behavioural reasoning
for pedestrian crossing prediction remains largely unexplored.
Existing pedestrian crossing prediction approaches primarily
rely on appearance-based or motion-related features, which may
not explicitly represent the physical structure of the environment,
such as road boundaries, sidewalks, or curb geometry. This work
addresses this gap by introducing surface normal maps as a com-
plementary geometric modality for pedestrian crossing prediction.
The proposed approach is motivated by the hypothesis that incorpo-
rating explicit scene geometry can support more reliable reasoning
about pedestrian behaviour, particularly in complex urban envi-
ronments where spatial layout and surface configuration influence
crossing decisions.
3 Methodology
We address the task of pedestrian crossing prediction as follows: 
given a sequence of 𝑚+1 consecutive frames captured by a forward-
facing, vehicle-mounted camera, the objective is to predict whether 
a target pedestrian 𝑖 will begin crossing the road at time 𝑡, which is 
also known as crossing event time. A critical aspect of this predic-
tion is the Time-to-Event (TTE), defined as the interval between 
the last frame of the model’s observation window and the actual 
start of the crossing event. Our model aims to make this predic-
tion for TTE values typically ranging from 30 to 60 frames (1 to 
2 seconds) [20]. We adopt an established inspired by prior multi-
stream designs [1, 11, 26] enabling a controlled evaluation of the 
contribution of surface normal maps while maintaining a consis-
tent architectural baseline. The proposed model, shown in Figure 1, 
utilises three primary visual input streams (local context, seman-
tic segmentation, and surface normal maps) and three non-visual 
modalities (body pose, bounding box, and ego-vehicle speed). The 
main model also uses an overlaid representation for the semantic

Surface normals maintain spatial consistency through piecewise

guish planar surfaces (e.g., roads) from vertical ones (e.g., curbs or
pedestrians). Sharp changes in normals at geometric boundaries
further refine segmentation masks, ensuring alignment with the
physical environment. While normals lack semantic identity, their
fusion with semantic features creates a physically coherent rep-
resentation that enhances generalisation in unpredictable urban
settings.
Each stream is processed through a CNNśGRUśattention pipeline

by a hybrid fusion strategy.

In the non-visual branch, we hierarchically fuse pose keypoints,
bounding boxes, and ego-vehicle speed. First, pose sequences are

produce a 256-dimensional non-visual embedding 𝑉kin.

viously, each yielding a 256-dimensional embedding. These three
embeddings are then concatenated to form the visual embedding
of 𝑉vis ∈ R768.

bedding 𝑉vis ∈ R768 are fused using a modality attention mechanism
before the final prediction layer. This mechanism learns to scale the

4 Experiments and Results
We conduct a series of experiments to evaluate the impact of in-
corporating surface normal maps into an existing pedestrian cross-
ing prediction model. Our evaluation focuses on three aspects: (1)
comparing various visual feature configurations through ablation
studies, (2) analysing the temporal robustness of predictions across
time-to-event (TTE) intervals, and (3) evaluating the contribution
of surface normals when used alongside appearance- and semantics-
based features.
4.1 Experimental Setup
We evaluate our approach on the well known PIE dataset [19] in the
research community, which contains over six hours of annotated
urban driving footage captured from a vehicle-mounted camera.
The dataset includes 1,842 pedestrian instances across diverse sce-
narios, with 512 labelled as crossing and 1,322 as non-crossing. Each
instance is annotated with bounding boxes, crossing behaviour, and
ego-vehicle speed.
Following the standard protocol of Yang et al. [ 26], we use an
observation window of 𝑚+1 = 16 frames at 30 FPS to predict
whether the target pedestrian will begin crossing shortly after the
final frame. Performance is evaluated using Accuracy, Precision,
Recall, F1 score, and Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) .
All models are implemented in TensorFlow and trained using the
Adam optimiser with a learning rate of 5 × 10−5 and a batch size of 2
for 60 epochs. Binary cross-entropy serves as the loss function. We
apply a dropout rate of 0.5 in the attention modules and L2 regular-
isation (𝜆 = 0.001) in the final classification layer. All experiments
follow the dataset split defined in the PIE benchmark [ 13].

4.2 Visual Feature Ablation Study
We conduct an ablation study with five model configurations to
assess contributions of various visual scene representations, partic-
ularly semantic and geometric cues. Our baseline includes essential
features widely used in pedestrian prediction: local context (L), body
pose (P), bounding boxes (B), and ego-vehicle speed (V). These are
kept constant to isolate the impact of varied visual streams. The
study focuses on ablating two key additional visual inputs from the
broader scene: semantic segmentation and surface normal maps. To
isolate the contribution of geometric and semantic visual features,
all non-visual modalities (pose, bounding box, and vehicle speed)
were kept constant across experiments.
The ablated features are: 𝑁 (surface normal maps), 𝑆 (raw seg-
mentation masks), and 𝑆𝑂 (segmentation masks overlaid on RGB
frames with 0.5 opacity).
The ablation results in Table 1 demonstrate the progressive ben-
efit of integrating richer visual cues. Config #2 (𝐿 + 𝑆𝑂 ) shows that
overlaying semantic masks on RGB frames improves performance
(e.g., +0.02 F1 over Config #1 using raw segmentation masks, likely
by providing the VGG19 with both low-level texture and high-level
semantic structure, enhancing spatial understanding.

predictions based on observations made further in advance of the
event. Figure 3 presents Accuracy, F1 score, and AUC for key model
configurations across these TTE values.

normals with the segmentation overlay ( Config #5 : 𝐿 + 𝑆𝑂 + 𝑁 ,
green line) consistently yields the highest or near-highest scores
across all metrics and most TTE horizons. Notably, it maintains an

normals with local context ( Config #3, blue line) across many TTEs.
This validates overlaid RGB-Segmentation as a strong stand-alone
visual representation. However, the consistent gap between Config
#2 (orange line) and Config #5 (green line) underscores the added
benefit of surface normals for temporal robustness. Overall, these
temporal results confirm that the synergistic combination of ap-
pearance (local context), rich semantics (segmentation overlay), and
explicit geometric structure (surface normals), as in Config #5, leads
to more generalisable and reliable pedestrian crossing predictions,
particularly in challenging scenarios requiring decisions based on
earlier observations (i.e., larger TTEs).
5 Conclusion and Future Work
This work explored the integration of geometry-aware features,
specifically surface normal maps, into a pedestrian crossing predic-
tion framework. Extending an existing multi-stream GRU-attention
architecture, we incorporated surface normals as a complementary
modality alongside RGB appearance, semantic segmentation, pose
keypoints, bounding boxes, and ego-vehicle speed.
Comprehensive evaluations on the PIE dataset, including abla-
tion studies and TTE analysis, showed that surface normals consis-
tently improve robustness, particularly in visually ambiguous or
structurally complex scenes. The combination of surface normals
and overlaid semantic segmentation achieved the most consistent
gains across all evaluation metrics, especially at longer prediction
horizons, which is critical for A V’s safe and timely manoeuvring.
This highlights the importance of encoding geometric structure
in a noisy road environment in order to complement traditional
appearance and motion cues in crossing prediction tasks. Unlike
appearance-based features, surface normals provide fine-grained
spatial information that remains stable across lighting and texture
changes, offering more reliable inference in challenging urban sce-
narios.
This study intentionally adopted a pre-existing architecture, in-
cluding a VGG19 backbone and GRU-based temporal encoders,
following Yang et al. [ 26], but isolated the main part of the model
in order to conduct a fair evaluation and comparison of the surface
normal maps’ efficiency.
The current pipeline relies on multiple task-specific models 
(OpenPose, DeepLabV3, and DSINE) for feature extraction, which in-
creases computational cost and limits real-time applicability. Since 
the primary objective of this work was to evaluate the predictive 
contribution of geometry-aware representations under a controlled 
experimental setting, runtime performance was not explicitly eval-
uated. Future work will investigate lightweight end-to-end archi-
tectures and more efficient feature extraction pipelines suitable for 
real-time autonomous driving applications.
This study focused on evaluating the contribution of surface
normal representations estimated from RGB video data. Although
surface normals can also be derived from other sensing modali-
ties, such as LiDAR or radar, the present work deliberately adopts
a single-modality (video-only) setup to isolate and examine the
geometric contribution of surface orientation cues within a vision-
centric framework.
In addition, the evaluation was conducted exclusively on the PIE
dataset. While PIE is a widely used benchmark for pedestrian be-
haviour analysis, validating the proposed approach across datasets
with different environments, viewpoints, and traffic conditions
would provide further insight into its generalisability. Future work
can therefore explore cross-dataset validation and investigate the

integration of geometry-aware representations within broader sens-
ing configurations.
