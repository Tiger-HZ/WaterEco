Abstract
Soil pollution caused by the accumulation of potentially toxic elements (PTEs) has become a serious
problem due to industrial and urban pollution as well as pesticide use. Primula forbesii Franch. is a biennial
ower native to China with excellent stress resistance and ornamental value. In this study, we examined the
phenotypic traits, growth indexes, and physiological properties of P. forbesii in response to ve representative
PTEs (Cd, Ni, Cr, Cu, and Zn) under hydroponic culture conditions. High concentrations of Zn and Cr had little
effect on the growth and physiological properties of P. forbesii, indicating that the species has strong
tolerance to Zn and Cr stress. Alternatively, high concentrations of Cd, Ni, and Cu seriously affected plant
growth and development, resulting in leaf chlorosis and even death, and therefore may have a serious
negative impact on the growth of P. forbesii. However, activity levels of some antioxidant enzymes and
osmotic regulatory substances remained high, indicating that P. forbesii resisted heavy metal stress by
regulating physiological and biochemical metabolism to a certain extent. Furthermore, principal component
analysis and membership function were used to comprehensively evaluate P. forbesii resistance to PTEs.
These analyses revealed that P. forbesii exhibits distinct sensitivities and physiological responses to different
PTEs and suggested that the resistance to ve PTEs in decreasing order is Zn > Cr > Cd > Cu > Ni. These
results provide a theoretical basis for the future application of P. forbesii in environments with heavy metal
pollution and may expand its practical utilization.
Introduction
Pollution by potentially toxic elements (PTEs) refers to the phenomenon in which the contents of metal
elements in the soil exceed background values as a result of human activities, such as mining, industrial
discharge, and overuse of chemical agents in agricultural production. This pollution is widespread and
detrimental, seriously threatening sustainable ecological development as well as human health through
natural biogeochemical cycles. Cadmium (Cd), zinc (Zn), copper (Cu), chromium (Cr), and nickel (Ni) are
common, representative PTEs presenting ecological risks in cities. Although some of these PTEs, such as Cu,
Zn, Cr, and Ni, are necessary for normal plant growth and development, excessive accumulation has toxic
effects (Ghori et al. 2019). Other PTEs, such as Cd, are not regarded as nutrients and pose a threat to plant
growth after safe values are exceeded (Shanying et al. 2017). Plants have evolved complex mechanisms for
detoxication and protection in response to PTE stress. Plant resistance to PTEs mainly includes two
strategies: avoidance and tolerance mechanisms (Toppi and Gabbrielli, 1999). The rst strategy is to avoid
PTE accumulation in sensitive tissues, reduce PTE absorption through the excretion of organic acids and
carbohydrates, and accumulate excessive PTEs in roots, exoplasm, and vacuoles (Meharg, 1993). To protect
against oxidative damage caused by PTEs, the second strategy is to promote the chelation of plant chelates
(Pcs), metallothioneins, organic acids, and proteins with PTEs in plants and activate ion transporters to
remove excess PTE ions from the protoplasm, in addition to activating and enhancing the activity of
antioxidant systems to remove metal-induced reactive oxygen species (ROS) (Hall et al. 2002; Jozefczak et
al. 2014; Gillet et al. 2010). Recent research on PTEs has mainly focused on crops and hyperaccumulators. In
the practical application of metal-resistant plants, ornamental plants have lower biosecurity risks than those
of crops and better landscape effects than those of most hyperaccumulators, with benets related to
Page 3/28
beautication, protection, and restoration of the environment. However, studies of the resistance and repair of
ornamental plants under heavy metal stress are scarce.
Primula is a well-known ornamental ower worldwide. There are more than 500 species in the world, mostly
distributed in the moist and cool regions of the northern hemisphere (Richards, 2003). China is a major center
of Primula biodiversity with approximately 300 species (Hu, 1990). Primula forbesii Franch. is a biennial
ower abundant in many locales across Sichuan and Yunnan Provinces in China; it was introduced to Europe
by the seed company Vilmorin in 1891 (Richards, 2003). The species exhibits desirable horticultural traits,
such as a long owering season, plentiful owers, and a pleasant fragrance (Jia, 2014). In addition, it has
excellent resistance to environmental stresses. Studies have shown that P. forbesii has a high tolerance to
salt and cadmium stress in the soil, although it has not been regarded as hyperaccumulator of PTEs (Jia,
2020a; Jia 2020b). Hydroponic culture enables direct measurements of plant physiological parameters and
metabolism in a time-dependent manner, and this method is widely used to determine the phytotoxic effects
of PTEs (Kopittke, 2010).
In this study, taking P. forbesii as the research object, the effects of various concentrations of Cd, Ni, Cr, Cu,
and Zn on seedlings under hydroponic conditions were evaluated. Additionally, we measured growth and
physiological indexes as well as heavy metal contents in the above-ground and underground parts of the
plants. A principal component analysis (PCA) and membership function were used to analyze the resistance
of P. forbesii to Cd, Ni, Cr, Cu, and Zn stress. This study provides a theoretical basis for the future application
of P. forbesii in areas with heavy metal pollution and has the potential to expand the practical applications of
the species.
Materials And Methods
Plant material and cultivation conditions
P. forbesii seeds were collected from plants grown in the Sichuan Agricultural University greenhouse. The
seeds were sown in June 2019, grown in pots in a controlled-environment growth chamber programmed for
16/8 h at 20°C/15°C day/night. After 60 days, seedlings with 5–6 true leaves were transferred to a
hydroponic container of 95 mm in diameter and 140 mm in height, which was lled with 600 mL of 1/8
modied Hoagland nutrient solution (147.594 mg/L Ca(NO3)2·4H2O, 63.194 mg/L KNO3, 17.011 mg/L
KH2PO4, 6.161 mg/L MgSO4·7H2O, 0.040 mg/L MnSO4·4H2O, 0.012 mg/L Na2MoO4·2H2O, 0.029 mg/L
ZnSO4·7H2O, 0.009 mg/L CuSO4·5H2O, 0.695 mg/L FeSO4·7H2O, 9.356 mg/L EDTA-Na2, 3.478 mg/L H3BO3).
The pH of the solution was adjusted to 5.8 ± 0.1 with NaOH as required. Each container was used for one
plant and the outer surface was wrapped with tinfoil to shade the roots. Hydroponic culture was carried out in
a culture room set at 20°C, 16/8 h light/dark cycle, and a light intensity of 4000 Lux. The hydroponic solution
was changed every 7 days. After 2 weeks of growth, healthy and uniform seedlings were selected and
transplanted to the hydroponic solution, which was replaced with new 1/8 modied Hoagland nutrient
solution containing either CdCl2·2.5H2O, CrCl3·6H2O, CuSO4·5H2O, NiCl2·2H2O, or ZnSO4·7H2O at
concentrations of 10, 50, 100, or 200 µmol/L for each PTE. The PTE-containing hydroponic solutions were
Page 4/28
changed at 7-day intervals up to day 21. There were 12 seedlings per treatment and three biological
replicates. Plants grown in hydroponic solution without the addition of PTEs were used as controls.
Morphological observation and measurements
Morphological changes of plants were observed under different PTEs every day. After cultivation for 21 days,
images were obtained to record the plant morphology. Plant height, crown width, and root length were
measured using Vernier calipers. The fresh weights (FW) of the above-ground parts and roots were
determined using an electronic balance. Then, the dry weight (DW) of the above-ground parts and roots was
determined after samples were dried in a vacuum oven at 105°C for 30 min and at 80°C to a constant weight.
Chlorophyll content
Chlorophyll (Chl) contents were extracted from 0.2 g of fresh leaf samples in the dark for 24 h, following the
method of Wellburn and Lichtenthaler (1984). Pigment was extracted in a mixed solution of acetone: ethanol 
= 2:1 (V:V) and centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min. The absorbance of the extracts was measured at
wavelengths of 663 and 645 nm for Chlorophyll a (Chl a) and Chlorophyll b (Chl b), respectively, by using a
UV/visible spectrophotometer (BioMate 3S; Thermo, Waltham, MA, USA). According to the equation Chl
(mg/L) = (20.2 × OD645 + 8.02 × OD663) × (V/W × 1000), the total chlorophyll (Chl T) contents were
calculated. The ratios of Chl a to Chl b were calculated.
Detection of soluble sugar and soluble protein
Anthrone colorimetry was adopted to determine the content of soluble sugar (SS) (Li, 2002). The soluble
protein (SP) content was determined according to the method described by Bradford (1976), using bovine
serum albumin (BSA) as the standard protein and Coomassie brilliant blue G-520 as the staining dye.
Detection of malondialdehyde and hydrogen peroxide
contents
The methods of Health and Packer (1968) were followed to estimate malondialdehyde (MDA) contents. The
hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) contents were evaluated by previously established procedures (Wang et al. 2013).
Assays of antioxidant enzyme activity
Fresh leaf samples (0.2 g) were ground in a precooled mortar and pestle. Then, the samples were
homogenized by using phosphate buffer (0.05 mol/L, pH 7.8). The homogenate was centrifuged at 10000
rpm for 20 min at 4°C and the supernatant was collected. Levels of antioxidant enzymes, including
superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), catalase (CAT), ascorbic acid (ASA), and glutathione (GSH),
were evaluated by the methods of Hess and Foster (1976), Shah (2001), Aebi (1984), Law (1983), and Yang
et al. (2018).
Measurements of mineral nutrient accumulation and PTEs
Plants were carefully separated from containers and rinsed with deionized water 5–6 times before element
content analyses. The fresh leaves and roots were dried in a vacuum oven at 110°C for 30 min and at 80°C to
a constant weight, ground into a ne powder, and sieved through a 2 mm nylon mesh screen. The samples
Page 5/28
(0.2 g) were kept overnight in a mixture of concentrated nitric acid (HNO3) and perchloric acid (HClO4) (V:V = 
4:1). Afterwards, the digestion process was completed on hot plate until a clear solution was obtained. The
solution was allowed to cool to room temperature and then increased to 50 mL with ultrapure water. Contents
of mineral nutrients (Kalium, K; Calcium, Ca; Magnesium, Mg; Manganese, Mn) and PTEs (Zn, Cr, Cd, Cu, and
Ni) in the decomposed samples were measured by inductively coupled plasma atomic emission
spectroscopy ICP-MS (Agilent 7700; Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA).
Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 19.0 for Windows. Data are expressed as the mean ± 
standard deviation (S.D.). Least Signicant Difference (LSD) tests were used to determine whether
differences between treatments were signicant (P < 0.05). Finally, a combination of a principal component
analysis (PCA) and membership function (MF) method was used for a comprehensive evaluation of the
effects of ve PTEs on P. forbesii.
Results
Plant growth performance
During the rst 7 days of PTE hydroponic stress, the phenotypes of P. forbesii plants were essentially similar
to those of the control plants (Fig. 1). After 11 days of treatment, groups treated with high concentrations of
Cu, Cd, and Ni (200 µmol/L) began to show symptoms of toxicity, e.g., the leaves turned yellow and wilted
slightly; however, the symptoms in the Cu treatment group were mild. It took 16 days for groups treated with
high concentrations of Cr and Zn (200 µmol/L) to show mild symptoms of toxicity. After 21 days of stress,
the toxicity symptoms were severe under the 100–200 µmol/L Ni treatment; a large number of leaves were
withered and the whole plants became soft and died. In general, the lowest concentrations (10 µmol/L) of
PTEs had no signicant effect on the growth of seedlings of P. forbesii, while at the highest metal
concentration of 200 µmol/L, plant growth was strongly inhibited, especially in plants treated with Ni,
followed by Cd and Cu, with weaker effects of Cr and Zn stress.
After 21 days of exposure to a medium dose of PTEs, various plant properties were measured, i.e., height,
crown width, root length, fresh and dry biomass production of shoots and roots (Table 1). At a PTE
concentration of 10 µmol/L, the plant height and crown width after treatment with each of the ve metals
were not signicantly different from those in the control, consistent with the observed phenotypic
characteristics. Most of the PTEs began to reduce the plant height and crown width signicantly when the
concentration was 50–100 µmol/L, except for Cr at 50 µmol/L, which increased the plant height and crown
width. Similar results were obtained for root length. Lower concentrations of Cr (10 µmol/L) and Zn (50
µmol/L) promoted root elongation. Except 10 µmol/L Zn, most other concentrations of the PTEs led to
remarkable decreases in dry and fresh weights of both aboveground and underground parts. In particular, the
dry weights of plants treated with 200 µmol/L PTEs decreased signicantly by 55–70% as compared with
those of the control.

Chlorophyll content
As shown in Fig. 2, as the concentrations of Cd, Ni, and Cu increased, Chl a and ChlT contents showed similar
patterns characterized by an initial increase and subsequent decrease. Under Cd exposure, the content of Chl
a reached the maximum value of 1.8410 mg/g at a concentration of 50 µmol/L. In Ni solutions, the contents
of Chl a and ChlT were highest when the concentration was 10 µmol/L and were 12.51% and 5.59% higher
than those of the control, respectively. Alternatively, under treatment with Cr and Zn solutions, the contents of
Chl a and ChlT decreased gradually as the concentration increased. When the concentrations of Cr and Zn
reached 200 µmol/L, the contents of Chl a and ChlT were the lowest. Compared with those in the control, the
contents of Chl a and ChlT under Cr treatment were 38.81% and 33.70% lower, respectively, while those under
Zn treatment were 38.96% and 33.21% lower, respectively.
Chl b showed a gradual increase as the Cd concentration increased. Conversely, under treatment with Ni, Cr,
and Zn solutions, the content of Chl b decreased gradually as the solution concentration increased. With the
increase in the Cu solution concentration, the content of Chl b increased initially and then decreased. As the
concentrations of Cd, Cu, and Zn increased, the Chl a/b ratio in P. forbesii decreased gradually. When the
stress concentration reached the maximum value of 200 µmol/L, Chl a/b decreased to the lowest value (i.e., it
decreased by 32.62%, 23.79%, and 20.80% compared with that of the control, respectively). Under treatment
with Ni and Cr solutions, Chl a/b increased initially and then decreased as the concentration increased. Based
on the observed changes in the Chl content, responses to different heavy metals under the same
concentration differed signicantly.
Changes in plant osmotic regulation
As shown in Fig. 3, the SS content gradually increased as the Cd concentration increased and reached the
maximum value when the concentration of Cd reached 200 µmol/L, which increased by 98.45% compared
with that of the control group. In addition, with increases in the Ni, Cr, and Zn concentrations, the SS content
of P. forbesii increased initially and then decreased. Under treatment with the Cu solution, the SS content of P.
forbesii decreased rst and then increased as the Cu concentration increased, and the differences were
signicant. When the Cu concentration was between 10 and 50 µmol/L, the SS content was signicantly
lower than that of the control. When the Cu concentration was 200 µmol/L, the SS content was 73.26% higher
than that of the control group.

Under treatments with various concentrations of Cd, Cu, and Zn, the SP content tended to increase initially
and then decrease as the metal concentrations increased. The increase in the SP content under Cd was
signicantly higher than those under Cu and Zn, and all SP contents reached the maximum value when the
stress concentration was 10 µmol/L. Compared with those in the control group, SP contents increased by
30.16%, 13.27%, and 14.65%, respectively. Under Ni treatment, the SP content of P. forbesii decreased initially
and then increased as the Ni concentration increased; the SP content of P. forbesii increased as the Cr
concentration increased. When the Cr concentration was 200 µmol/L, the SP content was highest, i.e., 38.41%
higher than that of the control.
Determination of membrane lipid peroxidation
As shown in Fig. 4, under treatment with Cd, Ni, Cr, and Zn solutions, the MDA content in P. forbesii leaves
decreased initially and then increased as the concentrations increased. The MDA content reached a
maximum value at 200 µmol/L. Of note, under treatment with Cd and Ni solutions, the nal MDA content was
signicantly higher than that of the control (i.e., 51.42% and 64.47% higher, respectively). Under treatment
with Cr and Zn solutions, the MDA content under the highest concentration in P. forbesii did not differ
signicantly from that of the control. In addition, as the Cu concentration increased, the MDA content showed
a continuous upward trend and was signicantly higher than that of the control at all concentrations.
With the gradual increase of the concentration of the ve PTEs, the H2O2 content generally showed a
continuous upward trend. However, under treatment with Cd, Cr, Cu, and Zn solutions, when the concentration
was lowest (10 µmol/L), the H2O2 content was signicantly higher than that of the control, whereas there was
no signicant difference among metals. When the stress concentration was higher than 10 µmol/L, the
increase in the H2O2 content slowed. However, under Ni treatment, when the stress concentration was
between 0 and 50 µmol/L, the H2O2 content increased slightly. When the stress concentrations were ≥  100
µmol/L, the H2O2 content increased signicantly and reached the highest value when the Ni concentration
was 200 µmol/L (i.e., 98.70% higher than that of the control). The H2O2 contents under Cd and Ni treatments
(200 µmol/L) were signicantly higher than those under Cr, Cu, and Zn treatments.
Determination of antioxidant system indicators
As summarized in Fig. 5, as the concentrations of Cd, Ni, Cr, and Cu increased, the SOD activity in P. forbesii
showed an initial increase, followed by a decrease. The peak value was reached at concentrations of 100
µmol/L, 100 µmol/L, 50 µmol/L, and 10 µmol/L, respectively, and these values were signicantly higher than
those of the control.
Furthermore, the POD activity increased with as the concentrations of Cd and Cu increased and reached the
peak values at 200 µmol/L. The peak value under Cu treatment was signicantly higher than that under Cd
treatment. In addition, similar POD activities were observed with the increases in Ni, Cr, and Zn
concentrations, with an initial increase and subsequent decrease, and the increases in POD activity under Ni
treatment were signicantly higher than those for Cr and Zn.
As the Cd, Cu, and Zn concentrations increased, the CAT activity in leaves decreased initially and then
increased. When the Cd concentration was 50 µmol/L, CAT activity was the highest and was signicantly
Page 10/28
higher than that of the control. While when Cu and Zn concentrations reached 200 µmol/L, CAT activities
reached peak levels; however, only 200 µmol/L Cu resulted in signicantly higher CAT activity than that of the
control.
With increases in Cd and Cu concentrations, the ASA content of the plant showed a gradual upward trend.
Under the same concentration, there was no signicant difference in ASA contents between Cd and Cu
treatments. When the concentration was 200 µmol/L, the ASA content reached a peak, which increased by
47.96% and 42.91% compared with that of the control, respectively. Furthermore, the ASA contents of the
leaves increased initially and then decreased as the Ni, Cr, and Zn concentrations increased. ASA contents
reached the maximum value at 50 µmol/L under both Ni and Cr treatments and at 100 µmol/L for Zn; these
maximum values were signicantly different from those in the control.
With the continuous increases in Cd, Ni, and Cr concentrations, the GSH contents gradually increased and
reached peak values at the highest concentration (200 µmol/L), which were signicantly higher than those in
the control. In addition, the GSH contents in leaves rst increased and then decreased with increases in Cu
and Zn concentrations. When the Cu concentration was 50 µmol/L, the GSH content reached peak values,
which were 2.5 times higher than that in the control. When the Zn concentration was 100 µmol/L, the GSH
content was the highest, (a 1-fold increase over that in the control).
Accumulation of heavy metals and metabolism of essential
minerals
The PTE concentrations in shoots and roots of P. forbesii were measured (Fig. 6). PTE uptake in both parts
increased due to PTE stress. The maximum uptake of Cd, Ni, and Cu in roots was observed at 200 µmol/L,
whereas the peak uptake of Cr and Zn in roots was observed at 100 µmol/L, which then decreased
signicantly at higher concentrations. The accumulation of PTEs in the shoots under Cd, Ni, Cr, and Zn
showed similar trends, characterized by a gradual increase with peak values at 200 µmol/L (100 times, 50
times, 2 times, and 2.4 times higher than those of the control, respectively).
The inuence of PTE exposure on the shoot and root contents of representative essential mineral nutrients,
i.e., K, Ca, Mg, and Mn, were analyzed at a PTE concentration of 10 µmol/L in hydroponic solution. Although
the concentrations of these essential mineral nutrients did not change signicantly under many conditions, a
13.21% decrease in the shoot Mg content and a 25.27% increase in the root Ca content were observed in Ni-
treated plants. Both decreases and increases in nutrients in shoots and roots were observed in some cases. A
decrease in the shoot K content was observed in Ni-, Cr-, and Cu-treated plants. Cd and Cu in roots
signicantly reduced the plant uptake of Mg, K, and Mn. Ca and Mn contents in the shoots increased in Ni-
and Zn-treated plants.

Discussion
Our results suggested that exposure to different PTEs led to different degrees of growth retardation and
physiological response in P. forbesii. Integrating various physiological indicators revealed that the toxicity of
the ve PTEs in P. forbesii decreased in the order of Ni > Cu > Cd > Cr > Zn, consistent with results obtained in
barley (Hg > Pb > Cu > Cd > Cr > Ni > Zn) and Chlorella vulgaris (Hg > Cu > Cd > Fe > Cr > Zn > Ni > Co > Mn)
(Nieboer and Richardson, 1980). However, it is noteworthy that Ni treatment resulted in the most severe
growth defects in P. forbesii in this study, whereas this metal was reported to have relatively low toxicity in
barley and Chlorella. However, this observation is consistent with results for physiological responses to PTEs
in Jatropha curcas (Yamada et al. 2018). Ni is an essential trace element in plants and contributes to plant
growth and development, morphology, physiology, and biochemistry (Li et al. 2003). However, excessive Ni
will disrupt the normal balance of plant hormones, inhibit plant growth and development, cause plant
phenotypic deformities, and destroy the roots (Ali et al. 2015). Since the effects of Ni toxicity on plant
physiology depend on many factors, such as growth state and cultivation conditions, more detailed studies
of the physiological processes of P. forbesii after Ni treatment are necessary.
Under exposure to low concentrations (10–50 µmol/L) of ve PTEs, hydroponically grown P. forbesii showed
normal growth and even showed increases in root length (10 µmol/L), crown width, and plant height (50
µmol/L) under low concentrations of Cr. Inversely, under high concentrations of Cd, Ni, and Cu (100–200
µmol/L), plant growth and biomass were seriously affected; the leaves began to wilt, curl, yellow, and even
die. Although high concentration concentrations (100–200 µmol/L) of Zn and Cr also caused some damage
to plants, the degree of damage was minor, and the plants exhibited normal growth.
Photosynthesis is the most basic and vital physiological activity of plants. The photosynthetic capacity of
plants is closely related to plant health. The chlorophyll content is the most intuitive indicator of the strength
of the photosynthetic capacity of plants. In this study, low concentrations of Cd, Ni, and Cu (10 µmol/L)
increased the chlorophyll content of plants signicantly, thereby enhancing photosynthesis and improving
plant resistance to PTEs. In contrast, the chlorophyll contents under Cr and Zn stress decreased gradually,
showing a negative correlation with the Cr and Zn concentrations. This may be due to the inhibition of ROS
under stress, leading to a signicant decrease in the photosynthetic capacity and downregulation of PSII via
non-photochemical quenching (Huang et al. 2018).
Page 15/28
SS plays signicant roles in plant defense responses to environmental stress (Gill et al. 2001). The SS
content of plants increased signicantly under Cd stress, indicating that the plants responded positively to Cd
stress and showed increased sugar accumulation to regulate the water potential balance and prevent the
leakage of plant cell contents. Similar phenomena have been observed in wheat subjected to Zn stress, and
this response is considered a stress adaptation in plants (Su et al. 2017; Li et al. 2013). Under 10–50 µmol/L
Cu, the SS content in P. forbesii plants was lower than that of the control, consistent with the response of
Cinnamomum camphora under the same concentration of Cu (Zhou et al. 2019). This may be explained by
the ability of plants under Cu stress to break down SS to regulate the osmotic balance and maintain normal
growth under low concentrations of metals. However, under Ni, Cr, and Zn stress, the SS contents of plants
increased initially and then decreased. This may be because low levels of heavy metal stress hindered SS
metabolic pathways or the signal transmission pathways, resulting in elevated SS contents. However,
osmotic regulation in plants was highly damaged under high concentrations of metals. To maintain the
osmotic balance, plants consume large quantities of SS. The source of SS synthesis was hindered by stress,
leading to a signicant decrease in the SS content. The SP content in organisms is an important indicator of
reversible and irreversible changes in metabolism and can respond to exogenous stimuli, such as heavy
metal stress (Singh et al. 2003). Three general trends have been observed in SP variation under in response
to ve PTEs, characterized by an initial increase followed by a decrease, a continual increase, and an initial
decrease followed by an increase. Therefore, the increase in the SP content under low concentrations of
heavy metals might be an adaptation to heavy metal stress. Plants could reduce stress-induced damage by
synthesizing specic proteins that bind to heavy metals (Yang et al. 2018). Under high concentrations of
heavy metals, the decrease in SP content may be caused by the combination of heavy metal enrichment in
the plants, causing damage to the SP, or may be explained by the heavy metal-induced inhibition of protein
synthesis, resulting in a signicant decrease in the SP content (Singh et al. 2003).
Plant cells produce high levels of ROS and free radicals under metal stress, which can destroy intracellular
macromolecules and lead to membrane lipid peroxidation (Srivastava et al. 2007). With the production of
excessive free radicals, MDA, a cytotoxic product of lipid peroxidation, increased signicantly. The contents
of MDA in P. forbesii varied substantially in response to the ve PTEs, indicating that stress could inuence
MDA accumulation. High concentrations of Cd, Ni (100–200 µmol/L), and Cu (10–200 µmol/L) led to high
levels of MDA accumulation, and the plants showed a high level of antioxidant stress, resulting in serious
oxidative damage and a lack of self-repair. This is consistent with previous results for the response of
Koelreuteria paniculata to Cd stress (Yang et al. 2018). However, Cr stress (10–100µmol/L) reduced the MDA
content signicantly, and the antioxidant system functioned eciently and repaired the stress-induced
oxidative damage. There was no signicant difference in the MDA content between Zn treatments and the
control, indicating that the oxidative damage in P. forbesii was minor and the membrane lipid peroxidation
was in a controllable state. These ndings differ from those of a study of pigeon pea (Madhava et al. 2000).
P. forbesii had strong adaptability under Zn and Cr stress and maintained normal metabolic processes via its
repair mechanism.
In this study, the H2O2 content of P. forbesii tended to increase under PTE stress, indicating that the species
showed an oxidative stress response to improve survival, resulting in the production and accumulation of
H2O2, consistent with the results of Coronopus didymus under Cd stress (Sidhu et al. 2017). The
Page 16/28
accumulation of H2O2 in plant tissues can cause oxidative damage to plant cells and even plant death
(Wrzaczek et al. 2013), which may be due to the effects of elevated ROS induced by heavy metals on
membrane lipid peroxidation, thereby interfering with the oxidative repair function of cells and causing
irreparable damage and H2O2 accumulation.
To remove the large amount of ROS produced by lipid peroxidation, several antioxidant defense systems are
activated. SOD, POD, and CAT play crucial roles (Radoti et al. 2000; Matés et al. 2000). In this study, SOD
activity continued to increase under Zn stress, indicating that the antioxidant enzyme system of plants
promoted the removal of ROS (Sidhu et al. 2017). Under Cd, Ni, and Cu stress, the SOD activity in P. forbesii
increased initially and then decreased rapidly, indicating that when the metal concentration exceeded a
certain threshold, the antioxidant enzyme system began to fail and accumulated ROS continued to produce
toxic effects, consistent with previous results for Koelreuteria paniculata (Yang et al. 2018). In addition, the
trends in POD activity in P. forbesii under Cd and Cu stresses were consistent with previous results for Mentha
piperita and Pistia stratiotes (Li et al. 2013; Khair et al. 2020). However, the changes of POD activity under Ni,
Cr, and Zn stress showed an initial increase and subsequent decrease, consistent with results for Zostera
japonica (Lin et al. 2016). CAT activity in P. forbesii under Cr and Ni stress showed a similar trend (i.e., an
early increase followed by a decrease), indicating that under low concentration of Cr and Ni stress, leaves of
P. forbesii could activate CAT activity, thereby enhancing lipid peroxidation in leaves to remove ROS, while
high concentrations of heavy metals would harm the plant cell defense system, consistent with previous
results (Hou et al. 2007; Singh et al. 2020). It is worth mentioning that although a high concentration of Cr led
to a decrease in CAT activity, the rate of decrease was relatively slow and CAT activity was still higher than
that of the control, indicating that the catalase system of P. forbesii could still effectively control the level of
ROS; however, slight inhibition in CAT activity was detected.
ASA has an important role in ROS quenching and ascorbate-glutathione pathways of antioxidant metabolism
(Noctor and Foyer, 1998). The ASA content increased signicantly as the Cd and Cu concentrations
increased, which promoted the removal of ROS and maintained the normal growth of P. forbesii. The
production of a large amount of ASA not only contributes to heavy metal stress resistance but also affects
the energy supply for plant growth and development. Therefore, the role of ASA to plant resistance needs to
be studied further. Under Ni, Cr, and Zn stress, the ASA content increased and then decreased and was
consistently higher than that of the control, indicating that ASA can effectively promote the removal of ROS
in plants and maintain the balance of redox levels in cells under low concentrations of heavy metals.
Nevertheless, as the concentration gradually increased, the level of ROS exceeded the capacity of ASA. With
the consumption of a large amount of ASA, its concentration decreased signicantly, indicating that the ROS
scavenging pathway began to use ASA as a substrate (Hojati et al. 2017); however, the nal content was
slightly higher than that of the control, indicating that the synthesis of ASA was not inhibited, supporting the
high tolerance of P. forbesii to Ni, Cr, and Zn stress. In addition, the effectiveness of plant oxidative defense
systems can be measured by the levels of non-enzymatic antioxidants, such as GSH (Ahmad, 2012). Under
Cd, Ni, and Cr stress, the GSH content in P. forbesii continued to increase, which improved the ROS and free
radical scavenging ability of P. forbesii. Plants could use GSH to alleviate stress-induced damage. These
ndings indicated that P. forbesii had good tolerance to Cd and Cu stress, consistent with previous ndings
for tobacco seedlings (Ye et al. 2016).
Page 17/28
Ion homeostasis refers to the balance of nutrient elements in plant tissues, which is an essential condition for
normal growth and development (Rouached, 2011; Rouached et al. 2010). Once the heavy metal content
exceeds a threshold value, the absorption and transport of essential mineral nutrients are altered, thus
leading to an imbalance in nutrient elements in plants and eventually produce toxic effects (Matraszek et al.
2016a). In this study, notable abnormalities in the abundance of essential mineral nutrients were found after
Cd exposure, including an increase in the Ca content in the shoots. Cd can gradually accumulate in plants
through cation channels or other element transporters and interfere with normal photosynthesis (Matraszek
et al. 2016b). The absorption of some nutrients is strengthened by self-protection mechanisms. For example,
the absorption of Ca can prevent the replacement of Ca by Cd in proteins, thus alleviating the inhibition of
photosynthesis induced by Cd (Zhang et al. 2014). The Mg and K contents in the Ni treatment were
signicantly lower than those in the control, and the Mg and K contents in the Cu treatment also showed a
downward trend. In addition, the Mg and K contents in Zn and Cd treatments showed no signicant change,
suggesting that P. forbesii had stronger resistance to Zn and Cd than to Ni and Cu. This is consistent with the
results obtained by a PCA. From the perspective of heavy metal accumulation, the accumulation of Ni in
different parts of P. forbesii was greater in the roots than in the shoots, and the Ni contents in both parts of
the plant increased as the Ni concentration increased, consistent with results for peppermint (Khair et al.
2020). Heavy metal accumulation in the underground part of P. forbesii increased signicantly as the Cd and
Cu concentrations increased, consistent with the results of studies f Celosia argentea under Cd stress (Liu et
al. 2020) and Cinnamomum camphora under Cu stress (Zhou et al. 2019). Our quantitative results for P.
forbesii indicated that the absorption of Cr and Zn in the roots of the plant was limited. The decrease in
absorption under high heavy metal concentrations may be due to the loss and death of some roots of P.
forbesii, resulting in a decrease in roots available for absorption.
Conclusion
Phenotypic and physiological indexes of P. forbesii varied substantially in response to ve PTEs. By
qualitative observations and quantitative analyses, including a PCA and membership function evaluation, the
mechanisms underlying the response of P. forbesii to heavy metal stress were claried from a physiological
point of view. Our preliminary analysis showed that P. forbesii resistance to ve PTEs decreased in the
following order: Zn > Cr > Cd > Cu > Ni. Heavy metal pollution in soil is a serious issue, and various plants have
been selected to repair the landscape and improve the soil ecology. As a fragrant plant in the genus Primula,
P. forbesii not only has certain adaptability to heavy metal soil but also has landscape value; accordingly, it
can be used as a component of phytoremediation strategies for soil contaminated by heavy metals. This
study provides a reference for the application of P. forbesii to environments polluted by heavy metals.
Nevertheless, this experiment adopted the hydroponic method, and it is necessary to verify the heavy metal
resistance of P. forbesii in soil by culture experiments.
