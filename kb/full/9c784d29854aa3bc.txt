Worldwide, consumption of ultra-processed foods (UPFs) is increasing in parallel with a rising incidence of obesity, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, and related cardiometabolic complications. Among the many features of UPFs that may drive these outcomes, industrial thermal processing is a defining characteristic that promotes the formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs) and other food neo-contaminants, including acrylamide, furan, 5-hydroxymethylfurfural, heterocyclic amines, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, 3-monochloropropane-1,2-diol, and glycidol. This review evaluates the hypothesis that AGEs and thermally generated neo-contaminants contribute significantly to the metabolic and toxicological effects of UPFs.
