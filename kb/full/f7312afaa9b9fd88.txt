Oil spillage is a persistent environmental challenge in the Niger Delta, where agricultural communities depend heavily on soil resources for food production and rural livelihoods. This study assessed the effects of oil spillage on selected soil quality indicators in agricultural farmlands in Ogbodo-Isiokpo Community, Ikwerre Local Government Area, Rivers State, Nigeria. A field-based comparative approach was adopted, involving reconnaissance surveys, identification of oil-spill-affected locations, and laboratory analysis of soil samples collected from polluted and unpolluted control sites. Soil quality was assessed using pH, organic matter, nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium as key indicators. The results revealed marked differences between contaminated and control soils.
