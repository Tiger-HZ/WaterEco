Abstract Coastal ecosystems play a major role in global carbon cycling and storage, yet the contribution of calcifying macroalgal habitats, such as coralline algal beds (CABs; rhodolith and maerl beds), remains poorly understood. Because carbon cycling in CABs is governed by community productivity and calcification, which exert opposing effects on seawater carbonate chemistry, understanding the balance between these processes is essential for determining whether CABs function as net carbon sinks or sources. Yet, community‐level measurements remain scarce, and the environmental and biological drivers of CAB carbon balance are largely unknown.
