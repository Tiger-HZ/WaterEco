ABSTRACT Over the past three decades, tropical forest governance has increasingly shifted from state‐centered regulation toward hybrid arrangements in which private companies, certification bodies, and civil society organizations jointly shape sustainability standards. This study examines how forestry companies operationalize sustainable development in concession management by comparing PALLISCO and GRUMCAM in Cameroon. Drawing on Political Ecology and Legitimacy Theory, the study addresses three questions: (1) how companies interpret sustainable development; (2) whether voluntary certification produces more substantive outcomes than legality‐based compliance; and (3) under what conditions certification risks becoming symbolic.
