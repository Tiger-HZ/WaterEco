Pesticide exceedance—concentrations surpassing regulatory or health-based thresholds—is a key criterion for assessing pesticide use acceptability. Global agricultural pesticide use doubled to 3.7 million tonnes in 2022, yet existing frameworks may underestimate breach frequency. Comparisons between FOCUS Step 3/4 predictions and field measurements show scenario outputs exceeded at 23–31% of monitoring sites, while monitoring data document aquatic-life benchmark exceedances in over half of agricultural streams worldwide. This review examines the interconnected data and modeling frameworks in the exceedance assessment chain, identifying structural mechanisms introducing downward bias at each stage. We evaluate use databases, spatial products, covariates, remote sensing, fate/transport models, monitoring, dietary exposure, machine learning, and mixture methods—from an exceedance-oriented perspective. Four compounding sources of underestimation emerge: spatiotemporal aggregation of use data; limited representation of preferential flow and storm-driven transport in regulatory models; insufficient monitoring temporal resolution for capturing short-duration peaks; and exclusion of transformation products and mixture interactions. Field biological assessments using SPEAR have independently documented invertebrate community degradation at concentrations at or below regulatory thresholds, corroborating the evidence. Strengthening human and ecosystem health protection would benefit from shifting from average-exposure-oriented toward probabilistic, exceedance-centered frameworks that explicitly represent peak dynamics, spatial heterogeneity, and mixture toxicity across the full exposure pathway.
