Effect of spacings and nutrient levels on yield and 
economics of finger millet (Eleusine coracana L.) 
 
Pradheepa S, AA Choudhary, Nishigandha R Mairan, Shital S Pahurkar and 
Devyani S Ilhe 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9d.6514 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment was conducted at Agronomy farm, College of Agriculture, Nagpur (M.S.), India. The 
experiment was laid out in factorial randomized block design with three replications. The treatments 
consisted of three spacings viz., S1 – 30 x 10 cm, S 2 – 45 x 5 cm and S 3 – 30-60 x 10 cm, and three nutrient 
levels viz., F1 – 25:20:00 kg, F2 – 30:20:00 kg and F3– 35:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1. The soil of the experimental 
site was clayey in texture, slightly alkaline in reaction (pH – 7.7), low in available nitrogen (271.62 kg ha-1), 
medium in available phosphorous (22.18 kg ha-1) and high in available potassium (384.15 kg ha-1). Result of 
the experiment revealed that among different spacings, the yield attributes were significantly higher with the 
paired row spacing of 30-60 x 10 cm. However, grain, straw and biological yield and gross monetary returns, 
net monetary returns and B:C ratio were significantly higher with the spacing of 45 x 5 cm. Among nutrient 
levels, application of 35:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 recorded more length of panicle, panicle weight, grain and straw 
yield plant-1, grain, straw and biological yield and gross monetary returns, net monetary returns and B:C 
ratio. Interaction effect between fertilizer level and spacing was found non-significant in all the parameters. 
 
Keywords: Finger millet, spacings, nutrient levels, yield attributes, yield 
 
Introduction 
Eleusine coracana, commonly known as finger millet or African millet, is one of the most widely 
cultivated millets and ranks fourth in importance after sorghum, pearl millet and foxtail millet. In 
India, finger millet is popularly known as ragi and is referred to by different regional names such 
as Nachani (Hindi and Marathi), Kezhvaragu (Tamil), Ularly (Telugu) and Mandia (Oriya) 
(Chandra et al. 2016) [2]. 
Finger millet is an important crop for ensuring food and nutritional security in semi -arid regions 
and is well adapted to a wide range of environments, from the high Himalayan hills to the coastal 
plains. It produces both nutritious grain and palatable straw for livestock and is recognized for its 
strong climate resilience. Nutritional composition of finger millet per 100gm is; phosphorus (130–
283 mg), potassium (430–490 mg), magnesium (78–201 mg), calcium (162–398 mg), sodium (49 
mg), zinc (2.3 mg), iron (3.3–14.39 mg), manganese (17.61–48.43 mg) and copper (0.47 mg) (Rai 
and Reddy, 2024). 
Recently awareness on small millets has been increased. The year 2023 was also declared as the 
international years of millets by the United Nations General Assembly [UNGA]. This declaration 
also paved way for the researchers around the globe to conduct various research on small millet. 
This announcement also opened the way to raise public awareness about the nutritional and health 
benefits of these millets. 
Despite its nutritional and agronomic importance, the productivity of finger millet remains 
comparatively low, mainly due to inadequate crop management, poor plant establishment, 
imbalanced nutrient application and inappropriate plant geometry. Among the various agronomic 
practices, spacing plays a critical role in determining plant population, canopy development, light 
interception, nutrient utilization and ultimately yield. Optimum spacing provides sufficient space 
for individual plants to develop their r oot and shoot systems, whereas excessively close spacing 
may increase competition among plants for light, moisture and nutrients. Conversely, wider 
spacing may reduce plant population and result in inefficient utilization of available land.

Materials and Methods 
A field trial was conducted at the P.G. Research farm, College of 
Agriculture, Nagpur, during kharif 2025 to study the effect of 
spacings and fertilizer levels on finger millet. The soil of the 
experimental site was clayey in texture, slightly alkaline in 
reaction (pH – 7.7), low in available n itrogen (271.62 kg ha-1), 
medium in available phosphorous (22.18 kg ha-1) and high in 
available potassium (384.15 kg ha-1). The experiment was laid out 
in factorial randomized block design with three replications. The 
nine treatment combinations, consisted of three spacings viz., S1 
– 30 x 10 cm, S 2 – 45 x 5 cm and S 3 – 30-60 x 10 cm, and three 
nutrient levels viz., F1 – 25:20:00 kg, F 2 – 30:20:00 kg and F 3– 
35:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1. Finger millet variety PDKV Adishree 
was sown at varied spacings and full dose of phosphorous along 
with 50% nitrogen was applied as basal and remaining 50% was 
applied as top dressing as per treatments. During the crop growth 
period (June -November) total rainfall received was 1217 mm, 
data pertaining to yield attributes were recorded from randomly 
selected five plants from each net plot. Cost of cultivation was 
worked out with help of prevailing market prices of finger millet. 
The data was statistically analysed with standard method outlined 
for randomized block design (Gomez and Gomez, 1984) [3]. 
Statistically significance was tested by F- value at 0.05% level of 
probability and critical difference was worked out wherever the 
effect was significant. 
Results and Discussion 
Yield attributes 
Effect of Spacing 
Spacing had a significant effect on yield attributes. Highest length 
of panicle, panicle weight, grain yield plant-1 and straw yield 
plant-1 were obtained with the spacing of 30 -60 x 10 cm (S 3), 
followed by 30 x 10 cm (S 1). The above aspects were lowest in 
closer spacing of 45 x 5 cm (S 2). This might be due to better 
carbohydrates synthesis and its efficient translocation from source 
to sink under wider spacing. Similar results were also reported by 
Hebbal et al. (2018) [4]. 
 
Effect of Nutrient levels 
Significantly higher yield attributes viz., highest length of panicle, 
grain yield plant-1 and straw yield plant-1 were recorded under the 
nutrient level of 35:20:00 kg N: P: K ha-1 (F3), followed by 
30:20:00 kg N: P: K ha-1 (F2), which was at par with the treatment 
receiving 25:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 (F1). This might have resulted 
from higher availability of nutrients to the crop, particularly 
nitrogen, which might have boosted plant growth and 
photosynthetic efficiency. Adequate nutrient supply likely 
promoted greater production and translocation of assimilates 
towards the development of longer panicle, resulting in high er 
length of panicle, panicle weight, grain yield plant-1 and straw 
yield plant-1. Similar observations have been documented by 
Anitha et al. (2020) [1].

Yield 
Effect of spacing 
Greater grain, straw and biological yield was obtained from closer 
spacing of 45 x 5 cm (S2), followed by 30 x 10 cm (S1). The lowest 
grain and straw yield were obtained from the crop under paired 
row spacing of 30-60 x 10 cm (S3). Although wider spacings gave 
improved performance of yield attributing characters, it cannot 
outperform overall grain and straw yield per unit area obtained 
with close spaced crop at 45 x 5 cm (S 2). This spacing might be 
optimum for potential growth and development with the sufficient 
availability of space and other resources such as moisture, 
sunlight and nutrient for this crop. Similar findings were also 
reported by Hebbal et al. (2018) [4]. 
 
Effect of Nutrient levels 
Application of 35:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 (F3) to finger millet 
resulted into significantly highest grain, straw and biological 
yield, followed by application of 30:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 (F2) 
which was at par with fertilizer dose of 25:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 
(F1). The higher yield obtained may be due to increased fertilizer 
application which enhanced the availability and uptake of 
essential nutrients, which might have improved the metabolic 
activities and overall growth of the crop. Adequate nutr ient 
supply promoted vigorous vegetative growth, efficient 
photosynthetic activity and better translocation of assimilates 
from source to sink. The results obtained in the present study are 
consistent with the findings of Triveni et al. (2018) [8]. 
 
Economics 
Effect of spacings 
Higher gross monetary returns, net monetary returns and B:C 
ratio were obtained with closer spacing of 45 x 5 cm (S 2), 
followed by 30 x 10 cm (S 1). The lowest economic returns were 
obtained with wider spacing of 30-60 x 10 cm (S3). Increment in

Effect of nutrient levels 
Data pertaining to economics of finger millet showed that gross 
monetary returns, net monetary returns and B:C ratio were higher 
in response to more fertilizer application. Among the different 
nutrient levels, application of 35:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 (F3)

level of 30:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 (F2). The lower gross monetary 
returns, net monetary returns and B:C ratio was obtained with 
lower fertilizer level of 25:20:00 kg N:P:K ha-1 (F1) which could 
be attributed to the decreased grain yield. Similar observations 
have been documented by Thakur et al. (2019) [7].

Interaction effect 
Yield attributes, yield and economics were not influenced by the 
interaction effect of spacings and nutrient levels of finger millet 
(Table 1 and 2). 
 
Conclusion 
Crop sown with spacing of 30-60 x 10 cm (S3) resulted in higher 
values for yield attributes viz., length of panicle, panicle weight, 
grain yield plant-1 and straw yield plant-1. Whereas, grain, straw 
and biological yield and gross monetary returns, net monetary 
returns and B:C ratio were obtained higher with closer spacing of 
45 x 10 cm (S 3). With respect to nutrient levels, application of 
35:20:00 kg N: P: K ha-1 (F3) recorded higher yield attributes, 
yield and net returns.
