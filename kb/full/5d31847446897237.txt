Abstract7
The practice of open defecation has persistently remained high i n Nigeria despite the grave danger it8
poses to public and environmental health, and the several inte rvention programs put in place over the years9
to curtail the ugly practice. This study quantiﬁes the space an d time trends in open defecation practice in10
Nigeria with the aim of highlighting the changes that have tak en place at various locations in Nigeria over a11
ﬁfteen-year period. A Bayesian spatio-temporal model was appl ied to cross-section data obtained from the12
Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey conducted in 2003, 2008, 201 3, and 2018, and inference was based13
on integrated nested Laplace approximation technique. The ﬁn dings indicate a north-south spatio-temporal14
patterns that are similar among the rural and urban dwellers. State s such as Kwara, Kogi, Oyo, Ondo,15
Osun, Ekiti, Enugu, and Ebonyi all of which are neighbors to eac h other are among those with persistent16
high prevalence of open defecation in the country. Given the div ersity of the Nigerian population groups17
within the states, a more understanding of the socio-cultural s tandard of the di ﬀerent communities would18
be required to implement policies that recognize opportunities to explore, while being culturally responsive19
to community needs in ending open defecation in Nigeria.20

There has been some e ﬀorts albeit scanty, to understand variations in OD practice based on soc io-demographic110
and economic characteristics of di ﬀerent populations across developing countries. Desai et al. (2015) examined111

perceptions in relation to available infrastructure. Using data from Demographic and Health Survey, Malik113
5
et al. (2021) analyzed the potential predictors of OD practice in Pakistan and report ed that the location where114
an individual resides, social norms, educational level, and poverty status are linked to the unwholesome practice115
among the populace. Rahman et al. (2020)’s study rea ﬃrmed the direct link between OD and various forms of116
malnutrition among young children using data from India. In Kenya, Busienei et al. (2019) interviewed some117
residents of Lodwar settlements, Turkawa County and reported that c ulture was the main factor that explained118
why the people practiced OD. The study also found that latrines in som e residential areas are constructed with119
poor quality timber which could collapse on the users and thus, people tend to avoid it for suitable open place120
to defecate. Crocker et al. (2017) analyzed data from Ethiopia and Ghana and found that in both countries,121
households in villages with history of high prevalence of latrine use are likely sustain the practice, while in122
Ethiopia, households in SNNP region of the country were less likely to su stain latrine use than those living in123
Oromia region. Intensive behavioral change through health promotion campai gns played some important roles124
in reducing OD practice in Mali and Tanzania ( Gertler et al. , 2015). In Nigeria, Abubakar (2018) used data125
from the 2013 Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey to analyze the determinan ts of OD practice and reported126
signiﬁcant di ﬀerences across the six geo-political zones that makes up the country. How ever, an analysis at the127
geo-political level would conceal local variations at state levels, which could be useful to governments at state128
and local levels in designing and implementing state-speciﬁc e ﬀective intervention strategies. It is against this129

2.1 Data Description140
This study relies on data from four waves of Demographic and Health Survey s conducted in Nigeria in 2003, 2008,141
2013, and 2018. The survey, carried out by The DHS Program was implemented by the National Population142
Commission of Nigeria. The primary purpose was to make available real time d ata on household characteristics143
and information regarding demographic and health issues particularly as th ey a ﬀect women of reproductive age144
(15-49 years) and their children younger than ﬁve years of age. The survey u sually follow a two-stage sampling145
technique with enumeration areas (clusters) selected from the cou ntry’s census frame at the ﬁrst stage and146
households to be surveyed are selected at the second stage. The data anal ysed in this study came from the147
Household data set that consist of information about the household characteri stics. The response variable of148
interest was a binary variable created from the responses give to the q uestion on type of toilet facility available in149
the household. The variable takes a value of 1 if the household had no suc h facility or uses bush or open ﬁeld for150
defecation and zero if it has a toilet facility irrespective of the ty pe. We included variables such as type of place151
of residence, household water source (whether protected or not), whe ther or not the household has electricity,152
number of rooms used for sleeping, sex of household head, household type of cooking fuel, educational level of153
head of household, and household wealth index. The state of residence w here the household was located was154
geo-referenced and used as the unit of the spatial analysis. Nigeria comp rises of thirty six states and a Federal155
Capital Territory, Abuja (Figure).156

2.2 Statistical Model157
We adopted a Bayesian spatio-temporal modeling technique based on a hi erarchical binomial model that at-158
tempts to simultaneously estimate spatial and temporal structured p attern and allowing for interaction between159
time and space, which would explain di ﬀerences in time trend of the practice of OD for the di ﬀerent geographical160
areas of Nigeria. Let Yij be the number of households that practiced OD in the ith state during survey period j161
and nij is the total number of households sampled in a given area during a surve y period. Then Yij is assumed162
to follow a binomial distribution, that is, Yij ∼ binomial(nij, ωij) for i =1 , ··· , 37, j =1 , 2, 3, 4 and ωij is the163
risk (prevalence) of practicing OD in state i during survey period j. We considered the logit of the prevalence164
for the OD outcome as follows:165
logit(ωij)= ηij = b0 + x0β + νi + γj + φj + δij (1)
where b0 is the model intercept that quantiﬁes the overall baseline risk, x0 is a vector of categorical variables166
considered as linear e ﬀects with β as the vector of linear parameters; νi is the area-speciﬁc random e ﬀect167
assumed to be independent and identically distributed, γj, another area-speciﬁc e ﬀect considered to be spatially168
structured. We considered and modeled this assuming Gaussian int rinsic conditional autoregressive (iCAR),169
where the spatially correlated random e ﬀect of the ith state is based on the sum of its weighted neighborhood170
values deﬁned through an adjacency matrix of common boundaries of a given regi on. The term φj is a structured171
temporal e ﬀect assumed to follow a second order random walk, δij is a space-time interaction e ﬀect such that172
the parameter vector δ follows a Gaussian distribution with a precision matrix given by τδRδ where τδ is an173
unknown scalar, while Rδ is a structured matrix that speciﬁes the type of temporal and spatial d ependence174
between the elements of δ (Blangiardo and Cameletti , 2015). For parameter estimation, we relied on integrated175
nested Laplace approximation technique ( Rue et al. , 2009), which is a deterministic algorithm for Bayesian176

4 Discussion228
The practice of OD is the worst form of sanitation crisis that pollutes th e environment and endangers public229
health. Its continuous practice in any population and in particular, in a rapidly growing population with high230
population density like Nigeria poses a grave environmental and health c hallenges to millions of people including231
young children who have tender immunity and are closer to the ground . Human feces in any open environment232
contaminants public spaces, roadsides, surface water, and pollutes th e air with unpleasant odor. Aside the233
public health challenges as a consequence, the environment where i t is practice will continue to loose economic234
value because local and international tourists and investors would avoid unpleasant places that endanger their235
health, as found in the case of some beaches in Lagos and other Nigerian coaster ci ties ( Abubakar and Dano ,236
2018). A number of factors at individual and community levels have been id entiﬁed to inﬂuence the practice of237
15
OD in Nigeria and other developing countries where it is practice. How ever, identifying the variability across238
geographical settings could contribute to address the challenge faced wi th understanding community/inter-state239
based characteristics that encourage OD or cause variability in the impac t of sanitation hygiene programme240
implemented over time in various geographical location, and allow for targe ted interventions at various locations241
in Nigeria. In this study, we used a Bayesian spatio-temporal model to explore the pattern across space and242
over time in Nigeria while controlling for other possible determinan ts.243
The ﬁndings from the spatio-temporal maps have revealed similar patter n of occurrence among the rural and244
urban dwellers from most of the states with many states in the North Cent ral and southern regions of the country245
having the highest rates over time. Historically, Nigeria often formu late the right policies including those to end246
OD particularly in places where the burden is high, but these usual ly fail during the process of implementation247
(UNICEF, 2021). In fact, Nigeria was probably among the few nations of the world that formul ated a road-248
map to ﬁx sanitation and hygiene issues within a reasonable time-frame as far back as 2004, much longer249
before the United Nations modiﬁed the target of global sanitation coverage. Bu t, despite the ambitious and250
painstaking policies developed around sanitation and hygiene issues in Nigeria, not many signiﬁcant outcomes251
have been achieved over a ﬁfteen-year period as evident from our ﬁn dings. Without giving consideration to the252
diversity of the Nigeria population, the CLTS program, which was one of the leading innovative methodology253
used to mobilize di ﬀerent communities to stop OD and attain total sanitation coverage primaril y featured a254
rural strategy that assumes homogeneity of the population in every communi ty. The program failed to properly255
recognise and cater for the heterogeneity in population groups within the states, sub-urban and urban areas256
in the country. Hence, there was a limitation from inception on how far t he CLTS strategy would be applied257
eﬀectively in diﬀerent settings within the country ( FMWR, 2016; Abramovsky et al., 2016; Eﬃong, 2013). Given258
the diversity in the Nigerian population, research ﬁndings from publi c health and environmental studies have259
16
found that it is crucial to examine and adapt models that consider the pe culiarity of each community, in order to260
develop evidence-based approaches that could lead to notable improvem ent in e ﬀorts to improve the well-being261
of the populace at their di ﬀerent geographical settings ( Gayawan et al., 2019a,b; Egbon et al. , 2021). The same262
approach would be crucial in ending the practice of OD in Nigeria.263
As reported elsewhere ( Busienei et al. , 2019), cultural background, beliefs, norms, leadership structure,264
and other community change agents could contribute to the huge variations i n OD practice across Nigerian265
communities because, these community characteristics di ﬀer in no small measure across the various ethno-266
geographical environments that make up Nigeria. A clear understanding of th e socio-cultural standard of the267
diﬀerent communities, will enhance implementing policies that rec ognize opportunities to explore, while being268
culturally responsive to the community needs. Consequently, th e societal norms and barriers that encourage269
OD can be successfully managed, leading to a transformed society whe re the problem of OD is completely270
eradicated ( Oluwatuyi et al. , 2020; Abramovsky et al. , 2016).271
The statistics reported on the linear e ﬀects of the determinants of OD in this study, highlighted that the272
prevalence or trend of OD continued to increase in Nigeria, especially in 2013, which was about 10 years that273
the CLTS policy was developed and implemented in all the states in th e country. One would think that OD274
practices would have reduced in Nigeria, considering how long the CL TS policy was implemented, but the275
reverse was observed, as this study and other studies like, Gbadegesin and Akintola (2020), Abubakar (2017),276

36 states and the federal capital territory. Hence, we considered state levels and time components in modelling354
OD, as opposed to geo-political level analysis that had been done in past stu dies. Speciﬁcally, we adopted the355
Bayesian spatio-temporal modeling technique based on a hierarchical binomial model that attempts to simulta-356
neously estimate spatial and temporal structured pattern and allowing for interaction between time and space,357
which would explain di ﬀerences in time trend of the practice of OD for the di ﬀerent states in Nigeria, while358
controlling for other possible determinants. Findings from the stud y suggest the need for culturally responsive359
approaches that identify the variability across time and geographical sett ings in addressing the challenge faced360
with understanding community/inter-state based characteristics that encourage OD or caused variability in the361
