Abstract Sleep onset depends on a decline in core body temperature, and sleep quality degrades when the thermal environment falls outside an optimal window, making active regulation of sleep surface temperature a well-supported target for intervention. Thermoregulating mattress covers are now a rapidly growing consumer category. Across published evaluations of this device class, the significant findings are concentrated in subjective measures of perceived sleep quality and daytime functioning. These devices are conspicuous in use, which leaves such measures critically exposed to expectancy bias.
