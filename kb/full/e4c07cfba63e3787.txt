Abstract The removal of major and trace metal ions in real-contaminated urban surface water from the Nakivubo channel and leachate from the Kampala Capital City disused landfill in Kiteezi, Central Uganda, was studied in fixed-bed columns using biosorbents derived from Coffea canephora husks and Musa spp. peels as an alternative to conventional water treatment methods. The maximum bed capacity, metal removal efficiency, and equilibrium metal uptake were determined, and breakthrough curves were then plotted for selected major and trace elements.
