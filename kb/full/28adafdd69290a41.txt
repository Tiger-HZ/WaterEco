Effect of crop geometry and nutrient levels on growth, 
yield and economics of maize 
 
Shital S Pahurkar, IM Nagrare and Pradheepa S 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9e.6515 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment was conducted during kharif 2025–26 at the Agronomy Farm, College of Agriculture, 
Nagpur, to study the effect of crop geometry and nutrient levels on growth, yield and economics of maize 
(Zea mays L.) cv. PDKV Aarambh. The experiment was laid out in a Factorial Randomized Block Desi gn 
with three crop geometries and three nutrient levels, replicated thrice. Among the crop geometries, 45 cm × 
20 cm recorded the highest grain yield (75.47 q ha-1), straw yield (78.91 q ha-1), gross monetary returns (Rs. 
1,75,805 ha-1), net monetary returns (Rs. 1,32,573 ha-1) and benefit: cost ratio (4.07). Among nutrient levels, 
180:90:90 kg N: P₂O₅:K₂O ha-1 resulted in the highest grain yield (69.65 q ha-1), straw yield (71.51 q ha-1), 
gross monetary returns (Rs. 1,62,123 ha-1), net monetary returns (Rs . 1,17,423 ha-1) and benefit: cost ratio 
(3.63). The interaction between crop geometry and nutrient levels was non-significant for growth, yield and 
economic parameters. Thus, 45 cm × 20 cm crop geometry and 180:90:90 kg N: P₂O₅:K₂O ha-1 nutrient level 
may be considered a suitable on for improving productivity and profitability of maize under the agro -
ecological conditions of Nagpur. 
 
Keywords: Maize, crop geometry, nutrient levels, plant population, grain yield, straw yield, economics, 
benefit:cost ratio 
 
1. Introduction 
Maize (Zea mays L.) often referred to as the “Queen of Cereals” is an important cereal crop of 
India and has wide applications as food, feed and industrial raw material. Its adaptability to diverse 
environments and capacity to produce substantial biomass within a compara tively short period 
make it an important crop for improving farm productivity. The productivity of maize is closely 
associated with appropriate agronomic management, particularly crop geometry and nutrient 
supply. Crop geometry determines plant distribution and consequently influences the capture and 
utilization of solar radiation, water and nutrients. Suitable spatial arrangement can improve plant 
performance, photosynthetic activity and source–sink relationships (Aliveni et al., 2025) [1]. 
Nutrient manage ment is another major determinant of maize growth and yield. Nitrogen, 
phosphorus and potassium have complementary roles in vegetative development, energy transfer, 
photosynthesis, assimilate production and grain formation. Increasing nutrient availability can 
enhance biomass accumulation and yield components when the additional supply is effectively 
utilized by the crop (Maeda et al., 2018; Hu et al., 2019; Medici et al., 2019) [8, 6, 10]. Previous 
studies cited in the present investigation have reported b eneficial effects of appropriate planting 
geometry and increased fertility levels on maize growth, yield and economics (Mahapatra et al., 
2006 and Kar et al., 2006) [9, 7]. 
Despite the recognized importance of these practices, their combined response can v ary with 
genotype and growing conditions. Therefore, the present investigation was undertaken at Nagpur 
to study the effects of different crop geometries and nutrient levels on growth, yield and economics 
of maize variety PDKV Aarambh. The objectives were to study the effect of crop geometry on 
maize growth, to assess the impact of nutrient levels on maize yield and to work out the economics 
of the treatments.

Maize was sown on 28 June 2025 by dibbling two seeds per hill 
according to the assigned geometry. Gap filling and thinning were 
carried out to maintain the required plant population. Nitrogen 
was applied through urea in two splits, with half as basal and half 
at 30 days after sowing, while the full doses of phosphorus and 
potassium were applied at sowing through single super phosphate 
and muriate of potash, respectively. The crop was maintained 
weed-free through intercultural operations and herbicide 
application. Harvesting was carried out manually at physiological 
maturity. During the crop growth period (June -November) total 
rainfall received was 1217 mm, data pertaining to yield attributes 
were recorded from randomly selected five plants from each net 
plot and the mean value was calculated. Cost of cultivation was 
worked out with help of prevailing market prices of finger millet. 
The data was statistically analyzed with standard method outlined 
for randomized block design (Gomez and Gomez, 1984) [5]. 
Statistically significance was tested by F- value at 0.05% level of 
probability and critical difference was worked out wherever the 
effect was significant. 
 
3. Results and Discussion 
3.1 Yield attributes 
Number of cobs plant −1 was not significantly affecte d by either 
crop geometry or nutrient level. Numerically, the paired -row 
geometry recorded 2.19 cobs plant −1. Cob length, cob weight, 
number of grains cob−1, grain yield plant−1 and straw yield plant−1 
were significantly improved under the tested crop geom etries, 
with the paired -row geometry recording 22.74 cm cob length, 
120.31 g cob weight, 400.28 grains cob −1 66.16 g grain yield 
plant−1 and 77.14 g straw yield plant−1. The improved individual-
plant performance under paired rows was associated with greater 
space and resource availability. Similar responses were reported 
by Sultani and Bakhtyar (2025) [11] and Tanisha et al. (2026) [12]. 
Nutrient level 180:90:90 recorded the highest values for these 
yield attributes, except that number of cobs plant−1 and seed index 
were non -significant. The improved yield components with 
higher nutrient supply were associated with better nutrient 
availability and assimilate production.

3.2 Grain and straw yield 
Grain yield was significantly influenced by crop geometry. The 
highest grain yield (75.47 q ha −1) was obtained with 45 cm × 20 
cm followed by 60 cm × 15 cm (72.13 q ha−1) while 45/90 cm × 
20 cm produced 52.76 q ha −1. Although the paired -row 
arrangement improved individual-plant yield attributes, its lower 
plant population reduced yield ha−1. The 45 cm × 20 cm geometry 
combined adequate plant population with favoura ble resource 
distribution and consequently recorded the highest grain and 
straw yields. Similar results were reported by Sultani and 
Bakhtyar (2025) [11], Bhusal et al. (2025) [4] and Tanisha et al. 
(2026) [12]. 
Nutrient levels produced a progressive incre ase in grain yield, 
from 64.11 q ha−1 with 120:60:60 to 69.65 q ha−1 with 180:90:90 
kg N: P 2O5:K2O ha −1. The increased nutrient supply likely 
enhanced vegetative growth, photosynthetic activity, assimilates 
production and grain development. These findings are supported 
by Alka et al. (2019) [3] and Abhigna et al. (2024) [2]. Straw yield 
followed a similar pattern, with the highest value under 45 cm × 
20 cm among crop geometries and under 180:90:90 among 
nutrient levels. The interaction effect was non-significant. 
 
3.3 Economics 
Economics clearly reflected the yield response of the treatments. 
Crop geometry significantly influenced gross monetary returns, 
net monetary returns and benefit:cost ratio. The 45 cm × 20 cm 
geometry recorded the highest gross monetary returns (Rs. 
1,75,805 ha −1), net monetary returns (Rs. 1,32,573 ha −1) and 
benefit: cost ratio (4.07), followed by 60 cm × 15 cm. The paired-
row geometry had lower returns because of its lower grain and 
straw productivity. Similar economic advantages of suitable 
planting geometry have been reported by Kar et al. (2006) [7]. 
Nutrient levels increased gross and net monetary returns 
progressively. The highest gross monetary returns (Rs. 1,62 ,123

4. Conclusion 
Under the agro -ecological conditions of Nagpur during kharif 
2025–26, crop geometry had a pronounced influence on plant 
population, growth, yield attributes, grain & straw yield and 
economic returns. The 45 cm × 20 cm geometry improved the 
plant population, grain yield (75.47 q ha −1), straw yield (78.91 q 
ha−1), gross monetary returns (Rs. 1,75,805 ha −1), net monetary 
returns (Rs. 1,32,573 ha −1) and benefit: cost ratio (4.07). The 
45/90 cm × 20 cm paired -row geometry enhanced several 
individual-plant yield attributes but its lower population reduced 
productivity on a hectare basis.
