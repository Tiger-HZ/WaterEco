ABSTRACT Integrates eutrophication drivers, wastewater pathways, and NBS-based treatment within the Mwanza-Lake Victoria system for nutrient mitigation. Urban expansion, population growth, and inadequate wastewater management have accelerated eutrophication and impaired water quality in Lake Victoria, Africa's largest freshwater lake. Mwanza City, a major urban center on its shores, contributes substantially through discharges of untreated and poorly treated effluents. Conventional wastewater treatment technologies are limited by high capital and energy demands, raising sustainability concerns. This study evaluates nature-based solutions as context-appropriate, low-energy alternatives for wastewater treatment in Mwanza's hilly terrain to mitigate nutrient loading and protect lake health. C
