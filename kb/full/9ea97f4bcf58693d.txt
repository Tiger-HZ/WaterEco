Egg production rates of Calanus finmarchicus were measured in the Labrador Sea (spring) and on the Scotian Shelf (spring, summer, and autumn), in conjunction with indices of gonad maturity and food availability, female body size, surface layer temperature, and surface nitrate concentrations. Egg production rates were often two to four times higher than any previously reported at similar temperatures. Estimates of egg production rates of populations were most closely related to the proportion of reproductively mature females (as measured by a gonad stage index) and female size.
