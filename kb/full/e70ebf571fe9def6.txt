Two new species of rare fireflies from mainland China, both having unusual and distinctive features, are described as Pygoluciola nanling sp. nov. and Aquatica chibi sp. nov. from males, females, and first-stage larvae. We discuss rarity in Luciolinae in a pragmatic sense, distinguishing species that are genuinely scarce or range-restricted from those that appear rare because of limited detectability or uneven collecting effort. Both species were first recognised as new and distinctive by both authors using morphological characteristics, and their generic and specific placements confirmed by phylogenetic analysis of their mitogenomes. Aquatica chibi sp. nov. was taken alongside the similarly coloured Aq. ficta in rice paddies, and may have been overlooked; males have a distinctive asymmetrical anterior dorsal margin of the aedeagal sheath not seen in any other Luciolinae males. Pygoluciola nanling sp. nov. has only ever been found in small numbers in an otherwise well collected area. In what until recently has been a male-based taxonomy, Aquatica Fu &amp; Ballantyne is characterised from females of all but one species, and Pygoluciola Wittmer from 19 of the 29 described species. Pygoluciola nanling sp. nov. female is without bursa plates, and the strong development of the anterior corners of abdominal tergite 8, in wrapping around the abdomen, could replace the need for bursa plates. Keys to males and females are expanded and modified to accommodate these new species. The branching pattern of the phylogenetic analysis was highly consistent with the taxonomic conclusions supported by traditional morphological and anatomical characteristics, demonstrating a strong correspondence between the molecular data and the classical taxonomic evidence.
