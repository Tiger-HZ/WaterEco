As the world’s population grows and rapid development of the global economy, the water demand is constantly increasing and water resources, water pollution and water scarcity has become serious problems in the world agenda. Today, tourism has become one of the top five export categories in the world and the number of tourists’ arrivals each year continues to rise, with the exception of the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite tourism’s potential to ensure economic growth and create jobs worldwide, it is well known that it also places increasing pressure on the environment. In other words, on all scales, tourism is a significant water-consuming industry and has put strong pressures on freshwater resources and caused some water-related problems in the destinations. This study aims to investigate water footprint (WF) estimations to determine the efficiency of tourism in Türkiye using input-output (I-O) analysis. In tourism, the water footprint means the amount of water required to produce the goods and services consumed by foreign and domestic tourists. The database used in the empirical analyses is actually the input-output tables for Türkiye in 2014 and obtained from the Global Trade Analysis Project (GTAP) platform. I-O tables which are based mainly on national agricultural, industrial, services statistics as well as water data. Results show that the water of tourism in Türkiye in 2014, was about 3.31 km3/year, approximately per tourist 8.1 m3/day. Results also reveal that 48% of Türkiye’s service exports come from foreign tourists. This study aims to contribute the sustainability of tourism in Türkiye.
