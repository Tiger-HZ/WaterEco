Lakes are an integral part of urban landscapes providing ecological, recreational, and public health benefits. These lakes are typically located in densely populated areas and subject to urban influences on water quality. Nutrient inputs promote algal blooms that may include toxic forms, while harmful bacteria (Escherichia coli) are introduced through stormwater runoff and sewage overflow events. We monitored nine lakes in the Richmond area to assess potential public health threats associated with the presence of algal toxins (microcystin) and E. coli. The lakes were sampled every 3–4 weeks during May–October 2025 to assess seasonal patterns and inter-lake differences.
