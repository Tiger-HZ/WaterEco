Abstract Determining reliable groundwater natural background levels (NBLs) in heterogeneous mountainous regions remains challenging because multiple flow domains (pore, fracture, and karst) generate complex hydrochemical patterns governed by recharge–flow–discharge processes. A critical yet unresolved question is whether predefined hydrogeological units are consistent with hydrologically controlled groundwater organization. In this study, we develop a process-informed framework that integrates unsupervised Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) and supervised K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and use this combined SOM–KNN approach as a diagnostic tool to evaluate whether statistical zonation reflects hydrologically meaningful groundwater organization and to quantify transitions between flow regimes.
