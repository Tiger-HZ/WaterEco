Abstract Nanodiamond seeding and subsequent polycrystalline diamond growth were investigated on five substrates using two suspensions with opposite zeta-potential signs: a negatively charged H 2 O-based suspension and a positively charged DMSO-based suspension. Zeta potential, pre-seeding surface roughness, seed density, and film structure were characterized by atomic force microscopy, scanning electron microscopy, Raman spectroscopy, and x-ray diffraction. Oppositely charged suspension–substrate pairs generally promoted higher initial seed adsorption. The H 2 O-based suspension produced the highest seed density on weakly positive Al-free AlN nanorods, whereas the DMSO-based suspension gave higher seed densities on negatively charged substrates such as Si, SiO 2 , and quartz.
