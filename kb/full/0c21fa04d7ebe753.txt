Hulunbuir Grassland is a crucial ecological security barrier and livestock production base in northern China. In recent years, due to climate change, overgrazing, pests, and other factors, some areas have experienced vegetation degradation and sparse coverage, prompting discussions about applying chemical fertilizers to improve grassland conditions. However, natural grasslands differ fundamentally from farmland ecosystems. Large-scale chemical fertilizer application not only fails to address grassland degradation but also trig gers biodiversity loss, soil salinization, compaction, and water eutrophication. Based on the ecological characteristics of Hulunbuir Grassland, this paper systematically analyzes the core reasons for prohibiting chemical fertilizer use from four dimensions: ecological risks, economic feasibility, policy constraints, and true causes of degradation. Combined with local ecological conditions, scientific and sustainable improvement strategies are proposed. The study reveals that the primary causes of poor grassland growth are not nutri ent deficiency but drought, overgrazing, rodent and insect damage, and loss of soil humus layers. Chemical fertilizers only provide short-term greening while exacerbating long-term ecological depletion. Only by adhering to ecological priority and adopting green measures such as rotational grazing, rest grazing, biological reseeding, organic fertilizer application, and pest control can the sustainable restoration of Hulunbuir Grassland be achieved.
