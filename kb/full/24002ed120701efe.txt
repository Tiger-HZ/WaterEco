The release of potentially harmful metals from waste materials into soil represents a significant pathway for soil contamination. This study quantitatively examined the release potential of metals (Cd, Cr, Ni, Pb, and V) from diverse waste streams at predetermined depths (0–20 cm and 20–40 cm). The principle of mass balance was employed to assess waste-metal release potential, with contamination factor (CF), metal pollution index (MPI), and pollution load index (PLI) employed as pollution indicators. Findings indicated that the investigated wastes caused significant pollution within the studied soils, with automechanic- and paint-related wastes having the highest metal-release potentials.
