TPAD (Teacher Performance Appraisal and Development) is an essential instrument in guiding school and classroom management practices for quality and relevant education for all. It also enables teachers to respond to global shifts professionally and to diverse learners’ needs through structured evaluation. However, teachers’ know-how, accountability, and job experience hinder an effective appraisal process. Thus, aggravates the gap between policy expectations and teachers’ intentions in the implementation of this policy framework in traditional educational environments. This also adds to inefficiencies of the appraisal process for teachers. Against this background, the study determined the level of influence of professional practice on teacher quality in public secondary schools in Nairobi
