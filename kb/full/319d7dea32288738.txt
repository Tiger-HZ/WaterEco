This study explores the potential implications of rapid urban growth and land use/land cover (LULC) changes in Al-Madinah Al-Munawarah city on water resources. The methodology highlights the efficiency of geospatial techniques (remote sensing and geographic information systems) for mapping, identifying, understanding, and measuring the spatiotemporal dynamics and change rates of different LULC categories over 60 years (1965–2025). The change detection analysis of multisource and multitemporal data reveals substantial modifications in the initial landscape configuration, in which the human footprint was insignificant.
