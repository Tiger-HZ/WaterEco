Sea ice algae are a vital food source in Arctic marine ecosystems, yet the variability of lipid- and isotope-based trophic markers within natural sea ice algal communities remains poorly understood and challenges the use of trophic marker approaches. This study tracked a bottom sea ice algal bloom in northwestern Hudson Bay, examining how environmental factors influence polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), highly branched isoprenoids (HBIs), and δ¹³C ratios of fatty acids. Trophic markers showed high spatiotemporal variability that was significantly correlated with sea ice nutrients and dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) concentrations.
