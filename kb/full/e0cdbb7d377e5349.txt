We quantified temporal changes in catch rate, fishing effort, and catch misreporting for two sectors of the fixed-gear fishery for Newfoundland's northern Atlantic cod, Gadus morhua, from 1980 to 1991, the year preceding the stock's commercial fishing moratorium. Over the 12-year period, fishermen reported catch rate declines of 40 and 75% in the trap and gillnet fisheries, respectively, associated with significant increases in nominal fishing effort. Additional changes to effort included smaller gillnet and trap mesh sizes, larger traps, longer soak times, and modifications to trap design to increase catch retention probabilities.
