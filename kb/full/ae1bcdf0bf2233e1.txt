Abstract Background Late preterm (LP) children (34 0/7 – 36 6/7 weeks of gestation) are at risk for neurocognitive challenges, yet the neuroanatomic and functional disruptions underlying these developmental differences remain poorly understood. Purpose or Hypothesis Investigate (1) regional differences in cortical surface area and thalamic nuclei volume and (2) structural variations that may explain heterogeneity in neurocognitive functioning in LP versus full term (FT) school-aged children, using two complementary approaches: a vertex-wise analysis of cortical surface area and a region-of-interest volumetric analysis of thalamic nuclei. Study Type Secondary analysis of a prospective observational cohort study. Population or Subjects 48 LP and 72 FT children aged 6–13 years. Field Strength
