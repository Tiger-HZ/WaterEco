Conventional wastewater treatment processes are unable to eliminate persistent pharmaceutical contaminants such as levonorgestrel (LVG) and efavirenz (EFA), highlighting the need for advanced materials capable of effectively adsorbing and degrading these chemically stable compounds. In this study, titanium-oxide-doped carbon nanotubes (TiO 2 -CNTs) were synthesised and evaluated for the simultaneous removal of LVG and EFA from wastewater. Synthesised TiO 2 -CNTs were analysed using SEM-EDX, FTIR, and XRD for their physicochemical and crystal structures. The effectiveness of the synthesised TiO 2 -CNT in removing EFA and LVG was evaluated through batch experiments under ultraviolet light. The SEM micrographs of TiO 2 -doped CNT showed tiny, porous tube-like structures on the surface. The maximum adsorption capacity of 5.84 mg/g and 6.34 mg/g for EFV and LVG was achieved using a 0.3 g adsorbent dosage, 2.5 ppm initial concentration, and 90 min contact time. EFA and LVG removal occurred through a combined mechanism involving multilayer surface adsorption via both physical and chemical interactions, followed by photocatalytic degradation driven by reactive species generated on the TiO 2 -CNT interface. The presence of Ti2p, C1s, and O1s signals in the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy data indicated active photocatalytic processes responsible for the synergistic reduction, oxidation, and surface-interaction mechanisms driving EFA and LVG removal. Overall, TiO 2 -CNT proved effective in simultaneously removing LVG and EFA from wastewater.
