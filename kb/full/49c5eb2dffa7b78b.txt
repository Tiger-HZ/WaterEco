Many aquatic consumers have flexible feeding habits, and the diet and trophic position of a species can be expected to vary both within and among populations. In this study, we quantify the importance of both within- and among-population trophic variation for lake trout (Salvelinus namaycush) using stable isotope designations of trophic position from 13 Ontario and Quebec lakes. Lake-to-lake differences explained 78% of the total variation in lake trout trophic position. Analysis using both stable isotopes and published dietary data demonstrated that the trophic position of lake trout failed to increase appreciably as a function of animal body size.
