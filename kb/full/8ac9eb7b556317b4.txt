Abstract Parrot feather ( Myriophyllum aquaticum ) is an introduced, noninvasive amphibious plant found in coastal provinces, but it has the potential to become an invasive species in China. The plant is heterophyllous, with both emergent (aerial) and submerged (aquatic) leaves, and has two distinct root types: aquatic (adventitious) and edaphic (sediment) root systems. This morphological plasticity allows M. aquaticum to effectively absorb nutrients from different layers of the environment, making it a suitable model plant for exploring nutrient dynamics in both water and soil systems and investigating how wetland plants respond to cultural eutrophication.
