The Gulf of Nicoya, Costa Rica’s largest small-scale fishing ground, faces escalating pressures from overfishing, land-based human activities, and climate change. This study investigates recent historical trends in coastal eutrophication, ocean warming, and marine heatwaves in this tropical estuarine system. We also assess the impacts of the 2023 marine heatwave on the biomass and size structure of fish landed by small-scale fisheries. Our results show that peak annual chlorophyll-a concentrations exceed the global eutrophication risk threshold of 5 mg/m3 by up to 13-fold, although these values have declined since 2002.
