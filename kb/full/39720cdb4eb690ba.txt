Fluxes of nutrients at the ice-ocean interface are affected by the smooth or turbulent nature of the flow under the ice. The nature of the flow depends on the friction velocity, which determines the thickness of the molecular sublayer, and on the roughness of the ice bottom. Based on in situ boundary layer studies, the range of variability of the thickness of the molecular sublayer and that of bottom roughness suggest that the flow under sea ice may easily shift from smooth to turbulent. This transition enhances nutrient exchanges at the ice-ocean interface.
