 Abstract Background The development of straw-derived controlled-release fertilizers (SCFs) as sustainable alternatives to petroleum-based products represents a promising strategy for environmentally friendly agricultural intensification. However, the inherent hydrophilicity of straw and other biomass materials significantly limits the performance of SCFs. Moreover, conventional modification methods often depend on costly external chemical agents, compromising their sustainability. Prior works only explored ICSE for straw liquefaction or external silica for polymer hydrophilization separately, yet none recover straw’s native silica via ICSE as in-situ membrane modifier to build a low-cost closed-loop SCF route. Results In this study, a valorization strategy for rice straw was developed by integrating instant catapult steam explosion with chemical desilication. This process simultaneously improved the liquefaction yield of rice straw by 13.09% and utilized the extracted nano-SiO₂ as an intrinsic hydrophobic modifier for rice straw-based resin membranes. At a nano-SiO₂ loading of 10 wt%, hierarchical rough structures were generated on the membrane surface, thereby increasing the water contact angle from 84.01 to 93.77° and extending the nitrogen (N) release period from 49 to 77 days. This design greatly reduces the reliance on high-dose external superhydrophobic modifiers; the in-situ extracted biogenic nano-SiO₂ serves as the core hydrophobic filler, forming a mutually beneficial closed-loop route coupling straw silica extraction and coating modification. Lettuce cultivation experiments further demonstrated that Si-modified SCF exhibited higher N-use efficiency than conventional straw-based controlled-release fertilizers. Conclusions This study introduces a sustainable paradigm for converting agricultural waste and proposes an exogenous modifier-free method to enhance SCF performance.
