ABSTRACT 22 
In this research, the degradation of seven different micropollutants (MPs) and the 23 
formation of their transformation products (TPs) have been assessed during the 24 
application of different advanced oxidation processes: photolytic and photocatalytic 25 
activation of peroxymonosulfate (PMS) and persulfate (PS). The results were compared 26 
with those obtained from the photolytic experiments using hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) as 27 
oxidant. A significant abatement of almost all MPs was achieved , even with very low 28 
UV-C contact time (9 and 28 s). The degradation of atenolol (ATN) and caffeine (CFN) 29 
ranged from 84 to 100% with a dose of 0.5 mM of any oxidant . The efficiencies for 30 
bisphenol-A (BPA), carbamazepine (CBZ), diclofenac (DCF), ibuprofen (IBP) , and 31 
sulfamethoxazole (SMX) varied depending on the oxidation system and operating 32 
conditions (oxidant dose and UV -C contact time), leading to the photolysis of PMS to 33 
higher efficiencies than PS and H2O2. In all cases, the abatement of MPs ranged from 63 34 
to 83%, even with the lowest PMS dosage. Moreover, the addition of Fe(II) as a catalyst 35 
enhanced the removal efficiency, reaching almost total removal, especially over CBZ, 36 
DCF, and IBP. The Dissolved Organic Carbon (DOC) removal ranged between 44 and 37 
62%, suggesting the transformation of MPs in intermediate compounds. The 38 
identification of transformation products was carried out for each micropollutant and each 39 
oxidation treatment, being observed some transformation products specific of oxidation 40 
by sulfate radicals. For example, m/z 165.0432 only appeared after PMS/Fe(II)/UV-C on 41 
the degradation of BFA, m/z 251.082 appeared after photolytic activation of PMS and PS 42 
on CBZ removal, and m/z 128.0452 was observed after any sulfate radical oxidation 43 
treatment, but not after photolysis of H2O2. 44 
Keywords: sulfate radicals; micropollutants; transformation products; mechanisms; UV-45 
C radiation. 46

1. Introduction 47 
Reports of micropollutants (MPs) occurrence in water bodies have increased in recent 48 
years. MPs pollution is the result of human activities , such as industrial, hospital, 49 
veterinary, domestic, etc. The real effects that these MPs could exert on ecosystems and 50 
humans are currently undetermined , although some researchers assert that they could 51 
negatively affect aquatic ecosystems , biological processes of organisms , and overall 52 
human health ( Boxall et al. , 2012; Schwarzenbach et al., 2006 ). Due to the chemical 53 
stability and low biodegradability of most MPs, conventional wastewater treatment plants 54 
(WWTP) are unable to achieve their complete removal (Collado et al., 2014; Casas & 55 
Bester, 2015; Gruchlik et al., 2018). For these reasons, although the vast majority of these 56 
compounds are not yet legislatively regulated, monitoring them is strongly recommended 57 
(i.e., in the European Union through 2013/39/EU) . Therefore, wastewater reuse could 58 
constitute an important route for the release of MPs to the environment, and if MPs reach 59 
water bodies or water supplies (catchment of fresh water, or reuse of treated wastewater), 60 
they could present a serious concern due to their unknown consequences (Badia-Fabregat 61 
et al., 2015). 62 
Sulfate Radical-based A dvanced Oxidation Processes ( SR-AOPs) are becoming 63 
emerging alternatives to the widely reported hydroxyl radicals-based AOPs (HR-AOPs). 64 
Among the main advantages of SR-AOPs are the low cost of persulfate salts, their lack 65 
of toxicity, ease of handling and storage, and the low pH dependency of their activity 66 
(Wacławek et al., 2017). Sulfate radicals could be generated from the activation of 67 
different persulfate salts, such as potassium peroxymonosulfate (HSO 5-; PMS) and 68 
sodium or potassium persulfate (S2O82-; PS). Nevertheless, these persulfate anions are not 69 
efficient by themselves, and they need to be activated by the following routes: i) heat; ii) 70 
UV radiation; iii) elevated pH; or iv) addition of transition metals . Their high redox 71 
4 
 
potential (E0 = 2.5–3.1 V) and reactivity make sulfate radicals, as a strong oxidant species, 72 
able to degrade any recalcitrant organic substance (Ling et al., 2017; Ahmed et al., 2014; 73 
Pang et al., 2016; Xia et al., 2017; Oncu et al., 2015), and they have been proven efficient 74 
in the inactivation of microorganisms (Rodríguez-Chueca et al., 2017a, b). 75 
Monitoring of the transformation products generated during the process is as important 76 
as the r emoval of MPs present in wastewater. Some authors have investigated the 77 
degradation mechanisms of different MPs by sulfate radicals (Pang et al., 2016; Ling et 78 
al., 2017; Mercado et al., 2018 ). H owever, to the best of our knowledge, no studies 79 
currently exist about degradation mechanisms by SR-AOPs of MPs in real wastewater 80 
effluents. 81 
This study is focused on the evaluation of the efficiency of different homogeneous AOPs 82 
at the laboratory scale (H2O2/UV-C; PS/UV -C; PS/Fe(II)/UV -C; PMS/UV -C and 83 
PMS/Fe(II)/UV-C) towards the degradation of detected MPs in real WWTP effluents, 84 
including assessment of their transformation mechanisms and the extent of the 85 
mineralization process. 86

2.2. UV-C photoreactor 96 
All of the treatments were performed in continuous mode using flow rates of 1 and 3 97 
L/min, and at the natural pH of samples without further adjustment (7.17 ± 0.08). These 98 
flow rate values were selected to provide a UV-C contact time ranging from 9 to 28 s, in 99 
the order of the actual values in real wastewater treatment plant s (Rodríguez-Chueca et 100 
al., 2018a). The feed system consists of a stirred tank with a volume of 1m 3 (enough to 101 
feed the reactor for few hours to allow the analysis of the system’s performance under 102 
steady state), a centrifugal pump, and a flow meter. The reactor is a 46 cm in length and 103 
3.60 cm in inner -diameter quartz tube illuminated by a UV -C lamp (Philips TUV PL-L 104 
95W/4P HO 1CT/25, λmax=254 nm) placed 10 cm above the tube in the axis of a parabolic 105 
reflector. The total illuminated volume is ca. 470 mL . The UV-C dosages from 19 to 57 106 
J/L, calculated from the experimental value of 95.5 W/m 2 measured with a UV -C 107 
radiometer (Delta OHM, Model HD 2102.2). The assay started when the UV-C radiation 108 
system was switched on, also corresponding to the addition of oxidants (PMS, PS, or HP) 109 
and the metal activator [ Fe(II)]. After reaching steady state o peration, three replicate 110 
samples were collected at 10 minutes of time interval to present results as average data. 111

3. Results and Discussion 170 
Micropollutants detected in this research have been previously detected by other authors 171 
in treated wastewater samples and water bodies (Collado et al., 2014; Luo et al., 2014; 172 
Ben et al., 2014). In general terms, the concentration of the studied MPs are in the same 173 
range than those reported in literature, with the exception of CBZ and SMX, with values 174 
slightly higher than those reported by Collado et al. (2014) and Ben et al. (2018). 175 
However this result may be not surprising taking into account the widespread use and 176 
high refractoriness of these molecules to common wastewater treatments. 177 
3.1. Photolysis of HP, PMS, and PS 178 
The photolysis efficiency of HP, PMS, and PS was investigated on the removal of seven 179 
target MPs detected in real wastewater samples, and the results are shown in Table 1. In 180 
general, these treatments showed an efficiency significantly higher than UV-C radiation 181 
alone (Rodríguez-Chueca et al., 2018a; Rodríguez-Chueca et al., 2018b), although some 182 
exceptions exist, for instance, in the case of IBP for which no degradation was observed 183 
under some operating conditions (photolysis of H2O2, and with molar flux lower than 300 184 
mmol/h of PMS and PS). In this case, just the PMS/UV-C system obtained notabl e 185 
abatement when the highest dosage o f PMS (5 mM) was used (68 ± 3 % and 100% 186 
depending on the UV-C contact time). 187 
CFN exhibited the highest abatement levels in all experimental conditions . Three 188 
oxidation systems reached total removal of CFN with 5 mM as oxidant dose, although 189 
efficiency between 84 to 100% was obtained already using 10-fold less concentration of 190 
oxidants. ATN was also totally abated, but in this case , because of the very low initial 191 
concentration (220 ± 150 ng/L), it was easier to reach the limit of quantification (LOQ). 192

For the remaining MPs (BPA, CBZ, DCF, and SMX) , efficiencies varied depending on 193 
applied oxidation system and operating conditions (oxidant dose and UV-C contact time). 194 
In all of these cases, the c ombination PMS/UV -C reached higher efficiencies than 195 
HP/UV-C and PS/UV-C. These differences were especially evident for DCF, CBZ, and 196 
SMX. Moreover, as expected, the highest abatements were obtained by using the highest 197 
dose of oxidants (5 mM). However, the use of lower PMS doses (0.5 mM) achieved 198 
removal values higher than 90% for DCF and SMX, and in the range from 63 to 83% of 199 
SMX with the other two oxidants. Finally, IBP was not adequately removed under all of 200 
the applied operating conditions in the different photosystems. Indeed, only the PMS/UV-201 
C system was able to achieve partial removal when a dose of 5 mM was used. 202

The application of photolytic activation of PMS and PS to remove organic MPs is widely 216 
reported in the literature (Gao et al., 2012; Tan et al., 2013; Mahdiahmed & Chiron, 2014; 217 
Ao & Liu, 2017; Yang et al., 2017). Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, no 218 
references have yet applied sulfate radicals to different MPs at the same time, with just 219 
seconds of UV-C contact time and with very low dosages of PMS and PS. For instance, 220 
Sharma et al. (2015) reported almost total abatement of BPA (96.7%) with an optimum 221 
dose of PMS of 0.66 mM, which is very close to the dose (0.5 mM) reported here, but 222 
with a higher UV-C contact time (60 min) because of the different reactor configuration 223 
(batch). On the other hand, Khan et al. (2017) reached total removal of lindane after 100 224 
min of treatment with 1 mM of PMS/UV-C. Dhaka et al. (2018) compared the efficiency 225 
of three photolytic systems (HP/UV -C, PS/UV-C, and PMS/UV-C) on the removal of 226 
ethyl paraben. Dhaka et al. ’s (2018) results showed the highest efficiency of PS/UV-C, 227 
followed by HP/UV -C and PMS/UV -C, with the following operating conditions : batch 228 
reactor, clean water samples, and high UV-C contact time (90 min) . On the other hand, 229 
Tan et al. (2013) reported a higher efficiency of the removal of antipyrine from HP/UV-230 
C than from the combination PS/UV-C, with an oxidant dose of 0.5 mM (equal to that 231 
used in this research) and 1 h of reaction time. Furthermore, Ao and Liu (2017) found the 232 
degradation of SMX in Milli-Q water samples spiked wi th 23.69 µM of this antibiotic, 233 
using photolysis of HP, PS , and PMS. With an oxidant dosage of 1 mM and UV -C 234 
dosages of 200 mJ/cm 2, the highest SMX removal rates are for the PMS/UV-C system 235 
followed by PS/UV -C and HP/UV -C. Overall, all of these references point out the 236 
importance of the results of this study, demonstrating the efficiency of photolytic 237 
activation of PMS and PS on the removal, at the same time , of different MPs in a very 238 
complex water matrix (real secondary effluent from WWTP) with low oxidan t dosages 239 
and very low UV-C contact time (9 and 28 seconds) . However, it is also demonstrated 240 
11 
 
that the concentration of PS or PMS required for the removal of organic compounds 241 
changed with the types and initial concentration of organic compounds . This constitutes 242 
a great challenge for the treatment of real wastewater, and the optimal concentration of 243 
PS or PMS should be investigated when dealing with real wastewater (Wang & Wang, 244 
2018). 245 
3.2. Photocatalytic activation of PMS and PS with Fe(II) 246 
Table 2 shows the results of MPs removal after the use of Fe(II) as an activator of PMS 247 
and PS in c ombination with UV -C radiation. In general , it can be observed that the 248 
addition of Fe(II) enhanced the MPs removal. However, because of the high abatement 249 
obtained in photolytic treatments, improvement by Fe(II) was clearly observed over CBZ, 250 
DCF, and IBP only. For instance, in the case of CBZ, the PS/Fe(II)/UV-C (96 – 100%) 251 
system notably increased efficiency compared to the PS/UV-C system (47 – 79%). A 252 
significant enhancement was also found by adding Fe(II) in the PS and PMS systems for 253 
IBP removal. In this case, in the absence of Fe(II), photolytic treatments almost did not 254 
reduce the concentration of IBP; in the presence of Fe(II), however, total abatement was 255 
observed under almost all of the operating conditions. 256 
For the other target MPs (ATN, BPA, and SMX), abatement results by combination of 257 
Fe(II) with PMS and PS were quite similar to those obtained in the absence of Fe(II). For 258 
ATN, CFN and SMX, total or almost total abatement was achieved. It is well known that 259 
the addition of a transition metal as an activator increases the generation rate of free 260 
radicals (Wang & Wang, 2018). The activation of both persulfate salts, PS and PMS, by 261 
metal ions is as follows: 262

3.3. Degree of mineralization of organic matter 298 
In terms of mineralization, Table 3 shows DOC removal. The highest DOC abatement 299 
was shown for PMS/Fe(II)/UV-C, with values ranging from 49 to 62% in all of the tested 300 
operating conditions, while DOC removal was slightly lower with the PS/Fe(II)/UV-C 301 
system (44 – 54%). 302 
Moreover, in contrast with that observed in terms of MPs removal, clear differences 303 
existed regarding whether or not F e(II) is added, especially in the case of PMS. In the 304 
absence of Fe(II), removal efficiency ranged from 17 to 27% using PMS, being slightly 305 
lower than when PS was used (27 – 54%). 306 
These high mineralization rates constitute a promising result. Nevertheless, it is worth 307 
noting that treatments were applied over real treated effluents, with other substances 308

4. Conclusions 370 
From the present study on the efficacy and transformation products of different hydroxyl 371 
and sulfate radical advanced oxidation processes on the degradation of seven MPs 372 
detected in real wastewater samples, the following conclusions can be drawn: 373 
- UV-C-driven SR -AOPs have been proven as a successful technology to reduce 374 
micropollutants concentration in treated wastewater effluents. This technology can be 375 
easily applied in current UV-C reactors to improve the efficiency of the tertiary 376 
treatment for MPs removal for regeneration and water reuse purposes. 377 
- The global efficiency of the MPs degradation is very variable. Some compounds, such 378 
as ATN and CFN , were easily removed (84 – 100%) with low oxidant dosages (0.5 379 
mM) and very low UV-C contact time (9 s). In contrast, other MPs such as BPA, CBZ, 380 
DCF, IBP , and SMX presented a removal efficiency strongly dependant on the 381 
oxidation system and operating conditions (dose and reaction time). Therefore, system 382 
17 
 
operation in real process should be optimized depending on the MPs chosen to monitor 383 
the process efficiency. 384 
- As a general trend, the relative order of efficiency of the studied processes is : 385 
PMS/UV-C > PS/UV -C > H 2O2/UV-C. The addition of Fe(II) as a catalyst clearly 386 
enhanced the removal of those compounds that show low removal efficiencies by the 387 
direct photolysis of oxidants, allowing the almost total degradation of compounds such 388 
as CBZ, DCF, IBP , and SMX. Only in the case of BPA, similar or even lower 389 
efficiencies were obtained in the presence of Fe(II). 390 
- Finally, different transformation products were identified during the degradation of 391 
each MP depending on the oxidation treatment. The use of sulfate radicals leds to the 392 
generation of specific intermediates that suggest different degradation pathways in 393 
comparison with hydroxyl radicals based processes. This could have an impact on the 394 
evolution of the toxicity during the process that should be carefully analysed in future 395 
studies. 396
