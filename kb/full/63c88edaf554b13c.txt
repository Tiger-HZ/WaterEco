Abstract Sedimentary geochemical proxies and carbon stable isotopes of subfossil chironomid head capsules (δ 13 C) were combined to reconstruct long‐term changes in organic matter sources contributing to chironomid biomass in two soft‐water lakes (Vosges Mountains, France). Here, we test how basal carbon sources fueling the benthic food web responded to two millennia of environmental changes. Sedimentary organic matter characteristics were strongly variable over time, and were mainly influenced by human activities within the watershed basin. During the pre‐eutrophication phase, subfossil chironomid δ 13 C (δ 13 C HC ) values were lower than those of sedimentary organic matter.
