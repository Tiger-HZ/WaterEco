ABSTRACT: Lough Neagh is the largest freshwater lake (392km2) in Ireland and Britain, providing drinking water to 40% of Northern Ireland’s population. Recently, however, it has been described as ‘dying’, due mainly to the effects of nutrients enabling eutrophication by blue-green algae. Solutions have been proposed to reduce runoff, but there has been limited discussion of establishing the equivalent of the diverse native woodlands that once existed and protected its catchment, particularly in the buffer zones adjacent to waterways. With a focus on woodland history, we present here the pollen record of a lake sediment core obtained from the deepest part of Lough Neagh.
