Ensuring the safety of drinking water requires not only expanded access but also sustained compliance with physicochemical and microbiological quality standards throughout the supply chain. This study presents a source-to-tap assessment of drinking water quality across major supply schemes serving wards 26–32 of Pokhara Metropolitan City, Nepal. A cross-sectional sampling design was adopted, covering surface and groundwater sources, reservoirs, and household taps operated under both centralized and community-managed systems.
