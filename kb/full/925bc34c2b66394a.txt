The interaction among the quantity of available labour, the speed at which tea leaves can be harvested, and the quality of leaves constitutes the major bottlenecks of every production system in tea harvesting. The tea business advocates mechanisation of tea harvesting due to the shortage of available labour, but quality and alignment of operational activities along with potential business underperformance are still concerning. The vast majority of available literature tends to focus on this topic as a mere performance or adoption problem in a vacuum, without considerations on the intricacies of worker resource shortages and quality governance within the framework of mechanisation. This absence of literature is what this research aims to address through a socio-technical conceptual framework. The socio-technical conceptual framework seeks to address the challenges of preserving quality and labour efficiency with the mechanisation of tea harvesting. This framework employs literature on mechanisation from productivity theory, socio-technical systems theory, task-technology fit, and technology adoption to demonstrate that the intensity of mechanisation can either be a function of or a means of preserving quality with respect to the productivity of labour. Furthermore, it contends that the balanced development of a workforce is a vital measurable variable within the system. The document also provides an outline of several labour-technology configurations which accounts for the differences in achievements among estates that practice the same level of mechanisation.
