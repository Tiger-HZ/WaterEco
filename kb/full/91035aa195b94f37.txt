Open Weights, Open Classrooms: Fine-Tuning Estella Explainer as an Experimental Bench for Usable Math Gattupalli, Sai ; Maloy, Robert ; Edwards, Sharon Something has quietly shifted in artificial intelligence over the past two years, and most public school and college classrooms have not caught up to it yet. For most of the generative AI era, the most capable models have lived behind a login screen––owned, hosted, and metered by a handful of companies, with every question a teacher or student types passing through a server none of them control. That is no longer the only option. Open-weights models––systems whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, inspect, modify, and run on their own hardware––have closed most of the capability gap with proprietary systems, and in some cases surpassed them. Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.6 and DeepSeek's V4 now match up with the best closed models on coding and reasoning benchmarks, while smaller open models from Google, Meta, Alibaba, and Mistral run comfortably on a laptop. This paper is a report from inside that shift, told through one small, specific experiment. For several years, our Usable Math project (usablemath.org) has used a virtual reading coach named Estella Explainer to help elementary and middle school students understand what a math word problem is actually asking––first as a carefully prompted GPT-4 chatbot, and now, for the first time, as an open-weights model we fine-tuned ourselves and published freely on Hugging Face. We describe why we made this move, exactly how we built it (the base model, the fine-tuning method, the data, the design rules), what it produces, and how a teacher with no engineering background could run it entirely on their own laptop using free tools like LM Studio or Jan.ai––with no account, no subscription, and no student data ever leaving the room. We close with what we think this means for a broader audience: privacy-conscious educators, environmentally conscious technologists, and especially the pre-service teachers who are being trained right now, often without ever learning that an alternative to the subscription-based frontier model exists. We are not claiming a finished product or a classroom trial. We are reporting an initial experiment openly in the hope that other education researchers will treat open weights not as a niche development but as an exciting direction for educational research. 2026-08 Loading... Disinformation-for-Hire: Challenges Addressing the Industrial Dimension of Media Manipulation Ong, Jonathan Corpus ; Lanuza, Jose Mari Hall Disinformation-for-hire refers to the burgeoning industry of influence operators offering services of online community mobilization and media manipulation to help clients achieve their communication objectives. As an analytical frame, it shifts disinformation studies away from normative debates about true or false content and legal definitions of hate speech and toward exploring the business operations and organizational architectures of disinformation campaigns. Reviewing mainstream disinformation studies and the critique offered up by the disinformation-for-hire framework, this chapter discusses three areas where mainstream and for-hire frameworks diverge in explaining the problem of disinformation and identifying corresponding solutions. Allowing Generative AI in Class: Evidence from a Semester-Long Controlled Teaching Study Rojas, Christian ; Rong, Rong ; Bloomfield, Luke We report a controlled, semester-long teaching experiment in an upper-division antitrust economics course. Two back-to-back sections were held constant in content, assessment, and grading; they differed only in policy and guidance on generative AI: one section was permitted to use AI with disclosure and structured training, the other was prohibited from using AI and received parallel non-AI study guidance (n = 29 vs. n = 28). We find no detectable effect of AI permission on proctored exam scores or final grades. By contrast, AI access is associated with higher engagement on in-class activities, longer and more concentrated AI sessions in other courses (15–30 minutes), greater metacognitive behaviors (preferring one’s own answers, catching errors, modi- fying outputs), more positive perceptions—especially regarding efficiency, confidence, and engagement—and stronger intentions to continue using and studying AI, as well as choosing AI-intensive careers. Standardized course evaluations are also consistently higher in the AI section. In this dissertation I aim to contribute to re-materializing queer theory with land - both by looking at activist epistemologies of sexual politics rooted in the politics of land and redistribution and by asking what Black queer activists making claims to land in Cape Town unsettle. I trace how the shelter faces ongoing pressure from homeowners and municipal authorities to relocate "closer to the people they serve"—a racially coded suggestion that characterizes Cape Town’s post-Apartheid political economic and spatial divisions. Reading the area as an archive through what I term the palimpsest of displacement enables me to consider how queer and transgender exclusion is constituted through interlocking spatial struggles across time and space that are not usually read together. My thesis is that the queer liberal subject is operationalized and thus produced in relation to land dispossession and displacement, and that Black queer claims to land challenge both the formation of the subject, the fixity of private property and the regulation of the commons. This is because Black queer claims to land destabilize liberal identity politics by challenging the technology of the individual - the Enlightenment ideas of rights, responsibility and private property still central to neoliberalism - by surfacing the limits of homosexual and transgender inclusion into normative racial capitalism formed through colonial logics of dispossession. While my study is at the municipal level, the methodological intervention contributes to Queer diasporic and Queer Global Anthropology to think with fields such as Queer of Colour and African Queer Studies about how queer studies could engage more directly with how the ‘post-Apartheid’ city is shaped by ongoing settler-colonial structures. Show more Publication Embargo GIs in Jail: Military Dissent and Incarceration During the Vietnam War ( 2026-05 ) Kershner, Seth Show more The U.S. military’s prison system functioned during the Vietnam era to control dissent among servicemembers who were at that time disobeying orders, going AWOL or deserting in record numbers. At its peak, in 1968, the military held around 15,000 of its own soldiers, sailors and marines in a prison system that spanned the globe. This dissertation argues that Vietnam era U.S. military prisons have much to teach historians both about the breadth and scale of GI resistance, and the persistence of militarized tactics in the civilian prison sector. 
The extent of dissent among U.S. servicemen during the Vietnam War was unprecedented in American history, encompassing refusal of duty, desertion, and labor organizing. For their actions, those associated with what historians call the GI movement spent lengthy terms of confinement—years, in some cases—in the military prison system. This dissertation makes several contributions to the literature on GI dissent during the Vietnam War. First, by centering the brig and stockade as a locus of revolt, the dissertation reveals how dissident soldiers, sailors and marines continued to agitate and organize even while incarcerated. Moreover, while previous scholarship has emphasized the violent rebellions that took place at military prisons, this research accents nonviolent methods of protest—hunger strikes, radical reading groups and underground publications—that have received less attention from historians and explores how the military prison system reacted to this activism. 
The dissertation also deepens our understanding of the GI Movement by exploring how incarcerated GIs received support and expressions of solidarity from civilian peace activists. By revealing how incarcerated GIs received encouragement from a “free world” network that included civilian peace activists, civil liberties groups, and other social movements, this dissertation complicates the familiar story of how, when, and to what extent the civilian peace movement linked up with American GIs. 
By tracing how forms of penal domination evolved over time, this dissertation also shows how prison officials adapted to activism and organizing behind bars by launching experimental projects aimed at deradicalizing dissident GIs and restoring them to duty in the Army. A final chapter, explores how tactical innovations developed to quell uprisings in U.S. military prisons during the Vietnam War were later widely adopted across the American penal field, contributing to the militarization of American prisons. That technology and expertise developed to quell dissent among incarcerated servicemen continues to play a vital role in civilian prisons demonstrates the enduring relevance of military prisons, despite their near invisibility in contemporary criminal justice discourse. Show more Publication Embargo THE ASSEMBLY OF CHARGED LINEAR AND BOTTLEBRUSH POLYMERS AT THE INTERFACES ( 2026-05 ) Jin, Zichen Show more Assembly at the liquid-liquid interface is the spontaneous organization of molecules or particles at the boundaries between two immiscible liquids. This process is driven by the minimization of free energy and can easily be seen in biological processes like membrane formation and viral assembly.1–5 By utilizing pair-interaction, such as electrostatic interaction, and self-assembly of functional polymers, liquid-liquid interfacial assembly opens new platforms for nanotechnology and material synthesis for optical, electronic, and magnetic applications.6–8 Previous investigations within our group have explored interfaces for electronic and ion transport in all-fluid systems primarily using nanoparticles to stabilize the interfacial interaction.9–18 In this work, we presented an extended investigation into how polymer conformation and architecture (linear polyelectrolytes, bottlebrush polyelectrolytes, and linear copolymers) impact interfacial assembly in the absence of nanoparticles at both water-water and water-oil interfaces. 
We introduced a physically crosslinked gel matrix into polyelectrolyte complexes consisting of poly(2-(dimethylamino) ethyl methacrylate) (PDMAEMA), poly (sodium 4-styrenesulfonate)) (PSS) and alginate. When printed at all-aqueous interface, this hybrid system significantly enhanced mechanical integrity and selective ion transport membrane. To address the inherent instability of weak polyelectrolytes compared with strong polyelectrolyte complexes like PSS and PDADMAC (poly (diallyldimethylammonium chloride), we investigated bottlebrush polyanions with linear polycations. These new architectural combinations increased electrostatic interactions to achieve superior salt resistance and robust interfacial stability exceeding 48 hours, facilitating ion transport and drug delivery. 
Moving from homopolymer to copolymer, we also established a predictive relationship between bulk conformation and interfacial thermodynamics using solvent-sensitive copolymers. We demonstrated that flexible Gaussian chains in chloroform formed stable viscoelastic network at water-oil interfaces, whereas star-micelles in toluene favor desorption and interfacial instability. By correlating solubility parameters and block architecture with interfacial behavior, this work provided a framework for designing robust, all-fluid functional materials based on bulk polymer characteristics. Show more Publication Embargo MODELING AND OPTIMIZING RIDE-SOURCING ROUTING UNDER A SEMI-MARKOV DECISION FRAMEWORK ( 2026-05 ) Jiang, Guocheng Show more Ride-sourcing platforms such as Uber and Lyft have reshaped on-demand urban mobility, yet their efficiency critically depends on how vacant vehicles move through large and heterogeneous transportation networks. This dissertation develops and integrates two complementary semi-Markov decision process (SMDP) frameworks to model vacant vehicle routing behavior and to optimize fleet-level routing policies, together with an empirical study on real-time travel information (RTTI) for public transit recovery.
The first part of the dissertation formulates a structural, infinite-horizon SMDP model for vacant taxi routing and estimates drivers’ turn-by-turn behavior on a
large road network. Unlike traditional Markov decision process (MDP) approaches that impose a uniform transition time, the SMDP framework accommodates link-dependent holding times arising from both link traversal and passenger trips. A joint estimation procedure is developed to simultaneously recover routing preferences and an endogenous passenger–vehicle matching function. Theoretical results generalize classic dynamic discrete choice properties—including the existence of optimal stationary policies and the uniqueness of the fixed point for value functions—to the SMDP setting. Using large-scale GPS trajectory data from Shanghai, the model yields behaviorally interpretable estimates and demonstrates the advantages of capturing multiple pickup and drop-off cycles and endogenous competition.
The second part optimizes vacant vehicle routing at the fleet level. State transition probabilities depend endogenously on the stationary distribution of vacant vehicles and the resulting matching rates, forming a fixed-point problem solved through a scalable vectorized algorithm. Existence and uniqueness results are established for both the stationary distribution and the average-reward gradient. The method is implemented on a metropolitan-scale network with over 30,000 links, and numerical experiments show substantial performance gains over baseline heuristics and derivative-free optimization, with further improvements enabled by gradient-based methods.
The final part examines the role of Outdoor Information Panels (OIPs) in disseminating RTTI to highway drivers as a means to support post-pandemic transit ridership recovery. Through interviews, a household survey, and regional emissions analysis, the study identifies the most influential information items and highlights the potential of OIPs as a cost-effective communication channel for reaching non-users.
Collectively, this dissertation advances the modeling and optimization of routing behavior in on-demand mobility systems and provides methodological and empirical insights with broader implications for urban transportation operations. Show more Publication Restricted GUT MICROBIOTA-MEDIATED DEGRADATION OF FOOD-GRADE LAMBDA-CARRAGEENAN BY Bacteroides xylanisolvens AND ITS IMPACT ON HOST HEALTH ( 2026-05 ) Guo, Xiaojing Show more Lambda-carrageenan (L-CGN) is a widely used food-grade additive commonly applied as a thickener and stabilizer in processed foods. Although food-grade carrageenan has long been considered biologically inert due to its high molecular weight and minimal absorption, growing evidence suggests that interactions with the gut microbiota may alter its biological effects. However, whether gut microbiota-mediated degradation of L-CGN generates harmful products and raises health risks has remained poorly understood. This dissertation aimed to systematically investigate the role of gut microbiota in degrading food-grade L-CGN and to elucidate the downstream effects of this process on host health. 
Using an integrated approach combining microbial isolation, in vitro cell models, and in vivo mouse studies, this work identified and functionally characterized gut bacterial strains capable of utilizing L-CGN. Two isolates, Bacteroides xylanisolvens C3 and S11, demonstrated robust growth on L-CGN as the sole carbon source and exhibited clear degradation activity, accompanied by carbohydrate depletion, medium acidification, and distinct short-chain fatty acid production profiles. These findings established that L-CGN could be actively metabolized by specific gut bacteria in a strain-dependent manner.
To evaluate the biological consequences of microbial degradation, metabolites derived from bacterial-mediated L-CGN degradation were applied to RAW264.7 macrophages. L-CGN-derived metabolites significantly increased nitric oxide production and induced the expression of pro-inflammatory genes, including Il-6, Il-1β, and Tnf-α, compared with controls. These responses were associated with activation of NF-κB-mediated inflammatory signaling and Nrf2-related oxidative stress pathways. Notably, bacteria-derived degraded carrageenans (dCGNs) generated during L-CGN fermentation were identified as key mediators of immune activation.
The physiological relevance of these findings was further validated in mice. Co-exposure to L-CGN and degrading bacteria resulted in pronounced intestinal inflammation, characterized by epithelial damage, immune cell infiltration and activation of inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways. These changes were accompanied by increased circulating endotoxin and elevated systemic cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-6, and MCP-1, indicating systemic inflammation. Furthermore, co-exposure promoted hepatic inflammation and early fibrotic responses, as evidenced by histopathological alterations and upregulation of fibrosis-related genes such as Tgf-β1, Col3, and Timp1.
Mechanistically, this dissertation identified gut microbiota-mediated degradation as a critical determinant of L-CGN bioactivity. The generation of dCGN and associated metabolites drove inflammatory and oxidative stress responses in a strain-dependent manner, highlighting the importance of microbial functional variability in shaping host outcomes. Across all experimental systems, combined exposure to L-CGN and degrading bacteria consistently produced stronger biological effects than L-CGN or bacteria alone.
Collectively, this dissertation demonstrated that food-grade L-CGN was not biologically inert but could be transformed by gut microbiota into bioactive products that disrupted intestinal homeostasis and promoted systemic inflammation. These findings revealed a previously underappreciated mechanism linking dietary additives, gut microbial metabolism, and host health, and underscored the necessity of incorporating microbiota-mediated transformation into the safety assessment of carrageenan and other food additives.
