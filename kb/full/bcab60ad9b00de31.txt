Let's begin (where Gurnah often ends) at the fissure where the empire ebbs without vanishing, flowing onward into the ceremonial birth of the postcolonial nation-state. In his speech at the Independence Ceremony of Tanganyika on 9 December 1961, Julius Nyerere declared: I have listened to your Highness's expressions of goodwill and I have received these Instruments which are the embodiment of my country's freedom with the deepest emotion. This is the day for which we have looked so long, the day when every Tanganyikan can say, ‘I am a citizen of a sovereign, independent state.’ ( Freedom and Unity 142)
