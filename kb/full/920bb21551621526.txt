Purpose: This study examines the practice of bush burning and its multifaceted impacts on the environment. The objective is to synthesise existing literature on the patterns, causes, and impacts of bush burning on air and soil quality, thereby highlighting gaps and proposing sustainable mitigation strategies. Design/Methodology/Approach: The study adopts a narrative literature review approach. Relevant past articles and conference papers were reviewed to present current knowledge on bush burning, its environmental impacts, and possible mitigation strategies. Research Limitation: The review is largely dependent on published, peer-reviewed literature, which tends to favour studies reporting significant or positive findings. Finding: The evidence indicates that bush burning represents a major anthropogenic disturbance with both localised and global implications. Atmospherically, biomass combustion releases substantial quantities of greenhouse gases, including carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O), as well as particulate matter and black carbon. Practical Implication: The review emphasises the need for stricter legislation and enforcement to control indiscriminate bush burning. Social Implication: Educating communities about the negative consequences and promoting alternative land clearing methods are crucial steps. Originality / Value: This study's originality lies in synthesising evidence from multiple impact domains to demonstrate that bush burning is a significant anthropogenic disturbance with far-reaching, interconnected consequences.
