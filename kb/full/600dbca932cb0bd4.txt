Effects of animals on the transport of solids (e.g., sediments) can modify earth‐surface processes and landforms (role as geomorphic agents) and resources for other organisms (role as ecosystem engineers). Therefore, we studied the impact of crayfish ( Orconectes limosus ) behavior on sand‐gravel erosion and bottom habitat over riffle‐pool sequences in experimental streams. We manipulated the availability of crayfish food and refugia. Refugia availability had clear effects on overall crayfish activity. The establishment of dominance hierarchies among the crayfish produced patterns in crayfish activity that differed between riffles and pools.
