Abstract Black spruce (Picea mariana) dominates the boreal forest of North America, provides important wildlife habitat, and contributes substantially to long-term carbon storage. Wildfire shifts forest composition immediately following fire, but under climate change an intensifying wildfire regime may hinder black spruce regeneration and drive persistent compositional change away from black spruce. Further, wildfire can lead to permafrost thaw with unknown implications for forest regeneration. We monitored post-fire regeneration in black spruce stands in the Northwest Territories, Canada in permafrost and permafrost-free areas and explored how permafrost thaw rate (increased active layer thickness) impacted tree composition and density.
