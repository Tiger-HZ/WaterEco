Statistical methods were developed for analyzing the results of a study of pathogen densities for sludge samples taken over the four stages of the solids processing trains (SPTs) operating at the Stickney and Calumet Water Reclamation Plants of the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District of Greater Chicago, Illinois (District). These methods also apply to pathogen density studies for other biosolids processes. Analysis of covariance models were used to estimate expected pathogen densities for individual solids processing stages. Cross‐validation was used to select appropriate analysis of covariance models. Nonparametric methods were used to estimate distributions for pathogen density reductions between solids processing stages and to assess the effect of hypothetical surges and expansions
