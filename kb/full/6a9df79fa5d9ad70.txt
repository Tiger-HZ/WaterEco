Abstract 23 
Nutrient enrichment threatens freshwater biodiversity by altering community assembly, 24 
reducing taxonomic diversity, and affecting functional redundancy, undermining 25 
ecosystem stability. However, how seasonal nutrient dynamics impact multitrophic 26 
biodiversity and functional redundancy is unclear. Using environmental DNA (fish, 27 
benthic macroinvertebrates, zooplankton, phytoplankton) and environmental data from 28 
a shallow lake in China's North Temperate Zone during dry and rainy seasons, we 29 
investigated these effects. Nutrient enrichment (Trophic Level Index) was higher in the 30 
rainy season, coinciding with significant seasonal variation in multitrophic community 31 
structure, particularly plankton spatial heterogeneity. Nutrient enrichment increased 32 
taxonomic and functional diversity across trophic levels, with higher values in the rainy 33 
season, which enhanced plankton functional redundancy. Moreover, seasonal nutrient 34 
variation shifted community assembly from deterministic to stochastic processes under 35 
higher enrichment. Our findings highlight seasonal nutrient enrichment as a key driver 36 
of multitrophic aquatic biodiversity, assembly mechanisms, and functional redundancy. 37

1. Introduction 42 
In the context of global climate change, the increasing frequency of extreme rainfall 43 
and drought events has profoundly reshaped the structure and function of freshwater 44 
ecosystems by altering hydrological conditions and precipitation patterns(Dahal et al., 45 
2025; Lu et al., 2026). Beyond direct habitat disruption, climate change exacerbates 46 
indirect environmental stressors, such as nutrient enrichment, through pathways 47 
involving modified surface runoff and intensified land use(Farooq et al., 2025; Tripathi-48 
II et al., 2025). As one of the most vulnerable habitats on Earth, freshwater ecosystems 49 
exhibit high sensitivity to these anthropogenic and climatic pressures, which affect their 50 
community structure and ecological processes(Lyu et al., 2025; Navaandorj et al., 2025; 51 
Nimma et al., 2025). Consequently, research on the relationship between biodiversity 52 
maintenance mechanisms and environmental stresses has emerged as a critical issue 53 
that necessitates urgent and in-depth exploration within the current global ecological 54 
field. 55 
Nutrient enrichment has emerged as a significant global issue, posing a serious 56 
threat to biodiversity and the stability of the ecosystems it supports(Dubey and Dutta, 57 
2019; Isbell et al., 2013). Seasonal precipitation differences induced by climate change 58 
represent a key driving force regulating the dynamics of nutrient enrichment in aquatic 59 
systems(Ristea et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2025). This seasonal regulatory effect is 60 
particularly pronounced in shallow lake and wetland ecosystems(Morant et al., 2024; 61 
Zhang et al., 2025a). Abundant precipitation during the rainy season generates 62 
substantial surface runoff, significantly increasing the input intensity of exogenous 63 
nutrients and leading to a sharp rise in the degree of nutrient enrichment over a short 64 
period(Barathan et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2023). Conversely, during the dry season, 65 
reduced precipitation extends water retention time and enhances the concentration of 66 
nutrients, while the release of endogenous nutrients from sediments intensifies(Chen et 67 
al., 2024; Özen et al., 2010). This process facilitates a sustained accumulation of 68 
nutrients, resulting in a state of nutrient enrichment that markedly differs from that of 69 
the rainy season(Lin et al., 2021). 70 
The multidimensional response of biodiversity to environmental stressors is 71 
fundamental for maintaining the stability of ecosystem functions(Schäfer et al., 2025; 72 
Sun et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2024). Functional redundancy serves as a crucial buffering 73 
mechanism within ecosystems, reflecting the capacity of multiple species to perform 74 
similar ecological functions(Gaikwad et al., 2026; Wohl et al., 2004). Seasonal 75 
fluctuations in nutrient enrichment may directly regulate community redundancy levels 76 
by altering species composition and diversity, thereby affecting ecosystem stability in 77 
the face of species loss(Zhang et al., 2025b). Furthermore, environmental changes 78 
driven by nutrient inputs can significantly impact community assembly processes, such 79 
as enhancing environmental filtering, altering dispersal limitations, and modifying 80 
interspecific interactions(Wen et al., 2025). Notably, these processes do not occur in 81 
isolation within ecosystems; rather, they interact through interconnections and 82 
cascading effects across trophic levels, transmitting impacts through 'bottom-up' or 'top-83 
down' effects, thus collaboratively shaping the composition and distribution patterns of 84 
biotic communities across different trophic levels(Faburé et al., 2025). Therefore, 85 
focusing solely on a single trophic level is insufficient for comprehensively revealing 86 
the overall impacts of nutrient enrichment on ecosystem structure and function; 87 
systematic studies across trophic levels are essential for gaining a deeper understanding 88 
of its ecological effects and mechanisms. 89 
Baiyangdian, a typical shallow lake-wetland complex ecosystem in the North 90 
China Plain, has recently exhibited significant seasonal variations in nutrient 91 
enrichment due to the combined effects of climate change and human activities(Cheng 92 
et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2025). This distinctive seasonal dynamic serves as an ideal 93 
natural laboratory for systematically investigating how nutrient enrichment regulates 94 
the biodiversity and community structure of multitrophic aquatic organisms. To 95 
elucidate the mechanisms by which seasonal nutrient enrichment influences 96 
biodiversity and ecological processes in shallow lakes, this study focuses on 97 
Baiyangdian Lake, emphasizing the impacts of nutrient enrichment disparities between 98 
the dry and rainy seasons on aquatic organisms across multiple trophic levels, including 99 
fish, benthic macroinvertebrates, and algae. The specific research objectives are: (1) to 100 
clarify the effects of nutrient enrichment during the dry and rainy seasons on 101 
biodiversity and community structure across multiple trophic levels; (2) to reveal the 102 
regulatory role of seasonal biodiversity changes on functional redundancy; and (3) to 103 
analyze the differences in the effects of nutrient enrichment levels during the dry and 104 
rainy seasons on the mechanisms of community assembly for organisms across multiple 105 
trophic levels. 106

2. Materials and methods 108 
2.1 Study area 109 
Field surveys were conducted at 44 sampling stations across Baiyangdian Lake 110 
(115°38′-116°07′E, 38°43′-39°02′N) (Fig. 1) between August 2023 and April 2024. As 111 
the largest freshwater lacustrine wetland in the Haihe Plain of China, this expansive 112 
system spans 366 km², with a mean storage capacity of 1.32 billion m³ and an average 113 
depth of 5.5-5.6 m(Han and Bu, 2023). It plays a critical role in climate mitigation, 114 
pollution control, and hydrological regulation, while also serving as a vital migratory 115 
corridor for rare avian species(Gao and Yang, 2025; Tian et al., 2025). 116 
Baiyangdian Lake experiences a typical temperate semi-arid continental monsoon 117 
climate, characterized by four distinct seasons and synchronous rainfall and warmth, 118 
with a multi-year average temperature of 12.3°C(Wenfeng et al., 2026). In recent years, 119 
however, influenced by climate change, the average annual precipitation has been 120 
recorded at 570.2 mm, while the average annual evaporation reaches 1369 mm, 121 
significantly exceeding the precipitation(Zhou et al., 2025). Furthermore, 122 
approximately 80% of the rainfall occurs in July and August, when precipitation 123 
exceeds temperature. In contrast, from March to June and September to November, 124 
rainfall is low, and temperature exceeds precipitation(Han and Bu, 2023). This climatic 125 
pattern has contributed to a gradual reduction in the lake's area and depth over the years. 126 
Concurrently, intensified human activities in the surrounding regions have increased 127 
nutrient loading into the lake due to agricultural and urban pollution(Liu and Zhang, 128 
2024). As a result of both climate change and anthropogenic pressures, the nutrient 129 
enrichment of the water body in this area has become increasingly severe. 130 
2.2 Data collection 131 
2.2.1 Environmental factors 132 
A total of 12 environmental variables were measured. The latitude and longitude of 133 
each site were recorded using a MAGELLAN GPS system. Water quality parameters 134 
including pH, water temperature, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, and turbidity (NTU) 135 
were measured on site with a portable multiparameter meter (LH-MUP230, V11), while 136 
water transparency was determined using a Secchi disk. From each sampling site, a 5 L 137 
water sample was collected, transported to the laboratory, and analyzed within 24 hours 138 
following collection. Permanganate index (COD Mn), total phosphorus (TP), total 139 
nitrogen (TN), ammonium nitrogen (NH 3-N), chlorophyll-a (Chl-a), and chemical 140 
oxygen demand (COD) were determined according to the protocols specified in the 141 
"Water and Wastewater Monitoring and Analysis Methods" (Fourth 142 
Edition)(Administration, 2002) 143 
2.2.2 Collection and processing of eDNA 144 
To ensure the reliability of community characterization and minimize stochastic 145 
sampling bias, three replicate surface water samples (2 L each) were collected at each 146 
of the 44 sampling stations per survey event using sterile polyethylene bottles. Samples 147 
were immediately stored in insulated containers and processed within a 6-hour window. 148 
Each 2 L water sample was filtered independently through a 0.45 μm pore size, 50 mm 149 
diameter membrane via a vacuum filtration system. The resulting three filter 150 
membranes per site were individually transferred into 2 mL sterile microcentrifuge 151 
tubes and cryopreserved at temperatures ranging from −20°C to −80°C pending 152 
genomic DNA extraction. Total environmental DNA (eDNA) was extracted from each 153 
replicate membrane using the DNeasy PowerWater Kit (QIAGEN, Germany). To 154 
monitor for potential cross-contamination, a 1 L ultrapure water field blank was filtered 155 
and processed alongside the triplicate samples at each sampling occasion. 156 
 157 
To characterize multitrophic diversity, the 18S rRNA (V7), COI, and 12S rRNA gene 158 
regions were amplified using Euka02F/02R, fwhF2/EPTDr2n, and Tele02F/02R 159 
primers, respectively. Following purification, the PCR products from the three 160 
biological replicates were pooled for subsequent sequencing to obtain an integrated 161 
view of the local community, while the raw replicates were retained for evaluating 162 
within-site heterogeneity. Sequencing was performed on an Illumina PE250 platform at 163 
Genewiz Co., Ltd. (Suzhou, China). Raw sequences were quality filtered via fastp and 164 
merged through FLASH based on overlapping regions. Using the QIIME2 pipeline, 165 
clean reads were clustered into Operational Taxonomic Units (OTUs) at a 97% 166 
similarity threshold, with the simultaneous removal of chimeras and singletons. 167 
Taxonomic assignment was performed using the RDP Classifier against the PR2 168 
(plankton), NCBI/local COI (aquatic insects), and MitoFish (fish) databases to 169 
determine community composition and relative abundance across all sampling sites. 170

2.2.3 Functional traits 173 
To investigate the impacts of nutrient enrichment on functional diversity, fish, benthic 174 
macroinvertebrates, and plankton were categorized into distinct functional guilds based 175 
on a suite of biological traits (Table S1)(Branco et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024; Villéger et 176 
al., 2017; Wu et al., 2019). A trait based framework was established using 8 functional 177 
traits for fish, 11 for benthic macroinvertebrates, and 5 each for zooplankton and 178 
phytoplankton, all selected for their strong linkages to ecosystem processes and 179 
representative consistency across the studied taxa. These attributes, inextricably 180 
coupled with life history strategies and habitat preferences, serve as salient indicators 181 
of community responses to environmental perturbations, particularly those induced by 182 
nutrient enrichment. 183

2.4 Biodiversity 212 
For taxonomic alpha diversity (α-TD), four indices: Species Richness (SR), the 213 
Shannon-Wiener Index (H'), Simpson's Diversity Index (D), and Pielou's Evenness 214 
Index (J)—were calculated from the OTU abundance matrix generated by eDNA 215 
analysis, using the “ vegan” package in R(Oksanen J, 2026). For functional alpha 216 
diversity, the dbFD function in the “FD” package was employed to compute Functional 217 
Richness (FRic), Functional Evenness (FEve), Functional Divergence (FDiv), and 218 
Functional Dispersion (FDis) (Laliberté E, 2010; Lalibert é E, 2014). 219 
2.5 Statistical Analyses 220 
Seasonal variations in TLI and biodiversity between the dry and rainy seasons were 221 
evaluated via Wilcoxon rank sum tests, with the results visualized through boxplots. 222 
Simultaneously, Principal Coordinate Analysis (PCoA) within the “vegan” package was 223 
employed to delineate shifts in community structures across the four biological taxa. 224 
Pearson correlation coefficients between the five nutrient enrichment related 225 
parameters and biodiversity indices were quantified using the psych and pheatmap 226 
packages. Significance was assessed via two tailed tests (* p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p 227 
< 0.001), followed by the generation of hierarchically clustered heatmaps to identify 228 
associations between environmental drivers and community attributes. 229 
Based on a Random Forest model, the contributions of biodiversity metrics to 230 
functional redundancy were assessed to identify significant variables for Partial Least 231 
Squares Path Modeling (PLS-PM). Using the " plspm" package, this path analysis 232 
clarified the causal relationships among TLI, biodiversity, and functional redundancy, 233 
while also highlighting the role of latent variables in the overall model fit (Sanchez G, 234 
2022). 235 
The relative importance of stochastic versus deterministic processes in shaping the 236 
four biotic communities was determined using the Dispersal Niche Continuum Index 237 
(DNCI) via the “ DNCImper” package (Vilmi et al., 2021). Calculated from 238 
presence/absence data, the DNCI serves to quantify assembly processes, where positive 239 
and negative values signify the prevalence of niche based and dispersal based 240 
mechanisms, respectively. Statistical execution of all models was carried out within the 241 
R environment (version 4.4.1). 242 
3. Results 243 
3.1 Seasonal variations in nutrient enrichment levels and community structure 244 
TLI scores ranged from 49.54 to 63.47 during the dry season, indicating a generally 245 
light eutrophic state, whereas they escalated to between 61.94 and 76.76 in the rainy 246 
season, signaling a shift toward heavy nutrient enrichment. Wilcoxon rank sum tests 247 
confirmed that TLI values in the rainy season were significantly elevated compared to 248 
the dry season (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 2). 249 
 Biological surveys yielded 48 fish, 17 benthic macroinvertebrates, 212 250 
phytoplankton, and 97 zooplankton species during the dry season, compared to 44 fish, 251 
17 benthic macroinvertebrates, 222 phytoplankton, and 78 zooplankton species 252 
identified during the rainy season. PERMANOV A results underscored significant 253 
spatial heterogeneity in multitrophic aquatic community structures across both seasons 254 
(p < 0.001) (Fig. S2). Notably, the most pronounced spatial divergence was observed 255 
within the plankton communities (Fig. S2c, d). 256 
3.2 Effects of nutrient enrichment on multitrophic aquatic biodiversity 257 
Pearson correlation analysis revealed that environmental factors (TP, TN, Chl-a, 258 
CODMn and SD) exerted substantial influence on multitrophic aquatic biodiversity, 259 
underscoring that nutrient enrichment significantly reshapes community diversity 260 
across multiple levels. During the dry season, TN was positively correlated with fish 261 
FDiv (p < 0.01), SR (p < 0.05), and FRic (p < 0.01), while fish FDiv exhibited a positive 262 
association with TP (p < 0.05), and SD showed a positive correlation with fish FEve (p 263 
< 0.05). Furthermore, Chl-a displayed significant positive correlations with fish H' and 264 
J (p < 0.05) (Fig. 2a). Regarding benthic macroinvertebrates in the dry season, SD was 265 
negatively correlated with H' and J (p < 0.05), whereas TP showed a significant positive 266 
correlation with FRic ( p < 0.01); additionally, H', J, and FDis were all positively 267 
associated with COD Mn (p < 0.01, p < 0.05, p < 0.05, respectively), while Chl-a was 268 
negatively correlated with FDiv ( p < 0.05) (Fig. 2b). For zooplankton, TP exhibited 269 
positive correlations with J, FDiv, and FDis (p < 0.05, p < 0.01, p < 0.01, respectively), 270 
with CODMn also showing positive associations with H' and SR (p < 0.05) (Fig. 2c). In 271 
phytoplankton communities, TN correlated positively with FDiv ( p < 0.05), SD 272 
exhibited a positive relationship with FEve ( p < 0.01), and FDiv, FDis, and H' all 273 
showed positive correlations with TP ( p < 0.05); notably, COD Mn was negatively 274 
correlated with FEve (p < 0.01) but positively with SR (p < 0.01) (Fig. 2d). 275 
In contrast, during the rainy season, SD exhibited pronounced negative correlations 276 
with fish FDiv (p < 0.01), SR (p < 0.05), FRic (p < 0.01), J (p < 0.01), H' (p < 0.001), 277 
and FDis (p < 0.01), while fish FDiv ( p < 0.05), SR ( p < 0.01), and FRic ( p < 0.001) 278 
were positively correlated with TN, and CODMn showed negative correlations with fish 279 
SR (p < 0.05) and FRic ( p < 0.01) (Fig. 2e). Macrozoobenthos FDis was negatively 280 
associated with CODMn (p < 0.05), with SD also showing negative correlations with SR 281 
and FRic (p < 0.01, p < 0.05), whereas TN maintained positive correlations with SR, 282 
FRic, and H' (p < 0.01, p < 0.01, p < 0.05) (Fig. 2f). For rainy season zooplankton, SD 283 
correlated positively with SR ( p < 0.05), Chl-a was positively associated with SR and 284 
FRic (p < 0.01, p < 0.001), and FDis showed a negative correlation with TN (p < 0.05) 285 
(Fig. 2g). Finally, SD was positively correlated with phytoplankton FEve ( p < 0.05) 286 
(Fig. 2h). 287 
3.3 The relationship between nutrient enrichment, biodiversity and functional 288 
redundancy 289 
Prior to the PLS-PM analysis, Random Forest models were employed to identify the 290 
multitrophic biodiversity indices that contributed most significantly to functional 291 
redundancy (FRe). For the dry season, the selected indices included the H' for fish (Fig. 292 
S5a), H' and FDis for benthic macroinvertebrates (Fig. S5b), and FRic for both 293 
zooplankton and phytoplankton (Fig. S5c, d). In the rainy season, the optimal predictors 294 
shifted to FEve and SR for fish (Fig. S5e), SR for benthic macroinvertebrates (Fig. S5f), 295 
H' and FRic for zooplankton (Fig. S5g), and J for phytoplankton (Fig. S5h). 296 
Subsequently, PLS-PM was constructed to disentangle the complex relationships 297 
between nutrient enrichment (TLI), biodiversity, and functional redundancy across 298 
seasons. After iterative optimization of the structural and measurement models, the final 299 
Goodness of Fit (GoF) values reached 0.45 and 0.39 for the dry and rainy seasons, 300 
respectively (Fig. 3a, c). 301 
In the dry season, the TLI exerted a direct positive effect on the functional richness 302 
of phytoplankton and zooplankton (coefficient = 0.78, p < 0.001), while phytoplankton 303 
FRic, in turn, imposed a direct negative regulation on functional redundancy 304 
(coefficient = -0.51, p < 0.05). Beyond these indirect pathways mediated by biodiversity, 305 
TLI also manifested a direct positive influence on functional redundancy (coefficient = 306 
0.21, p < 0.05). Furthermore, fish Shannon-Wiener diversity was found to directly 307 
enhance functional redundancy (coefficient = 0.60, p < 0.01) (Fig. 3a, b). 308 
In the rainy season model, TLI maintained a direct positive effect on functional 309 
redundancy (coefficient = 0.20, p < 0.05) while significantly increasing zooplankton H' 310 
and FRic (coefficient = 0.59, p < 0.01). This elevation in zooplankton diversity directly 311 
and positively contributed to functional redundancy (coefficient = 0.74, p < 0.01). 312 
Contrasting with the dry season, phytoplankton evenness (J) was found to negatively 313 
regulate functional redundancy during the rainy season (coefficient = -0.57, p < 0.01) 314 
(Fig. 3c, d). 315 
3.4 Community assembly mechanisms driven by seasonal nutrient enrichment 316 
The relative contributions of stochastic and deterministic processes to multitrophic 317 
aquatic community assembly were quantified using the Dispersal Niche Continuum 318 
Index (DNCI). In the dry season, DNCI values for all multitrophic communities were 319 
consistently greater than zero, demonstrating that deterministic processes served as the 320 
overarching mechanism governing community assembly across all taxa (Fig. 4). 321 
In contrast, during the rainy season, while fish communities maintained DNCI 322 
values above zero, the indices for all other taxonomic groups exhibited a marked 323 
decline relative to the dry season. Specifically, the DNCI values for benthic 324 
macroinvertebrates and plankton shifted to below zero, indicating a fundamental 325 
transition in assembly mechanisms where stochastic processes became the dominant 326 
drivers of community structure in these groups (Fig. 4). 327 
4. Discussion 328 
In the context of global change, nutrient enrichment and seasonal hydrological 329 
fluctuations profoundly reshape the biodiversity and functional structure of freshwater 330 
ecosystems(Fan et al., 2025; Shang et al., 2023). Hydrological disturbances induced by 331 
intense rainfall events and extreme droughts can alter nutrient inputs, subsequently 332 
modifying ecosystem functional characteristics and their maintenance mechanisms by 333 
influencing the structure of multi-trophic aquatic communities(Bernal et al., 2025). In 334 
this study, we conducted environmental and biological surveys in Baiyangdian Lake 335 
during both the dry and rainy seasons. Our findings indicate that seasonal variations in 336 
nutrient enrichment levels significantly affect the taxonomic and functional diversity of 337 
the communities, which in turn alters functional redundancy. Concurrently, varying 338 
degrees of nutrient enrichment in both seasons were accompanied by shifts in 339 
community assembly mechanisms. These results suggest that seasonal nutrient 340 
fluctuations exert a significant influence on community structure and functional 341 
composition. 342 
4.1 Differences in nutrient enrichment and community structure 343 
Our findings reveal a pronounced seasonal shift in the trophic status of Baiyangdian 344 
Lake, transitioning from light nutrient enrichment during the dry season to a heavy 345 
eutrophic state in the rainy season. This seasonal variation is likely driven by frequent 346 
precipitation events. Changes in precipitation patterns, exacerbated by climate change, 347 
are crucial for controlling nutrient transport dynamics(Stephens et al., 2022). On one 348 
hand, agricultural runoff resulting from the expansion of agricultural land is the primary 349 
factor contributing to lake nutrient enrichment. On the other hand, there is a correlation 350 
between rainfall and land use, as strong rainfall events disturb the hydrological 351 
environment, enhance surface runoff, and further increase external nutrient inputs, 352 
thereby exacerbating nutrient enrichment(Khan et al., 2025). 353 
Under fluctuating nutrient enrichment gradients, multitrophic aquatic 354 
communities exhibit significant spatial heterogeneity, with plankton demonstrating 355 
more pronounced structural divergence than fish or benthic macroinvertebrates. This 356 
disparity can be attributed to the distinct biological traits and life history strategies of 357 
each taxon. Specifically, the shorter life cycles and limited dispersal capabilities of 358 
plankton facilitate a more rapid and sensitive response to hydrological perturbations 359 
and nutrient loading(Liu et al., 2024). Furthermore, elevated nutrient concentrations not 360 
only directly modulate fundamental ecosystem processes, such as algal development 361 
and proliferation, but also reconstruct habitat complexity and heterogeneity, ultimately 362 
reshaping plankton community structure(Chen et al., 2026). 363 
4.2 The relationship between nutrient enrichment and multitrophic aquatic 364 
biodiversity 365 
Our results revealed an ascending trend in multitrophic biodiversity alongside 366 
increasing nutrient enrichment, aligning with the rising limb of the unimodal 367 
productivity–biodiversity relationship (Fig. S3, Fig. S4). In oligotrophic states, severe 368 
resource limitation restricts survival to a few specialized species, resulting in 369 
diminished diversity. As nutrient levels escalate, the enhancement of primary 370 
productivity bolsters food web complexity and niche availability, thereby supporting a 371 
broader array of consumers(Wang et al., 2025). This resource enrichment mitigates 372 
competitive exclusion, facilitating higher biodiversity through expanded resource 373 
partitioning. However, once nutrient loading exceeds a critical threshold, deleterious 374 
effects such as severe dissolved oxygen depletion and cyanobacterial blooms may 375 
trigger massive species loss and a subsequent collapse in biodiversity(Jan et al., 2022). 376 
 During the rainy season, a significant negative correlation was observed between 377 
Secchi disk depth (SD) and both the taxonomic and functional diversity of fish. In 378 
shallow lake ecosystems, intense wind induced disturbance and sediment resuspension, 379 
coupled with heavy runoff, increase suspended solids and lower SD. This reduction in 380 
water clarity inhibits photosynthesis, constrains basal energy supply, and imposes 381 
substantial foraging pressure on visually oriented predatory fish(Zanghi and Ioannou, 382 
2025). Furthermore, increased exogenous inputs, which may contain heavy metals and 383 
organic pollutants, exert environmental stress on sensitive taxa, ultimately driving the 384 
observed decline in biodiversity(Gutierrez et al., 2025). 385 
4.3 Nutrient enrichment enhances functional redundancy 386 
During the dry season, we observed that increased nutrient enrichment enhanced the 387 
FRic of phytoplankton, whereas the increase in functional richness led to a reduction in 388 
FRe. The slower flow rates and lower water levels in the dry season intensified biotic 389 
interactions, resulting in coexisting species exhibiting greater differences in functional 390 
traits, thereby increasing the functional richness of the community. Consequently, as 391 
nutrient enrichment intensified, most species displayed divergent functional traits rather 392 
than overlapping with the traits of existing species. When a species becomes extinct or 393 
declines, its ecological functions may not be compensated or replaced by functionally 394 
similar species due to the absence of “insurance species”(Török and Tóthmérész, 2025). 395 
In contrast, during the rainy season, increased nutrient levels enhanced the FRic of 396 
zooplankton, which in turn increased FRe. The high nutrient loads in the rainy season, 397 
accompanied by substantial exogenous inputs, promoted primary production and 398 
increased the diversity of available resources(Liu et al., 2025). This facilitated the 399 
survival of a broader range of functional types of zooplankton, such as filter-feeders 400 
and predators. With the increased resources, functionally similar zooplankton could 401 
coexist, thereby enhancing FRe. 402 
Furthermore, we found that nutrient enrichment can directly increase functional 403 
redundancy. Previous studies have shown that elevated nitrogen and phosphorus levels 404 
enhance taxonomic diversity, allowing generalist species with broad tolerance to 405 
dominate the community(Dittrich et al., 2023). However, nutrient enrichment often acts 406 
as an environmental filter, disadvantaging species with specialized traits and leading to 407 
functional homogenization(Silva et al., 2025). Even when species richness remains 408 
relatively high, overlapping functional traits among species can result in increased 409 
functional redundancy. 410 
4.4 Seasonal changes in community assembly mechanisms 411 
Using DNCI analysis, we found that with increasing nutrient enrichment, the assembly 412 
of multitrophic aquatic communities gradually shifted from deterministic to stochastic 413 
processes. The importance of deterministic processes, such as biotic interactions, is 414 
typically more pronounced under resource limited conditions(Wei et al., 2024). During 415 
the dry season, when nutrient enrichment is low, limited resources allow only species 416 
with specific traits to occupy ecological niches, resulting in community assembly being 417 
dominated by deterministic processes. However, as nutrient enrichment intensifies, 418 
inputs of nitrogen, phosphorus, and organic detritus provide abundant resources. 419 
According to neutral theory, when resources are abundant or in excess, competitive 420 
exclusion among species is no longer strong. Consequently, community composition 421 
becomes increasingly governed by ecological drift(Huang et al., 2023). 422 
High nutrient levels in the rainy season are often associated with increased surface 423 
runoff and hydrological disturbances. Changes in the physical environment enhance 424 
hydrological connectivity, under high hydrological connectivity, dispersal processes 425 
may outweigh local environmental filtering as the dominant factor shaping community 426 
assembly(Hu et al., 2025). However, we found that fish community assembly remained 427 
dominated by deterministic processes in both the dry and rainy seasons. This is 428 
attributed to the strong mobility of fish, which allows them to actively select suitable 429 
habitats and refugia even during the hydrologically disturbed rainy season. Moreover, 430 
increased hydrological connectivity in the rainy season enables fish to track 431 
environmental changes along the river continuum. Therefore, deterministic processes 432 
can continue to dominate fish community assembly. 433 
 434 
5. Conclusion 435 
Our results demonstrate that nutrient enrichment under seasonal hydrological 436 
perturbations exerts differential impacts on multitrophic biodiversity and its associated 437 
ecological processes. The pronounced disparity in nutrient enrichment levels between 438 
the dry and rainy seasons not only reshaped community structures but also modulated 439 
functional redundancy, with plankton exhibiting high sensitivity to nutrient loading and 440 
playing a pivotal role in regulating functional stability. As nutrient enrichment increases, 441 
the assembly processes of aquatic communities across different trophic levels generally 442 
shift from deterministic to stochastic processes. Our findings highlight that, under 443 
nutrient enrichment, seasonal hydrological conditions are key regulators of multitrophic 444 
biodiversity, providing data support and scientific guidance for ecosystem management 445 
and the assessment of anthropogenic impacts in shallow lake wetlands. Considering the 446 
strong anthropogenic pressures on Baiyangdian, future studies could further integrate 447 
factors such as river-lake connectivity, land-use change, and pesticide pollution, in 448 
combination with multidimensional biodiversity metrics, to enhance our predictive 449 
capacity regarding the impacts of human disturbances on freshwater ecosystems. 450
