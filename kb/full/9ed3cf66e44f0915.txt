Globalization and industrialization have reshaped modern society, making it heavily dependent on commercial food production and the convenience of global supply networks. However, the growing demand for convenience has also posed severe challenges to sustainability of the food system. This essay analyzes the contradiction between convenience and the sustainability of global food systems and explores how to strike a balance between daily convenience and ecological protection. Based on a critical review of existing literature, this article assesses the environmental burden, social resource waste, and health impacts brought about by convenience-oriented food consumption. The analysis suggests that these challenges arise not only from individual consumption choices, but also from industrialized food production systems and the organization of global supply chains. To address these challenges, consumers, enterprises, and governments must collaborate and seek a balance between the demand for convenient life and food sustainability, jointly promoting a healthier, fairer, and more sustainable global food system.
