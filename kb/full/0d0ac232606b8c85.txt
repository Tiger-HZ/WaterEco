Abstract Averaging the concentration inhibited 75% growth (IC75) is shown in the result of 
orthogonal polynomial regression analysis on plant height, root length, root fresh weight, root 
dry weight, shoot fresh weight and shoot dry weight yielded an IC75 threshold of ~1600 ppm 
AlCl₃·6H₂O (≈ 179 ppm Al³⁺or ≈ 6630 µM Al³⁺), which is proposed as the critical concentration 
for effective hydroponic screening. The (STI) analysis on the data collected from IC75 
hydroponic evaluation on 41 cayenne pepper genotypes provided a robust classification into five 
tolerance groups, ranging from highly tolerant (HT) to highly sensitive (HS), thereby offering a 
clear picture of genotypic diversity in response to aluminum stress. The highly tolerant (HT) 
group was represented by ‘CMK Lolay’ and ‘Tanggo’, which were categorized tolerant across 
multiple traits. A slightly lower but still strong level of tolerance that was observed in the tolerant 
(T) group, they were ‘Dumay’, ‘Seulawah Aceh’, ‘Awe Aceh’, ‘Iggo’, ‘Kawat’, ‘Laris’, 
‘Perintis’, ‘Landung’, and ‘PBC396’. The rest genotypes were considered as medium tolerant 
(MT), sensitive (S) and highly sensitive (HS). The HT and T group were potential genetic 
resources for breeding Al-tolerant cultivars. However, further field validation under acidic soil 
conditions is recommended. 
 
Keywords: Acid stress, Hot pepper, IC75, Selection, STI 
 
Introduction 
 
Cayenne pepper (Capsicum annuum L.) is the most important vegetable 
crop in Indonesia, contributing significantly to food diversity, income generation, 
and rural livelihoods. To meet increasing market demand, efforts to expand the 
planting area are underway into new agricultural frontiers, the acidic soil of 
Ultisols which cover nearly 40% of the Indonesia’s potentially arable lands

(Prasetyo and Suriadikarta, 2006). However, crop productivity on these acidic 
soil is severely constrained by aluminum (Al) toxicity (Shetty et al., 2021). When 
soil pH drops below 5.0, insoluble aluminosilicates and oxides release Al³⁺, the 
phytotoxic ionic form that rapidly inhibits root elongation and division, disrupts 
membrane stability, interferes with nutrient uptake, and induces oxidative 
damage (Kocjan et al., 2025). Ultimately, it hamperes shoot growth, resulting in 
stunted plants and reduced yields (Rahman and Upadhyaya, 2021) (Tan et al., 
2025). 
At the physiological level, there are two complementary strategies to 
fortify Al tolerance in higher plants. The first is external exclusion, mainly 
through root exudation of organic acids (malate, citrate, oxalate) that chelate Al³⁺ 
in the rhizosphere, thereby reducing its activity near root surfaces. The second 
strategy is internal detoxification, involving Al transport into vacuoles and 
activation of antioxidant defense pathways (Wei et al., 2021) (Yan et al., 2022). 
Root growth inhibition in the apex is the earliest and most consistent 
symptom of Al toxicity (Shetty et al., 2021), early-stage (seedling) screening in 
controlled solutions has become a widely used approach for high -throughput 
evaluation of genotypic differences in Al tolerance (Herison et al., 2022). 
Hydroponic and nutrient-solution assays allow precise control of Al³⁺ activity 
and pH, rapid measurement of root-growth parameters (e.g., relative root length, 
root re-growth), and the integration of physiological and biochemical 
measurements and tissue Al accumulation (Brhane et al., 2018) (Kulkarni et al., 
2021). When appropriately designed to reflect agronomic exposure levels, 
seedling assays can provide predictive phenotypes for breeding selection 
(Herison et al., 2022) (Langrai et al., 2025). 
Mitigation strategies such as liming and organic amendments (e.g., 
biochar) have been applied to alleviate soil acidity and improve crop productivity 
(Zhang et al., 2023); (Dhandapani et al., 2024). However, their effectiveness is 
inconsistent and often not cost-effective at scale. Lime application can enhance 
soil physical, chemical and biological properties, yet its impact on lime 
composition, timing and soil moisture, and requires repeated application over 
time (Ameyu, 2019). Similarly, biochar can raise soil pH and adsorb Al ions, 
but its effectiveness dependent on feedstock type and soil characteristic (Shetty 
et al., 2021). Consequently, breeding and selecting genetically Al -tolerant 
cultivars offers a more sustainable solution for long-term productivity on acid 
soils (Wei et al., 2021). Robust selection protocols that exploit plant genetic 
diversity are critical in identify acid-soil–tolerant genotypes to develop of high-
yielding, acid-tolerant varieties. Equally important is the availability tolerant 
parental lines that can serve as sources of aluminium tolerance controlling 
gene(s).

Previous studies have shown that pepper seedlings exhibit genotypic 
variation in shoot and root growth under Al stress (Herison et al., 2022). Since 
aluminium tolerance in higher plants is quantitatively inherited (Njobvu, 2020) 
(Chauhan et al., 2021), a broader range of parental lines required for the 
development of tolerant cultivars. 
Accordingly, this study evaluated diverse cayenne pepper genotypes at the 
seedling stage under controlled hydroponic aluminum exposure, with the 
objectives of determining the threshold concentration of AlCl₃·6H₂O for an 
effective screening protocol and identifying the most tolerant genotyp es as 
potential sources of aluminum tolerance gene(s). 
 
Materials and methods 
 
The research was conducted at the Greenhouse of the Agroecotechnology 
Laboratory, Faculty of Agriculture, University of Bengkulu, from January to July 
2025, at an altitude of ±10 meters above sea level. The study was carried out in 
two stages. The first stage focused on determining the concentration threshold of 
aluminum chloride (AlCl₃·6H₂O), and the second stage aimed to evaluate 41 
cayenne pepper genotypes for aluminum stress tolerance. 
 
Determination of AlCl₃·6H₂O concentration that inhibit 75% growth (IC75) 
 
Aluminum stress assay was conducted in a deep water cultivated (DWC) 
hydroponic system. The planting medium consisted of an AB mix nutrient 
solution supplemented with AlCl₃·6H₂O, and placed in rectangular containers 
measuring 30 x 50 x 20 cm. The experiment used five levels of AlCl₃·6H₂O 
concentrations, i.e. 0 ppm (control), 500 ppm, 1000 ppm, 1500 ppm, and 2000 
ppm. Fifteen plants of a randomly selected genotype were used per treatment. 
Observed variables included plant height, root length, fresh root weight, dry root 
weight, fresh shoot weight, and dry shoot weight. The collected data were 
analyzed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) and followed by the orthogonal 
polynomial at α=5% to determine the concentration of AlCl₃·6H₂O that inhibits 
growth by 75% (IC75). 
 
Evaluation for aluminum stress tolerance 
 
Evaluation for Al tolerance was conducted in an assay technique similar to 
the former stage. Forty one cayenne pepper genotypes were evaluated for 
aluminum stress tolerance, including ‘Mada’, ‘Baja F1’, ‘Lotanbar’, ‘Rotane’, 
‘Locker’, ‘Kopay’, ‘Radha’, ‘Ateng’, ‘Paten’, ‘Dumay’, ‘Lokal Legum’, ‘CMK

‘Sempurna’, ‘Horison’, ‘Anies IPB’, ‘Iggo’, ‘Andalas’, ‘Kawat’, ‘Glora’,

was added to the medium, the pH was maintained between 6.0 and 6.5. For the 
stress treatment, AlCl₃·6H₂O was added to the growing medium, and the pH was 
maintained at 4.0–4.5 following the method of (Herison et al., 2020). The pH 
was increased using KOH or decreased using HNO₃, as needed. The pH solution 
was measured every three days, and the nutrient solution was replaced 
everyweek. Variables measured included plant height, leaf area, number of 
leaves, leaf greenness, root length, root volume, root fresh weight, root dry 
weight, shoot fresh weight, and shoot dry weight. Data analysis was conducted 
by ANOVA and means clustering by the Scott-Knott test at α=5%.

Results 
 
Determination of aluminum concentration for selection 
 
The growth pattern observed under varying aluminum concentrations 
indicated a clear reduction in plant height as the concentration of AlCl₃·6H₂O 
increased. In the control treatment (without AlCl₃·6H₂O), plant height increased 
by approximately 8 to 10 cm weekly. In the treatment of 500 ppm, plant height 
increased by 3 to 10 cm from the previous week. For the 1000 ppm, growth was 
slower, with a height increase of 2 to 3 cm weekly. The treatment of 1500 ppm 
resulted in a minimal growth increased of 0.45 to 1.66 cm per week, while in

Biomass-related traits showed intermediate sensitivities. Root fresh weight 
responses followed the trend equation of y = 4E–07x² – 0.0017x + 2.1614 (R² = 
0.963), and shoot fresh weight, described by y = 3E–06x² – 0.0102x + 11.297 (R²

y = 5E–08x² – 0.0002x + 0.2757 (R² = 0.957), with growth reduced at 1626 ppm, 
while shoot dry weight fitted the equation of y = 4E–07x² – 0.0016x + 1.811 (R² 
= 0.965), was 75% inhibited at 1223 ppm. The consistently high R² values (>0.95 
for most traits) indicate that the regression models accurately captured the close 
relationship between aluminum concentration and growth inhibition. 
These results indicated that plant height and shoot dry weight were more 
reliable early indicators of aluminum stress, as their low inhibition thresholds 
and strong model fits. Conversely, root length was the least sensitive to Al stress, 
with a slightly weaker model fit (R² = 0.891), suggesting that root elongation still 
occur under stress, and it may be influenced by other uncontrolled factors. 
Averaging across all traits, the inhibitory threshold of ~1641 ppm AlCl₃·6H₂O 
(equal to 183 ppm Al³⁺or 6790 µM Al³⁺) is proposed as the critical concentration 
for effective hydroponic screening techniques. This result provides a practical 
reference for distinguishing tolerant from sensitive genotypes in future cayenne 
pepper breeding programs aimed at improving acidic soil adaptation

Stress tolerance index (STI) and genotype classification 
 
Examination of STI values across individual traits showed that tolerance 
was not uniformly distributed across growth components. For plant height, 
several genotypes exhibiting high STI, Vitra Unggul’ (0.57), ‘Lokal Legum’ 
(0.55), ‘Laris’ (0.52) and ‘Horison’ (0.51), sustained vertical growth under stress, 
whereas ‘Andalas’ (0.19) and ‘Paten’ (0.23) were highly inhibited. Leaf 
greenness (LG) became one of the most informative indicators, with 
exceptionally high STI values in ‘Awe Aceh’ (0.93) and ‘Kawat’ (0.95 ). 
Conversely, ‘CMK Tavi’ (0.37) and ‘Bali 77’ (0.37) were more highly reduced. 
For leaf area (LA), ‘Seulawah Aceh’ (1.01) and ‘Awe Aceh’ (0.99) exhibited 
highly tolerance, whereas ‘Mada’ (0.38) and ‘Ateng’ (0.27) were among the most 
sensitive (Table 6). 
The root traits showed the greatest variation, which reflect their central role 
in aluminum stress adaptation. For root length, genotype ‘CMK Lolay’, 
‘Seulawah Aceh’ and ‘Awe Aceh’ consistently showed higher STI compared 
other genotypes. In contrast, ‘Indarapura Reborn’ and ‘Lontanbar’ exhibited low 
STI values across root traits, consistent with their HS classification. For the 
shoot-related traits (SFW, SDW), ‘Dumay’ (SFW = 0.55; SDW = 0.47) and 
‘Romario’ (SDW = 0.32) demonstrated relatively stronger shoot biomass 
accumulation despite stress. Conversely, ‘Mada’ and ‘Indarapura Reborn’ 
recorded the lowest STI values, reaffirming their high susceptibility (Table 6). 
Considering all variables observed, the STI analysis classifi ed the 
evaluated cayenne pepper genotypes into five tolerance groups, they were highly 
tolerant (HT), tolerant (T), medium tolerant (MT), sensitive (S), and highly 
sensitive (HS). The classification indicated genotypic diversity in response to 
aluminum stress. The highly tolerant (HT) group was represented by ‘CMK 
Lolay’ and ‘Tanggo’, with average STI values above 0.40. The STI value in the 
tolerant (T) group, which included ‘Dumay’, ‘Seulawah Aceh’, ‘Awe Aceh’, 
‘Iggo’, ‘Kawat’, ‘Laris’, ‘Perintis’, ‘Landung’, and ‘PBC396’, was a slightly 
lower, ranging between 0.32 and 0.37. The medium-tolerant (MT) group was 
relatively large and diverse, consisting of ‘Locker’, ‘Radha’, ‘Lokal Legum’, 
‘Vitra Unggul’, ‘CMK Rencong Aceh’, ‘Anies IPB’, ‘Andalas’, ‘Labek ’,

Discussion 
 
IC75 determination 
 
The present hydroponic assay showed trait-specific responses of cayenne 
pepper seedlings to increasing AlCl₃·6H₂O concentrations. Plant height was most 
sensitive to aluminum stress, where a 75% reduction relative to control was 
obtain at ~1006 ppm. In contrast, root length was more tolerant, requiring ~3101 
ppm to reach the same level of inhibition. Biomass traits (fresh and dry weights 
of shoots and roots) showed intermediate thresholds (≈1,200–1,600 ppm). These 
findings implied the differential sensitivity of shoot and root traits to aluminum 
stress and signified the importance of multi-trait evaluation for effective 
screening. Such patterns are consistent with other crops, where aluminum 
toxicity produces complex, trait-dependent responses that can be exploited in 
breeding programs (Herison et al., 2022; Ofoe et al., 2023). 
The high coefficients of determination (R²) for most equation models 
(≥0.95 for plant height, shoot biomass, and root biomass) indicate d that the 
models explained nearly all variation across the concentration range, which 
provided strong confidence in the estimated inhibitory concentrations obtained. 
For example, the R² of 0.987 for plant height means that ~98.7% of the observed 
variation was represented by the model, which means that it was a robust 
predictor. By contrast, the lower R² for root length (0.891) suggests that 
additional environmental factors may influence this trait. This aligns with 
findings that root elongation is often more variable under Al stress, due to 
genotype-specific root system architecture and physiological plasticity (Wei et 
al., 2021). 
Our calculated average inhibitory concentration (IC75) across traits was 
~1641 ppm AlCl₃·6H₂O. This averaged threshold provided sufficient stress to 
differentiate tolerant and sensitive genotypes without completely suppressing 
growth in hydroponic screening. Comparable studies in finger millet identified 
~100 µM Al³⁺ as the optimal concentration for genotype discrimination (Brhane 
et al., 2018). Thus, ~1600 ppm is recommended as an effective threshold for 
cayenne pepper screening in deep water culture hydroponics. 
Mechanistically, aluminum toxicity interferes with root cell wall 
expansion, triggers oxidative stress, disrupts nutrient uptake, and ultimately 
restricts both root and shoot development. Tolerant genotypes mitigate these 
effects through mechanisms such as organic acid exudation, vacuolar 
sequestration, and regulation by transcription factors (Wei et al., 2021) (Ofoe et 
 
 
 
 
2160 
al., 2023). These mechanisms help explain why shoot biomass collapsed earlier 
while root elongation persisted longer in some cases. Importantly, because 
aluminum tolerance is quantitatively inherited and controlled by multiple genes, 
identifying diverse tolerant parents is critical in breeding programs (Herison et 
al., 2022). 
 
Genotype differences under Al stress condition 
 
The genotypes evaluated exhibited different responses to the applied 
aluminum concentrations. Genotypes under aluminum stress expressed a plant 
height inhibition compared to non-stressed genotypes (Musyimi, 2023). Plants 
subjected to aluminum stress typically showed smaller and stunted shoots. 
Aluminum stress can cause a significant reduction in shoot dry weight, plant 
leaves to yellow and drop prematurely, reducing the leaf surface area available 
for photosynthesis. Exposure to aluminum also leads to smaller and thinner leaf 
blades, which is associated with reduced cell size and a lower number of stomata 
(Rahman et al., 2024). 
When dissolved aluminum enters the root zone, one of the primary effects 
is the inhibition of root growth. Aluminum can damage the root system by 
inhibiting root elongation, reducing lateral root growth, and causing 
deformations such as root tip thickening. This inhibition is caused by disruptions 
in cell division and elongation processes in the root meristem. Additionally, 
aluminum can interact with the cell walls of roots, increasing cell wall rigidity, 
which limits cell expansion and reduces cell wall elasticity, preventing normal 
root growth and reduce the ability of roots in absorbing water and nutrients from 
the soil (Fan et al., 2020). Some genotypes showed relatively good growth 
despite being grown under the same stress conditions. This difference s is 
attributed to the genetic factors of each plant (Njobvu, 2020) (Devi et al., 2021) 
(Maksimović et al., 2020). 
 
STI and genotype classification 
 
Leaf greenness (LG) acted as a particularly discriminating trait. This trait 
frequently associated with improved stress resilience and stability (Ofoe et al., 
2023). Genotypes 'Awe Aceh' and 'Kawat' showed high STI values for LG. They 
might preserved chlorophyll content under aluminum stress. Conversely, several 
HS genotypes lost chlorophyll rapidly . Chlorosis is one of the common 
International Journal of Agricultural Technology 2026 Vol. 22(5):2147-2166 
2161 
 
 
 
symptoms of Al-induced damage to photosynthetic apparatus observed across 
species (Yan et al., 2022). 
Root traits (root length, root volume, root fresh and dry weight) were major 
drivers of the HT/T vs HS/S separation as they showed high spread of STI values. 
Tolerant genotypes-maintained root biomass and architecture under stress, 
whereas sensitive genotypes showed drastic root suppression. This pattern is in 
harmony with numerous screening studies and physiological reviews that root 
morphology and biomass retention under Al exposure are strong predictors of 
tolerance (Bhukya et al., 2024)). Root integrity and continued growth under Al 
stress allow sustained water and nutrient uptake, which in turn supports shoot 
growth and yield components. 
Shoot biomass (SFW, SDW) also contributed to the classification. 
Genotypes such as 'Dumay' and 'Romario' revealed relatively high STI in shoot 
traits with good root performance. This emphasizes that effective tolerance is 
often multi-component. Maintaining root function alone is necessary but not 
always sufficient. The genotype must also support shoot maintenance to translate 
tolerance into productive potential (Dhandapani et al., 2024). 
The STI-based classification indicates the diversity of adaptive responses 
to aluminum stress among cayenne pepper genotypes. While highly tolerant 
genotypes such as ‘CMK Lolay’ and ‘Tanggo’ showed broad resilience across 
traits, others exhibited trait-specific tolerance, either in leaf greenness, shoot 
biomass, or root development. This differential expression of tolerance 
mechanisms suggests that pyramiding complementary traits from divers e 
genotypes could be a promising strategy in breeding aluminum-tolerant cultivars 
adapted to acidic soils. 
The STI classification provides a concise, comparative view of how the 
evaluated capsicum genotypes withstand aluminum stress. 'CMK Lolay' and 
'Tanggo' were uniquely classified as highly tolerant (HT), while a larger set (e.g., 
'Dumay', 'Seulawah Aceh', 'Awe Aceh', 'Iggo', 'Kawat', 'Laris', 'Perintis', 
'Landung', 'PBC396') formed the tolerant (T) group. These genotypes 
consistently expressed resilience across multiple traits. They may possess both 
morphological and physiological mechanisms conferring aluminum tolerance. 
Medium-tolerant (MT) and sensitive (S) genotypes filled the middle classes, and 
several genotypes were assigned to the highly sensitive (HS) class. This 
stratification indicates genetic variation for aluminum response across the trial 
population and provides a practical guide for selecting parental material for 
breeding programs. 
 
 
 
 
2162 
From a breeding and screening perspective the STI results lead to three 
practical recommendations. First, prioritize 'CMK Lolay' and 'Tanggo' (HT) as 
donor parents in crossing programs because they show high resilience. Second, 
include several T genotypes (e.g., 'Dumay', 'Seulawah Aceh', 'Landung', 
'Perintis', 'PBC396') to capture complementary tolerance mechanisms and allelic 
diversity. Third, use HS genotypes as contrasting parents in mapping populations 
studies as their sensitivity helps revealing tolerance loci (Chauhan et al., 2021). 
Tolerant genotypes exhibit similar characteristics under both stressed and 
non-stressed conditions. The mechanism of tolerance to aluminum toxicity is 
thought to operate externally, where aluminum detoxification occurs through the 
secretion of oxalic acid from the roots, and internally, where aluminum is 
detoxified by forming non-toxic aluminum-organic acid complexes that are 
stored in vacuoles. The ability to secrete oxalate to neutralize aluminum and to 
accumulate aluminum in vacuoles varies significantly between species and even 
between varieties within a species, resulting in different tolerance levels (Taunk 
et al., 2023). 
Highly tolerant genotypes to aluminum stress hold great potential as donor 
parents in plant breeding programs. Their superior traits can be combined with 
good agronomic traits and high yielding potential in a breeding program to 
develop high yielding and acidic adaptive cultivars. Consequently, the 
importance of these tolerant genotypes extends beyond solving agricultural 
problems, as they also drive innovation and advancement in the development of 
superior and sustainable plant varieties. STI -based ranking from controlled 
assays is an efficient pre-screen, but field validation is essential. Hydroponic or 
controlled screenings control the environment to observe genetic differences. 
However, soil chemistry, Al speciation, organic matter interactions, and 
microbiome effects in Ultisols can alter genotype performance under farmers’ 
conditions (Dhandapani et al., 2024). Thus, candidate genotypes identified here 
should be evaluated in representative acid-soil field trials. 
The IC75 estimates and model performance, ~1600 ppm AlCl₃·6H₂O is 
appropriate as a threshold concentration for discriminating aluminum-tolerant 
and sensitive cayenne pepper genotypes under deep water hydroponic conditions. 
Of the 41 genotypes evaluated, ‘Tanggo’ a nd ‘CMK Lolay’ exhibited high 
tolerance to aluminum stress, while ‘PBC396’, ‘Laris’, ‘Perintis’, ‘Awe Aceh’, 
‘Dumay’, ‘Iggo, Landung, Kawat, and Seulawah Aceh demonstrated moderate 
tolerance. Validation of tolerant genotypes in acid soil environments is required 
before integration into breeding pipelines. These findings suggest that these

of valuable sources of Al tolerance controlling genes for future breeding
