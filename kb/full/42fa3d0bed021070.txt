Conventional fertilizers release nutrients quickly thereby resulting in a poor nutrient use efficiency and environmental losses. In the present study, we developed a MAP fertilizer from lignocellulosic biomass and compared nitrogen phosphorus (NP) release characteristic from it with those of urea and DAP. The nutrient release studies showed that N from MAP was released 9 folds slower than from urea and 3-folds less than DAP. Phosphorus release was also found to be 2.4 times lower compared to DAP, suggesting controlled (and sustained) nutrient availability.The tomato field test (Solanum lycopersicum L.) showed that MAP treatment significantly enhanced plant vigor and sustained leaf greenness throughout the growing season.
