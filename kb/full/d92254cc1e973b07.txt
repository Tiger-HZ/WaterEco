A BSTRACT
An experiment was conducted on “Nutrient and Weed Management Strategies in Organic Sorghum” at
the Regional Agricultural Research Station, Lam, Guntur, during the rabi 2025-26. The experiment was
laid out in a split plot design with four main plots (M1 : 50% N through FYM+ 50% N through
Vermicompost, M2 : 75% N through FYM, M3 : 50% N through Ghanajeevamrutham + 50% N through
Vermicompost, M4 : 75% N through Ghanajeevamrutham), four sub plots (S1 : Weedy check, S2 : one
hand weeding + one intercultural operation, S3 : Two hand weedings + two intercultural operations, S4 :
Mulching @ 5 t ha−1 with maize stover residue) and were replicated thrice. The experimental results re-
vealed that two hand weedings and two intercultural operations recorded the lowest weed density and
weed dry weight which was fb one hand weeding and one intercultural operation, mulching @ 5 t ha−1
with maize stover residue. The highest density and dry weight of weeds were found in weedy check.
Keywords: Hand weeding, Intercultivation, Weed management, Mulching
1. Introduction
Sorghum is a versatile crop cultivated widely across the world and is considered as one of the major millet
crops (Dimple et al., 2024) [1]. It serves as an important staple food for millions of people, particularly in
semi-arid and arid regions, owing to its ability to withstand drought, high temperatures and low soil fertility.
In addition to its importance as a food grain, sorghum is extensively utilized as animal feed and for biofuel
production.
Weed infestation is a major constraint to crop productivity, particularly in organic farming systems, where
weeds compete with crops for essential resources such as water, nutrients, light and space, which adversely
affecting the crop growth and yield. The susceptibility of sorghum to weed competition is greater during the
early growth stages due to its slow initial growth and low vigour (Mohammad et al., 2024) [2]. Uncontrolled
weed competition during this period can cause yield losses of 15–40 per cent (Priya et al., 2013) [3].
Although, chemical herbicides provide effective weed control with reduced labour requirements, their exces-
sive and indiscriminate use may contribute to herbicide resistance, environmental pollution and deterioration
of soil health. Therefore, greater emphasis is being placed on sustainable weed management approaches, in-
cluding timely hand weeding, inter-row cultivation, crop rotation, mulching and intercropping, which can
provide effective and environmentally safer alternatives.
2. Materials and Methods
The experiment was conducted during rabi season 2025–26 at the Regional Agricultural Research Station,
Lam, Guntur, Andhra Pradesh. The experimental site is situated at an altitude of 31.5 m above mean sea
level, at 16°36′ N latitude and 80°43′ E longitude, and falls under the Krishna Agro-climatic Zone of Andhra
Pradesh. The experiment was laid out in a split-plot design with four organic nutrient management treat-
ments in the main plots and four weed management treatments in the sub-plots, each replicated three times.
The main plot treatments comprised M₁ : 50 per cent N through FYM + 50 per cent N through vermicompost,
M₂ : 75 per cent N through FYM, M₃ : 50 per cent N through ghanajeevamrutham + 50 per cent N through
vermicompost and M₄ : 75 per cent N through ghanajeevamrutham. The sub-plot treatments consisted of S₁ :
weedy check, S₂ : one hand weeding + one intercultural operation, S₃ : two hand weedings + two intercultural
operations and S₄ : mulching with available crop residue @ 5 t ha ⁻ ¹.
Sorghum variety NTJ-5 (Nandyal Tella Jonna) was sown on 21-10-2025 with a spacing of 45 cm × 15 cm.
Organic nutrient sources viz., FYM, ghanajeevamrutham and vermicompost were applied as basal on nitro-
gen equivalent basis. The recommended dose of fertilizers for sorghum was 74 kg N, 39 kg P2O5, 30 kg
K2O ha−1. The experimental soil was clayey in texture, slightly alkaline and non-saline, with medium or-
ganic carbon content (0.5%), low available nitrogen, medium phosphorus and high potassium (175 kg ha ⁻ ¹,
32 kg ha ⁻ ¹, 289 kg ha ⁻ ¹ respectively).

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Weed flora
The dominant weed flora observed in the experimental field consisted
of grasses viz. Cynodon dactylon, Echinocloa colonum, sedges
Cyperus rotundus and broad-leaved weeds Phyllanthus niruri,
Trianthema portulacastrum, Digera arvensis, Cleome viscosa,
Chrozophora rottleri. The interaction effect of organic nutrient and
weed management practices on weed flora of sorghum was found to
be non-significant.
3.2 Weed density (No. m−2) and weed dry weight (g m−2)
Among organic weed management practices, two hand weedings +
two intercultural operations (S3) recorded significantly the lowest
weed density and weed dry weight (Table. 1 & 2) of grasses, sedges
and broad- leaved weeds at 20, 40 and 60 DAS followed by one hand
weeding + one intercultural operation (S2) and mulching with maize
stover residue @ 5 t ha−1 (S4). At all the stages of crop growth, two
hand weedings and two intercultural operations significantly reduced
the weed growth due to effective and season-long weed control by
maintaining a weed-free environment throughout the critical crop-
weed competition period. Similar findings were reported by Ram et
al. (2005) [4] in pearl millet , Sharma et al. (2010) [5] in maize.
3.3 Weed control efficiency (WCE %) and weed index (WI %)
The highest weed control efficiency and lower weed index (Table. 3)
were recorded in two hand weedings + two intercultural operations
(S3) fb one hand weeding + one intercultural operation (S2) and
mulching with available maize stalk residue @ 5 t ha−1 (S4). The
minimum weed index and maximum weed control efficiency
recorded with two hand weedings + two intercultural operations may
be attributed to effective and timely suppression of weeds throughout
the critical crop-weed competition period, which reduced weed popu-
lation and dry weight of weeds and minimized crop-weed competi-
tion, these results were in line with Bhandari et al. (2004) [6],
Arvadiya et al. (2012) [7], and Sangwan et al. (2016) [8].

4. Conclusion
Two hand weedings combined with two intercultural operations re-
sulted in a substantial reduction in the weed density and dry weight of
grasses, sedges and broad-leaved weeds in sorghum throughout the
crop growth period. This could be attributed to the effective mainte-
nance of a relatively weed-free environment in the experimental plots.
This treatment was followed by one hand weeding combined with one
intercultural operation and mulching with maize stalk residue @ 5 t
ha−1. Among the different organic nutrient and weed management
strategies, the differences in weed density and weed dry weight were
found to be non-significant. The interaction effect of organic nutrient
and weed management practices in sorghum on weed density, weed
dry weight was found to be non-significant.
