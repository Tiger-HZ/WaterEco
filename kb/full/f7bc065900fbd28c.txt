Abstract
Fused deposition Modelling (FDM) technique is extensively used in industrial 
applications. The main advantage of this technique is to provide flexibility and ease 
of operation, but at the same time, surface roughness is a significant drawback to 
FDM, caused by layer-by-layer deposition resulting in staircase effects and surface 
irregularities. For this purpose, the present study used Abrasive Flow Machining 
(AFM) to enhance the surface quality of PLA/Cu composite specimens produced 
by FDM with a biodegradable abrasive medium made from paper pulp, peanut 
oil, and stainless-steel particles. Experimental investigations were carried out to 
analyze the effect of the number of strokes, abrasive concentration, and extrusion 
pressure on surface roughness. Taguchi L9 experimental data were used to 
construct a predictive surface roughness model and then combined with a hybrid 
optimization algorithm, namely Cuckoo Search-Differential Evolution (CS-DE), to 
determine the optimum parameters of the AFM process. The optimum parameter 
combination was determined as 100 strokes, 0.00981 MPa extrusion pressure, and 35 
wt% abrasive concentrations from the optimization results. In such a situation, the 
surface roughness predicted was 6.193624 μm, and the confirmation experiment 
result was 6.30 μm with a prediction error of about 1.71%. The SEM-based surface 
characterization results confirmed a significant decrease in layer-induced asperities 
and ridge height, which confirmed an effective surface smoothing process. The 
results show that the proposed hybrid CS-DE optimization approach offers an 
effective and reliable methodology to improve the surface finish of the FDM-
fabricated parts treated with AFM, and the agreement between the proposed and 
experimental results is excellent.
 1 Introduction
FDM is considered to be one of the most widely adopted additive manufacturing tech -
nologies [ 1] due to its operational simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and capability to pro -
duce geometrically complex parts [ 2, 3]. The process is carried out by extracting the

thermoplastic filament through a heated nozzle, where it is dispersed over the surface 
and converted into the desired shape. Despite all these advantages or benefits still some 
major concern like surface roughness, which affects both performance and aesthetic 
characteristics of manufactured components [4, 5].
The value of Surface roughness in FDM parts depends upon multiple things like the 
layer-by-layer deposition process, staircase effect on curved surfaces as shown in Fig.  1, 
material extrusion characteristics, and various process parameters [ 3, 4]. The optimiza -
tion of these parameters is critical for minimizing surface roughness while maintaining 
dimensional accuracy and mechanical properties. traditional approach of optimiza -
tion depends upon the hit-and-trial method and design of experiments, which is not 
suitable for the complex and multi-level parameter approach. Thereby, Metaheuristic 
optimization algorithms have emerged as powerful tools for addressing complex, multi-
parameter optimization problems in manufacturing [ 1, 2, 6–8]. Apart from this, hybrid 
approaches that combine multiple algorithmic strategies show highly effective of sim -
plify and providing strength to the mechanism adopted for optimization. Specifically, 
hybrid algorithms integrating Cuckoo Search (CS) and Differential Evolution (DE) have 
shown promise in balancing globally and locally accepted techniques for addressing pre -
mature convergence and enhancing population diversity [1, 9–12].
Post-processing techniques such as AFM offer significant potential for enhancing the 
surface quality of FDM parts [ 3, 13–19]. AFM is a non-traditional method where vis -
coelastic fluid is passed with extruded pressure through a small opening, and thereby 
internal and external improved surface finish is achieved by micro-cutting action and 
rubbing of abrasive particles against each other. The optimization of AFM parameters, 
including the number of cycles(stroke), abrasive concentration(slurry), and extrusion 
pressure, is essential for achieving desired surface characteristics.
Enhanced dimensional accuracy and surface integrity of parts produced from con -
ventional and non-conventional machining processes are the focus of recent advances 
in advanced manufacturing. Electrical Discharge Machining (EDM), Micro-EDM 
(µ-EDM), and Wire Electrical Discharge Machining (WEDM) have gained a lot of inter -
est because these can machine complex geometries and difficult materials [20]. Recently, 
it has been shown that the dielectric media have a significant effect on the efficiency of 
the machining process, tool wear, surface morphology, and re-cut layer formation. How-
ever, despite these advances, there is still a need for better quality surface finish in poly -
mer components fabricated by additive manufacturing processes, which requires the 
identification and application of appropriate post-processing and optimization methods.

1.1 Background and theoretical foundations
1.1.1 Fused deposition modelling and surface quality challenges
FDM is the process which is done by using thermoplastic material heated and extruded 
through a nozzle and poured onto the surface layer by layer to make printed parts. As 
the FDM parts are made layer by layer, so it contain or contribute to roughness, orienta -
tion marks or the surface, staircase effects on inclined and curved surfaces, and inter-
layer bonding irregularities [4, 5], as shown in Fig. 1.
Surface roughness of FDM-manufactured parts depends on plenty of factors. Vaha -
bli and Rahmati [ 4] did research on a hybrid estimation approach combining analytical 
Modelling and empirical investigation to understand the effect of surface roughness on 
FDM parts. It tried to demonstrate the complexity of roughness prediction and make it 
easier to apply [ 4]. The work showcases the surface roughness that varies significantly 
across different surface orientations and geometries of the same workpiece.
The advanced characterization of the techniques is mostly used for FDM parts for 
their quality and structural orientation. Computed tomography analysis shows dimen -
sion precision, deviation in the parts made up by FDM techniques, and their geometry 
[22]. These dimensional variations, coupled with surface roughness, require a compre -
hensive optimization strategy and the multiple quality attributes that play important 
roles simultaneously [23].
1.1.2 Metaheuristic optimization algorithms
Metaheuristic algorithms are high-level problem-independent algorithmic whom frame-
works provide sufficiently good solutions to optimization problems, particularly when 
classical optimization methods are impractical because of complexity, non-linearity, or 
computational constraints [ 24]. These algorithms are inspired by natural phenomena, 
such as physical processes or social behaviours, and have been successfully applied to 
manufacturing optimization problems.
Cuckoo Search (CS) is a nature-inspired metaheuristic algorithm based on the obli -
gate brood parasitism of some cuckoo species combined with Levy flight behavior of 
certain birds and fruit flies [ 1, 12, 25]. CS employs a combination of local random walks 
and global explorative jumps, making it effective for exploring large search spaces. The 
Page 4 of 25
Shah et al. Discover Mechanical Engineering (2026) 5:174 
algorithm has been successfully applied to various manufacturing optimization prob -
lems, including surface roughness optimization in machining operations [26].
Differential Evolution (DE) is a population-based stochastic optimization algorithm 
that works by mutation, crossover, and selection operations [ 1, 27]. DE is particularly 
effective for continuous optimization problems and provides local search capability. 
Population diversity is maintained by the algorithm through its mutation strategy, which 
generates trial vectors by combining randomly selected population members [27].
Hybrid Metaheuristic Approaches combine multiple algorithm approach will pro -
vide the strength by mitigating or reducing the weakness of each algorithm alone. There 
are different hybrid algorithm which have their own strength and weaknesses; some 
are acceptable globally, or some are best locally [ 1, 11, 12]. Hybrid approaches aim to 
achieve balanced and overall optimized performance.
There are plenty of other metaheuristic algorithms also applied to FDM optimization, 
namely as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Genetic Algorithms (GA), Teaching-
Learning Based Optimization (TLBO), Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), and Dragonfly 
Algorithm (DA) [ 2, 6, 22, 28]. Comparative study of these algorithms is different and 
relatively different for different situations and objectives [24].
Biswas et al. [29] performed experimental study on enhancing the prediction and opti-
mization of Wire Electrical Discharge Machining (WEDM) for titanium alloy Grade 
5 (Ti-6Al-4  V) using a hybrid approach combining Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) 
with meta-heuristic algorithms. The authors systematically analyzed key WEDM pro -
cess parameters—such as pulse on time, pulse off time, servo voltage, and wire feed rate 
— and their effects on critical output responses including material removal rate, surface 
roughness, and wire wear ratio. An ANN model was developed for accurate prediction 
of machining outcomes, and meta-heuristic optimization techniques were applied to 
identify optimal parametric combinations, resulting in significantly improved machining 
performance and surface quality for this difficult-to-machine aerospace alloy.

2.1 Abrasive flow machining for surface enhancement
AFM operates through the controlled flow of a viscoelastic abrasive medium across or 
through workpiece surfaces under pressure [ 13–19]. The abrasive media generally con -
tain abrasive particles, which are blended with polymer based carries, so they also con -
tain viscous and elastic properties, as shown in Fig.  2. This unique Rheological pattern 
will help to develop and maintain the complex geometric structure without compromis -
ing the stiffness, and it also helps in abrasive action. In this study, vertical AFM is used 
with the specifications as shown in Table 2.
The material removal during AFM is done by the abrasive particle and its micro-cut -
ting action, which flows transverse to the workpiece and smooths the surface, as shown 
in Fig. 2. Shao et al. [18], working on the integrating model of AFM, which deals with the 
micro-cutting mechanism and surface generation model, provide detailed insights into 
the material removal process at the micro level. The work explained that MRR is mainly 
due to plastic deformation and microchip generation, which is integrated with the effect 
of abrasive particle geometry, their properties, and other process conditions, etc.

Number of Cycles (or Strokes) represents the count of abrasive material moved from 
one end to the other end of the workpiece during the AFM process [ 13–15, 17]. Gen-
erally, increasing the number of cycles improves the surface finish with this process. 
However, the returns will diminish, and too many cycles will result in over-polishing, 
dimensional change, or the creation of undesirable surface characteristics. Optimization 
studies have pinpointed the maximum cycle numbers that effectively increase surface 
quality without sacrificing processing efficiency [15, 17].
Abrasive Concentration is the number of abrasive particles that are present in the 
totality of the flowing medium [ 14, 15, 17]. Abrasive concentration has a direct relation 
with the material removal rate. The higher the concentration, the higher the material 
removal rate, but it may also increase surface roughness if the concentration becomes 
excessive. Lower values of concentration may result in lowering the material removal 
rate. The optimum value of abrasive concentration depends upon the material, the type 
of finish required, and other process parameters [15, 17].
Extrusion Pressure controls the force with which the abrasive medium is pressed 
against the workpiece surface [ 14, 15, 17, 21]. At higher pressures, materials are 
destroyed rapidly, and, due to more frequent contact of abrasive particles with the work 
surface, a better surface finish can also be attained. Still, the application of very high 
pressure can result in the deformation of the workpiece, especially if it is a thin-walled 
or compliant structure, which are typical of FDM parts. Kumar et al. [ 17] tested the sur-
face roughness improvement as a function of extrusion pressure, along with other AFM 
parameters [17].
Algorithm Flow.
a. Initialization: Randomly sets the initial population within the given range for Stroke, 
Pressure, and Abrasive Concentration.
b. Initial Evaluation: For each individual in the starting population, the fitness (predicted 
roughness) is assessed by the MLP Regressor. The first best is marked.
c. Iteration Cycle: Starts a loop of a set number of iterations. Each generation, the 
population is divided into two exclusive subsets according to a previously defined 
ratio (cs_ratio).
d. Cuckoo Search (CS) Phase : (i) A Levy flight random walk is applied to generate 
new candidate solutions for the selected CS individuals, directing the search toward 
the current best solution identified during the optimization process. (ii) The newly

previous solution, it replaces the previous solution. (iii) An “alien egg discovery” 
mechanism is applied, in which, with a probability of p.a., the current individual is 
discarded and replaced with a new randomly generated solution within the specified 
search bounds.
e. Phase Differential Evolution (DE) : (i) For everyone else, creates a new vector by 
picking three different individuals from the population at random and creating a 
new mutant vector using these. (ii) Carries out the binomial crossover operation to

The AFM process involves extruding an abrasive medium against the wall of the sur -
face of the workpiece under controlled pressure. The abrasive particle embedded in the 
viscoelastic material removes the extra materials or chips by means of micro-cutting 
action or by a micro-plow mechanism [ 18]. There are plenty of parameters that play an 
important role in material removal rate, and hence the resulting surface characteristics 
depend on multiple process parameters, which include parameters like abrasive con -
centration, particle size and type, medium viscosity or consistency, extrusion pressure, 
number of cycles or strokes, and flow velocity [13–15, 17].
Wang et al. [ 13] investigated and found that the surface roughness of the workpiece 
depends upon the parameters that are selected for operations [ 13]. Their work demon -
strates that the surface finish depends upon the surface material removal rates in the 
number of cycles; it can be improved by means of controlling these parameters, so it’s 
important to determine the optimal number of cycles.
Dhull et al. [18] performed an experimental study on computational and mathematical 
analysis of hybrid AFM processes; it developed models that relate process parameters 
to surface quality outcomes [ 14]. Also, try to explain the importance of mathematical 
Modelling for predicting AFM performance and optimizing process parameters. Sim -
ilarly, Shao et al. [ 18] conducted experimentation on integrated models of micro-cut -
ting mechanics with precision surface generation in AFM and improved the theoretical 
understanding of material removal mechanisms.
The optimization of AFM process parameters has been addressed through various 
approaches. Gupta et al. [ 15] examined the effect and optimization of process param -
eters on surface roughness in AFM, they identified critical parameters and their interac -
tions. Kumar et al. [ 17] focused on optimizing process parameters for effective material 
surface roughness improvement, demonstrating that systematic parameter optimization 
can significantly enhance AFM performance. Hashmi et al. [ 19] worked on a compre -
hensive computational study and performed simulations on the parameters of AFM.

The process of optimization of AFM was carried out using the Crow Search–Differ -
ential Evolution (CS-DE) optimization algorithm. The data acquired from the Taguchi 
L9 orthogonal array was used as the input data for optimization. The CS-DE algorithm 
used the response values that were measured in the experiment to assess the fitness of 
each solution and find the optimum set of process parameters. The optimization process 
was performed directly on the experimental Taguchi data without the use of an objec -
tive function built out of a regression model. The nine experimental results obtained 
from the Taguchi L9 design were first used to train a predictive Artificial Neural Net -
work (ANN) model based on a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). This initial model was 
subsequently used to generate additional synthetic data points within the experimen -
tal design space, thereby expanding the training datasets. A final ANN model was then 
trained using the expanded datasets. During optimization, the objective function cor -
responded to the ANN-predicted surface roughness. Each candidate solution generated 
by the CS-DE algorithm was supplied to the trained ANN model, which predicted the 
corresponding surface roughness value. These predicted values were used as the fitness 
function during the optimization process.
Figure 3 presents the overall experimental methodology employed in this study. Ini -
tially, a PLA-Cu composite filament was fabricated using a single-extruder machine for 
use in the FDM process. The tubular specimens were subsequently fabricated using a 
Creality Ender-3 3D printer with a build volume of 0.220 × 0.220 × 0.250 m3. The other 
important specifications of the printer, such as the diameter is 1.75 mm, layer thickness 
range varying from 100 to 400 microns. The given filament is printed at ASTM stan -
dard D638. Then the parts are finished by a vertical AFM machine with specifications 
as mentioned in Table  2. In this vertical AFM machine, biodegradable material made

3.1 Material removal mechanism in AFM
The surface finish improvement is due to the repeated rubbing of abrasive particles with 
the surface of the workpiece under extrusion pressure. Abrasive particles remove peaks 
of the surface in each machining stroke by a micro-cutting and micro-plough mecha -
nism. For a greater number of strokes, the removal process is more uniform, yielding a 
gradual decrease in surface roughness. Figure  6 shows the impact of optimized selected 
parameters on the surface roughness. Table 5 shows the experimental results for surface

against the surface under extrusion pressure to improve the surface finish [ 13–19]. The 
process is suitable for finishing components with complex shape structures having intri -

type of structure is generally found in the additive manufacturing process. The effect of

3.2 Effect of stroke on the surface
The changes in surface roughness with abrasive concentration are plotted in Fig.  6a. 
The average Ra value decreases considerably with the increase of abrasive concentra -
tion from 20 to 35%, and the lowest value is obtained at around 35% concentration of 
the abrasive. This improvement is due to the increase in abrasive content, which means 
there are more cutting particles in the media, giving more uniform contact with the 
workpiece surface and improving the material removal process. Anything over 35% con -
centration leads to a rise in surface roughening again. When abrasive loading is too high, 
the viscosity of the media may increase, affecting the flowability of the media and abra -
sive particles’ mobility. Consequently, the effectiveness of the micro-cutting action is 
diminished due to particle crowding and inter-particle interference, which causes non-
uniform finishing and increased surface roughness. Thus, a concentration of around 35% 
is recommended as the most balanced concentration to maximize the cutting efficiency 
and media flow. Figure 6 shows the impact of the optimized selected parameters on the 
surface roughness.

3.3 Effect of pressure on the surface
In Fig.  6b, the effect of extrusion pressure on the surface roughness is shown. It can be 
seen from the above results that the Ra value decreases significantly from about 8.7 μm 
to about 3.2  μm, which is about 40%, with an increase in extrusion pressure from 
0.2 MPa to 0.4 MPa. This improvement is believed to be due to the increase in the nor -
mal force acting on the abrasive particles and thus their ability to probe the irregularities 
more deeply into the surface. Thus, the abrasive particles are capable of conducting an 
effective micro-cutting operation, which increases material removal and smooths sur -
faces. But if the pressure is further increased over 0.4 MPa, then surface roughness grad-
ually increases. When the pressure is increased, the particle mobility may be reduced 
by the excessive compaction of the abrasive media, which causes ploughing instead of 
controlled cutting. Also, if the contact force is too high, the surface scratches, which will 
further reduce the surface finish. From these observations, it can be concluded that the 
optimum extrusion pressure for balancing the cutting efficiency and media flow charac -
teristics is 0.4 MPa.

3.7 Mathematical modelling and optimization
Through mathematical Modelling of the AFM process, surface finish or optimization 
of the process parameters for the outcome of the surface can be easily predicted. Dhull 
et al. [ 14] carried out experimentation on the mathematical model for optimization of 
AFM process parameters, taking the basic parameters that play an important role in the 
AFM process, like abrasive concentration and pressure, and performed the optimiza -
tion over it. Hashmi et al. [ 19] worked on a mathematical model which integrate with 
numerical simulation, that give insight into abrasive flow finishing processes [ 19]. The 
work largely depends upon the material removal mechanism, and their research goes 
beyond the empirical relation of the parameter, so the process gives better and deeper 
process behavior. Gupta et al. [ 15] have researched the impact of surface roughness and 
how it is optimized by changing process parameters in AFM. The study proved that a 
systematic optimization of parameters could result in an enormous improvement in 
AFM performances then the random testing methods. Kumar et al. [ 17] focused on pro-
cess parameter optimization of material surface roughness in AFM to get the maximum 
effect. Through design of experiments and statistical analysis, the study helped to iden -
tify the optimal parameter setting, thus proving that structured optimization method -
ologies are a very effective means of performance improvement. Kumar et al. [ 21] also 
describe Electro-Chemical AFM (ECAFM), which is a combination of AFM and electro-
chemical, where the latter is used as the abrasive material. It used the Taguchi method 
as a tool for optimization of their parameters and also shows how the hybrid model 
combined with parameters works better and provides the best and most desired solu -
tion for the same. Diyaley et al. [ 38] analyzed different metaheuristics for parametric 
optimization of abrasive water-jet machining, which is a related abrasive-based process. 
Perhaps similar techniques could be employed to solve the problem of AFM optimiza -
tion. Thus, combining mathematical models and metaheuristic optimization algorithms 
allows us to create the potential framework for AFM parameter optimization [ 42–46]. 
Once the model is established, the evaluation of a proposed parameter set can be done 
swiftly, even with a view to finding optimum connection settings. Metaheuristic algo -
rithms enhance the search in parameter space, as well as the effective utilization of run-
time parameters, turning up settings that are best suited to a given test or process. It is 
especially worthwhile for AFM optimization when experimental evaluations necessitate

A polynomial regression model integrated with the optimization algorithm was 
employed to determine the optimal process parameters for minimizing the surface 
roughness during AFM. The optimization results predicted that the minimum surface 
roughness could be achieved at a stroke count of 138.1284 cycles, an extrusion pressure 
of 0.0098  MPa, and an abrasive concentration of 32.8423 wt%. Under these optimum 
conditions, the predicted minimum surface roughness was found to be 6.192333 μm.
The developed polynomial regression model demonstrated excellent predictive capa -
bility. The statistical performance indicators revealed an R² value of 0.9831, indicat -

developed model. Furthermore, the model exhibited a very low Mean Squared Error 
(MSE) of 0.002322 and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.03948, confirming the high 
accuracy and reliability of the prediction model.
The relationship between process parameters and surface roughness was represented 
by a third-order polynomial regression equation. The third-order polynomial regression 
was chosen because, according to the model performance measures (R 2, MAE, MSE, 
and RMSE), it offered the best predictive performance among the models assessed in

4 Future implications
An extensive comparative research of the proposed CS-DE algorithm and other state-
of-the-art optimization methods is an important research direction. These studies will 
allow for the benchmarking of the optimization accuracy, convergence rate, computa -
tional cost, and stability of the proposed algorithm, which will help enhance the knowl -
edge of the algorithm’s capabilities and limitations. The present study mainly aims at 
the evaluation of machining performance of the fabricated biodegradable abrasive 
media, but comprehensive physicochemical characterization of the media is a promis -
ing research trajectory. In the future, the viscosity, the Rheological behavior, the particle 
size distribution, the stability of the media, and the flow behavior of the media should be 
measured so that a deeper insight into the transport behavior, abrasive dispersion, and 
long-term stability of the media during the machining process can be gained. This char -
acterization will further validate the performance of the proposed biodegradable abra -
sive media and help to optimize it for industrial AFM applications.
