Abstract Acid mine drainage (AMD) often contains high concentration of iron (Fe), which can exceed 100 mg/L, while speciation and precipitation are key factors controlling the removal efficiency of Fe and other elements. This study monitored seasonal variations in AMD quality at A mine, Japan (pH: 2.6–3.2, Total-Fe: 128–162 mg/L), and found the dominant dissolved Fe species shifted from ferrous ion (Fe(II) aq ) in summer to ferric ion (Fe(III) aq ) in winter. Neutralization experiments using simulated AMD containing either Fe(II) aq or Fe(III) aq revealed that initial Fe speciation had little effect on arsenic and copper removal efficiency at this site.
