Background: Chickpea (Cicer arietinum L.) is an important grain legume crop with high nutritional value due to its protein content, essential amino acids and functional properties. Genetic variation among chickpea germplasm collections provides valuable opportunities for improving seed nutritional quality and identifying promising materials for breeding programs. However, comprehensive evaluation combining technological traits, biochemical composition and storage-protein polymorphism remains limited for many chickpea collections. The present study aimed to characterize the variation in technological quality traits, nutritional composition and globulin storage-protein profiles among chickpea accessions to identify potential breeding materials with improved seed quality.
