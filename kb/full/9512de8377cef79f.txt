The quality of a choral performance is well known to be affected by the nonverbal behaviors of the conductor. The conductor strives not only to perform the score as written, but also to facilitate musical communication among the singers during the performance. However, whether the conductor’s intention regarding musical expression or sound images through nonverbal behavior influences group cohesion within a choir and enhances the quality of a performance remains unclear. In the present study, we conducted a questionnaire survey to examine the influence of conducting, involving expressive intention, on group cohesion within a choir and the quality of the performance.
