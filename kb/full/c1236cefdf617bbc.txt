Hydroponics is a soilless cultivation technique that has gained increasing interest in its capacity to address specific constraints of conventional agriculture, notably through the precise management of mineral nutrition and growing conditions. This study evaluated the effects of different nutrient solution regimes defined by nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) concentrations and covarying potassium (K) concentrations on basil (Ocimum basilicum L.) cultivated in a hydroponic nutrient film technique (NFT) system. Agronomic parameters and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) were analyzed at final harvest, while response surface methodology (RSM) was applied to identify model-predicted candidate nutrient combinations associated with the production of linalool, eugenol, and eucalyptol.
