Human activities are carried out by the consumption of different types of sources, many of which are nonrenewable resources, one of the most important being water, which is essential for life and highly demanded by industrial processes. The use of water for diverse activities results in the production of wastewater, which is defined as water with modifications in its physicochemical characteristics (e.g., pH, color, turbidity, and temperature) and decreased quality (measured as the microbiological load or by the concentrations of different elements and compounds). Since the introduction of sustainability, reducing the amount of wastewater generated or even the reuse of wastewater has become necessary. These necessities have favored the development of wastewater treatment processes with relatively high efficiencies focused on restoring water characteristics before their use. Today, there are processes for the elimination of organic matter from bacteria (aerobic or anaerobic), but the presence of specific pollutants in wastewater requires the inclusion of specific steps to allow their elimination, which is referred to as tertiary treatment. In these extra steps, microalgae and cyanobacteria have shown potential. Microalgae and cyanobacteria can efficiently eliminate phosphorus, nitrogen, colorants, heavy metals, and emerging pollutants (e.g., medicines, analgesics, and personal care products) with acceptable efficiencies. In this chapter, the potential of microalgae and cyanobacteria application in the tertiary treatment of domestic and industrial effluents is discussed, highlighting the types of pollutants eliminated and the efficiencies allowed.
