The efficient removal of heavy metals and hazardous toxins from wastewater is imperative to mitigate environmental pollution and safeguard human health. This manuscript explores the potential of composite adsorbents comprising microalgae, waste biomass-derived high-performance biochar, magnetic materials, and chitosan for the removal of contaminants. It begins with an introduction to high-performance biochar, elucidating its characteristics, properties, mechanisms of adsorption, and recent developments in microalgae and waste biomass-derived biochar. Waste biomass-derived biochar, produced from microalgae, agricultural residues, forestry waste biomass and industrial by-products, exhibits excellent adsorption properties due to its porous structure and surface functional groups. Additionally, magnetic nanoparticles and chitosan-based materials provide enhanced adsorption capacity and magnetic separability, facilitating the recovery and regeneration of adsorbents. The combination of these materials into composite forms enhances synergistic effects, leading to improved removal efficiency and selectivity for various contaminants. This review comprehensively discusses the synthesis methods, characterization techniques, adsorption mechanisms, and recent advancements in the application of microalgae and waste biomass-derived composites for water and wastewater treatment. Furthermore, challenges and future perspectives for the development and commercialization of these innovative remediation technologies are critically examined, emphasizing the importance of sustainable approaches for addressing water pollution issues.
