Governance quality is widely regarded as a fundamental pillar of democratic governance and a key determinant of public trust in state institutions. Effective governance is characterized by transparency, accountability, rule of law, government effectiveness, political stability, regulatory quality, and active citizen participation, all of which contribute to the legitimacy and sustainability of democratic systems. Public trust reflects citizens' confidence that government institutions operate fairly, efficiently, and in the public interest. In developing democracies such as Pakistan, governance challenges including political instability, corruption, administrative inefficiency, weak institutional capacity, and limited accountability continue to affect citizens' confidence in democratic institutions. This article examines the relationship between governance quality and public trust by reviewing relevant theoretical perspectives and empirical literature. It discusses the major dimensions of governance quality, analyses factors influencing public trust, identifies governance challenges facing Pakistan, and proposes practical strategies for strengthening democratic institutions. The study argues that improvements in transparency, accountability, judicial independence, public service delivery, digital governance, and citizen participation can significantly enhance institutional legitimacy and democratic resilience. It concludes that sustainable democratic development requires continuous governance reforms, evidence-based policymaking, and stronger institutional capacity to foster public confidence, promote political stability, and support inclusive democratic governance in Pakistan.
