The Arabian Sea is among the most productive basins
in the global ocean, and it is important in the context of
reversal of monsoon, eddies, convection processes, win-
ter cooling and significant upwelling phenomena. These
processes influence the biogeochemical properties of the
region. The formation of a phytoplankton bloom in the
Arabian Sea during the winter monsoon is an example
of biophysical coupling. Low sea surface temperature
(SST) and high nitrate concentration result in enhanced
concentration of chlorophyll-a (Chl-a) which is consid-
ered as the signal of phytoplankton bloom. The present
study is carried out using Oceansat-3 Ocean Colour
Monitor (OCM) and Moderate Resolution Imaging
Spectroradiometer (MODIS) Aqua derived Chl-a, SST,
photosynthetically active radiation (PAR), and mod-
elled nitrate nutrient image analysis to study details
of phytoplankton bloom dynamics on a weekly and
monthly scale. The study has been carried out using
the satellite data during the winter monsoon (November
2023 to February 2024) using 8-day composite/weekly-
scale data and respective monthly data. Chl- a concen-
tration ranged between 0.3 to 1.1 mg m -3, SST ranged
26.98°C–29.38°C, PAR ranged 35.87–50.26 Einstein m-2
day-1, nitrate ranged 2.3–4.1 µ mol/L during the winter
monsoon. The peak bloom condition is observed in the
month of February during the winter monsoon. But
PAR is observed to be increased in the peak bloom
stages. Nitrate is the major controlling factor for the
formation of bloom as compared to other parame-
ters. OCM-3 data is showing matching results with
MODIS data with little overestimation. The present
study proves the suitability of the Oceansat-3 OCM
sensor data for bloom observation in the Arabian Sea
during the winter monsoon from its initiation phase to
its peak phase and for understanding the dynamics and
correlation of Chl- a, SST, PAR, and modelled nitrate
with phytoplankton biomass variability in bloom and
non-bloom sites.
Keywords: Arabian Sea, chlorophyll, MODIS-Aqua,
Oceansat-3 OCM, phytoplankton bloom.
THE Arabian Sea (AS) is one among the highly productive
regions in the tropical oceans, and its boundary and open
ocean processes are impacted by summer upwelling and
winter cooling, which introduces large amounts of nutrients
into the upper ocean, improving primary production and
eventually the fisheries 1. In recent years, the phytoplank-
ton blooms are observed at regular intervals and are being
reported in the Arabian Sea, mainly occurring during win-
ter monsoons2,3. Such blooms tend to increase in species
cell density over the time period. Phytoplankton consists of
small, single-celled organisms, which constitute the base
of the oceanic food web. The abundance of phytoplankton
in the oceans is controlled by biophysical processes and
anthropogenic and climate-induced variables. Hence, the
phytoplankton growth, which leads to a bloom, is triggered
by spatiotemporal variation in water quality parameters 4,5.
The spatio-temporal observations of phytoplankton blooms
on a synoptic scale have been possible through space tech-
nology via satellites with larger area coverage 6. The in
situ based measurements are not cost-effective and have
limitations to cover a broad area in a short time span. Re-
mote sensing observations have been vital to estimate and
monitor important ocean parameters such as chlorophyll-a
(Chl-a) concentration, phytoplankton absorption, sea sur-
face temperature (SST) and photosynthetically available
radiation (PAR) over large areas. Satellites are capable
of estimating various parameters in surface waters on a
synoptic scale by measurement of spectral changes of the
upwelling light. Satellite sensors observe and quantify the
colour of the ocean surface, providing valuable information
about various parameters and processes. This approach,
known as ocean colour remote sensing, measured by satel-
lites, unveils chlorophyll content, a key proxy for phyto-
plankton biomass. Ocean colour satellite sensors like the
Sea-viewing Wide Field-of-View Sensor (SeaWiFS), Mod-
erate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS)-
Aqua, Ocean Colour Monitor (OCM), Visible Infrared
Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS), etc. have successfully
mapped and visualised the phytoplankton biomass in terms
of Chl- a concentration on a regional and global scale 7.
Oceansat-3 satellite configured and launched into orbit to
measure physical, biological and atmospheric parameters.
Its sensor, OCM-3, is designed for providing quantitative
information of ocean colour variables, e.g., Chl- a concen-
tration, total suspended matter, etc. It is operating in two
modes: (i) local area coverage at 366 m resolution and (ii)

global area coverage mode at 1.1 km resolution, providing
observations in 13 narrow (10–20 nm) spectral channels
in visible, near-infrared and short-wave infrared ranges
(400–1030 nm). A significant improvement in bandwidth
(10–20 nm) from its predecessor, OCM-2, having 8 wider

The MODIS-Aqua sensor views the entire Earth’s
surface every 2 days, acquiring data in 36 spectral bands
covering the range from 400 nm to 14.4 µm with 4 km res-
olution, and its bands of primary interest to ocean colour
applications are the 9 channels covering the spectral range
from 400 nm to 900 nm (ref. 9). The present study provides
an insight into the recent phytoplankton bloom phases in
the northern Arabian Sea and their dynamics using two dif-
ferent sensors in space orbit for the retrieved data and their
linkage to relevant corollary surface ocean parameters.
Methodology
Study area
The study area selected for the present study is the Arabian
Sea (55°–78° E and 6°–24° N). Data were acquired for
the period of November 2023 to February 2024 (win-
ter monsoon). A phytoplankton bloom was recorded in
irregular patches based on chlorophyll concentrations in
the subregions of the northern Indian Ocean (21°–23° N
and 63°–65° E) and observations were made in non-bloom
subregions (16°–19° N and 68°–70° E) for the compara-
tive observations on prevailing environmental conditions
(Figure 1).
Results and discussion
Satellite data
For the estimation of phytoplankton pigment variability
in the Arabian Sea, data were acquired from two satel-
lites. The Indian remote sensing satellite Oceansat-3 OCM
Chl-a concentration level-2 data with a spatial resolu-
tion of 1 km were downloaded from the Bhoonidhi Por-
tal ( nrsc.isro.gov.in) for the period November 2023 to
February 2024. The dates of access of OCM-3 data from
the National Remote Sensing Centre-Indian Space Re-
search Organization (NRSC-ISRO) Bhoonidhi Portal were
10 March 2024, 20 March 2024 and 12 April 2024. The
OCM-3 on-board Oceansat-3 has 13 narrow (10–20 nm)
spectral channels with 2 days’ temporal resolution.
MODIS Aqua level-3 Chl- a, SST and PAR data
during November 2023 to February 2024 were downloaded
from ocean colour, National Aeronautics and Space
Administration (NASA) (http://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov), with
a spatial resolution of 4 km. Data products such as Chl-
a and SST were used as proxies for the estimation of sea
surface nitrate.
Data processing
Processing and analysis of the Oceansat-3 OCM and
MODIS-Aqua data have been carried out using SeaWiFS
Data Analysis System (SeaDAS) version 9.0 soft-
ware. The SeaDAS, a comprehensive image anal-
ysis software developed and maintained by NASA
(http://seadas.gsfc.nasa.gov) was used for processing, dis-
playing, analysing and quality controlling data related to
ocean colour. Mosaicking and resampling of OCM-3 data
had been done to get the distribution of Chl- a in the entire
Arabian Sea to compare with MODIS data.
Results and discussion
Phytoplankton pigment variability
To substantiate the trend of variation in Chl- a during the
winter monsoon, data were acquired from MODIS dur-
ing four seasonal years (2020–2024). The bloom indicat-
ing high Chl-a was recorded during the month of February
(Figure 2).
Figures 3 and 4 have shown the variation of Chl- a (8-
day composite) from OCM-3 and MODIS, respectively, in
the Arabian Sea during the winter monsoon. Chl- a con-
centration observed during the winter monsoon ranged be-
tween 0.1 to 2.0 mg m-3 in the Arabian Sea. OCM-3 data
in Figure 5 shows matching results with MODIS data, even
if it is overestimated. This indicates the suitability of the
Oceansat-3 OCM sensor data for bloom observation in the
Arabian Sea during the winter monsoon from its initiation
phase to peak phase. The winter cooling phenomenon in
the Arabian Sea is caused by the northeast monsoon winds
impact on dry, cool continental air, which increases evapo-
ration and causes the water’s surface to cool 10,11. The so-
lar insolation gets reduced during January– February in the
Arabian Sea, which induces the increase in mixed layer
depth. Concurrently, the densification and cooling ob-
served in the northern Arabian Sea are a winter effect; this
process leads to sinking and convective mixing. Subse-
quently, the water column nutrients are injected into surface
layers from thermocline12. As understood, the detrainment
also played a key role in thinning the mixed layer and en-
abling more light availability for phytoplankton growth,
favouring blooms13. However, these systems are typically
short-lived, as detrainment fails to replenish nutrients in
the mixed layer, limiting their persistence to the duration
of the initial nutrient availability. Detrainment blooms
also die due to nutrient constraint, although the first peak

Impact of SST on phytoplankton bloom
To analyse the impact of SST on variation in chlorophyll
concentrations in the Arabian Sea, MODIS 8-day and
monthly images are processed (Figure 6). From November
2023 to February 2024, SST decreased from 29.38°C to
28°C (Figure 7).
Interestingly, a significant negative association was
observed between SST and chlorophyll a concentration.
In the present study, Oceansat-3 and MODIS showed an
inverse relationship between chlorophyll and SST with
R2 values of 0.457 for OCM-3 and 0.3975 for MODIS.
The distribution of Chl-a and SST are inversely correlated
(Figure 8). The relationship between SST, chlorophyll con-
centration, and phytoplankton blooms in the Arabian Sea
is complex and interconnected. The increased concentra-
tion of chlorophyll during the winter months is caused by
mesoscale eddies and winter convective mixing that oc-
curred due to wind stirring, which raise the mixed layer
of rich nutrients to the surface 14,15. The enrichment of
the mixed layer through cool thermocline water and this
cooling event is responsible for the increase in surface
chlorophyll concentrations16. The phytoplankton in denser
concentrations have been able to trap shortwave solar
radiation and its absorption, which increased the SST17.
Impact of PAR on phytoplankton bloom

2023– February 2024. PAR is observed to be increased
in the peak bloom stages (Figures 10 and 11). PAR
ranged from 35.877 to 50.265 Einstein m -2 day-1 during
winter monsoon. PAR is enhancing phytoplankton bloom
up to a limit. Phytoplankton blooms in the Arabian Sea
are strongly influenced by the amount of PAR reaching
the surface waters. PAR is the specific range of wave-
lengths of sunlight that phytoplankton can use for pho-
tosynthesis, the process by which they capture energy to
grow and reproduce. PAR is related to phytoplankton

Some may be more efficient at utilising lower levels of PAR
compared to others. Overall, PAR acts as a key regulator
for phytoplankton growth in the Arabian Sea, influencing
the timing, intensity, and overall succession of blooms.

Conclusion
Phytoplankton blooms are frequent in the Arabian Sea.
There are different parameters that impact the phytoplank-
ton bloom. However, its impact and influence are com-
plex and interrelated with various environmental factors
like light availability, nutrients, and water column dynam-
ics. Strong PAR coinciding with upwelling events during
the monsoons is a key factor for triggering massive phy-
toplankton blooms in the Arabian Sea. The phytoplankton
biomass in terms of chlorophyll variability has been a mea-
sure of SST and the thermal structure of the water column.
Blooms can occur despite cooler SST compared to summer,
as long as light availability is sufficient. Nitrate plays a crit-
ical role in fuelling phytoplankton blooms in the Arabian
Sea, acting as a primary nutrient source. Nitrate is a vital
nutrient for phytoplankton blooms in the Arabian Sea. The
Arabian Sea experiences blooms during winter monsoons
due to upwelling events that bring deep, nutrient-rich wa-
ters to the surface. Oceansat-3 OCM data analysis in-
ferred its suitability to observe phytoplankton blooms from
initiation to peak phase as a proof-of-concept satellite
launched by India with its indigenous development, having
an excellent high resolution and advanced ocean colour
sensor, OCM-3. Observations inferred that February is
more productive during winter monsoon. More detailed
studies, such as validation, nutrient ratio, and stoichiometry
related to phytoplankton types and taxonomy, are required
over the study area for further analysing phytoplankton
bloom.
