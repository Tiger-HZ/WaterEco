Nickel mining in high?rainfall tropical regions produces metal?rich runoff that can substantially alter biogeochemical processes at the water–sediment interface. However, the mechanisms governing nickel partitioning into dissolved, particulate, and sedimentary geochemical fractions in tropical river systems remain insufficiently resolved. This study investigates the fractionation and mobility of Ni, Fe, Mn, Cr, Cu, and Zn in a mining?impacted river in North Maluku, Indonesia, by integrating dissolved–particulate metal separation in water with five?step sequential extraction in sediments. Most metals in sediments were predominantly bound to the residual (RES) fraction, reflecting strong lithogenic control and incorporation into mineral lattices.
