Saudi Arabia is experiencing a massive construction boom. From brand new cities to amazing tourist spots and busy transport hubs, it's clear that the country is transforming in a huge way. However, this growth comes with intense working conditions due to the heat. It's no secret that working in these temperatures is tough and dangerous. The soaring heat can exhaust workers, increasing the likelihood of accidents. Managing such situations is also difficult for companies. Fortunately, there are ways to improve worker safety. For example, smart planning and adjusted shift timings help avoid the worst of the heat. Additionally, teaching workers about heat safety is crucial. Carrying out risk assessments and careful structure design are important too. These strategies can really change the game. This essay examines studies and health tips from Saudi Arabia and other regions, primarily from 2020 to 2025. One common suggestion is to adjust work schedules to avoid the peak heat. Yet, this alone won’t cover morning shifts or work in uncovered areas. Properly addressing heat requires additional measures, including establishing shady areas, adding frequent rest periods, conducting regular health checks, and outlining clear guidelines. In short, experts believe firms must implement robust heat stress plans. Some even recommend using technology, such as temperature sensors, for smoother management. At the end of the day, protecting workers from the heat needs to be an all-year focus, not just during the hottest months.
