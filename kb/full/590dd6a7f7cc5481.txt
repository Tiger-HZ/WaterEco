This paper presents an integrated facility management (IFM) and risk-resilient infrastructure framework applied to high-security government residential complexes in Lahore, based on direct field implementation across three major complexes comprising 650+ residential units, a state aviation facility, and a large-scale climate-resilient electrification project. The study documents outcomes across five integrated workstreams: GOR-II – 250+ houses with 20-30 simultaneous renovations completed in 2021 alongside renovation of a gymnasium hall; GOR-III – 400 houses and flats ranging from 10 Marla to 2 Kanal with 70+ complete renovations completed over 1.7 years; GOR-I – 24/7 integrated MEP facility management for high-security government housing (senior judiciary and senior executive leadership) covering plumbing, electrical, sewerage, water supply, and HVAC with 2-hour emergency response; a state aviation hangar for a newly inducted government aircraft; and a PKR 1.5 Billion Weather Risk Reduction Project to underground the entire electrical distribution network of the GOR-I complex to prevent weather-related outages caused by trees and wind. The IFM framework achieved 35% reduction in renovation time (16-day 3-stage model vs 28-day conventional), 18-22% cost optimization through bulk procurement and reuse, 35% energy saving via LED conversion, and 99.2% stakeholder satisfaction with 90% projected reduction in weather-related power outages. Keywords: Integrated Facility Management, High-Security Government Housing, GOR, Renovation Optimization, Aviation Hangar, Underground Electrification, Weather Risk Reduction, Climate-Resilient Infrastructure, Public Infrastructure, C&W Punjab.
