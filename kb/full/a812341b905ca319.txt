Abstract: 
This study aimed to determine the best-fit model of Individual Work Performance (IWP) 
among public elementary school teachers in the Department of Education Region XII as 
influenced by Talent Management (TM), Intellectual Capital (IC), Psychological Capital 
(PsyCap), and Total Quality Management (TQM). A quantitative, non -experimental, 
cross-sectional predictive-correlational design was employed with 400 public elementary 
school teachers from the divisions of Sarangani, General Santos City, Koronadal City, and 
South Cotabato selected through stratified random sampling. Data were gathered using 
a researcher-made questionnaire and analyzed using descriptive statistics, Pearson’s r, 
multiple regression, path analysis, and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). All five 
constructs were rated Very High. TM, IC, PsyCap, and TQM were significantly related to 
IWP. In the final structural model, IC was the strongest predictor of IWP (β = .587, p < 
.001), followed by TQM (β = .280, p < .001) and PsyCap (β = .153, p < .05), whereas TM 
was not a significant direct predictor (β = -.085, ns). Model 3 demonstrated excellent fit: 
p = .124, CMIN/DF = 1.302, GFI = .984, CFI = .997, NFI = .989, TLI = .994, RMSEA = .028, 
and P-close = .949. The findings indicate that teacher performance is best explained by 
the integration of intellectual resources, quality -oriented management practices, and 
positive psychological resources. The study provides an evidence -based model for 
strengthening teacher effectiveness and educational quality in public elementary schools.

1. Introduction 
 
Individual Work Performance (IWP) is a central concern in public education because 
teachers are expected to sustain effective instructional, collaborative, and professional 
performance while responding to workload, accountability, organizational, and resource 
demands. The present study focuses on public elementary schools in DepEd Region XII, 
where teachers work across varied rural and urban contexts and are expected to meet 
institutional and learning standards. 
 The study examines four organizational and psychological resources —Talent 
Management, Intellectual Capital, Psychological Capital, and Total Quality 
Management—as potential predictors of IWP. Talent Management concerns attraction, 
retention, development, m otivation, and succession planning. Intellectual Capital 
comprises human, structural, and relational capital. Psychological Capital includes hope, 
self-efficacy, resilience, and optimism. Total Quality Management emphasizes 
management commitment, stakeholder focus, training and education, staff involvement, 
and continuous improvement. IWP is represented by task performance, contextual 
performance, and counterproductive work behavior. 
 Prior studies cited in the manuscript indicate that these constructs are associated 
with employee and teacher performance, but they have often been investigated 
separately or in corporate and higher -education settings. The manuscript identifies a 
research gap in the integrated examination of TM, IC, PsyCap, and TQM among public 
elementary school teachers in Region XII and in the use of SEM to develop a best -fit 
model for IWP. 
 The study therefore aimed to determine the levels of TM, IC, PsyCap , TQM, and 
IWP; determine their significant relationships; identify their predictive influence on IWP; 
and develop and validate a best-fit SEM explaining Individual Work Performance among 
public elementary school teachers. 
 
2. Literature Review 
 
2.1 Conceptual and Theoretical Foundations 
Talent Management is viewed as a strategic organizational process that supports 
recruitment, retention, development, motivation, and succession. In the study, TM is 
linked to Social Exchange Theory: organizational investments in teachers are expected to 
be reciprocated through commitment, engagement, and improved performance. 
 Intellectual Capital is grounded in the Knowledge -Based View, which treats 
knowledge, expertise, organizational systems, and relationships as strategic resources.

Human capital reflects teachers’ knowledge and skills, structural capital reflects systems 
and processes, and relational capital reflects collaboration and stakeholder relationships. 
The manuscript positions IC as a central resource for teacher effectiveness. 
 Psychological Capital is anchored in Conservation of Resources Theory and 
includes hope, self-efficacy, resilience, and optimism. These psychological resources help 
teachers sustain motivation, cope with demands, and remain effective. Self -
Determination Theory further highlights the importance of autonomy, competence, and 
relatedness in supporting motivation and work performance. 
 Total Quality Management is associated in the manuscript with Deming’s quality 
management principles. Management commitment, stakeholder focus, staff 
involvement, training and education, and continuous improvement are expected to 
create a quality -oriented environment that supports teacher performance and school 
effectiveness. 
 Individual Work Performance is treated as the endogenous construct. It 
encompasses task performance, contextual performance, and counterproductive work 
behavior. The integrated framework proposes that organizational resources, intellectual 
resources, psych ological capacities, and quality -management practices jointly explain 
differences in teacher performance. 
 
2.2 Research Gap and Integrated Model 
Although the literature reviewed in the manuscript supports relationships between the 
individual constructs and performance, limited evidence was identified on their 
combined influence among public elementary school teachers in DepEd Region XII. The 
study addresses this gap through an integrated SEM framework and compares three 
generated models to identify the structure that most closely fits the observed data. 
 
3. Material and Methods 
 
3.1 Research Design and Locale 
The study employed a quantitative, non -experimental, cross -sectional predictive -
correlational design. Data were collected from public elementary school teachers in four 
selected DepEd Region XII divisions: General Santos City, Koronadal City, Sarangani 
Province, and South Cotabato. The design allowed the researcher to examine 
relationships and predictive effects without manipulating the variables. 
 
3.2 Participants and Sampling 
The target population across the selected divisions was 21,344 public elementary school 
teachers. A total of 400 respondents were selected through stratified random sampling 
with proportional allocation: General Santos City (100), Koronadal City (31), Sara ngani 
Province (122), and South Cotabato (147). Respondents were public elementary teachers 
aged 23–60 years with at least five years of teaching experience who voluntarily provided

3.3 Instrumentation 
A researcher-made questionnaire measured five constructs using a five-point Likert scale

produced an average rating of 4.5/5.00. Pilot testing with 50 public elementa ry school 
teachers established acceptable internal consistency, with Cronbach’s alpha values 
exceeding .70 for all constructs.

3.4 Data Collection and Ethics 
The researcher secured ethics clearance from the University of Mindanao Ethics Review 
Committee (UMERC) and formal permission from DepEd Region XII, participating 
Schools Division Offices, and school heads. Participation was voluntary and based on 
informed consent. Responses were treated confidentially, and data were securely stored 
and used for academic purposes. The study cites UMERC Compliance Certificate No. 
2025-483. 
 
3.5 Statistical Treatment 
Mean and standard deviation were used to describe the levels of the five constructs. 
Pearson Product -Moment Correlation was used to determine relationships, while 
multiple regression and path analysis examined predictive and direct effects. Structural 
Equation Modeling was used to test the hypothesized model and identify the best -fit 
structure. Model fit was evaluated using p-value, CMIN/DF, GFI, CFI, NFI, TLI, RMSEA, 
and P-close, following the criteria stated in the manuscript.

4.3 Predictive Effects and Best-Fit Structural Model 
When the predictors were examined simultaneously, Intellectual Capital, Psychological 
Capital, and Total Quality Management significantly predicted Individual Work 
Performance, while Talent Management did not demonstrate a significant direct effect. 
The manuscript interprets this result as evidence that Talent Management may operate 
as an enabling or background condition whose effects are expressed through other 
organizational and psychological mechanisms.

5. Recommendations 
 
• School heads and principals should strengthen succession planning, mentoring, 
leadership preparation, and transparent performance-based recognition to sustain 
teacher development. 
• DepEd and Schools Division Offices should strengthen access to instructional 
materials, ICT resources, training budgets, and technical assistance, particularly in 
areas related to structural and organizational capital. 
• Schools should institutionalize continuous improvement mechanisms, including 
systematic feedback, monitoring, evaluation, and school -level quality 
improvement cycles.

• Teachers should be supported through wellness, resilience-building, collaborative

• Parents, community stakeholders, and school governing councils should be

• Future researchers should test the model in other regions and educational levels

6. Conclusion 
 
Public elementary school teachers in the selected DepEd Region XII divisions reported 
Very High levels of Talent Management, Intellectual Capital, Psychological Capital, Total 
Quality Management, and Individual Work Performance. All four exogenous constructs 
were significantly related to IWP. However, when examined simultaneously in the 
structural model, Intellectual Capital, Total Quality Management, and Psychological 
Capital emerged as significant direct predictors, while Talent Management did not. 
Model 3 provided the best-fit explanation of teacher Individual Work Performance, with 
excellent goodness-of-fit indices and Intellectual Capital as the strongest predictor. The 
findings emphasize that teacher effectiveness is not explained by talent pract ices alone 
but is strengthened by the interaction of knowledge resources, collaborative and 
organizational systems, psychological capacities, and quality -oriented management 
practices.
