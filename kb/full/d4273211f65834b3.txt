Chlorinated ethenes (CEs) are major contaminants in soil and groundwater. Pseudonocardia sp. D17 (D17) aerobically degrades trichloroethene (TCE), cis -1,2-dichloroethene (cDCE), trans -1,2-dichloroethene (tDCE), 1,1-dichloroethene (1,1-DCE), and vinyl chloride (VC). This study examined how the co-occurrence of two of these five CEs affected their biodegradation by D17. Biodegradation experiments showed that TCE and cDCE biodegradation were inhibited in the presence of other CEs, although the inhibition of cDCE degradation was alleviated over time. tDCE degradation was significantly inhibited by TCE, cDCE, and 1,1-DCE. 1,1-DCE degradation was consistently inhibited by TCE and transiently stimulated by VC, whereas the effects of cDCE and tDCE on 1,1-DCE shifted from inhibition to stimulation over time. VC degradation by D17 was significantly inhibited by TCE, slightly inhibited by cDCE and 1,1-DCE, and slightly stimulated by tDCE. These results demonstrate that co-occurring CEs exert compound-specific effects on the degradation of individual CEs by D17. Heterologous expression experiments showed that propane monooxygenase catalyzed the degradation of all five, whereas tetrahydrofuran monooxygenase catalyzed the degradation of three CEs. These results may suggest that competitive enzyme inhibition, together with cytotoxic intermediates, contributed to the inhibitory effects observed in CE biodegradation experiments. Overall, D17 shows strong potential for application at sites contaminated with mixed CEs and provides new insight into the enzymes involved in aerobic CE degradation.
