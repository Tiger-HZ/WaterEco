Abstract Coral reefs have undergone phase shifts from coral to algal dominance due to anthropogenic pressures such as nutrient enrichment, overfishing, and climate change. Large herbivorous fishes play a critical role in preventing algal overgrowth, yet this top-down regulation is increasingly threatened by size-selective fishing. However, the role of herbivore body size and algal composition in mediating top-down control remains poorly understood, particularly in the Caribbean. We conducted a field experiment using exclusion cages of varying mesh sizes on a fringing coral reef on the west coast of Barbados to test the role of herbivore body size in regulating algal biomass and community composition.
