Standing waves on river surfaces encode flow velocity and depth through the dispersion relation, but their use for non-contact monitoring has been limited to wavelength and frequency because no field-deployable method resolves the three-dimensional water-surface geometry, including wave amplitude. We introduce a novel inverse-modeling framework that reconstructs time-averaged water-surface geometry from a single oblique camera by treating angular deviations of tracked surface flow paths from the streamwise direction as a signature of local surface slope. Synthetic tests recovered imposed wavelengths to within 5% in 35 of 39 cases and reproduced amplitude trends.
