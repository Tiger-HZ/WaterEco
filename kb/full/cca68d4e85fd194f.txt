Nutrients and oxygen are sensed within the muscle to control growth and disruption of either signal is sufficient to lead to muscle atrophy. While nutrient limitation is sensed via a conserved transcriptional atrophy program (commonly referred to as atrogenes) dictated via the Forkhead box O (FoxO) transcription factors, how low oxygen promotes muscle loss remains unknown. Accordingly, the downstream mechanisms that initiate muscle loss when oxygen and nutrients are limiting are only partly understood. Here, we find Hypoxia Inducible Factor (HIF), the master regulator of our adaptation to low oxygen, is necessary and sufficient to mediate muscle loss under hypoxia in mice.
