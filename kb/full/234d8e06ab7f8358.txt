Abstract
With the rapid development of industrial IoT and intelligent scheduling systems, 
real-time fusion of multi-source heterogeneous data and decision optimization 
have become critical challenges. Traditional centralized data collection methods 
face privacy leakage and communication bottlenecks, while federated learning (FL) 
protects data privacy through distributed model training but has limited temporal 
decision-making capabilities in dynamic scheduling scenarios. This study combines 
federated learning with Decision Transformers (DT) to propose a multi-source 
data fusion architecture for real-time industrial scheduling dashboards, addressing 
privacy protection and temporal prediction challenges in cross-departmental, cross-
regional data collaboration. The system employs a FedAvg-based federated learning 
framework for local training at 20 distributed nodes and global model aggregation, 
enhancing model generalization while safeguarding data privacy. Leveraging 
Decision Transformers’ strengths in modeling long-range temporal dependencies, 
it dynamically optimizes dispatch strategies via trajectory-based sequence learning. 
Experiments were deployed in a simulated industrial scheduling environment 
incorporating five heterogeneous data sources (device sensor data, historical 
scheduling records, environmental parameters, network status data, and user 
demand data) with 100,000 temporal samples. We compared the proposed method 
with traditional centralized LSTM, single federated learning, XGBoost, LightGBM, and 
modern tabular deep learning models. Results demonstrate a 12.7% improvement 
in scheduling accuracy over the traditional centralized LSTM model, a 63% reduction 
in cumulative parameter-transmission volume relative to the FedAvg-GRU baseline 
under the same client participation schedule, and an empirical privacy-attack success 
rate below 0.05%. Under dynamic load fluctuation scenarios, the single-step inference 
latency remains below 200 ms, whereas the average time required to complete an 
end-to-end scheduling cycle is 3.1 min; the system processes more than 2000 real-
time data streams per second with an average CPU utilization of 51.6% and a peak 
memory usage of 8.9 GB. This research offers a novel solution for multi-source data 
fusion in industrial scheduling dashboards, balancing privacy security with decision 
efficiency, and validates the effectiveness of integrating federated learning with 
decision-making time-series models.

 1 Introduction
With the continuous deepening of industrial intelligentization and the extensive deploy-
ment of information infrastructure, multi-source heterogeneous data has shown 
explosive growth in complex systems. Especially in scheduling scenarios involving cross-
domain collaboration and real - time decision-making, the effective fusion and intelligent 
utilization of massive data have become the key to improving the system’s responsive -
ness and operational robustness [ 1–3]. When faced with the real-world environment 
of wide geographic distribution, strict privacy constraints, and dispersed computing 
resources, the traditional centralized data processing model has gradually revealed bot -
tlenecks, including high communication costs, difficulty breaking down data silos, and 
increased security risks. Therefore, it is urgent to explore a new architecture that bal -
ances privacy protection, collaborative efficiency, and decision-making intelligence [ 4, 
5]. As a distributed machine learning paradigm, federated learning can realize the col -
laborative optimization of multi-party models without the need to aggregate the original 
data centrally, providing a feasible path for the joint mining of cross-domain data value. 
Its decentralized and privacy-friendly characteristics meet the requirements of multi-
institutional linkage and the coexistence of sensitive information faced by scheduling 
dashboards [6]. At the same time, as a type of generative policy network that introduces 
the idea of sequence modeling into the reinforcement learning framework, Decision 
Transformer can model long-range dependencies with the help of historical trajectories 
and task goals, and directly learn a robust decision mapping under offline data condi -
tions, opening up new ideas for the scheduling system to achieve forward-looking plan -
ning and adaptive adjustment in an uncertain environment [7, 8]. Organically integrating 
the two into the multi-source data fusion system of the scheduling dashboard is not only 
expected to break through the limitations of existing methods in limited data acquisition 
and insufficient policy generalization, but also can build a new form of intelligent sched -
uling with global perception, privacy security, and policy foresight [9, 10].
In the application of scheduling dashboards for complex production operations and 
public service networks, multi-source data often covers multi-modal information such 
as sensor time-series signals, business management records, environmental perception 
images, and user interaction logs. There are significant differences in the structural form, 
spatio-temporal scale, and semantic level among these data sources. The fusion process 
not only needs to address cross-domain feature alignment and noise suppression, but 
also maintain the timeliness and consistency of information under dynamically changing 
working conditions [11–13]. Most traditional fusion mechanisms rely on a central node 
for unified data cleaning and feature extraction, which easily weakens scalability due to 
communication overhead and trust issues. They may not be implemented due to data 
ownership and compliance requirements in cross-institutional collaboration [ 14]. The 
introduction of federated learning enables each participant to perform feature encod -
ing and model updates locally and to conduct only limited interactions via encrypted 
gradients or parameter summaries, thereby forming a globally consistent representation 
space while ensuring data sovereignty and providing robust support for the semantic

bridging of multi-source heterogeneous components [ 15]. However, simply relying on

Decision Transformer serializes states, actions, and rewards into a stream of tokens

pattern reproduction and generalization of high-quality trajectory data. This offline

risk caused by centralized collection of original decision-making data but also improves 
the adaptability of the policy to diverse scenarios through the sharing of cross-domain 
experiences. Further, at the presentation level of the scheduling large screen, the uni -
fied situational picture generated by multi-source data fusion and the forward-looking 
scheduling suggestions output by Decision Transformer can be visually linked, enabling 
operators to perceive the system evolution trend in an intuitive interface and intervene 
in key decision-making processes, thus bridging the gap between underlying data intel -
ligence and high-level cognitive control.
Currently, independent research on federated learning and deep reinforcement learn -
ing has yielded substantial results. However, in the application scenario of dispatch 
dashboards—which demands real-time processing, multi-agent collaboration, and visual 
interaction—the construction of an integrated framework that balances privacy secu -
rity, cross-domain coordination, and long-term decision-making capabilities remains an 
exploratory frontier. In this context, multi-source data fusion systems serve not only as 
technical vehicles for information integration but also as trusted intermediaries bridg -
ing distributed sensing and centralized decision-making. Their design must establish a 
dynamic equilibrium among model learning efficiency, policy robustness, and human-
machine collaboration usability.
Recent studies on adaptive machine-learning optimization and hierarchical feature-
driven decision-making have shown that robust model performance can be improved 
when algorithms explicitly consider informative feature selection, heterogeneous data, 
and dynamically changing operating conditions. This observation is relevant to the pres -
ent work, where multi-source scheduling data must be fused efficiently while maintain -
ing reliable and privacy-preserving decision performance.
To balance recent application-oriented studies with foundational work, this study is 
grounded in the original Decision Transformer formulation [ 17], FedProx for hetero -
geneous federated optimization [ 18], and the federated machine-learning framework 
summarized by Yang et al. [ 19]. These works provide the theoretical basis for sequence-
conditioned decision modeling, non-IID client optimization, and privacy-preserving 
cross-silo collaboration.
The core contribution of this article is to propose a multi-source data fusion archi -
tecture that combines FL and DT, and for the first time achieve long-term scheduling

2 Theoretical basis and analysis of relevant technologies
2.1 Basic principles and collaborative mechanism of federated learning
FL uses a distributed training strategy. Each device or client independently trains on its 
local dataset to enhance privacy protection and avoid sharing raw data [ 20]. After train-
ing, the client uploads the updated version of its local model to the central node, which 
integrates the update content and optimizes the global model parameters. By storing, 
processing, and learning data locally and avoiding direct exchanges, privacy protection 
is improved [21].
Federated learning adopts a collaborative strategy to jointly optimize and build a gen -
eral machine learning model on the device side. Each device retains its local data, as 
shown in Fig. 1. The training process is as follows:
The server initializes the global model and distributes it to all clients. This model can 
obtain basic model parameters through pre - training on the initial global dataset [ 22]. 
Each client starts local training from this global model, aiming to lay a unified founda -
tion for subsequent training and ensure that all clients have the same initial parameters 
and synchronous training [ 23]. First, pre-process the client’s local data without upload -
ing it to the central server to ensure privacy. After receiving the global model, the client 
iteratively trains the model with its own dataset to optimize the parameters, involving 
activities such as forward propagation, loss function calculation, back-propagation to 
calculate gradients, and weight updates [24]. Each client conducts multiple local training 
rounds, updating the weight parameters in each round to improve the learning ability.

2.2 Theoretical architecture and decision-making modeling mechanism of transformer
First, ViT (Vision Transformer) uses a Transformer as its backbone in the visual domain, 
and its architecture serves as a demonstration for subsequent Transformer-based back -
bone networks. The ViT architecture comprises three modules: patch embedding, posi -
tion encoding, and a Transformer [27].
As the first module for data input in Transformer, the patch embedding module is cru-
cial in the network. It converts the input image into a sequence that can be processed by 
Transformer [28]. Assuming the input image has dimensions H×W×3, it is first evenly 
divided into N blocks, and all blocks are then unfolded to obtain N-dimensional 3𝐻𝐻𝐻𝐻

vectors. After passing through a linear mapping layer, the dimension is mapped to C 
dimensions, resulting in N C-dimensional sequences. In the field of computer vision, 
these sequences are called tokens.
The position encoding module helps the ViT network locate tokens within the image. 
The Transformer’s self-attention is insensitive to the order of input tokens, and differ -
ent input orders do not affect network performance. However, each token represents a 
specific area of the image, and if the position changes, the image’s meaning will be lost.

token, assisting the network in identifying its position and correctly understanding the

3 Modeling of a multi-source data fusion system for the scheduling dashboard 
integrating federated learning and decision transformer
3.1 Design of the overall system architecture and functional modules
Based on the technical characteristics of federated learning and the Decision Trans -
former integration, and in line with the actual business requirements for multi-source 
data fusion in the scheduling dashboard, the overall architecture of this system adopts a 
hierarchical design [31–33]. From bottom to top, it consists of the data access layer, the 
federated learning layer, the Decision Transformer decision-making layer, the applica -
tion service layer, and the interactive display layer. The responsibilities of each layer are 
clearly defined, and efficient data and instruction transfer is achieved through standard -
ized interfaces. The overall system architecture is shown in Fig. 2.
In terms of functional module design, the system mainly includes the following core 
modules:

The multi-source data access and preprocessing module connects to heterogeneous 
data sources of different scheduling nodes. After data preprocessing, such as data clean -
ing, it converts the data into a standard data format and stores and manages it locally to 
ensure data security and privacy. The federated learning collaborative training module 
is the core support for distributed intelligent decision-making. Each participating party 
trains the model locally and only uploads parameter updates. The server integrates the 
parameters to form a global model. Then it distributes it, enabling “data remains station-
ary while models traverse” and addressing data silos and privacy concerns. The Decision 
Transformer dynamic decision-making module is the key to intelligent scheduling deci -
sion-making. It receives the output from the federated learning layer, combines it with 
real - time state input, and uses the model’s capabilities to predict and optimize deci -
sions. It can dynamically adjust strategies according to different objectives. The decision 
result parsing and execution module parses abstract decisions into executable instruc -
tions, has interface-adaptation capabilities, issues instructions, and tracks and feeds 
back execution status. The scheduling dashboard visualization and interaction module 
provides managers with an interface for multi-source data display and decision support, 
shows the status of each node and the scheduling situation, supports interactive opera -
tions, and realizes human-machine collaborative decision-making. The system manage -
ment and monitoring module is responsible for system configuration, user permissions, 
logs, operation monitoring, and alarms, ensuring the system’s stability, security, reliabil -
ity, and maintainability.
Multi-source inputs are processed through a single, reproducible data path. Time-
series variables are aligned to a one-second grid and organized with sliding windows, 
categorical variables are one-hot encoded, and numerical variables are normalized. The 
resulting client-local feature tensor is passed directly to a three-layer CNN encoder, 
which produces a 128-dimensional state representation for the downstream Decision 
Transformer. No LSTM encoder or separate cross-modal attention branch is used in the 
experiments reported in this paper.
The Decision Transformer is the sole temporal decision module used for scheduling. 
Each scheduling trajectory is serialized as a token sequence of the return-to-go R t, the 
128-dimensional CNN state s t, the action a t, and the time step t. Self-attention models 
the long-range dependencies in this sequence, and the current scheduling action is gen -
erated conditionally on the target return-to-go.
The state s t is a 128-dimensional vector containing device status, task-queue status, 
and environmental monitoring information. The action a t is a 20-dimensional one-hot 
vector that assigns a task to one of the numbered devices. The return-to-go R t is the 
inverse of the future cumulative scheduling cost; therefore, maximizing R t is equivalent 
to minimizing total scheduling cost. The context length is 16 time steps.
During federated training, each client optimizes the same CNN-DT model on its 
local heterogeneous trajectories and uploads only model-parameter updates. The server 
applies FedAvg to aggregate the CNN encoder and Decision Transformer parameters 
jointly and redistributes the resulting global model. The reported model is therefore a 
single end-to-end FL + CNN+DT architecture rather than an ensemble of independent 
temporal models.

3.2 Construction of federated learning access and synchronization mechanism for multi-
source heterogeneous data
In the federated learning system, multiple clients collaborate to train the global model. 
The direct coordination method involves simultaneous pulling, gradient calculation, 
and pushing operations, as in the BSP (Bulk Synchronous Parallel) method. However, 
BSP can lead to the “straggler” problem. For example, if Client 2 takes the longest time 
for each local iteration, the synchronization waiting times for Client 3 and Client 1 are 
τ2iter-τ3iter and τ2iter-τ1iter, respectively. Before Client 2 finishes its calculation, Client 
1 and Client 3 will be idle, resulting in wasted resources and slowing convergence. This 
problem becomes more severe when the computing and communication capabilities are 
heterogeneous.
At the decision-making level, short-term and long-term temporal dependencies are 
handled within the same Decision Transformer through its 16-step context window and 
multi-head self-attention, while the three-layer CNN performs only local feature encod -
ing. This definition is used consistently in model training, comparison, and deployment.
To solve the “straggler” problem of BSP , some people proposed the SSP (Stale Synchro-
nous Parallel) semi-synchronous method. Although SSP allows asynchronous parameter 
updates, there is a limit to the staleness between the fastest and slowest clients. When 
the predefined staleness cannot adapt to the heterogeneity, there is still synchronization 
waiting time. For example, Client 1 must wait for Client 2 when Client 2 reaches the 
threshold. The Chronos design concept is an optimal synchronization scheme. The local 
iteration time is affected by the gradient calculation time and the Batch size. Adjust -
ing the Batch size for all clients can reduce heterogeneity, and allocating an appropriate 
Batch size can eliminate synchronization wait time. Unlike SSP , Chronos’s batch size is 
flexible, enabling it to adapt to heterogeneous, dynamic environments.
3.3 Parameter allocation strategy of decision transformer in dynamic scheduling scenarios
Existing Decision Transformer (DT) parameter allocation mostly follows the gen -
eral Transformer architecture, which does not fully adapt to the core requirements of 
dynamic scheduling scenarios—i.e., the module importance differences in state encod -
ing, action prediction, and temporal dependency modeling. In most current designs, the 
Q, K, and V vector dimensions of the self-attention mechanism are identical, and the 
FFN layer expansion ratio is fixed at 4. While simple to implement, this approach leads 
to parameter redundancy and insufficient computing power for key modules under the 
real - time and resource-efficient demands of scheduling tasks.
To optimize the parameter efficiency and response speed of DT in dynamic sched -
uling, this study proposes a task-specific parameter allocation strategy for scheduling 
scenarios: module importance is quantified based on Taylor analysis, and computing 
resources are prioritized for core modules. For the core logic of “task state-resource state 
matching → action output” in scheduling scenarios, the Q (task state encoding) and K 
(resource state encoding) projection matrices of self-attention heads are set to smaller 
channel sizes, while the V (action mapping output) projection matrix size is kept consis -
tent with the input embedding, reducing parameter redundancy in non-critical mapping 
processes. Meanwhile, the FFN layer expansion ratio is reduced from 4 to 2, lowering 
model inference latency without compromising scheduling decision accuracy to meet 
the real - time requirements of dynamic scheduling.

To accommodate scheduling demands of varying complexity (e.g., single-task schedul-
ing, multi-task parallel scheduling, dynamic burst task scheduling), this paper constructs 
five lightweight scheduling-adaptive models (named DT-Sched-V0 to DT-Sched-V4), 
which achieve precise adaptation by adjusting core module width, network depth, and 
number of attention heads. The model width (corresponding to feature encoding dimen-
sion) increases sequentially to 48, 56, 80, 96, and 128; a larger width better captures the 
multi-source data correlation features of complex scheduling scenarios. The network 
depth design aligns with the temporal dependency characteristics of scheduling: fewer 
temporal encoding modules are stacked in the initial stage to reduce startup latency, and 
the number of modules gradually increases as the network deepens, enhancing feature 
extraction capabilities for long-cycle scheduling tasks.
This task-specific allocation idea is consistent with recent adaptive learning stud -
ies showing that selecting and weighting informative features or modules can improve 
robustness, accuracy, and computational efficiency in complex data-driven decision 
systems.
In terms of specific configurations: DT-Sched-V0 to V3 share the same depth of {1,2,3} 
(corresponding to three temporal encoding stages), suitable for regular-complexity 
scheduling tasks; DT-Sched-V4 adds one module in the final stage, adjusting the depth 
to {1,3,4} to handle complex scheduling scenarios with frequent burst tasks. The number 
of attention heads is configured differently based on module functions: DT-Sched-V1 
sets the number of attention heads to {4,3,2} in the second and third stages (core deci -
sion stages), balancing lightweight design and multi-task priority differentiation capa -
bilities; DT-Sched-V0, V2, and V4 maintain a balanced configuration of {4,4,3} to ensure 
parallel processing efficiency of multi-source scheduling data. Through the above differ -
entiated parameter allocation, the models can flexibly adapt to scheduling efficiency and 
accuracy requirements of different scenarios while remaining lightweight.
At the model level, L2 norm penalty (λ = 1 × 10−4) is applied to all trainable parameters 
to constrain their spatial scale, and Dropout operation (dropout rate p = 0.2) is embed-
ded after the fully connected layer of the network to enhance the robustness of feature 
representation through its forward propagation random masking mechanism. At the 
level of federated aggregation, we introduced weight decay (γ = 1 × 10−5) in the global 
model update on the server side. By applying slight parameter shrinkage in each aggre -
gation iteration, we stabilized the optimized trajectory and enhanced the generalization 
ability of the final global model to unseen multi-source scheduling data.
To achieve decision transparency, the system integrates an interpretable artificial intel-
ligence framework based on SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlans). Use KernelExplainer to 
calculate the marginal contribution (Shapley value) of each input feature to the final 
scheduling decision. In the system visualization dashboard, not only is a global feature 
importance heatmap provided to reveal the key factors affecting the overall scheduling 
strategy, but it also supports decision path tracing for any individual scheduling deci -
sion sample, intuitively demonstrating how each feature drives the model to guide spe -
cific decisions, thereby providing dispatchers with reliable and interventionist intelligent 
decision-making basis.
SHAP explanations are generated after scheduling inference and are presented at two 
levels: global feature importance over the evaluation samples and local contribution

4 Experimental setup and result analysis
4.1 Explanation of the experimental environment and dataset construction
Regarding the experimental environment, all experiments were conducted on a server 
running TensorFlow 2.0 with an Intel Xeon E5-2630 @ 2.6  GHz processor and an 
NVIDIA T4 GPU. The federated system contains 20 clients. Each client trains a three-
layer CNN encoder followed by the Decision Transformer, and the server maintains and 
aggregates the same global CNN-DT parameter set. The global model is randomly ini -
tialized, the local batch size is 256, and each selected client performs one local epoch per 
communication round.
When simulating resource limitations, the client participation ratio p controls the 
number of selected clients in each communication round. For example, p = 0.2 means 
that 20% of the clients participate, representing a severe resource-constrained setting. 
Scheduling accuracy is the primary decision-quality measure, while latency, on-time 
completion, overtime rate, throughput, resource consumption, communication over -
head, and privacy-attack success rate are reported as complementary indicators.
The 100,000 aligned temporal samples were first divided chronologically into 70% 
training, 15% validation, and 15% test partitions. Five-fold blocked cross-validation was 
conducted within the training partition only, with temporal order preserved in every 
fold; the validation partition was used for early stopping, and the test partition remained 
fixed. After hyperparameter selection, the model was retrained on the complete training 
partition and evaluated on the fixed test partition in five independent runs that differed 
only in model initialization and client sampling. Results are reported as mean ± stan-
dard deviation, and paired t-tests (p < 0.05) with 1000 bootstrap resamples were used for 
hypothesis testing.
Federated learning was run for 50 communication rounds. In each round, participat -
ing clients trained for one local epoch with a batch size of 256 using Adam at a learning 
rate of 1 × 10 − 3, after which FedAvg aggregated the complete CNN-DT parameter set. 
The Decision Transformer used a context length of 16, an embedding dimension of 128, 
and four attention heads. Communication overhead was measured as the cumulative 
number of bytes transmitted for model parameters between participating clients and the 
server over all 50 rounds. The reported 63% reduction was calculated relative to FedAvg-
GRU under the same client participation ratio and communication-round setting.
The simulation scheduling environment constructed in this study integrates five types 
of heterogeneous data sources, including: (1) device sensor data (35000 pieces, 1  Hz 
sampling, including voltage, current, and load rate); (2) Historical scheduling records 
(28000 records, including task ID, priority, and execution time); (3) Environmental 
parameter data (12000 records, 5-min sampling, including temperature, humidity, and 
wind speed); (4) Network status data (15000 pieces, 10 s sampling, including bandwidth, 
latency, and packet loss rate); (5) User demand data (10000 pieces, including submission 
time and resource requirements).
After one-second alignment, a temporal sample denotes one scheduling instant con -
taining the available values from the five data sources, the current system state, the 
Page 12 of 24
Su and Hu Discover Artificial Intelligence (2026) 6:1155 
selected device-assignment action, and the return-to-go. The simulator generated the 
supervision target by selecting the feasible assignment with the minimum cumulative 
scheduling cost under the current resource constraints.
The multi-source data-cleaning process removed sensor outliers using the 3σ rule, 
interpolated missing time-series values linearly, filled missing structured numerical val -
ues with the training-set mean, aligned all inputs to a one-second time grid, normalized 
numerical variables to [0,1], and one-hot encoded categorical variables. When a client 
did not contain a data modality because of the non-IID partition, the corresponding 
normalized channels were set to zero and a binary modality-availability mask was con -
catenated to the input. The same preprocessing parameters fitted on the training parti -
tion were applied to validation and test data.
The reproducible training sequence was: (1) preprocess and temporally align each cli -
ent’s local data; (2) encode the local feature tensor with the three-layer CNN; (3) con -
struct 16-step return-state-action sequences for the Decision Transformer; (4) perform 
one local epoch on each selected client; (5) aggregate the complete CNN-DT parameters 
with FedAvg; and (6) repeat Steps 2–5 for 50 communication rounds before evaluation 
on the fixed test partition.
Two timing measures are used. Single-step inference latency is the elapsed time from 
receipt of one aligned state vector to generation of one scheduling action. End-to-end 
scheduling-cycle time is the total elapsed time required to collect the current inputs, 
generate and parse all actions, complete resource allocation, and finalize one scheduling 
plan. The values below 200 ms refer to single-step inference.

5 Discussion
To strengthen the baseline comparison, two temporal decision-making methods were 
added. Fed-LSTM was used as a federated recurrent baseline, in which each client 
locally trained an LSTM scheduling model and the model parameters were aggregated 
using FedAvg. This baseline retains the federated-learning framework while replacing 
the Decision Transformer with a conventional recurrent temporal model, allowing the 
contribution of Decision Transformer-based sequence modeling to be evaluated. Con -
servative Q-Learning (CQL) was included as an offline reinforcement-learning baseline. 
CQL learns a conservative action-value function from historical scheduling trajectories 
and generates scheduling actions without federated parameter aggregation, thereby pro-
viding a comparison with value-based offline sequential decision-making.

lower the value, the higher the accuracy. MAE is the average absolute error, MAE=∑ | 
decision output optimal output |/n, the lower the value, the better the decision quality.
The experimental results show that the proposed fusion model improves scheduling 
accuracy by 12.7% compared with the traditional centralized LSTM model. The increase 
in scheduling accuracy, together with the higher on-time completion rate and lower 
overtime rate, indicates that the model more reliably selects feasible low-cost actions 
and maintains service quality under dynamic loads.
To evaluate the generalization performance and training stability of the model, the 
learning curve validation showed that the model tended to converge after 20 rounds 
of communication in the federated learning framework, and its validation set accuracy

rate.
Based on this lightweight block design, DT-Sched-V0–V4 form a progressive capac -
ity spectrum rather than five independent model structures. DT-Sched-V0, with a width 
of 48, has the smallest parameter budget and is suitable for low-complexity single-task 
scheduling. DT-Sched-V1 increases the width to 56 while retaining a lightweight tem -
poral structure. DT-Sched-V2, with a width of 80, provides a balanced configuration for 
routine multi-task scheduling. DT-Sched-V3 increases the width to 96 to strengthen the 
representation of cross-source correlations. DT-Sched-V4 adopts a width of 128 and a 
deeper stage configuration of {1,3,4}, allocating more capacity to later temporal stages 
for complex burst-task scenarios. Taking a standard 128-dimensional six-block Deci -
sion Transformer as the reference, the analytical parameter proxies of DT-Sched-V0–V4 
are approximately 8.2%, 11.2%, 22.8%, 32.8%, and 77.8% of the standard configuration, 
respectively. Thus, even the largest DT-Sched-V4 variant retains a lower dominant 
parameter budget than the standard model, while the smaller variants provide sub -
stantially greater reductions in model size and expected inference cost. This compari -
son demonstrates that the proposed strategy provides a controllable capacity–efficiency 
trade-off instead of uniformly reducing all model components.

6 Conclusion
In response to the challenges of multi-source heterogeneous data fusion and real - time 
decision-making in industrial scheduling scenarios, this paper proposes a multi-source 
data fusion system for the scheduling dashboard that combines federated learning and 
a Decision Transformer. The system achieves privacy-protected collaborative training 
among distributed nodes through federated learning. By combining the powerful mod -
eling ability of Decision Transformer for sequential decision - making, it improves the 
accuracy and response efficiency of scheduling decisions while ensuring data privacy. 
The experimental results show that:
1. In terms of scheduling accuracy, the fusion model reaches 92.5%, an increase of 12.7 
percentage points compared with the traditional centralized LSTM model.
2. In terms of communication and privacy, cumulative parameter-transmission volume 
is reduced by 63% relative to FedAvg-GRU under the same client participation 
schedule, and the mean empirical privacy-attack success rate is below 0.05% under 
the semi-honest threat model.
3. In terms of real-time performance, single-step inference latency remains below 200 
ms, whereas the average end-to-end scheduling-cycle time is 3.1 min. The system 
processes more than 2000 real-time data streams per second, with an average CPU 
utilization of 51.6% and a peak memory usage of 8.9 GB.
The limitations of this study include the semi-honest threat model, which does not 
cover malicious poisoning attacks; the absence of a formal differential-privacy budget 
and attack-specific confidence intervals; the offline training mode, which lacks online 
incremental updating; and possible performance degradation under extreme data het -
erogeneity. Future work will investigate federated adversarial training, formal privacy 
accounting, online federated learning, and heterogeneity-aware aggregation.
