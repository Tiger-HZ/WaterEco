Abstract In this study, Cu/Bentonite (Cu/Bent) nanohybrids were successfully prepared via a thermal decomposition method using copper chloride dihydrate (CuCl2 · 2H2O) and sodium oleate as raw materials, with bentonite (Bent) serving as the support. The structure and morphology of the as-prepared nanohybrids were systematically characterized by X-ray powder diffraction (XRD), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR). Characterization confirms that Cu nanoparticles are intercalated into Bent interlayers and anchored on its surface, forming a stable nanohybrid architecture.
