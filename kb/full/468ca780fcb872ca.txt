Moradabad, in western Uttar Pradesh, is known worldwide as the Brass City, and its brassware, electroplating, and metal-recycling clusters sustain the regional economy. The same industry discharges metal-laden effluent that has left a documented mark on the district's water: heavy metals including arsenic, chromium, copper, nickel, lead, and zinc accumulate in the sediments of the Ramganga-Ganga interfluve and threaten the shallow groundwater on which most of the district depends. This paper offers a geographical treatment of that pollution and a framework for governing it.
