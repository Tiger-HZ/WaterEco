Environmental pollutants pose serious threats to plants, and ecosystems. Plants respond to pollution through various physiological, biochemical, molecular, genetic, morphological, and anatomical adaptations that help them tolerate environmental stress. Because of their sensitivity and ability to accumulate pollutants, plants serve as important bioindicators and biomonitors for assessing environmental quality. Plants absorb pollutants on the surface of leaves, and onto the plant root-soil system to minimize the pollution load in a particular area. Moreover, forests act as a purification system absorbing airborne chemicals and releasing oxygen.
