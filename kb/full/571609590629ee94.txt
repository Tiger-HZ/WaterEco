Weakly consolidated sandstone reservoirs in the Bohai region are susceptible to sand production and wellbore instability during high-water-cut production because water–rock interaction progressively degrades rock strength while reservoir depletion increases effective stress. This study proposes a time-dependent critical production drawdown framework that couples water-induced weakening with pore-pressure depletion. Laboratory soaking data are mapped to field production through a cumulative equivalent soaking time weighted by water cut and operating time.
