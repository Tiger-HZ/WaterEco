Abstract Parabens are hydroxybenzoic acid esters, used as preservatives in personal care products, pharmaceuticals, and food. Parabens have been classified as endocrine-disrupting compounds due to their estrogenic activity. Parabens enter aquatic environments primarily through wastewater effluent and runoff, and continuous inputs allow their pseudo-persistence despite relatively rapid degradation. While the environmental occurrence and toxicity of parent parabens are well characterized, little is known about their transformation products. This study quantified parabens and transformation products in water (BDL-10.6 ng/L) and sediment (BDL-4.5 ng/g) upstream and downstream of a low water dam on the Brazos River, Texas, USA, and characterized their partitioning coefficients (Kd, Koc).
