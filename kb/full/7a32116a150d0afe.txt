Near-field sources generate more sound energy at the target seat with minimal interference at others and are now sought after for local active noise control and personal audio zones in automotive cabins. In this paper, a cost-effective design of near-field sound sources in a car is proposed that uses only a single loudspeaker driver and a back-vented box loaded with porous materials. To avoid the difficulties in modeling the porous material inside the source, a method is developed to model the sound source and to deduce the volume-velocity ratio between the loudspeaker diaphragm and the box vent based on measured free-field sound pressures and a finite element model.
