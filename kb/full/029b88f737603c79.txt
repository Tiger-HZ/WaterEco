Abstract United States trademark law treats licensing a mark without quality control over the licensee – ‘naked licensing’ – as inherently deceptive conduct resulting in abandonment. European Union (EU) trademark law contains no comparable rule. The EU Trademark Regulation and the Trademark Directive impose no duty of control on the licensor, and use with mere consent is attributed to the proprietor for genuine use purposes. The contrast is puzzling, because the Court of Justice defines the essential function of the trademark by reference to the control of a single undertaking responsible for product quality. This article argues that the doctrine cannot be received de lege lata, since the grounds for revocation form a closed catalogue in which supervisory failure as such does not appear. T
