Reproductive health is shaped by a continuous interaction between inherited susceptibility and a complex mixture of chemical, physical, nutritional, metabolic, occupational, behavioral, and psychosocial exposures. The exposome framework provides a comprehensive model for studying these influences across the life course, including sensitive periods before conception, during gametogenesis, throughout embryonic and placental development, and during pregnancy. Epigenetic regulation represents one of the principal mechanisms through which environmental signals may become biologically embedded without changing the underlying DNA sequence. DNA methylation, histone modifications, chromatin remodeling, genomic imprinting, and regulatory non-coding RNAs can influence germ-cell competence, endocrine signaling, fertilization, implantation, placental function, fetal growth, and pregnancy maintenance. Experimental studies provide substantial evidence that endocrine-disrupting chemicals, air pollutants, toxic metals, pesticides, persistent organic pollutants, nutritional imbalance, metabolic dysfunction, heat, and psychosocial stress can modify reproductive epigenetic pathways. Human epidemiological studies have also identified exposure-associated epigenetic signatures in sperm, follicular cells, oocytes, endometrium, placenta, cord blood, and maternal circulation. However, the biological interpretation of these signatures remains challenging because epigenetic variation is tissue-specific, dynamic, sex-dependent, and sensitive to differences in exposure timing, dose, mixture composition, genetic background, cell-type distribution, and technical methodology. Moreover, many observed epigenetic changes may represent adaptive responses, exposure biomarkers, consequences of reproductive pathology, or causal intermediates, and these possibilities cannot always be distinguished in cross-sectional research. This narrative review examines the molecular pathways linking exposome-related influences to fertility and pregnancy outcomes, with particular emphasis on epigenetic remodeling in gametes, early embryos, the endometrium, and the placenta. https://doi.org/10.52783/jchr.v16.i4.15648
