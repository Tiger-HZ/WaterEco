Abstract This study investigates the effects of water condition on discharge behavior and nitric oxide (NO) production in a kHz cold argon plasma jet. Four different water configurations---floating demineralized water at room temperature, heated demineralized water, saline water, and grounded demineralized water---are compared. Laser-induced fluorescence (LIF) and optical emission spectroscopy (OES) are employed to measure ground-state NO density and excited-state NO emission intensity, respectively. Heated water promotes a more uniform discharge and the most energy-efficient local NO production, whereas saline and grounded water enhance local electron density and discharge intensity.
