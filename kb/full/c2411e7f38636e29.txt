Abstract Land‐use and land‐cover transitions can affect biodiversity and ecosystem functioning in a myriad of ways, including how energy is transferred within food‐webs. Size spectra (i.e. relationships between body size and biomass or abundance) provide a means to assess how food‐webs respond to environmental stressors by depicting how energy is transferred from small to larger organisms. Here, we investigated changes in the size spectrum of aquatic macroinvertebrates along a broad land‐use intensification gradient (from Atlantic Forest to mechanized agriculture) in 30 Brazilian streams.
