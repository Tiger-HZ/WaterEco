Limited access to safe drinking water increases exposure to waterbornepathogens and contributes to diarrheal disease, particularly among youngchildren. Solar water disinfection (SODIS) is a low-cost householdwater-treatment method, but its public-health value depends on bothacceptability and sustained use, as well as its potential effects on diarrhealdisease. This study aims to assess the acceptance and use of SODIS and toevaluate the effect of assignment to a household SODIS program on diarrhea amongchildren aged 6–59 months in the Malulu-Sanga community, northern Mozambique. This randomized community trial will include 84 households, allocated ina 1:1 ratio to either a SODIS intervention group or a control group maintainingusual household water-treatment practices.
