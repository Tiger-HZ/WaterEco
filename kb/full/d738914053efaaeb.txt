Abstract Drought is one of the most severe and complex natural hazards causing changes in water balance and impeding crucial water‐dependent ecosystem services. Over the last 25 years, multiple severe droughts linked to El Niño events have affected the Zambian and Zimbabwean regions. As a result, Lake Kariba, world's largest artificial reservoir by volume, saw historically low water levels and power shortages from reduced hydropower generation. This study employs a novel integrated approach to investigate the effects of varying water levels on the spatio‐temporal dynamics of phytoplankton blooms across the lake, combining remote sensing, in situ measurements, and a three‐dimensional hydrodynamic model (Delft3D).
