Bivalves harbor nitrogen-transforming microbes. Warming and excess nutrients may alter coastal nitrogen cycling by affecting activities of bivalves and these microbes. N 2 and nitrous oxide (N 2 O) gas production from the eastern oyster Crassostrea virginica were compared under 2 temperatures (18 and 24°C) and a range of nitrogen (N) loads (65 to 140 µM ammonium nitrate) for up to 2 mo. Data were analyzed with multi-factor ANOVAs, principal component and factor analyses, and partial least squares regression. Denitrification (DNF) rates were greater at the warmer temperature (p = 0.05) by 5 d. By 2 mo, DNF rates were significantly affected by N additions (p &lt; 0.01).
