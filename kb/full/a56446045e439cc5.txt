Decentral, source-separated urine treatment with partial nitritation/anammox (PN/A) provides a resource-efficient way to remove nitrogen from sewage. Utilizing a rotating biological contactor (RBC), this study investigated the long-term efficacy of treating real, human urine with PN/A over a wide range of urine concentrations, ranging from very dilute (0.6 g N L −1 ) to undiluted urine (5 g N L −1 ). At urine concentrations expected in source-separated toilets (2 g N L −1 ), the RBC reactor achieved a nitrogen removal efficiency of 94% ± 6% at a removal rate of 0.3 ± 0.1 g N L −1 d −1 . On undiluted urine (5 g N L −1 ), the reactor maintained this rate and even reached superior nitrogen removal efficiency (97% ± 3%). Keeping a total ammoniacal nitrogen residual of 25–100 mg N L −1 at low dissolved oxygen (0.5 g O 2 L −1 ) enabled the growth of excess anoxic ammonium oxidizing bacteria (AnAOB) capacity, yielding resilient and robust long-term operation. Batch activity tests showed no significant change in maximum AnAOB nitrogen removal rate with increasing urine strength. The PN/A RBC had an average N 2 O emission factor of 1.32% ± 0.30% g N 2 O‐N g N −1 loaded, resulting in a similar carbon footprint compared to conventional nitrification/denitrification (6 kg CO 2 -eq kg N −1 loaded). The PN/A RBC therefore provides an effective, simple and resource-lean solution for low-maintenance human urine treatment in a building-level decentralized setting, and its wide implementation will result in a significant reduction of nitrogen load reaching the existing sewage treatment plants, extending their treatment capacity.
