In 2017, a single hospital in Michigan changed how it prescribed opioids after surgery. The intervention was unglamorous: a medical student asked patients how much pain medication they used, guidelines were drafted from their answers, and surgeons were asked to follow them. There was no conceptual model, no analytical plan, no control group. Within months, prescription size decreased by more than 60%, with no increase in pain scores or refill requests ( 1 ).
