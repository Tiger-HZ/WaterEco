ABSTRACT Amphibians are experiencing substantial population declines due to environmental stressors such as habitat degradation, disease, pollution and climate change. Melanophryniscus montevidensis , a near‐threatened anuran, experiences regional population declines, although the influence of water quality on its reproductive success remains understudied. We evaluated the effects of in situ biotic and abiotic variables on the development of larvae across three ephemeral ponds in a protected area in Uruguay. We placed eggs from natural breeding events (25 clutches) in semi‐permeable enclosures and monitored them for 34 days.
