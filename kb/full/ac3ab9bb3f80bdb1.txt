Poor aqueous solubility limits roughly 40% of marketed oral drugs and up to ~90% of development candidates. One established remedy is the prodrug strategy: the covalent attachment of a bioreversible promoiety that regenerates the parent drug in vivo. The same conjugation can move a molecule in either direction along the hydrophilic–lipophilic axis: lipophilization raises membrane and oil solubility for permeation-limited or topical uses, whereas hydrophilization raises aqueous solubility. This review addresses hydrophilization and asks whether enzymatic (biocatalytic) synthesis can replace conventional chemistry in building water-soluble prodrugs.
