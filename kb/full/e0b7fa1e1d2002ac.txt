ABSTRACT Land use intensification threatens river biodiversity and its stability, but effects across multiple trophic levels in real river systems remain unclear. Here, we combined environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding with 213 spatially and temporally stratified sampling sites across a river network to analyze the impacts of land use intensification on biodiversity and community stability across trophic levels. Our eDNA survey captured 379 families across six major organismal groups, revealing pronounced taxon‐specific variation in community dynamics. Community structure exhibited significant spatiotemporal variation, whereas richness showed recurrent seasonal peaks and community stability followed distinct longitudinal patterns among organism groups.
