Abstract Coral white syndromes are among the most prevalent diseases affecting Indo-Pacific reefs, yet their etiology remains poorly understood, particularly in relation to eutrophication and microbial dynamics. Here, we investigated the drivers of Porites cylindrica white syndrome (PCYLWS) across two reef sites in Guam differing in nutrient enrichment and disease pressure. We combined 14 years of long-term disease monitoring with short-term lesion tracking, microbial community profiling of coral tissue and surrounding environments, and measurements of nutrient and environmental conditions. Long-term prevalence of PCYLWS was consistently higher at the oligotrophic site (Luminao) than at the eutrophic site (Tumon Bay), largely due to differences in host size structure and colony abundance.
