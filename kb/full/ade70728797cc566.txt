Abstract Algal exopolymeric substances are produced for buoyancy, defence and nutrient uptake. Increasingly, exopolymeric substances are being adopted in several biotechnological applications, ranging from medicine delivery to environmental remediation. However, extracting EPS from phytoplankton cells for laboratory-scale analysis is tedious due to low yields and subsequent product loss. By adding calcium carbonate and magnesium sulphate to the growth media, we demonstrate the viability of a carrier co-precipitation method for facilitating the extraction and transfer of EPS from the large centric diatom Coscinodiscus radiatus.
