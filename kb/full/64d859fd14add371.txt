Cellulose-based water-remediation materials are increasingly engineered beyond conventional biosorbents, yet their practical significance cannot be judged from adsorption capacity or removal efficiency alone. This Review frames engineered cellulose as an interface-to-process platform, examining how chemical functionality, architecture, transport, mechanical behavior, wettability, and recoverability govern contaminant fate and treatment performance. Evidence spanning adsorption, membrane separation, oil–water treatment, catalytic transformation, disinfection, contaminant recovery, and water reuse is evaluated across major contaminant classes.
