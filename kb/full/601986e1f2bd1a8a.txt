The increasing presence of contaminants in water poses serious environmental and public health risks. Traditional laboratory-based detection methods are often expensive, time-consuming, and inadequate for rapid monitoring. This research explores the development of low-cost, portable electrochemical sensors using laser-scribed graphene for real-time environmental monitoring. The work addresses key limitations of conventional techniques by focusing on scalable fabrication, enhanced sensitivity, and on-site applicability. Two different sensors were developed using laser-scribed graphene electrodes. The first sensor platform targets trace-level detection of pharmaceutical compounds in water. By engineering interdigitated electrode geometries with reduced spacing, the sensitivity of the sensor was significantly improved. The sensors demonstrated reliable detection of target analytes at μg/L concentrations in both laboratory solutions and real wastewater samples. A portable detection system was also developed to enable on-site monitoring, integrating the sensors with custom electronics. The second sensor platform focused on continuous monitoring of ammonium ions, a key parameter in wastewater treatment. An ion-selective membrane was applied to the laser-scribed graphene electrode, and a reference electrode was fabricated on the same substrate. The sensor was characterized through open-circuit potential measurements and demonstrated a wide linear detection range in both deionized water and real wastewater. Integration into a closed-loop monitoring system allowed automated data collection and remote tracking of water quality. Both platforms were evaluated for sensitivity, repeatability, reproducibility, and selectivity, and were successfully applied in complex wastewater samples. The combination of laser-scribed graphene and electrochemical detection enabled the development of robust, scalable, and on-site sensors for environmental applications. This work advances the field of electrochemical environmental monitoring by providing practical and cost-effective solutions for real-time contaminant detection. The findings highlight the potential of these platforms to support decentralized water quality monitoring and improve response times in environmental management.
