Abstract. Eutrophication – i.e., biomass overproduction due to nutrient enrichment – persists as a threat to riverine ecosystems despite achievements in lowering phosphorus (P) concentrations. This study explores seasonal patterns and drivers of chlorophyll a (Chl-a) linked with P availability across a selection of human-impacted rivers in Germany. We analyzed Chl-a and total phosphorus (TP) concentration measurements from 133 river sites and quantified their relationship using the degree of realized eutrophication (αrealized) – the ratio of measured to maximum Chl-a at a given TP. By applying k-means clustering on seasonal αrealized cycles, we identified five archetypal patterns.
