This study assessed the potential human health risks associated with consuming smoked seafood contaminated with polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) from the Niger Delta, Nigeria. Specifically, it examined the levels and distribution of PCB congeners in commonly consumed species, compared observed concentrations with regulatory limits, estimated dietary exposure, and evaluated related health risks. Forty smoked seafood samples—small crayfish (Macrobrachium spp.), large crayfish (Penaeus notialis), bonga fish (Ethmalosa fimbriata), and catfish (Clarias gariepinus) were obtained from Ogbe-Ijoh main market.
