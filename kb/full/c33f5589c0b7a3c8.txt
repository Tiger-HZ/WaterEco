Food systems account for roughly one-third of global greenhouse gas emissions, with China’s pig sector alone contributing an estimated 120–130 Mt. CO₂-eq annually. Feed production dominates this footprint, yet evidence comparing alternative ingredients under Chinese conditions is limited by inconsistent methodology and the frequent omission of waste valorization credits. This study presents a cradle-to-farm gate life cycle assessment of four feeding scenarios for Chinese growing-finishing pigs: conventional corn-soybean meal, black soldier fly larvae (BSFL) meal at 10 and 20% dietary inclusion, and 30% processed food waste. Tier 1 manure emission factors and ISO 14044 system expansion for avoided landfill emissions were applied. The conventional baseline produced 3.9 kg CO₂-eq per kg live weight, consistent with recent Chinese benchmarks. Food waste feeding reduced this by 28% to 2.8 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW, driven by avoided maize production and a landfill methane credit. BSFL meal increased emissions by 31–36% above baseline due to energy-intensive processing at pilot scale, a burden not offset by co-product allocation alone. Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis (1,000 iterations) yielded non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals across all four scenarios, confirming that rank-order conclusions are robust to the full parameter space tested. Food waste feeding is near-term deployable within existing infrastructure, contingent on harmonized biosecurity regulation; BSFL meal requires energy system decarbonization to become climate-competitive, as a 30% reduction in production energy narrows the gap with baseline by only 10–11% under China’s current grid.
