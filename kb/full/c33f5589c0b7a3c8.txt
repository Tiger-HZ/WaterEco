Divergent climate impacts of 
alternative feed ingredients in 
Chinese pig production
Bochao Hou * and Zejun He 
School of Economics and Management, Henan Agricultural University, Zhengzhou, China
Food systems account for roughly one-third of global greenhouse gas emissions, 
with China’s pig sector alone contributing an estimated 120–130 Mt. CO₂-eq 
annually. Feed production dominates this footprint, yet evidence comparing alter-
native ingredients under Chinese conditions is limited by inconsistent methodol-
ogy and the frequent omission of waste valorization credits. This study presents a 
cradle-to-farm gate life cycle assessment of four feeding scenarios for Chinese 
growing-finishing pigs: conventional corn-soybean meal, black soldier fly larvae 
(BSFL) meal at 10 and 20% dietary inclusion, and 30% processed food waste. Tier 
1 manure emission factors and ISO 14044 system expansion for avoided landfill 
emissions were applied. The conventional baseline produced 3.9 kg CO₂-eq per 
kg live weight, consistent with recent Chinese benchmarks. Food waste feeding 
reduced this by 28% to 2.8 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW, driven by avoided maize production 
and a landfill methane credit. BSFL meal increased emissions by 31–36% above 
baseline due to energy-intensive processing at pilot scale, a burden not offset 
by co-product allocation alone. Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis (1,000 itera -
tions) yielded non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals across all four scenarios, 
confirming that rank-order conclusions are robust to the full parameter space 
tested. Food waste feeding is near-term deployable within existing infrastructure, 
contingent on harmonized biosecurity regulation; BSFL meal requires energy 
system decarbonization to become climate-competitive, as a 30% reduction in 
production energy narrows the gap with baseline by only 10–11% under China’s 
current grid.

1 Introduction
Food systems are responsible for approximately one-third of global anthropogenic green-
house gas (GHG) emissions, with livestock production contributing a substantial share (Food 
and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, 2024; Crippa et al., 2021). Kim et al. 
(2024) report that pork accounted for approximately 33–37% of global meat consumption in 
2022, confirming its position as a major contributor to the global supply. Thus, as demand for 
animal-source protein continues to grow, mitigating the environmental footprint of pig pro-
duction has become central to sustainable food system transformation.
China plays a pivotal role in this transition. As the world’s largest pork producer and con-
sumer, China accounts for approximately half of global pig production (Food and Agriculture 
Organization of the United Nations, 2024; Bai et al., 2018). The sector consumes over 180 
million tonnes of feed annually, with corn and soybean meal constituting the predominant

ingredients in commercial pig diets (Steinfeld et al., 2006). However, 
China imports the majority of its soybean requirements, creating sig-
nificant trade vulnerabilities and agricultural dependencies; this 
import reliance, coupled with rising environmental concerns about 
intensive livestock production, has prompted policy initiatives to 
diversify protein sources (Bai et al., 2018). Life cycle assessments esti-
mate that China’s pork sector emits approximately 120–130 Mt. 
CO₂-eq annually, with average emission intensities around 3.5–4.0 kg 
CO₂-eq per kg live weight ( Fan et al., 2023 ; Chen et al., 2021 ). 
Provincial-level analyses further highlight significant spatial hetero-
geneity in production efficiency and carbon intensity ( Fan et al., 
2023). Across production systems, feed cultivation, processing, and 
transport consistently emerge as the dominant contributors to life-
cycle emissions (Poore and Nemecek, 2018; Y ang et al., 2023).
As a result, recognition of feed production’s environmental impor-
tance has catalyzed exploration of alternative protein sources. For 
instance, Black soldier fly larvae (BSFL; Hermetia illucens) production 
converts organic waste streams into high-quality protein while gener-
ating marketable co-products including insect oil and organic fertil-
izer frass ( van Huis et al., 2013 ). Similarly, processed food waste 
feeding practiced extensively in several Asian economies diverts edible 
residues from landfill disposal while maintaining nutritional adequacy 
for pig production (Zu Ermgassen et al., 2016). Both approaches align 
with waste reduction and resource efficiency objectives, yet their com-
parative environmental performance within Chinese production sys-
tems remains incompletely quantified. Generally, three categories have 
gained attention: insect-based feeds, food waste valorization, and 
alternative plant proteins. Insect-based feeds, particularly BSFL, have 
been studied for their potential to upcycle organic waste streams into 
protein (Smetana et al., 2019). BSFL meal contains 40–60% protein 
with amino acid profiles suitable for monogastric animals (van Huis 
et al., 2013). Life cycle assessment studies demonstrate that environ-
mental performance depends critically on production system design, 
particularly energy requirements for climate control and processing 
(Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 2016).
Insect-based feeds have attracted particular LCA scrutiny because 
of their energy-intensive production requirements. Published life 
cycle assessments of BSFL meal production report global warming 
potential values ranging from 1.4 to 5.8 kg CO₂-eq per kg protein, a 
fivefold spread that reflects differences in energy sources, rearing sub-
strate composition, and system boundary definitions across studies 
(Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 2016). Energy inputs for heating, 
ventilation, and mechanical processing consistently account for 
60–80% of total BSFL production emissions across published studies 
(Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 2016), making grid carbon inten-
sity the single most influential production parameter. This finding 
carries direct relevance for China: with a coal-dominated electricity 
grid averaging approximately 0.60 kg CO₂-eq per kWh, Chinese BSFL 
facilities face a structurally higher emissions burden than the 
European pilot-scale operations that dominate the published LCA 
literature. Extrapolating European-derived GWP estimates to Chinese 
industrial contexts without grid-specific adjustment therefore risks 
systematically underestimating the climate cost of insect-based feed 
ingredients. Regarding alternative plant proteins, including rapeseed 
meal, pea protein, and single-cell protein, these were excluded to focus 
on waste-valorisation pathways with the highest circular economy 
potential under Chinese conditions and for which comparative LCA 
evidence is most critically lacking. Under circular economy principles, 
livestock fed on food system residuals rather than human-edible crops 
can deliver protein with substantially reduced land and resource costs, 
though the climate performance of specific alternative ingredients 
remains highly context-dependent (Van Zanten et al., 2018). When 
properly treated to eliminate pathogens, processed food waste can 
replace conventional feed ingredients, with environmental benefits 
extending beyond avoided feed crop production to include credits for 
prevented landfill methane emissions (Zu Ermgassen et al., 2016).
In Asian contexts, food waste feeding has a long operational his-
tory. Japan, South Korea, and China’s Taiwan have maintained regu-
lated swill-feeding programs for decades. Salemdeeb et al. (2017) , 
drawing on South Korean heat-treated processing as the working 
model, found that food waste pig feed outperformed anaerobic diges-
tion and composting across 13 of 14 environmental and health impact 
categories, with environmental benefits driven primarily by substitu-
tion of conventional feed production ( Zu Ermgassen et al., 2016 ). 
China operated comparable systems at substantial scale prior to 2018, 
when the emergence of African Swine Fever prompted a nationwide 
prohibition on raw food waste feeding, subsequently replaced by man-
datory thermally treated processed food waste protocols under revised 
national biosecurity regulations. These thermal treatment require-
ments, stipulating a minimum of 30 min at 70 °C, introduce measur-
able additional processing energy burdens that pre-2018 Asian studies 
did not account for. As a result, earlier GWP estimates for food waste 
feeding scenarios likely understate current operational emissions 
under Chinese conditions, while simultaneously the landfill methane 
avoidance credit remains underexplored in the Chinese waste man-
agement context, where methane capture rates at municipal solid 
waste facilities vary considerably by region and facility type. Taken 
together, these factors mean that the net climate benefit of food waste 
feeding under current Chinese regulatory and infrastructural condi-
tions remains genuinely unresolved in the published literature. 
Moreover, European studies report land-use reductions of 21.5% 
when food waste replaces conventional pig feed (Zu Ermgassen et al., 
2016). Accurately quantifying the net climate performance of food 
waste feeding under current Chinese biosecurity conditions therefore 
requires a purpose-built LCA rather than extrapolation from pre-2018 
Asian or European literature. Methodologically, this study employs 
attributional modelling for upstream background processes and con-
sequential system expansion for foreground waste diversion, a distinc-
tion elaborated in Section 2.
1.1 Research gaps in current literature
Despite growing interest in alternative protein sources for live-
stock production, critical knowledge gaps constrain evidence-based 
decision-making for Chinese pig production systems. First, method-
ological heterogeneity severely limits comparability across LCA stud-
ies, only 38% of published pig production LCAs provide complete feed 
composition descriptions, and merely 9% report environmental 
impacts disaggregated by individual feed ingredients (Gislason et al., 
2023; Andretta et al., 2021). Without standardized inventory report-
ing, cross-study comparisons remain unreliable and mitigation con-
clusions difficult to generalize. Second, existing insect LCA studies 
focus predominantly on European or laboratory-scale production 
contexts (Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 2016), with limited rep-
resentation of industrial Chinese facilities where energy sources, cli-
matic conditions, waste feedstock composition, and production 
technologies may differ substantially, differences that can substantially 
shift GWP estimates depending on grid carbon intensity, rearing

substrate, and production scale (Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 
2016). Third, food waste feeding assessments frequently neglect 
system expansion credits for avoided waste disposal impacts (Gislason 
et al., 2023; Andretta et al., 2021), particularly prevented landfill meth-
ane emissions, and published studies predate China’s intensified bios-
ecurity requirements following African Swine Fever outbreaks (Fan et 
al., 2023), which mandate thermal treatment protocols that materially 
alter processing energy requirements and operational logistics. ISO 
14044 (cl. 4.3.4) establishes system expansion as the preferred 
approach to allocation in multi-output processes, yet many studies 
default to cut-off allocation, assigning zero burden to waste inputs and 
forfeiting the environmental credit for avoided disposal ( ISO 
14044:2006, 2006), a methodological choice that can substantially 
underestimate food waste feeding benefits by up to 30% (Salemdeeb 
et al., 2017). Fourth, China-specific LCA studies remain scarce; recent 
assessments report GHG intensities of 3.5–4.5 kg CO₂-eq per kg live 
weight (Y ang et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025), but comprehensive com-
parisons of alternative feed formulations within Chinese production 
contexts accounting for China’s coal-intensive electricity grid, domes-
tic transport infrastructure, and locally sourced feedstocks are largely 
absent from the literature. Fifth, comparative assessments typically 
evaluate ingredients individually rather than within integrated multi-
scenario frameworks, limiting their utility for ranking options or iden-
tifying trade-offs between environmental objectives. Sixth, uncertainty 
propagation and sensitivity analysis remain underexplored; most stud-
ies report point estimates without confidence intervals or systematic 
parameter sensitivity evaluation, making it impossible to determine 
whether differences between scenarios are statistically meaningful or 
within the noise of input uncertainty.
This study directly addresses each of these gaps. It adopts a stan-
dardized cradle-to-farm-gate LCA framework with full inventory 
transparency across four feed scenarios, enabling direct comparison. 
China-specific Ecoinvent datasets and IPCC (2019) Tier 1 manure 
emission factors replace European proxies wherever data permit. ISO 
14044-compliant system expansion is applied to food waste valoriza-
tion, explicitly quantifying avoided landfill methane credits. All four 
scenarios are evaluated within a single integrated framework, and 
Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation across key parameters com-
bined with one-at-a-time sensitivity analysis produces confidence 
intervals that allow statistically grounded conclusions about scenario 
rankings.
1.2 Research questions, significance and 
study overview
The following research questions guided this study:
 1 RQ1: How do greenhouse gas emissions from Chinese pig pro-
duction compare across conventional, insect-based, and food 
waste feeding scenarios when assessed with IPCC (2019) 
manure calculations and proper food waste system expansion?
 2 RQ2: What are the relative contributions of feed production, 
on-farm operations, and manure management to total global 
warming potential in each scenario?
 3 RQ3: What do the observed GWP differences between feeding 
scenarios imply for the feasibility and prioritization of feed-
based mitigation strategies in China’s pig sector, and how do 
findings position against published Chinese production 
benchmarks?
Beyond the research questions above, this study makes three prin-
cipal contributions to sustainable livestock production knowledge. 
Methodologically, it demonstrates the quantitative importance of 
proper LCA accounting through explicit comparison of embedded 
database values versus region-specific IPCC (2019) calculations for 
manure emissions, and rigorous application of system expansion for 
waste valorization credits per ISO 14044 guidelines. Empirically, it 
provides, to our knowledge, one of the first comprehensive compari-
sons of insect-based and food waste feeding alternatives within 
Chinese pig production contexts using corrected methodology and 
China-specific datasets, addressing a critical geographic gap in exist-
ing literature. Practically, results inform evidence-based feed sourcing 
strategies for China’s pig sector, representing 45% of global pork pro-
duction, with direct relevance to the nation’s carbon neutrality com-
mitments by 2060.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 
presents the LCA methodology including goal and scope definition, 
life cycle inventory development with emphasis on corrected manure 
emission calculations using IPCC (2019) Guidelines and food waste 
system expansion implementation, and impact assessment approach. 
Section 3 reports global warming potential results for all evaluated 
scenarios with contribution analysis identifying major emission 
hotspots and benchmarking against published Chinese pig production 
values. Section 4 discusses findings in relation to existing literature, 
addresses study limitations, and proposes directions for future 
research. Section 5 concludes with implications for sustainable feed 
development and policy recommendations for China’s pig sector.
2 Methodology
2.1 Goal and scope definition
The goal of this LCA study is to evaluate and compare the envi-
ronmental impacts of pig production in China using conventional 
feed versus alternative feed formulations incorporating black soldier 
fly larvae (BSFL) meal and processed food waste. Four feeding sce-
narios were assessed to identify potential environmental benefits and 
trade-offs associated with replacing conventional protein sources with 
these alternative ingredients.
The functional unit was defined as 1 kg of pig live weight (LW) at 
the farm gate. This unit allows direct comparison between scenarios 
and aligns with standard practice in livestock LCA studies (Y ang et al., 
2023; Mackenzie et al., 2016). The system boundary followed a cradle-
to-farm gate approach, encompassing all upstream processes includ-
ing feed ingredient production, feed processing and transport, pig 
housing and management, and manure-related emissions. Post-farm 
processes such as slaughtering, meat processing, retail, and consump-
tion were excluded from the scope.
2.2 Life cycle inventory development
LCA Software and Database: Life cycle inventory (LCI) modeling 
was conducted using openLCA 2.5.0 with background data from the 
Ecoinvent database version 3.10 ( Wernet et al., 2016 ). The cut-off 
system model was applied, wherein environmental burdens from 
waste treatment and recycling are assigned to the primary producer 
of waste materials; consequently, waste inputs entering background

Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems 04 frontiersin.org
processes carry zero environmental burden. This is complementary 
to, and consistent with, the system expansion applied in the fore -
ground model: where food waste displaces landfill disposal, avoided 
landfill methane emissions are credited as a negative emission to air. 
To avoid burden-shifting while maintaining consistency with interna-
tional comparative practice, a hybrid attributional-consequential 
approach was utilised. The cutoff model and system expansion address 
different life cycle stages and therefore do not overlap: cutoff elimi-
nates the upstream input burden of food waste, assigning the GHG 
cost of food production to the waste generator rather than the feed 
user; system expansion credits avoided downstream disposal emis-
sions, quantifying the landfill methane that does not occur because 
the waste is diverted from landfill. These are distinct environmental 
flows at distinct system boundaries. In the interest of full transparency, 
under a pure attributional cutoff approach without the landfill credit, 
the food waste scenario GWP would be approximately 4.06 kg 
CO₂-eq/kg LW , marginally above the conventional baseline of 3.9 kg 
CO₂-eq/kg LW . This confirms that the environmental advantage of 
food waste feeding is contingent on the avoided landfill emissions 
credit. The hybrid approach is retained in the primary analysis because 
ISO 14044 (cl. 4.3.4) identifies system expansion as the preferred 
method for quantifying avoided waste disposal impacts, and because 
the landfill methane credit represents a real, measurable physical flow 
under Chinese municipal waste conditions. China-specific (CN) data-
sets were used where available in Ecoinvent 3.10 to ensure geographic 
representativeness, including electricity generation and industrial heat 
production. Where China-specific datasets were unavailable, Rest of 
World (RoW) datasets were employed as proxies for crop production 
(maize grain, soybean meal) and freight transport, representing global 
average production systems.
 1 Feed scenario design: Four feed scenarios were developed rep-
resenting different protein sourcing strategies 
(Supplementary Table S1 ), formulated to meet nutrient 
requirements for growing-finishing pigs (National Research 
Council, 2012). This baseline scenario consisted of a conven-
tional corn-soybean formulation representing standard com-
mercial practice in China. The insect-based scenarios replaced 
portions of soybean meal with BSFL meal on a protein-equiv-
alent basis at 10 and 20% dietary inclusion levels, yielding a 
substitution ratio of 0.8 kg soybean meal displaced per 1.0 kg 
BSFL meal added at 20% inclusion; amino acid and energy 
requirements per National Research Council (2012) were 
maintained by adjusting inclusion proportions accordingly. 
The food waste scenario incorporated 30% processed food 
waste replacing maize grain (reduced from 68 to 45% of diet 
DM) and eliminating the mineral fraction (7%), with soybean 
meal maintained at 25% to preserve dietary protein and amino 
acid targets. The energy contribution of the reduced maize 
inclusion was compensated by the carbohydrate and fat frac-
tion of the processed food waste, consistent with its reported 
proximate composition in the literature. All scenarios were 
formulated to meet the nutritional requirements of growing-
finishing pigs (110 kg final weight) according to National 
Research Council (2012) guidelines. A constant feed conver-
sion ratio (FCR) of 2.5 kg feed per kg live weight gain was 
applied across all scenarios to isolate the effects of feed com-
position from potential differences in animal performance. 
This conservative assumption may underestimate the benefits 
of alternative feeds if they improve feed efficiency but ensures 
that observed differences in environmental impacts are attrib-
utable solely to feed ingredient substitution. It is important to 
note that while some studies report FCR variation with insect 
inclusion (Hong and Kim, 2022) a constant FCR was applied 
to isolate the effects of feed composition on life-cycle impacts. 
Sensitivity analysis (Section 3.2) examines FCR variation of 
±30% to test this assumption’s influence on results. The ±30% 
range was selected to bracket plausible diet-related uncer -
tainty, including potential reductions in digestibility or palat-
ability associated with chitin in BSFL meal at higher inclusion 
levels (Hong and Kim, 2022) and variation in the energy den-
sity and nutrient consistency of processed food waste. The use 
of a constant FCR is therefore a scenario-control assumption 
that improves comparability across diets, rather than an 
empirical claim that animal performance would remain iden-
tical under all feeding scenarios. If BSFL inclusion worsens 
digestibility or intake, the GWP of the BSFL scenarios would 
likely increase because more feed would be required per kg 
live weight. For the food waste scenario, reduced nutrient con-
sistency or mineral imbalance could reduce growth efficiency 
and narrow the estimated climate advantage. The ±30% FCR 
sensitivity analysis should therefore be interpreted as a bound-
ing test of the model’s sensitivity to feed-use efficiency, not as 
proof of unchanged animal performance across diets. Within 
the tested range, the scenario ranking remained stable; how-
ever, diet-specific feeding trials under Chinese production 
conditions are needed to validate FCR values for BSFL meal 
and processed food waste. It should also be noted that the food 
waste diet omits external mineral supplementation 
(Supplementary Table S1 ), with calcium and phosphorus 
requirements assumed to be partly or fully met by the inherent 
mineral content of processed food waste. Processed food waste 
from urban food-service streams may contain calcium and 
phosphorus from dairy residues, eggshell fragments, fish and 
meat bones, and cereal-based materials, which have been dis-
cussed as nutritionally relevant components in swill-feeding 
literature (Zu Ermgassen et al., 2016; Salemdeeb et al., 2017). 
However, independent mineral profiling of the specific waste 
stream modelled here was not conducted. This assumption 
should therefore be interpreted as plausible but unverified, and 
its implications for nutritional equivalence and FCR validity 
are acknowledged as a study limitation in Section 4.3.
 2 Black soldier fly larvae (BSFL) meal production: BSFL meal 
production was modeled as a multi-output process based on 
pilot-scale data from Smetana et al. (2019) and Halloran et al. 
(2016). Larvae were reared on biowaste substrates (mixed 
kitchen and garden waste) under controlled conditions. The 
production system included rearing, handling, drying (to 
reduce moisture content), and defatting operations to produce 
a protein concentrate suitable for animal feed. The LCI for 
BSFL meal production per 1 kg of defatted meal is summa-
rized in Supplementary Table S2. The process generates three 
co-products: defatted meal (40–60% protein), insect oil (high 
in medium-chain fatty acids), and frass (nutrient-rich residue 
suitable as organic fertilizer). Environmental burdens were 
allocated among co-products using mass allocation as the pri-
mary approach (23% to BSFL meal, 7% to insect oil, and 70% 
to frass by mass yield). Biowaste substrate was treated as a

residual material with zero upstream production burden 
under the cut-off approach, meaning that emissions from pro-
ducing the original food or biomass were not assigned to BSFL 
production. However, foreground operations required to 
make the substrate usable for larvae, including collection, 
transport to the rearing facility, sorting, size reduction, and 
mechanical or thermal pretreatment, can contribute addi -
tional emissions. These flows were not fully represented in the 
pilot-scale inventory sourced from Smetana et al. (2019) and 
Halloran et al. (2016) , and therefore represent a potential 
source of underestimation for the BSFL scenarios under 
Chinese commercial conditions. A sensitivity analysis using 
economic allocation as an alternative showed that the choice 
of allocation method changed the GWP for the BSFL 20% sce-
nario by approximately ±12%, indicating that the conclusions 
are robust to this methodological choice.
 3 Food waste processing: Food waste processing was modeled to 
produce sterilized food waste feed suitable for direct pig con-
sumption, following safety regulations for swill feeding where 
permitted (Zu Ermgassen et al., 2016). Raw food waste (70% 
moisture content) undergoes collection, shredding, thermal 
treatment (70 °C for 30 min to eliminate pathogens), and 
moisture reduction to produce feed-grade material. The LCI 
for food waste processing is presented in 
Supplementary Table S3. The electricity input of 3.0 MJ/kg dry 
matter represents mechanical operations during food-waste 
conversion, including shredding or grinding, pumping, 
mixing, dewatering, and process control. The heat input of 
4.0 MJ/kg dry matter represents thermal sanitisation and 
moisture reduction required to produce feed-grade processed 
food waste. These values were adopted from published food-
waste-to-feed LCI data and applied as average processing 
inventory values rather than plant-specific engineering mea-
surements. Processing configuration may materially affect this 
inventory: continuous systems with heat recovery, steady-state 
operation, and integrated dewatering would likely reduce heat 
demand per kg dry matter, whereas batch systems with 
repeated heating and cooling cycles or limited heat recovery 
may increase thermal losses. Because Chinese plant-level data 
were unavailable, process configuration remains a source of 
uncertainty and should be evaluated in future sensitivity anal-
ysis. A critical component of this analysis is the application of 
system expansion to account for avoided landfill emissions. In 
the absence of use as animal feed, food waste would be dis-
posed of in landfills where anaerobic decomposition generates 
methane, a potent greenhouse gas. Following IPCC (2019) 
methodology for solid waste (Chapter 3), avoided methane 
emissions were calculated based on:
 a) Degradable organic carbon (DOC) content: 0.15 kg C/kg dry 
matter (IPCC Table 3.3)
 b) Methane correction factor (MCF): 1.0 for anaerobic landfill 
conditions
 c) DOC dissimilation fraction (DOCf): 1.0 (upper-bound value 
for food waste, reflecting near-complete anaerobic biodegrad-
ability; the IPCC (2019) default of 0.5 applies to mixed MSW , 
food waste decomposes almost entirely under anaerobic 
conditions).
 d) Methane fraction in landfill gas (F): 0.5 (IPCC default)
 e) Landfill gas capture efficiency: 40% (estimated for urban land-
fills in China)
 f) The calculation yields 0.06 kg CH₄ avoided per kg dry matter 
food waste (0.15 × 1.0 × 1.0 × 0.5 × 16/12 × 0.6 = 0.060 kg 
CH₄), equivalent to 1.68 kg CO₂-eq (using GWP₁₀₀ of 28 for 
fossil methane per IPCC AR5, consistent with ReCiPe 2016 
characterization factors).
Landfill was selected as the system expansion counterfactual 
because it represents the predominant disposal route for municipal 
food waste in China, where organised anaerobic digestion and com -
posting infrastructure for separately collected food waste streams 
remains limited outside designated pilot cities. Salemdeeb et al. 
(2017) explicitly compared food waste pig feeding against anaerobic 
digestion and composting across 14 environmental and health 
impact categories, finding that feeding outperformed both alterna -
tives in 13 of 14 categories. This provides contextual support for the 
landfill counterfactual and suggests that selecting AD or compost -
ing as the counterfactual would reduce rather than increase the 
estimated benefit of food waste feeding. The exclusion of AD and 
composting as alternative counterfactuals is acknowledged as a 
scope limitation in Section 4.3.
 4 Feed mixing and transport: Feed ingredients were transported 
to a centralized feed mill where mixing operations occurred. 
Transport distances were based on typical supply chain con-
figurations in China: 300 km for bulk ingredients (maize, soy-
bean meal) from production regions to feed mills, and 80 km 
for finished feed delivery to farms. Food waste collection 
involved 40 km average transport distance from urban collec-
tion points to processing facilities. All transport was modeled 
using Rest of World (RoW) freight lorry datasets (7.5–16 
metric ton capacity) from Ecoinvent 3.10 due to unavailability 
of China-specific transport data. Feed mixing energy was esti-
mated at 0.2 MJ electricity per kg finished feed, representing 
mechanical mixing, conditioning, and pelletization processes, 
consistent with processing energy values reported for com-
mercial livestock feed manufacture in comparable LCA stud-
ies. This is based on standard commercial pelletization energy 
requirements (0.15–0.25 MJ/kg feed) reported across pub -
lished livestock LCA studies (Smetana et al., 2019; Mackenzie 
et al., 2016).
 5 Pig Production System: Pig production was modeled for grow-
ing-finishing pigs from weaning (~25 kg) to market weight 
(110 kg) over a 140-day period, representing typical commer-

conditions assumed intensive indoor facilities with liquid 
slurry manure management systems, which account for 
approximately 75% of commercial pig production in China 
(Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, 
2024). Operational inputs per kg live weight gain included:
 a) Feed: 2.5 kg (scenario-specific composition)
 b) Electricity: 0.6 MJ (ventilation, lighting, automated systems)
 c) Heat (natural gas): 0.3 MJ (climate control during cold 
periods)
 d) Water: 5.0 kg (drinking water and facility cleaning)

2.4 Data quality and uncertainty
Data quality varied across system components. Foreground data 
(feed formulations, pig production parameters) were based on stan-
dard Chinese commercial practices documented in peer-reviewed 
literature (Fan et al., 2023; Smetana et al., 2019). Background data 
from Ecoinvent 3.10 represent global average or regional (China, 
Brazil) production systems with documented pedigree matrices indi-
cating good-to-fair quality for major agricultural commodities.
BSFL meal production data were sourced from pilot-scale opera-
tions in Europe (Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 2016); no pub-
lished LCI data from Chinese commercial-scale BSFL facilities 
currently exists, and the use of European pilot-scale inventories rep-
resents the best available proxy. To bound this uncertainty beyond the 
±30% range, a supplementary scenario was examined in which BSFL 
production energy was reduced by 50%, representing an optimistic 
commercial-scale efficiency gain. Under this scenario, BSFL 20% 
GWP decreased to approximately 4.6 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW , remaining 
18% above baseline. Climate parity with the conventional baseline 
would require approximately 55% reduction in production energy, a 
threshold achievable only through substantial renewable energy inte-
gration rather than efficiency gains alone. This confirms that the direc-
tional finding, BSFL meal increases GWP under current Chinese grid

conditions, is robust to a wide range of production energy

Food waste processing parameters were adapted from UK-based 
swill feeding trials (Zu Ermgassen et al., 2016) with adjustments for

Chinese urban contexts. The assumed landfill gas capture efficiency 
(40%) for system expansion credits represents a moderate estimate

Manure emission factors from IPCC (2019) carry typical uncer -
tainties of ±30% for CH₄ and ±50% for N₂O as noted in the IPCC 
guidelines. These uncertainties were propagated through Monte 
Carlo analysis (1,000 iterations) to estimate confidence intervals for 
final results. Uniform probability distributions were applied to all 
uncertain parameters, reflecting the absence of empirical distribu -
tion data from Chinese pig production systems rather than an 
assumption of genuine uniformity. While parameters such as CH₄ 
emission factors and process energy typically follow right-skewed 
or triangular distributions in practice, uniform distributions pro -
duce the widest possible confidence intervals for a given parameter 
range and therefore represent the most conservative assumption for 
testing scenario separability. Narrower, more realistic distribution 
shapes would yield tighter confidence intervals, making the 
observed non-overlapping 95% CIs across all four scenarios more 
pronounced rather than less. The choice of uniform distributions 
therefore strengthens rather than undermines the robustness of the 
rank-order conclusions. Future analyses with access to empirical 
Chinese production data should adopt distribution shapes derived 
from measured variability.
3 Results
3.1 Life cycle impact assessment results
All emission factors reported below are inventory quantities 
derived from IPCC (2019) Tier 1 methodology; their conversion 
to CO₂-equivalents uses ReCiPe 2016 Midpoint (H) characterisa -
tion factors (CH₄ = 28, N₂O = 265 kg CO₂-eq) as described in 
Section 2.3. The global warming potential (GWP) for producing 
1 kg pig live weight varied from 2.8 to 5.3 kg CO₂-eq across the 
four feed scenarios ( Figure 1 ). The baseline scenario using con -
ventional corn-soybean feed yielded a GWP of 3.9 kg CO₂-eq/kg 
LW , closely aligning with recent Chinese pig production LCA 
studies reporting 3.8 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW and 3.5–4.5 kg CO₂-eq/kg 
LW (Y ang et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025 ). The insect-based sce -
narios demonstrated higher GWP compared to baseline, with 
5.1 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW for 10% BSFL inclusion and 5.3 kg CO₂-eq/
kg LW for 20% inclusion. These represent increases of 31 and 36%,

BSFL inclusion also displaced additional soybean meal on a pro -

Detailed contribution analysis revealed distinct life-cycle stage 
dominance patterns across scenarios ( Figure 1D); all process-level 
flows expressed in units consistent with Supplementary Table S3; kg 
CO₂-eq per kg LW). Feed production contributed 2.4 kg CO₂-eq 
(62% of total) for the baseline scenario, rising substantially to 3.6 kg 
CO₂-eq (71%) and 3.8 kg CO₂-eq (72%) for BSFL 10 and 20% sce -
narios, respectively. This increase reflects the higher energy inten -
sity of BSFL meal production (15 MJ electricity plus 10 MJ heat per 
kg defatted meal) compared to conventional soybean meal process -
ing (approximately 8 MJ total per kg; Ecoinvent 3.10 database). The 
BSFL substitution followed nutritional equivalence principles 
accounting for crude protein content and amino acid digestibility 
coefficients (van Huis et al., 2013). At 20% BSFL inclusion, the feed 
formulation displaced 80% of baseline soybean meal on a protein-
equivalent basis, resulting in a substitution ratio of 0.8 kg soybean 
meal replaced per 1.0 kg BSFL meal added. For the food waste sce -
nario, total feed production contributed 3.06 kg CO₂-eq, of which 
food waste processing (collection, thermal treatment, grinding) 
accounted for approximately 1.3 kg CO₂-eq, with the remaining 
1.76 kg CO₂-eq attributable to the maize and soybean meal still 
present in the diet (45 and 25% respectively). The avoided emission 
credit from landfill diversion ( −1.26 kg CO₂-eq) assumed baseline 
landfill methane capture efficiency of 40%, consistent with Chinese 
municipal solid waste management practices. Sensitivity analysis on 
this parameter revealed that even under worst-case assumptions 
(20% capture efficiency), the avoided emission credit remained sub -
stantial at −0.50 kg CO₂-eq, maintaining the food waste scenario’s 
environmental advantage over conventional feed albeit with a nar -
rower margin. The food waste environmental advantage is therefore 
robust in direction across the full sensitivity range tested, but its 
absolute magnitude is highly dependent on regional landfill gas 
capture infrastructure, which varies considerably across Chinese 
municipalities.
Manure management emissions were calculated as 0.50 kg CO₂-eq 
per kg LW (constant across all scenarios), comprising methane 
(0.39 kg CO₂-eq from 0.014 kg CH₄) and nitrous oxide (0.11 kg 
CO₂-eq from 0.00040 kg N₂O). These emissions remained constant 
across scenarios as feed composition was not expected to substantially 
alter manure characteristics within the study’s scope. The elevated 
GWP in insect-based scenarios originated primarily from BSFL meal 
production. At 20% inclusion (0.5 kg BSFL meal consumed per pig at 
2.5 FCR), the energy-intensive drying and defatting processes contrib-
uted approximately 1.4 kg CO₂-eq, explaining much of the increase 
over baseline. This burden could be substantially reduced through 
process optimization, renewable energy integration, or utilization of 
waste heat from other industrial processes. The food waste scenario 
achieved GWP reduction through two mechanisms:
 1 Partial replacement of energy-intensive maize production with 
low-burden food waste feedstock.
 2 Avoided landfill methane emissions credited through system 
expansion.
The food waste credit of −1.26 kg CO₂-eq derives from 0.75 kg 
dry matter food waste consumed per kg LW (30% DM inclusion × 2.5 
FCR). At 70% moisture content, this corresponds to approximately 
2.5 kg wet weight food waste per kg pig live weight.
3.2 Comparison with published benchmarks
The study’s baseline GWP of 3.9 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW aligns closely 
with recent Chinese pig LCA studies: Y ang et al. (2023) reviewed 
Chinese pig production carbon footprints reporting values consistent 
with our baseline, while Chen et al. (2025) found a range of 3.5–4.5 kg 
CO₂-eq depending on regional production systems. This correspon-
dence falls within the range reported by recent Chinese pig LCA stud-
ies and confirms the representativeness of our baseline scenario for 
commercial Chinese pork production. The food waste scenario’s 28% 
GWP reduction is consistent with Zu Ermgassen et al. (2016), who 
reported 21.5% land use reduction and substantial climate benefits 
from swill feeding in European contexts (Zu Ermgassen et al., 2016). 
The study’s finding of increased GWP for insect-based feeds aligns 
with recent meta-analyses noting that pilot-scale insect production 
can be energy-intensive (Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 2016), 
though commercial-scale operations with renewable energy may 
approach or exceed the environmental performance of conven -
tional feeds.
3.3 Sensitivity and uncertainty analysis
Sensitivity analysis examined the influence of key parameters on 
GWP results:
 1 Feed conversion ratio (FCR): Varying FCR ± 30% (1.75–
3.25 kg feed/kg LW) produced asymmetric GWP changes of 
−21 to +25% relative to the central estimate. This asymmetry 
arises because manure management emissions (0.50 kg 
CO₂-eq/kg LW) were held constant across the FCR range, 
reflecting their dependence on animal physiology rather than 
feed quantity, while only feed-related emissions scaled propor-
tionally with FCR. The asymmetry therefore confirms that 
feed production dominates the GWP profile but does not 
account for it entirely, and that efficiency gains deliver some-
what smaller proportional benefits than efficiency losses 
impose penalties.
 2 Manure CH₄ emission factor: Adjusting the IPCC emission 
factor ±30% (2.8–5.2 kg CH₄/head/year) altered GWP by −19 
to +22%, underscoring the importance of manure manage-
ment strategies and accurate emission monitoring.
 3 BSFL production energy: Reducing electricity and heat inputs 
by 30% (representing potential commercial-scale efficiency 
gains or renewable energy integration) decreased insect sce-
nario GWP by 10–11%, while a 30% increase raised GWP 
by 11–13%.

4 Discussion
The following discussion interprets these findings within the con-
straints of current Chinese regulatory frameworks and economic fea-
sibility, recognising that environmental performance alone does not 
determine adoption trajectories.
4.1 Regional context and comparability
The study’s baseline GWP of 3.9 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW aligns with 
published Chinese estimates (Y ang et al., 2023) but remains higher 
than reported European averages (Nguyen et al., 2012; Mackenzie et 
al., 2016). This difference primarily reflects China’s coal-dominated 
electricity grid composition, longer average feed transport distances, 
and production system characteristics. The BSFL production energy 
requirements assumed in this study derive from available literature 
(Smetana et al., 2019; Halloran et al., 2016); actual Chinese industrial 
facilities may demonstrate different energy intensities depending on 
technology adoption, scale, and regional energy infrastructure. 
Similarly, food waste collection efficiency and processing infrastruc-
ture maturity varies substantially across Chinese provinces, affecting 
the practical applicability and environmental performance of food 
waste feeding scenarios.
The central finding of this study is that feed ingredient choice 
drives sharply divergent climate outcomes in Chinese pig production, 
and that the direction of that divergence is not predictable from ingre-
dient properties alone. Food waste feeding reduced GWP by 28% 
below baseline through two compounding mechanisms avoided 
upstream maize production and a landfill methane avoidance credit 
of −1.26 kg CO₂-eq per functional unit while BSFL meal increased 
GWP by 31–36% above baseline due to the energy intensity of pilot-
scale drying and defatting, a penalty that persisted even after eco -
nomic co-product allocation following ISO 14044 guidelines ( ISO 
14044:2006, 2006). The fact that manure emissions were constant 
across all scenarios at 0.50 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW is consistent with these 
divergent outcomes being attributable primarily to upstream feed pro-
duction, a finding aligned with the broader livestock LCA literature 
identifying feed as the dominant emission hotspot ( Poore and 
Nemecek, 2018; Y ang et al., 2023). This has a direct methodological 
implication: studies that aggregate feed and on-farm contributions 
without ingredient-level disaggregation will systematically obscure the 
leverage points available to producers (Andretta et al., 2021).
These results are broadly consistent with existing literature while 
resolving ambiguities it leaves open. The baseline GWP of 3.9 kg 
CO₂-eq/kg LW falls within the range of 3.5–4.5 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW 
reported by Y ang et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2025) for commercial 
Chinese production systems, providing confidence in inventory rep-
resentativeness. The food waste benefit aligns directionally with Zu 
Ermgassen et al. (2016) , who documented substantial climate and 
land-use gains from swill feeding in European contexts, and extends 
that finding by quantifying the landfill methane credit explicitly under 
IPCC (2019) methodology ( IPCC, 2019), a component frequently 
omitted in prior assessments. The elevated GWP for BSFL meal is 
consistent with Halloran et al. (2016) and Smetana et al. (2019), whose 
pilot-scale inventories underpin the present analysis, and corroborates 
the broader finding that insect production energy intensity is the criti-
cal variable.
However, this study should not be read as a general verdict against 
insect-based feeds. Van Zanten et al. (2018) demonstrate that under 
circular economy conditions, genuine food waste substrates and 
renewable electricity; BSFL systems can outperform conventional soy 
on climate metrics. Our results reflect pilot-scale production under 
China’s current grid intensity of approximately 0.58 kg CO₂-eq/kWh 
as characterised in Ecoinvent 3.10 (Wernet et al., 2016); a 30% reduc-
tion in BSFL production energy narrows the gap with baseline by only 
10–11%, indicating that grid decarbonisation matters more than pro-
duction efficiency gains alone. The conditions under which BSFL 
becomes climate-competitive are therefore specific and achievable, but 
not yet present at scale in China.
Two limitations warrant quantification rather than acknowledge-
ment alone. First, the BSFL inventory was derived from European 
pilot-scale operations (Smetana et al., 2019 ; Halloran et al., 2016 ) 
rather than Chinese commercial facilities; no such dataset currently 
exists. Sensitivity analysis shows that even a 30% reduction in produc-
tion energy leaves the BSFL 20% scenario 21% above baseline under 
current grid conditions, suggesting that full climate competitiveness 
requires either substantial grid decarbonisation or simultaneous gains 
in both energy efficiency and renewable sourcing. Second, the food 
waste system expansion credit is acutely sensitive to landfill gas cap-
ture efficiency, calculated here following IPCC (2019) solid waste 
methodology. Testing 0–60% capture rates produced a GWP range of 
2.3–3.3 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW for the food waste scenario, a spread larger 
than the gap between the baseline and food waste central estimates, 
reflecting genuine variability in Chinese municipal waste infrastruc-
ture rather than modelling imprecision. Despite both uncertainties, 
Monte Carlo analysis yielded non-overlapping 95% confidence inter-
vals across all four scenarios, confirming that the rank order of results 
is robust to the full parameter space tested.
The practical implications differ substantially between ingredient 
types. Food waste feeding represents a potentially near-term deploy-
able mitigation strategy requiring no new technology and delivering 
reductions robust across the full uncertainty range tested, with direct 
relevance to China’s carbon neutrality commitments by 2060, pro-
vided that harmonized biosecurity regulation is in place (Fan et al., 
2023; Chen et al., 2021). Its scaling, however, is contingent on regula-
tory conditions: following African Swine Fever outbreaks from 2018

the ingredient but specific to pilot-scale processing under grid elec-

incentives and waste-heat co-location mandates for insect feed facili-

here as the dominant emission source directly.
Beyond the substantive findings, this study demonstrates that 
methodological choices in livestock LCA are not neutral. Applying 
region-specific (IPCC, 2019) manure emission factors rather than 
Ecoinvent embedded values, and implementing ISO 14044-compliant 
(ISO 14044:2006, 2006) system expansion rather than cut-off alloca-
tion for food waste, materially altered GWP outcomes in ways that 
would have changed the ranking of scenarios under less rigorous 
accounting, consistent with concerns raised by Andretta et al. (2021) 
regarding methodological heterogeneity in pig production LCA. These 
choices should be adopted as minimum reporting standards in live-
stock feed substitution studies. Three research priorities follow directly 
from the limitations identified above: development of China-specific 
BSFL production inventories to replace the European pilot-scale proxy 
data used here; empirical measurement of landfill gas capture effi -
ciency at Chinese urban waste facilities to resolve the dominant uncer-
tainty in food waste scenario outcomes; and controlled feeding trials 
reporting scenario-specific feed conversion ratios for BSFL and food 
waste diets, to capture the asymmetric risk that FCR deterioration at 
high insect inclusion imposes a larger GWP penalty than an equiva-
lent efficiency improvement delivers as a benefit.
4.2 Policy implications and implementation 
considerations
The environmental performance differences identified here must 
be evaluated alongside regulatory frameworks and economic feasibil-
ity when considering practical implementation. Current Chinese 
regulations permit insect protein use in aquaculture feeds but main-
tain restrictions on pig feed applications pending additional safety 
evaluations and production standardization. This regulatory barrier 
persists despite BSFL ’s demonstrated nutritional adequacy and estab-
lished use in other jurisdictions, suggesting that policy advocacy and 
stakeholder engagement remain necessary prerequisites for commer-
cial adoption regardless of environmental performance. For food 
waste feeding, China’s post-African Swine Fever biosecurity require-
ments mandate thermal treatment (≥70 °C for ≥30 min) and regular 
pathogen monitoring, adding operational costs estimated at ¥150–200 
per tonne processed feed. These compliance costs may offset economic 
advantages relative to conventional ingredients, potentially limiting 
adoption to larger-scale operations with sufficient capital for process-
ing infrastructure investment. However, policy instruments could 
improve viability: waste processing subsidies under circular economy 
initiatives, feed ingredient diversification support programs, or inte-
gration with existing municipal organic waste management systems 
could reduce costs while advancing multiple policy objectives simul-
taneously (Jurgilevich et al., 2016). The constant manure emission 
contribution across all scenarios (0.50 kg CO₂-eq/kg LW , representing 
13% of total GWP) underscores that dietary interventions alone 
cannot fully address livestock greenhouse gas emissions. 
Complementary manure management strategies warrant investiga-
tion, including anaerobic digestion for biogas recovery, aerobic com-
posting systems, or integration with crop production systems for 
nutrient recycling. Such integrated approaches could address methane 
and nitrous oxide emissions while generating renewable energy co-
products or displacing synthetic fertilizer production, potentially 
achieving additional environmental benefits beyond feed ingredient 
substitution alone.
4.3 Study limitations
This study has several limitations that warrant acknowledgment. 
First, BSFL production inventory data derive exclusively from 
European pilot-scale studies; no Chinese commercial-scale LCI data-
set currently exists. Substrate composition, climatic control require-
ments, and operational scale in Chinese facilities may differ from the 
European baseline, and the magnitude of the GWP increase for BSFL 
scenarios should be interpreted with this provenance uncertainty in 
mind, even though the directional finding is robust across the full 
sensitivity range tested. Additionally, the BSFL production inventory 
does not fully represent China-specific substrate collection, sorting, 
transport, and pretreatment. Including these additional foreground 
burdens would likely increase the GWP of BSFL meal unless offset by 
avoided waste-management credits or low-carbon processing energy; 
therefore, the BSFL results reported here may be conservative under-
estimates. Second, we applied uniform probability distributions for 
uncertainty analysis; empirical distributions derived from Chinese 
production data would improve precision and representativeness. 
Third, our system boundary excluded capital equipment and infra-
structure; while typically contributing less than 5% of total environ-
mental impacts in agricultural LCA studies, their inclusion would 
provide completeness. Fourth, we focused exclusively on global warm-
ing potential; comprehensive sustainability assessment requires evalu-
ation of trade-offs across multiple impact categories including 
eutrophication potential, land use, water consumption, and biodiver-
sity impacts (Poore and Nemecek, 2018). Fifth, the assumed mineral 
adequacy of the processed food waste in the absence of external sup-
plementation remains an assumption requiring empirical validation, 
and any associated FCR fluctuations were outside the scope of this 
study. The constant FCR assumption should be interpreted as a sce-
nario-control device rather than an empirical claim of identical animal 
performance across diets; diet-specific feeding trials are needed to 
confirm whether BSFL meal and processed food waste maintain com-
parable intake, digestibility, and growth efficiency under Chinese pro-
duction conditions. Sixth, the system expansion credited only avoided 
landfill disposal as the counterfactual for food waste diversion; anaer-
obic digestion with biogas recovery and composting were outside the 
study scope. In contexts where AD infrastructure is more developed, 
the marginal counterfactual may differ from landfill, which could 
reduce the magnitude of the food waste feeding credit relative to the 
values reported here.

Several research priorities emerge from this analysis that warrant 
future investigation. First, primary data collection from operational

5 Conclusion
Feed production dominates the greenhouse gas footprint of pig 
production, yet rigorous China-specific evidence comparing alterna-
tive ingredients has remained scarce. This study addressed that gap 
through a comparative LCA of four feeding scenarios, applying IPCC 
(2019) manure emission factors and ISO 14044-compliant system 
expansion to ensure methodological choices did not obscure differ-
ences between ingredient types. The results reveal a consequential 
divergence. Food waste feeding reduced GWP by 28% to 2.8 kg 
CO₂-eq/kg LW , driven by avoided maize production and a landfill 
methane avoidance credit of −1.26 kg CO₂-eq per functional unit. 
BSFL meal increased GWP by 31–36% above the 3.9 kg CO₂-eq/kg 
LW baseline due to the energy intensity of pilot-scale processing, a 
penalty robust across Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis. Constant 
manure emissions across all scenarios are consistent with the diver-
gence being driven by upstream feed production, suggesting that 
ingredient substitution is a meaningful lever for mitigation. For 
China’s path to carbon neutrality by 2060, implications differ by ingre-
dient. Food waste feeding is deployable within existing infrastructure 
but depends on harmonized biosecurity regulation following African 
Swine Fever restrictions.
The BSFL penalty is not inherent to the ingredient but specific 
to pilot-scale processing under grid electricity; renewable energy 
incentives and waste-heat co-location mandates would target its 
dominant source directly. Three limitations define the research 
agenda: reliance on European BSFL inventories requires replace -
ment with China-specific commercial-scale data; food waste credit 
uncertainty requires empirical measurement of landfill gas capture 
efficiency at Chinese facilities; and the constant FCR assumption 
requires controlled feeding trials to capture the full performance 
consequences of ingredient substitution. Circular economy feed 
approaches can deliver measurable GWP reductions under the 
conditions modelled here. Whether they scale depends on decar -
bonizing insect processing, harmonizing food waste regulation, 
and developing China-specific production inventories. Each is 
tractable.
