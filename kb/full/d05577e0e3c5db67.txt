Abstract Water security on Small Island Developing States (SIDS) is threatened by worsening and disproportionate climate change impacts including sea-level rise and extreme weather. These can be investigated through longitudinal analysis of water metabolism, defined as the patterns of water flowing into, within, and out of a socio-ecological system to meet societal needs. Patterns of water throughput may build resilience, or embody systemic risks that cascade across spatial, temporal and sectoral dimensions. We refer to such risks as “socio-metabolic risks” (SMR), a subset of systemic risks associated with critical resource availability, circulation, and the distribution of resultant costs and benefits.
