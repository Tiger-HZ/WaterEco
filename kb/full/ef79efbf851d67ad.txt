Energy is used for many applications in modern society including heating, cooling, and lighting. There are substantial links between SDG6 for clean water and sanitation and SDG7 for affordable and clean energy, ventilation, transportation, electronic devices, and communications. Renewable and nonrenewable sources of energy are employed. Renewable energy sources include the use of solar, hydro, wind, and biomass, while fossil fuels (crude oil, natural gas, and coal) are considered nonrenewable due to their extensive production periods. Although water requirements for energy production are substantial, they are often not well documented. In the transition to renewable energies to reduce carbon emissions to mitigate carbon, attention must be paid to available water resources. These resources will also be impacted by climate change as the result of droughts or water shortages. Water assessments in the development of renewable energies must be included. Water for energy has not been studied as intensively as energy for water. Implementation of water resource management is needed through policy development. Comparisons can be difficult, however, as data is limited, and the boundaries for the comparison are not always the same. Water quality and thermal pollution are not usually considered. A more unified approach is needed for determining water consumption in relation to electricity and energy generation.
