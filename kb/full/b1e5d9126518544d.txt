Access to safe drinking water remains a major public health concern in rural Nigeria, where household characteristics may influence the choice of drinking water sources. This study aimed to examine the determinants of improved versus unimproved drinking water sources among rural households using secondary data from the 2024 Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS). A total of 62,143 rural households were included in the analysis. Data were analyzed using STATA 17, employing univariate, bivariate, and multivariate approaches. The prevalence of improved drinking water use was 56.96 percent, while 43.04 percent of households relied on unimproved sources.
