Diarrhea remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality for children under five, despite reductions in its global burden in recent decades. However, climate change, and its associated meteorological conditions - heavy rainfall, temperature, flooding - has the potential to impede or reverse progress, particularly in low-income, climate-vulnerable communities. Better characterization of the impact of weather and season on drinking water quality and child health will allow us to capture the anticipated health burden associated with climate change. We characterized the associations of weather and season on infant diarrhea and household stored water quality in Beira, Mozambique.
