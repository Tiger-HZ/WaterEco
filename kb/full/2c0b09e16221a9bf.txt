Manilkara bidentata is an endangered tropical tree species whose long-term conservation depends on efficient and scalable monitoring approaches. Nevertheless, accurately detecting individual trees in dense tropical forests remains difficult due to crown overlap, heterogeneous canopy structure, and the high visual similarity among neighboring trees. In this study, we assessed the performance of four state-of-the-art YOLO object detection architectures (YOLOv8, YOLOv10, YOLOv11, and YOLOv12) for identifying individual M. bidentata crowns from high-resolution UAV-derived RGB orthomosaics collected in the Chinchipe River basin, northern Peru. The orthomosaics were partitioned into 640 × 640-pixel image tiles, manually annotated, and divided into training (80%), validation (10%), and testing (10%) datasets. Eight model configurations (nano and small variants) were trained and evaluated using precision, recall, F1-score, mAP@0.5, and mAP@0.5:0.95. Among the evaluated models, YOLOv12n delivered the best overall performance, achieving a precision of 0.86, recall of 0.74, F1-score of 0.80, mAP@0.5 of 0.90, and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.56, thereby surpassing earlier YOLO versions in both detection accuracy and localization performance. Although complex canopy conditions, including crown overlap, shading, and morphological variability, reduced localization accuracy under stricter evaluation criteria, YOLOv12n remained the most reliable architecture across different confidence thresholds. Overall, the integration of UAV-based RGB imagery with advanced deep learning techniques provides an accurate, rapid, and cost-effective solution for monitoring threatened tropical tree species, supporting forest inventories, biodiversity conservation, and long-term ecosystem management.
