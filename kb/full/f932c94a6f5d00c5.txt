Abstract The redistributive transport of sedimentary, redox‐sensitive metals such as iron (Fe) and manganese (Mn) along depth gradients in lakes or marine basins, and in particular the concentration of these elements at local depressions, has been described as “geochemical focusing.” However, the impact of variable redox conditions at the sediment–water interface on this process is not entirely understood. In this study, we report that the enrichment and burial of Fe, Mn, and associated phosphorus (P) in dimictic Lake Arendsee, Germany, has been affected by a transition from oligo‐mesotrophic to eutrophic conditions in the first half of the 20th century.
