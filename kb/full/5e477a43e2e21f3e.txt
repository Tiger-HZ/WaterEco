Drip fertigation and mulching regulate post-harvest soil 
nutrient dynamics in turmeric (Curcuma longa L.) cv. 
Pragati 
 
Sowmya M, Yuvaraj KM, Lalitha Kadiri and Sreedhar D 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9d.6505 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment was conducted during the 2024 - season at Dr. YSRHU - College of Horticulture, 
Anantharajupeta, Andhra Pradesh, India, to evaluate the effect of fertigation levels and mulching practices 
on post-harvest soil available nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P₂O₅) and potassium (K₂O) in turmeric (Curcuma 
longa L.) cv. Pragati. The experiment consisted of 15 treatment combinations arranged in a split-plot design 
with three replications. Fertigation treatments included flood irrigation with 100% RDF, drip irrigation with 
100% RDF through soil application, drip fertigation with 100% RDF, drip fertigation with 75% RDF and 
drip fertigation with 50% RDF, while mulching treatments comprised no mulch, silver-black polyethylene 
mulch and organic mulch. Post-harvest soil available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O were significantly influenced by 
fertigation and mulching. The highest residual nutrient contents were recorded under F₅ (drip fertigation + 
50% RDF) with 234.73 kg ha⁻¹ N, 40.65 kg ha⁻¹ P ₂O₅ and 220.53 kg ha⁻¹ K ₂O, whereas the lowest values 
were observed under F₃ (drip fertigation + 100% RDF). Among mulches, no mulch recorded the highest 
residual nutrient status, while organic mulch recorded the lowest values. The study indicated that drip 
fertigation with 100% RDF enhanced nutrient uptake and utilization by turmeric, resulting in lower residual 
soil nutrient levels after harvest. 
 
Keywords: Fertigation, mulching, post-harvest soil fertility, nutrient use efficiency, residual soil nutrients 
 
Introduction 
Turmeric ( Curcuma longa L.), a perennial rhizomatous spice crop belonging to the family 
Zingiberaceae, is believed to have originated in Southeast Asia. It is a sterile triploid species (2n 
= 3x = 63) propagated through underground rhizomes and ispopularly known as the “golden spice” 
owing to its culinary, medicinal, cosmetic and industrial importance. Turmeric ( Curcuma longa 
L.) is an important spice crop of India and many other Asian countries. The rhizomes are widely 
used in traditional Indian systems of medicine (Ayurveda, Unani and Siddha), primarily due to 
the presence of curcumin, which possesses antioxidant, anti -inflammatory, antimicrobial, 
antiviral, hypoglycaemic and anticancer properties (Julie, 2009). Turmeric grows well in Andhra 
Pradesh, Maharashtra, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala and some parts of North East India 
including Assam. Turmeric is widely cultivated mainly for its rhizomes. It is used both as spice 
and as a raw material for dye making and cosmetic industries. Turmeric rhizome contains around 
3% curcumin, 2.2% α-phellandrene, 1.5% terpinolene and other essential oils. These essential oils/ 
alkaloids have very good medicinal properties (Khanna, 1999) [4]. Curcumin, the primary pigment 
of turmeric, is generally used in vari ous food industries as a food colourant. Turmeric is a long -
duration and nutrient-exhaustive crop that requires a continuous supply of water and nutrients for 
sustained productivity (Syed et al., 2010). Fertigation improves nutrient availability and 
minimizes nutrient losses, while mulching conserves soil moisture and enhances nutrient retention 
in the root zone (Jagadeeswaran et al., 2004) [2]. These practices influence not only crop growth 
and yield but also the residual soil fertility status. Therefore, asse ssment of post -harvest soil 
available nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P₂O₅) and potassium (K₂O) is important for understanding 
nutrient uptake, nutrient use efficiency and the sustainability of turmeric cultivation. Hence, the 
present study was undertaken to evaluate the effect of different fertigation levels and mulching 
practices on post-harvest soil available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O in turmeric.

Material and methods 
A field experiment was conducted during 2024– 25 at Dr. 
YSRHU–College of Horticulture, Anantharajupeta, Andhra 
Pradesh, India (13.98° N, 79.40° E). The experiment consisted of 
15 treatment combinations arranged in a split -plot design with 
three replications to study the effect of fertigation levels and 
mulching practices on post-harvest soil available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O 
in turmeric ( Curcuma longa L.) cv. Pragati. The main -plot 
treatments were F₁ – flood irrigation + 100% RDF through soil 
application, F₂ – drip irrigation + 100% RDF through soil 
application, F₃ – drip fertigation + 100% RDF, F₄ – drip 
fertigation + 75% RDF and F₅ – drip fertigation + 50% RDF. The 
sub-plot treatments were M₁ – no mulch, M₂ – silver-black 
polyethylene mulch and M₃ – organic mulch. Soil samples were 
collected before planting and after harvest (0 –30 cm depth) and 
analyzed for available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O. The data were subjected 
to analysis of variance appropriate to a split-plot design. 
 
Results 
Available nitrogen (kg ha⁻¹) 
Post-harvest soil available nitrogen was significantly influenced 
by fertigation levels and mulching practices. Among the 
fertigation treatments, F₅ (drip fertigation + 50% RDF ) recorded 
the highest available nitrogen content (234.73 kg ha⁻¹), followed 
by F₁ (flood irrigation + 100% RDF through soil application) with 
220.80 kg ha⁻¹. The lowest available nitrogen content was 
observed under F₃ (drip fertigation + 100% RDF) with 188.17 kg 
ha⁻¹. Among the mulching treatments, M₁ (no mulch) recorded the 
highest mean available nitrogen (213.72 kg ha⁻¹), whereas M₃ 
(organic mulch) recorded the lowest value (207.54 kg ha⁻¹). The 
interaction between fertigation and mulching was non-significant. 
Numerically, the highest available nitrogen content (235.60 kg 
ha⁻¹) was recorded under F₅M₃, while the lowest value (183.10 kg 
ha⁻¹) was observed under F₃M₃. 
 
Available phosphorus (P₂O₅) (kg ha⁻¹) 
Post-harvest soil available phosphorus differed sign ificantly 
among fertigation and mulching treatments. The highest available 
phosphorus content was recorded under F₅ (40.65 kg ha⁻¹), 
followed by F₁ (37.25 kg ha⁻¹), whereas the lowest value was 
observed under F₃ (27.43 kg ha⁻¹). Among the mulches, M₁ 
recorded the highest mean phosphorus content (35.12 kg ha⁻¹), 
followed by M₂ (34.50 kg ha⁻¹) and M₃ (33.22 kg ha⁻¹). The 
interaction effect was non-significant. The maximum phosphorus 
content (41.50 kg ha⁻¹) was observed under F₅M₁, while the 
minimum value (26.33 kg ha⁻¹) was recorded under F₃M₃. 
 
Available potassium (K₂O) (kg ha⁻¹) 
Significant variation in post-harvest soil available potassium was 
observed due to fertigation levels and mulching practices. The 
highest available potassium content was recorded under F₅ 
(220.53 kg ha⁻¹), followed by F₁ (199.05 kg ha⁻¹), while the lowest 
value was recorded under F₃ (164.65 kg ha⁻¹). Among the 
mulching treatments, M₁ recorded the highest mean potassium 
content (195.70 kg ha⁻¹), whereas M₃ recorded the lowest value 
(186.43 kg ha⁻¹). The interaction effect was non -significant. 
Numerically, the highest potassium content (223.14 kg ha⁻¹) was 
obtained under F₅M₁, while the lowest value (155.35 kg ha⁻¹) was 
recorded under F₃M₃.

Discussion 
Post-harvest soil available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O were significantly 
influenced by fertigation levels and mulching practices. The 
lower residual nutrient status observed under F₃ (drip fertigation 
+ 100% RDF) indicates greater nutrient uptake by turmeric under 
optimum fertigation. Similar findings were reported by 
Krishnamoorthy et al. (2012) [5], who observed reduced post -
harvest soil available N, P and K under 100% fertigation with 
water-soluble fertilizers due to enhanced nutrient u ptake and 
higher rhizome yield. Among the mulching treatments, M₃ 
(organic mulch) recorded lower residual N, P₂O₅ and K₂O than M₁ 
and M₂, suggesting greater nutrient utilization by the crop under 
improved soil moisture and rhizosphere conditions. Enhanced 
nutrient uptake and crop performance under organic mulch in

turmeric were also reported by Manhas et al. (2011) [6]. Similarly, 
Anshuman et al . (2021) [1] observed significant changes in soil 
available N, P and K under mulched turmeric plots. The similar 
trend observed for N, P₂O₅ and K₂O across fertigation treatments 
suggests a uniform response of soil nutrients to moisture and 
nutrient management. Comparable parallel responses of soil N, P 
and K under fertigation were reported by Krishnamoort hy et al. 
(2012) [5]. 
 
Conclusion 
Fertigation with 100% RDF (F₃) enhanced nutrient uptake and 
utilization in turmeric cv. Pragati, resulting in lower post- harvest 
soil available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O. Organic mulch further improved 
nutrient utilization, indicating be tter nutrient use efficiency and 
sustainability of turmeric cultivation under fertigation and 
mulching practices.
