The global rise in harmful algal blooms (HABs) is linked to nutrient enrichment, climate change, and resource competition, though their relative roles remain unclear. We hypothesized that nutrient limitation induced by surface density layers (SDL) gives <i>Ceratium hirundinella</i> (<i>C. hirundinella</i>) a competitive advantage over cyanobacteria in a tributary of the Three Gorges Reservoir. Over 26 days in spring 2023, daily samples from three transects recorded two bloom events. Each began with a cyanobacterial bloom dominated by <i>Dolichospermum circinale</i> and <i>Chroococcus minor</i>, followed by a larger bloom dominated by <i>C. hirundinella</i>. The SDL formation reduced vertical nutrient transport to surface waters, leading to surface nutrient-limited conditions that constrained cyanobacterial growth. In contrast, motile species <i>C. hirundinella</i> exploited deeper waters through diel vertical migration below the SDL into nutrient-rich layers, where it reached a peak biomass of 28.6 mg L<sup>−1</sup>, while cyanobacteria peaked at 0.86 mg L<sup>−1</sup>. At the bloom termination, hypoxia developed in the middle layers, where high <i>C. hirundinella</i> biomass was observed across all transects. These findings suggest that SDL-driven nutrient partitioning favored vertically motile <i>C. hirundinella</i> over cyanobacteria, promoting bloom succession and mid-water hypoxia, and underscoring the importance of stratification in reservoir-tributary phytoplankton dynamics.
