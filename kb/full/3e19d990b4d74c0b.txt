The Bolivian Amazon faces a severe environmental crisis driven by unregulated artisanal gold mining, making Bolivia the second-largest mercury emitter in Latin America with an estimated 133.1 tons released annually. The resulting contamination poses critical health risks to indigenous communities, while conventional ground-based water sampling remains financially and logistically impractical across the vast Amazonian terrain. This paper presents a scalable computer vision and deep learning framework leveraging multispectral satellite imagery from Sentinel and Landsat platforms to detect, classify, and map visual indicators of mercury pollution in Bolivian river systems without physical field access.
