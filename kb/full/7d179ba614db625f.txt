During recent times, patients have been increasingly using large language models (LLM) when seeking medical advice, as a source of first line information before or after clinical review. The onus is put on the capability of these LLMs to provide patients with accurate and comprehensible information, such as effectively describing possible surgical options for the individuals conditions to recognising red flag symptoms where present. Validated instruments such as DISCERN and Flesch-Kincaid readability metrics offer a reproducible means of appraising such content. However, the quality and comprehensibility of LLM generated ptosis information has not yet been systematically evaluated.
