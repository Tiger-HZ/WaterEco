Urbanisation and unreliable drinking water supply have increased reliance on packaged waters in Cameroon. However, their hydrogeological origin is poorly verified. This study investigated the spatio-temporal variation, origin, recharge and vulnerability of 35 brands (114 samples) in Cameroon (2012 to 2025) using stable isotopes and major anions. The δ18O range (-5.70 ‰ to -3.34 ‰) reflected wide spatial variation, controlled by altitude, local climate and distance from the Atlantic moisture source. The δ2H/δ18O relationship revealed meteoric-recharged groundwater sourcing through springs and boreholes. Brands sourced from Mount Cameroon had the highest elevation-recharge isotope characteristics. Those in Douala showed the lowest elevation-recharge isotope signatures and higher vulnerability to contamination (elevated NO3−). Measured and labelled concentrations differed, revealing poor labelling practices. Most brands showed limited temporal isotopic variation, exhibiting stable groundwater sourcing and aquifer buffering capacity. Stable isotopes and hydrochemistry provide a tool for commercial water verification and drinking-water management in Cameroon.
