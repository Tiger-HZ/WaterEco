Soil organic carbon is crucial for maintaining soil fertility, water retention, and resilience to climate change, particularly in semi-arid regions. This study explores the effects of no-till and conventional tillage systems on soil organic carbon dynamics in durum wheat (Triticum durum Desf.) crops in northwestern Algeria. Field experiments were conducted over two growing seasons (2022–2024) to assess temporal and spatial changes in organic carbon content and its relationship with other soil parameters, and to provide a preliminary ten-year projection of organic carbon levels. Results showed numerical trends suggesting greater carbon stability and accumulation under no-till, while conventional tillage exhibited higher temporal variability and a tendency to decline.
