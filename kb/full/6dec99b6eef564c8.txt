Sustainable food systems are critical for addressing hunger, improving public health, and ensuring environmental resilience. This study examines how food availability, food choice, and consumer behavior influence sustainable food systems in Yobe State, Nigeria. Using a purposive sampling technique, 540 respondents were selected from six local government areas across three geopolitical zones. Data were collected using a structured, closed-ended questionnaire and analyzed with descriptive and inferential statistics. Findings reveal that food availability moderately influences food sustainability, while food choice and consumer behavior strongly and positively affect sustainable food systems in the state.
