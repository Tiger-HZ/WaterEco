Abstract During urban flooding, pollutants from point releases (e.g., sewer overflows) transported in street runoff can invade city blocks, exposing residents to contamination risks. The present laboratory study focuses on the intrusion of pollutants from flooded streets into a porous city block under steady flow conditions. The modeled city block is rectangular, with a variable number of openings in its sidewalls. Five pollutant release durations are considered. This study aims at assessing the impact of block porosity and release duration on pollutant dynamics within the block by measuring the spatial distribution of concentration using a colorimetry technique, and assuming a uniform distribution over the depth.
