Abstract The conservation management of the facultative catadromous and critically endangered European eel Anguilla anguilla is inhibited by substantial knowledge gaps on their behaviours and migrations. Integral to understanding the silver eel migration back to the Sargasso Sea for spawning is generating knowledge on their initial marine movements. Here, the downstream migration of 25 silver eels from the River Test, southern England, and their initial movements in the sea were measured using acoustic telemetry. Of 24 eels detected migrating downstream, 19 were successfully tracked into the tidal area (taking 1–34 days), with in-river barriers not delaying their emigration.
