Systemic corruption represents a persistent governance challenge for public water provision in Nigeria, where institutional weaknesses can undermine service reliability, infrastructure development and equitable access. This study examines stakeholders’ perceptions of the prevalence, manifestations, drivers and consequences of corruption within Nigeria’s water provision sector, while identifying measures for strengthening accountability. The study adopted the qualitative research method using a semi-structured and unstructured telephone interview with 18 respondents representing a range of stakeholders, including public utility officials, civil society and regulatory stakeholders, private water providers and water contractors, and community representatives.
