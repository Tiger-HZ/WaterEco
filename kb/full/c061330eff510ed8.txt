Monitoring the dynamics of estuarine nutrients, specifically dissolved inorganic nitrogen (DIN) and silicate (DSi), is essential for assessing the ecological health of nearshore waters; however, traditional sampling methods struggle to provide the high-frequency temporal data and broad spatial coverage required for comprehensive assessment. Here, we developed a robust "two-step inversion" remote sensing framework (R2 &gt; 0.75) that bridges the gap between remote sensing reflectance and salinity, subsequently linking salinity to DIN and DSi concentrations. By applying this model to the Jiulong River Estuary, we achieved a high-frequency, large-scale, and accurate reconstruction of surface DIN and DSi concentrations over the past decade.
