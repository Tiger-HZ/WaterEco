Influence of Organo-Mineral Briquette-Based 
Nutrient Management on NPK Uptake and Grain 
Nutritional Quality of Lowland Rice 
 
Vaibhav Vasanta Virutkar , RM Ghodpage, Ommala D Kuchanwar , SS 
Potdar, SA Patil , SP Wagh , Dhanshri H Bulbule , AD Bhosale and HR 
Patil 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/26174693.2026.v10.i9Sd.9849 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment was conducted during the kharif season of 2025 –26 at the Research Farm, Anand 
Niketan College of Agriculture, Anandwan, Warora, District Chandrapur, Maharashtra, to assess the 
influence of organo-mineral briquette-based nutrient management on nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and 
potassium (K) uptake and selected nutritional quality attributes of lowland rice. The experiment was 
conducted in a randomized block design with eight treatments and three rep lications using rice variety 
PDKV Tilak. Treatments comprised conventional recommended dose of fertilizer (RDF), urea -DAP 
briquette with recommended potash, 100, 80 and 60% RDF through organo -mineral briquette, 
supplemented fertilizer treatments with sulphur, zinc and boron, and an absolute control. The total N, P 
and K uptake was highest with 100% RDF through organo -mineral briquette (T3), recording 84.71, 
16.78 and 97.96 kg ha⁻¹, respectively. Relative to conventional RDF (T1), T3 increased total N, P and 
K uptake by 29.56, 19.35 and 22.94%, respectively. Grain protein content ranged from 7.21 to 7.88%, 
with the highest value under T3. Starch content ranged from 77.04 to 78.24% and was highest under 
80% RDF through organo -mineral briquette (T4). Lysine ran ged from 0.26 to 0.37%, with the 
maximum under T7, while fat (0.63 –0.83%), fibre (0.42 –0.48%), moisture (11.33 –12.33%) and ash 
(1.43–1.53%) were not significantly influenced by nutrient management. The results indicate that 100% 
RDF supplied through the IN M-based organo -mineral briquette was particularly effective for 
improving NPK uptake and grain protein content, whereas the response of other grain quality attributes 
was parameter-specific. 
 
Keywords: Deep placement, grain quality, INM, lysine, NPK uptake, organo-mineral briquette, rice 
 
1. Introduction 
Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is an important cereal crop and requires substantial nutrient inputs to 
sustain productivity. Fertilizer management is an important component of rice production, 
while incomplete recovery of applied nutrients can occur through processes such as 
volatilization, leaching, runoff and fixation. Consequently, balanced fertilization, improved 
placement and synchronization of nutrient supply with crop demand are important for 
improving nutrient recovery in rice (Bijay -Singh and Vinod, 2017; Dobermann a nd 
Cassman, 2002; Nayak et al ., 2018) [4, 7, 15] . Integrated nutrient management provides an 
approach for combining organic and inorganic nutrient sources. In lowland rice, deep 
placement of fertilizer briquettes places nutrients below the soil surface and has been 
reported in the thesis literature as a strategy for reducing nitrogen loss and improving 
nutrient-use efficiency (FAO, 2000; Mikkelsen et al ., 1978; Rahman et al ., 2016) [14, 18 ]. 
Deep placement of urea and NPK briquettes has also been associated with greater nitrogen 
uptake than broadcast application of prilled urea (Sharna et al ., 2021) [21]. Nutrient 
management can also influence grain nutritional characteristics. Previous work cited in the 
thesis indicates that integrated nutrient management may improve grain protein content, 
while responses of proximate composition and lysine can vary with genotype and nutrient 
management (Sharma et al ., 2015; Bharali and Baruah, 2022; Karikat ti, 20 18; Verma and 
Srivastav, 2017; Umon et al ., 2018) [14, 3, 13, 23 ]. The present study therefore focused 
specifically on the response of N, P and K uptake an

2. Materials and Methods 
2.1 Experimental site and soil 
The experiment was conducted during kharif 2025–26 at the 
Research Farm, Anand Niketan College of Agriculture, 
Anandwan, Warora, District Chandrapur, Maharashtra. The 
experimental soil was a fairly uniform clay -loam Inceptisol. 
The initial soil was slightly alkaline, with low available N 
and P, high available K and medium organic carbon. The 
initial soil pH was 7.62, electrical conductivity 0.21 dS m⁻¹, 
organic carbon 5.1 g kg⁻¹, available N 187.6 kg ha⁻¹, 
available P 14.10 kg ha⁻¹ and available K 291.5 kg ha⁻¹. 
 
2.2 Experimental design and treatments 
The experiment was laid out in a randomized block design 
with eight treatments, each replicated three times, giving 24 
plots. Rice variety PDKV Tilak was transplanted at 20 × 15 
cm spacing. The recommended fertilizer dose was 
100:50:50 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹. 
 
Treatment Treatment description 
T1 RDF 100:50:50 NPK kg ha⁻¹ 
T2 Recommended urea-DAP briquette + recommended

T3 100% RDF through organo-mineral briquette 
T4 80% RDF through organo-mineral briquette 
T5 60% RDF through organo-mineral briquette 
T6 T1 + S 5 + Zn 5 + B 0.3 kg ha⁻¹ 
T7 T2 + S 5 + Zn 5 + B 0.3 kg ha⁻¹ 
T8 Absolute control 
 
In T₁, 50% N along with the full P, K and supplemental 
nutrients was applied at puddling and the remaining 50% N 
after 30 days of transplanting. In T₂ –T₇, treatment -wise 
quantities of organo -mineral or urea -DAP briquettes were 
placed at 5–7 cm depth within four hills after transplanting. 
The organo -mineral briquette was prepared using 
vermicompost, gliricidia leaves, DAP, urea, MOP, 
elemental sulphur, zinc sulphate and borax; the briquette 
size was 2.5–2.7 g. 
 
2.3 Plant sampling and NPK uptake 
Grain and straw samples were collected after harvest and 
chemically analysed. Total N in plant material was 
estimated by Kjeldahl’s method (Piper, 1966) [17]. For P and 
K determination, plant samples were digested using a nitric 
acid–perchloric acid di -acid mixture in a 9:4 ratio; total P 
was determined by the vanadomolybdate phosphoric yellow 
colour method and total K by flame photometry (Jackson, 
1973) [12]. Nutrient uptake was calculated from nutrient 
concentration and corresponding yield data. 
 
2.4 Grain quality analysis 
Protein content was determined by Kjeldahl’s method 
(AOAC, 2000), starch by the colorimetric method of Dubois 
et al. (1956) [8], lysine by the spectrophotometric method of 
Tsai et al . (1972) [22], and fat by the Soxhlet method 
(AOAC, 1998). Fibre was determined using 1.25% H₂SO₄ 
and 1.25% NaOH as extraction reagents according to 
AOAC (2000). Moisture was determined by the oven -dry 
method of Oxley et al . (1960) [16], and ash by the ignition 
method described by Jackson (1973) [12]. 
 
2.5 Statistical analysis 
Experimental data were analysed using Fisher’s analysis of 
variance for randomized block design following Gomez and 
Gomez (1984) [11], Significance was tested at p = 0.05, and 
critical difference (CD) values were calculated where the F -
test was significant. 
 
3. Results and Discussion 
3.1 Nitrogen uptake 
Total N uptake was significantly affected by the fertilizer 
treatments. It ranged from 40.43 kg ha⁻¹ in the absolute 
control (T₈) to 84.71 kg ha⁻¹ with 100% RDF through 
organo-mineral briquette (T₃). The corresponding straw and 
grain N uptake under T₃ were 31.39 and 53.33 kg ha⁻¹, 
respectively. T₄ recorded 80.21 kg ha⁻¹, while T₇ recorded 
76.94 kg ha⁻¹. Conventional RDF (T₁) resulted in 65.38 kg 
ha⁻¹. The total N uptake under T₃ was 29.56% higher than 
conventional RDF. The higher uptake under the briquette 
treatment is consistent with the experimental rationale that 
deep placement supplies nutrients within the reduced soil 
zone and can improve nutrient availability while reducing 
losses. The result also agrees with the thesis -cited findings 
of Sharna et al . (2021) [21], who reported higher N uptake 
with deep placement of urea and NPK briquettes than with 
broadcast prilled urea. The response is also consistent with 
Sharma et al . (2015) [19], who reported that nutrient 
management practices affecting N nutrition were reflected 
in rice protein content. 
 
3.2 Phosphorus uptake 
Total P uptake varied from 8.15 kg ha⁻¹ in T₈ to 16.78 kg 
ha⁻¹ in T₃. Under T₃, P uptake by straw and grain was 6.83 
and 9.95 kg ha⁻¹, respectively. T₄ and T₇ recorded 15.86 and 
15.89 kg ha⁻¹, respectively, whereas T₁ recorded 14.06 kg 
ha⁻¹. The total P uptake under T₃ was 19.35% higher than 
conventional RDF. 
The higher P uptake with the organo -mineral briquette 
treatment indicates that the combination of nutrient sources 
and placement used in the experiment supported greater 
crop acquisition of P. The thesis attributes the response to 
continued nutrient availability within the reduced soil zone 
and improved soil conditions around the placed briquette. 
Darade and Bankar (2009) [6] similarly reported higher N, P 
and K uptake with deeply placed urea -DAP briquettes in 
hybrid rice. 
 
3.3 Potassium uptake 
Total K uptake was highest under T₃ (97.96 kg h a⁻¹), 
followed by T₄ (91.73 kg ha⁻¹) and T₇ (88.70 kg ha⁻¹), while 
the lowest value was recorded in T₈ (55.09 kg ha⁻¹). 
Conventional RDF (T₁) resulted in 79.68 kg ha⁻¹. Straw and 
grain K uptake under T₃ were 77.78 and 20.18 kg ha⁻¹, 
respectively. The total K uptake under T₃ was 22.94% 
higher than T₁. The greater K uptake under T₃ is consistent 
with the overall NPK uptake response of the crop to organo -
mineral briquette placement. The thesis associates higher 
uptake under briquette treatments with balanced n utrient 
supply, steady nutrient availability and placement in the 
reduced soil zone. Similar improvement in NPK uptake 
under briquette -based nutrient management has been 
reported by Darade and Bankar (2009) [6].

3.4 Grain protein 
Grain protein content ranged from 7.21% in T₈ to 7.88% in 
T₃. Treatments T₂, T₃, T₄, T₆ and T₇ were significantly 
higher than the absolute control, while T₁ and T₅ were 
statistically at par with the control. T₃, T₄ and T₇ were 
mutually at par. The highest protein content under T₃ 
corresponded with its highest total N uptake, supporting the 
treatment relationship described in the thesis between N 
nutrition and grain protein accumulation. Sharma et al . 
(2015) [19] similarly reported a trend in rice protein content 
corresponding to N uptake, while Chauhan (2023) reported 
improved grain protein under integrated application of 
organic and inorganic nutrient sources. 
 
3.5 Grain starch 
Starch content varied from 77.04 t o 78.24%. The highest 
value (78.24%) was recorded under T₄, while the lowest 
(77.04%) occurred under T₃. The treatment effect was 
statistically significant. Thus, the treatment producing the 
highest NPK uptake and protein content did not produce the 
highest starch content. The observed range was narrow 
compared with the treatment differences in NPK uptake, 
indicating that the response of starch was distinct from the 
response of nutrient uptake. 
 
3.6 Grain lysine 
Lysine content ranged from 0.26% in T₈ to 0.3 7% in T₇. All 
fertilized treatments recorded numerically higher lysine than 
the absolute control. T₇ and T₆, both receiving supplemental 
S, Zn and B, recorded significantly higher lysine than T₁, T₂, 
T₃, T₄ and T₅ and were statistically at par with each ot her. 
Karikatti (2018) [13], as cited in the thesis, reported only a 
small influence of nutrient management on rice grain lysine 
concentration, indicating that lysine may show a 
comparatively limited response to fertilizer regime. 
 
3.7 Grain fat and fibre 
Grain fat content ranged from 0.63% in T₇ to 0.83% in T₅, 
and the treatment effect was non -significant. Fibre content 
ranged from 0.42 to 0.48%, with the highest numerical value 
under T₃; however, the differences among treatments were 
non-significant. There fore, neither fat nor fibre showed a 
statistically reliable response to the nutrient management 
treatments in the present experiment. The thesis literature 
similarly reports that grain proximate composition can vary 
among rice genotypes and that responses to nutrient 
management are parameter -dependent (Verma and 
Srivastav, 2017; Ekanayake et al., 2026) [23]. 
 
3.8 Grain moisture and ash 
Moisture content ranged from 11.33% under T₄ to 12.33% 
under T₁, T₃, T₆ and T₈. The treatment effect was non -
significant. Ash content ranged from 1.43 to 1.53% and was 
also not significantly influenced by the treatments. The 
thesis attributes the lack of a consistent treatment response 
in moisture partly to sampling and environmental 
variability. The observed moisture range falls within the 
range reported for Indian rice accessions by Verma and 
Srivastav (2017) [23], although the present study concerns a 
single rice variety.

5. Conclusion 
Under the conditions of the present kharif 2025–26 field 
experiment, application of 100% RDF through an INM -
based organo-mineral briquette (T3) was the most effective 
treatment for increasing total N, P and K uptake and grain 
protein content of lowland rice. Total N, P and K uptake 
under T 3 reached 84.71, 16.78 and 97.96 kg ha⁻¹, 
respectively, while grain protein reached 7.88%. The 80% 
RDF briquette treatment produced the highest starch content 
(78.24%), whereas the treatment combining urea -DAP 
briquette, potash and S, Zn and B supplementation (T 7) 
produced the highest lysine content (0.37%). Fat, fibre, 
moisture and ash were not significantly affected. Thus, 
within the scope of the present experiment, organo -mineral
