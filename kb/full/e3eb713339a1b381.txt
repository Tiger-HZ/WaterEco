We use a 15‐yr record from a 100‐km stretch of the tidal, freshwater Hudson River to examine the controls of phytoplankton biomass. Across years, seasonal mean chlorophyll a (Chl a ) and maximum Chl a varied by approximately 15‐fold, with mean growing‐season Chl a as high as 24 mg L −1 and maximum Chl a as high as 120 mg L −1 . For 3 of the 15 yr the river would be classified as eutrophic on the basis of lake standards for Chl a . Year‐toyear variation in Chl a was not closely related to either nutrients (phosphorus or nitrogen concentrations) or hydrologic flow.
