Abstract Produced-water management remains a major challenge in unconventional reservoirs as hydraulic fracturing generates large volumes of highly saline water that require treatment, reuse, or underground disposal. Wet in-situ combustion (ISC) offers an alternative approach by improving oil recovery while promoting steam distillation, mineral-fluid reactions, and the in-reservoir retention of impurities in hydrocarbons during thermal upgrades. This study investigates the effects of water injections, brine salinity, and reservoir mineralogy on oil recovery, produced-water quality, and combustion-gas composition.
