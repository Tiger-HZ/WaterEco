Wildfire has become increasingly common and severe across forested landscapes. Shortly after wildfire, loss of riparian cover along streams and subsequent increases in light can elevate stream temperatures, a key control on metabolic rates of biota. Increased light can also increase autotrophic basal resource availability with potential bottom-up effects. We evaluated wildfire impacts on aquatic ecosystems in a replicated Before-After Control-Impact study 1 year after a severe wildfire in western Oregon, U.S. Stream temperature, chlorophyll a accrual, and age-0 coastal cutthroat trout size, density, and biomass increased in all three burned streams relative to changes in three unburned references.
