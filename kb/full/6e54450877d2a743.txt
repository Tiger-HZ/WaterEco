Bell pepper ( Capsicum annuum L.) is a high-value vegetable crop, and its productivity in greenhouse and controlled-environment systems depends strongly on irrigation and nutrient management. Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) have been proposed as biological tools to improve plant water and nutrient acquisition, but their functional value in intensively managed vegetable systems remains uncertain. This study evaluated whether commercial AMF inoculation improves physiological performance, growth, yield, nutrient status, and root colonization in greenhouse-grown bell pepper. Two complementary experiments were conducted using a commercial AMF product containing Rhizophagus irregularis, Funneliformis mosseae , Rhizophagus aggregatum, and Claroideoglomus etunicatum.
