Abstract At many small-scale local markets, fish by-products are often disposed of using inefficient and unscientific methods such as unsanitary landfilling, open burning, or improper composting. These practices release NH₃ and H₂S, which negatively affect community life, as well as CH₄, a greenhouse gas that contributes to climate change. Therefore, various methods have been developed to utilize fish by-products, such as processing them into collagen, thermal processing to produce fish oil, or fish meal. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of using a microbial consortium consisting of Trichoderma sp. fungi, Bacillus sp. bacteria, and Streptomyces sp. actinomycetes to compost fish by-products into organic fertilizer.
