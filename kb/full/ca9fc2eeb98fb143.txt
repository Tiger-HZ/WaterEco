In marine ecosystems, cephalopods play a crucial role as high-trophic-level predators, making their ecological studies essential for understanding food web structures and resource management. This study investigates the feeding ecology of Acanthosepion esculentum in the Yellow–East China Sea through stable isotope analysis of eye lens δ¹³C and δ¹⁵N values, aiming to explore trophic niche differentiation and dietary shifts across different developmental stages. The findings reveal significant trophic niche differentiation, with the juvenile stage exhibiting the lowest δ¹³C and δ¹⁵N values, primarily reflecting a diet of low trophic level organisms such as zooplankton and small benthic invertebrates. As the A. esculentum matures into the immature and adult stages, δ¹³C values remain relatively stable, while δ¹⁵N values increase, indicating a dietary shift towards higher trophic level prey, including small fish and larger invertebrates. The analysis shows a clear correlation between eye lens diameter and mantle length (ML), allowing for accurate staging of developmental phases. Furthermore, the trophic ecological niche narrows as the A. esculentum matures, with minimal overlap between the immature and adult stages. These results highlight the adaptive feeding strategies of A. esculentum , emphasizing its role as an important mid- to high-trophic-level predator and energy transfer link in the Yellow–East China Sea food web, while also contributing to the sustainable management of its populations.
