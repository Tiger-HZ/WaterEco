The Upper Cretaceous and Jurassic karst aquifers of Sierra de Segura (southern Spain) represent an important groundwater resource within Sierras de Cazorla, Segura, and Las Villas Natural Park (the largest protected area in Spain). The two carbonate aquifer systems are separated by the low-permeability Utrillas Formation, composed mainly of sands, sandstones, and clay-rich sediments. Hydraulic connectivity between these aquifers remains poorly constrained in the literature.
