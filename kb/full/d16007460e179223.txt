Agricultural irrigation accounts for the large majority of global freshwater withdrawals, and mounting water scarcity is driving increased interest in reusing municipal, industrial, and agricultural wastewater as an irrigation source. Membrane-based treatment spanning microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, reverse osmosis, forward osmosis, membrane bioreactors, and membrane distillation already underpins much of this reuse, but conventional membranes remain constrained by fouling, high energy demand, and an inherent trade-off between removing contaminants and retaining agronomically valuable nutrients. This review examines nanocomposite membranes as a materials-based strategy for overcoming these limitations. It surveys the classification of nanomaterials used in membrane design
