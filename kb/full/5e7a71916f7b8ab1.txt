Background: Exercise-induced laryngeal obstruction (EILO) and exercise-induced bronchoconstriction (EIB), the clinical hallmark of exercise-induced asthma, are the two dominant medical explanations for exertional dyspnoea in athletes. They are physiologically distinct yet routinely confused, and EILO is frequently mislabelled and mistreated as refractory or difficult-to-control asthma. Aim: To critically compare EILO and exercise-induced asthma in athletes and to synthesise current evidence on their differentiation and clinical management. Material and methods: Narrative review of peer-reviewed clinical studies, consensus documents and reviews addressing the pathophysiology, epidemiology, diagnosis and treatment of EILO and EIB in athletic and adolescent populations.
