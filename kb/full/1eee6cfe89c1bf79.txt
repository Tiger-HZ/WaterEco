Abstract This study investigates how listing characteristics influence purchasing decisions in online auctions. Specifically, it examines whether an item description’s length (i.e., wordiness) and herding bias affect the likelihood of a successful sale and explores the moderating effects of quality rhetoric and linguistic vagueness. We quantify these rhetorical characteristics using computer-aided text analysis on 230,270 auction descriptions. Our findings confirm that herding bias positively predicts sales probability, while an optimal listing length exists beyond which excessive verbosity diminishes sales likelihood.
