Cities must become more resilient to counteract challenges due to increasing urbanisation, higher population density and climate change. Incorporation of blue-green infrastructure (BGI) in urban design and planning is one potential measure to achieve a higher level of resilience in a sustainable manner. In this work the Land Utilisation and Capability Indicator (LUCI) has been expanded to assess co-benefits of BGI and to analyse trade-offs and synergies at high spatial resolution. A first application was carried out for the Ōtākaro/Avon River catchment in Christchurch, New Zealand. The four ecosystem services (ES) studied were flood mitigation, carbon sequestration, habitat connectivity and evaporative cooling. Specific areas where the status quo should be preserved, and areas where the implementation of BGI will lead to an improvement of multiple ES were identified. This information enables effective urban planning and design of BGI, supporting development of sustainable and resilient cities.
