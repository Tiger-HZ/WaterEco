Introduction Arid agricultural soils are dynamic systems in which groundwater irrigation, intense evaporation, limited natural leaching, and heterogeneous parent materials jointly regulate salinity, nutrient accumulation, and potentially contaminating element mobility. Although previous studies in Hail, northwestern Saudi Arabia, have identified important water-quality and soil-geochemical constraints, laboratory soil chemistry, GIS-derived spatial patterns, and process-based interpretation have not been fully integrated. This study examined whether soil chemistry is better explained by isolated elemental distributions or by coupled spatial–chemical patterns associated with irrigation return flow, evapoconcentration, carbonate–gypsum buffering, fertilizer inputs, and lithogenic inheritance
