T he establishment of the Intergovernmental Science-PolicyPanel on Chemicals, Waste and Pollution (ISP-CWP) provides a long-needed mechanism to strengthen the global science-policy interface on chemicals, waste, and pollution prevention.With its Secretariat hosted by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), ISP-CWP now stands alongside the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) and the Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES) as part of the broader institutional response to the triple planetary crisis 1,2 (see Figure 1).Diamond et al. 2 recently framed this opportunity around scientific engagement, prioritization relevant to low-and middle-income countries (LMICs), capacity building, horizon scanning, holistic assessments, and complementarity with existing multilateral environmental agreements (MEAs).Following the first part of its inaugural plenary session in February 2026, and with key operating procedures still under development, 8,9 the central challenge is no longer ambition alone but whether ISP-CWP can translate a broad mandate into transparent, reproducible, inclusive, and implementation-oriented assessments.At this stage, methods and governance are therefore not merely administrative details; they represent the first scientific deliverables required for credible, decision-ready assessments.This Viewpoint proposes minimum assessment standards that could render ISP-CWP outputs transparent, traceable, inclusive, and usable for future policy decisions.
