Abstract Vegetated inland water habitats are important global emitters of methane (CH 4 ), but the variability in their CH 4 emission is poorly understood, making estimates and predictions uncertain. Methane is believed to be primarily released into the atmosphere via plant lacunal tissues in dense vegetated zones, although experiments have also shown that a large fraction of CH 4 can be consumed in the rhizosphere before it diffuses into the roots. Methane rhizospheric oxidation measurements have been limited to few species in situ, and it is still unclear how it differs between species.
