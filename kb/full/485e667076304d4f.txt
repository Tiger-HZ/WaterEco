Water quality prediction using enhanced 
parrot optimization algorithm with ensemble 
learning techniques
Kalaivanan K1, Karpagalakshmi RC2, Gobinath C3 and Rajkumar Y4* 
Abstract 
Water quality monitoring has become increasingly critical as urbanization and industrialization escalate global water 
pollution challenges, contributing to approximately 1.8 million deaths annually from waterborne diseases accord-
ing to World Health Organization estimates. This study introduces a novel integration of the Parrot Optimization 
Algorithm (POA) with ensemble learning (ANN, RF, XGBoost) for water quality prediction using the Cauvery River 
dataset (from 2018 to 2022, (642 samples, 26 parameters). The approach was validated using a comprehensive data-
set from the Cauvery River collected by the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board. Experimental results demonstrate 
that POA significantly outperformed traditional optimization techniques, achieving 85.5% accuracy, 86.3% precision, 
86.2% recall, and 87.3% F1 score. Crucially, we have statistically validated that POA exhibited 32% faster convergence 
and selected 50% more relevant features compared to Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Genetic Algorithm 
(GA). This research contributes a computationally efficient and highly accurate decision-support tool that enables 
stakeholders and policymakers to monitor water pollution in real-time, facilitating early intervention strategies 
and promoting sustainable water resource management practices globally.
Keywords Water quality, Ensemble, Feature selection, Parrot optimization, Prediction
Introduction
River water quality monitoring represents a critical global 
health imperative, directly impacting human well-being 
through its connection to waterborne disease transmis -
sion and ecosystem sustainability. The World Health 
Organization estimates that 1.8 million people died from 
waterborne diseases in 2018 alone [1]. This alarming fig -
ure underscores the necessity of continuous surveillance 
and evaluation of water bodies against established maxi -
mum permissible concentration (MPC) thresholds for 
various contaminants. When pollutant concentrations 
exceed these critical limits, immediate remedial actions 
must be implemented to restore water quality and miti -
gate contamination levels. Such monitoring protocols 
are essential for determining water suitability for diverse 
applications including potable water supply, fishing activ-
ities, and recreational purposes, while ensuring compli -
ance with regulatory frameworks [2].
Environmental researchers have increasingly focused 
on river water quality assessment as a fundamental indi -
cator for forecasting potential disease outbreaks. Over 
recent decades, the scientific community has employed 
numerous computational approaches to analyze water 
quality parameters and generate reliable disease pre -
dictions. However, the effectiveness of these predictive 
models is predominantly contingent upon the quality and 
characteristics of the training datasets [3]. These datasets 
typically encompass physical, chemical, and biological

Feature selection remains a critical issue in water qual -

unique characteristics that make it particularly suitable 
for feature selection in water quality assessment. Unlike 
traditional metaheuristic algorithms, POA incorporates

POA’s unique combination of Lévy-flight foraging, 
which enables large jumps in the search space to escape 
local optima, and communication-intensity scaling, 
which facilitates efficient information sharing between 
solutions, makes it particularly well-suited for navigat -
ing the complex feature landscape of water quality data. 
Additionally, POA’s fear response mechanism helps 
maintain population diversity, preventing premature con-
vergence a common issue in traditional algorithms when 
dealing with high-dimensional datasets.
Based on the above research gaps, the proposed work 
involves the following major contributions which are 
highlighted as follows: Firstly, the proposed work uti -
lized a comprehensive Cauvery River Dataset (2018–

data. Secondly, it utilizes KNN-based machine learn -
ing technique thereby preserving the spatial/temporal 
correlations with simple mean or median approach to 
clean the missing value and to improve the prediction 
accuracy. Thirdly, the proposed work involves the use of 
the Parrot Optimization Algorithm (POA) for feature 
selection, leveraging a trade-off between the exploration 
and exploitation phases. Additionally, it achieves speedy 
convergence when compared with the traditional meta-
heuristic algorithms such as particle swarm optimization 
(PSO), genetic algorithm (GA) and grey-wolf optimiza -
tion (GWO). Finally, a hybrid ensemble architecture has 
been developed that integrates artificial neural network 
(ANN), random forest (RF) and XGBoost (Extreme Gra -
dient Boosting) approaches through stacking and voting, 
leading to an accurate and computationally efficient deci-
sion-support tool for river water quality assessment.

A foundational comparative investigation by Barzegar 
et  al. [7] engineered a hybrid model that merged wavelet 
transforms with extreme learning machines to achieve an 
accuracy of 80.9%. This approach demonstrated the value 
of preprocessing techniques to improve model perfor -
mance by extracting meaningful features from complex 
data. The disadvantage of their method is that the selec -
tion of appropriate wavelet parameters can be complex 
and may require domain expertise. Offering a different 
perspective, Avila et  al. [8 ] used five machine learning 
(ML) models to predict *E. coli* levels in the Oreti River, 
located in Wallacetown, New Zealand. The probabilistic 
framework that furnished critical insights into prediction 
uncertainty an aspect frequently neglected in determinis-
tic models and attained an accuracy of 83.7%. The model’s 
ability to quantify uncertainty makes it valuable for risk 
assessment in water management. The major disadvantage 
of their approach is that the computational complexity can 
increase significantly with a larger number of variables, 
making it less scalable for high-dimensional datasets.
The period spanning 2020 to 2022 was distinguished 
by a diversification of methodologies, as the research 
community explored a broader array of models from 
advanced neural networks to complex ensembles often 
in pursuit of near-perfect predictive accuracy. Nayak 
et  al. [9] engineered ANN, effectively capturing time-
series dependencies to achieve 84.3% accuracy. The 
suggested method’s disadvantage is that LSTM models 
require large amounts of sequential data to be trained 
effectively, which may not always be available in environ -
mental monitoring contexts. In parallel, Jiang et  al. [10] 
introduced a deep convolutional feature fusion network 
that integrated multiple data sources, attaining 82% accu-
racy through the effective combination of spatial and 
temporal features. This highlighted the importance of 
multi-source data integration. The suggested method’s 
disadvantage is its architectural complexity, which makes 
it difficult to implement and optimize without specialized 
expertise in deep learning. This era was also defined by 
the creation of increasingly complex and high-perform -
ing ensembles. The pursuit of peak performance culmi -
nated in studies such as Wong et al. [11], who reported 
a perfect accuracy of 95% using a stacked model com -
bining multiple algorithms, and Khullar and Singh [12], 
who obtained 94% accuracy using a BiLSTM model. The 
former study represented the pursuit of peak perfor -
mance through highly complex ensembles. The suggested 
method’s disadvantage is its extreme complexity, which 
makes it computationally expensive, difficult to repro -
duce, and potentially prone to overfitting. A significant 
shift in recent research has been a movement beyond 
merely achieving high accuracy to ensuring model 
robustness, interpretability, and computational efficiency. 
In 2023, the power of deep learning to capture complex 
spatiotemporal patterns was demonstrated by Guo et al. 
[13], who developed a novel hybrid CNN-LSTM model 
to predict the WQI in Hainan Province, China. Their 
model exhibited superior performance over standalone 
models, achieving a high coefficient of determination 
 (R2 = 0.98). The suggested method’s disadvantage is that 
the hybrid architecture’s performance is highly depend -
ent on the quality and quantity of training data, and it 
may not perform well on data from different geographical 
regions. A pivotal development in 2023 was the integra -
tion of Explainable AI (XAI).
Bui et al. [14] introduced an integrated framework that 
coupled ensemble models (AB–ADTree model) tech -
nique for groundwater potential mapping. This research 
was instrumental in demystifying the ’black box’ nature 
of complex models, making the results highly actionable 
for policymakers. The suggested method’s disadvantage 
is that while SHAP provides local explanations, global 
interpretation of the entire model’s behavior can still be 
challenging. This trajectory towards more sophisticated 
and interpretable models accelerated into 2024. Ader -
emi et  al. [15] proposed an XAI method specifically for 
forecasting pollution in drinking water, further cement -
ing the importance of transparency. Their proposed 
method’s disadvantage is that the implementation of XAI 
techniques can add a layer of computational overhead to 
the modelling process. Goodarzi et al. [16] applied three 
models to predict groundwater quality, with Multivari -
ate Adaptive Regression Splines achieving an exceptional 
99.61% accuracy. This study showcased the continued 
high performance of advanced boosting models in water 
quality prediction tasks. The suggested method’s disad -
vantage is that achieving such high accuracy might indi -
cate overfitting to the specific dataset, and the model’s 
performance on new, unseen data needs careful valida -
tion. Saberioon et al. [17] focused on chlorophyll-a, a key 
eutrophication indicator in Třeboň Basin, finding that 
random forest provided the most accurate and stable 
predictions (R2 = 0.89). This suggested the strong poten -
tial of Random forest for lake water quality manage -
ment. Since the proposed method is based on tree-based 
models, it is highly sensitive to hyperparameter settings, 
requiring a systematic tuning process to achieve optimal 
results. Representing the cutting edge of architectural 
innovation, more recently, Zhu et  al. [18] introduced a 
hybrid deep learning model for predicting turbidity (UT)

Synthesis of research gaps and motivation
Critically, a comprehensive synthesis of the literature 
reveals that despite these methodological strides, per -
sistent weaknesses limit the effectiveness of current 
approaches and delineate clear research opportunities. 
The following key gaps have been identified:
i) Suboptimal feature selection methodologies:
ii) Feature selection remains a formidable challenge, as 
high-dimensional datasets frequently contain redun -
dant or irrelevant parameters that can impair model 
performance. Conventional optimization algorithms, 
such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and 
Genetic Algorithm (GA), have demonstrated inad -
equacies in navigating the complex search spaces 
inherent to water quality feature selection, often 
hampered by parameter sensitivity issues and pro -
tracted convergence rates.
iii) Limited exploration of sophisticated ensemble 
frameworks: the majority of existing studies have 
concentrated on individual machine learning mod -
els or simplistic ensemble approaches (e.g., voting). 
The considerable potential of advanced, dynamic 
ensemble architectures, such as stacking which can 
dynamically learn optimal weights for model combi -
nation—remains largely untapped in the water qual -
ity prediction literature.
iv) Novel optimizers and advanced ensembles: the inte -
gration of sophisticated, bio-inspired optimization 
algorithms with advanced ensemble techniques rep -
resents a significant and largely unexplored frontier. 
Marrying the superior feature selection capabilities 
of a novel optimizer with the predictive power of a 
hybrid ensemble architecture presents a promising 
avenue for creating a more comprehensive and effec -
tive framework.
The present research is strategically positioned to 
bridge these identified gaps. It introduces a novel inte -
gration of the Parrot Optimization Algorithm (POA) 
with advanced ensemble learning techniques (Stacking 
and Voting). Our methodology synergizes the superior 
feature selection capabilities of POA with the predictive 
strength of hybrid ensemble architectures, culminating 
in a holistic and robust predictive framework for accurate 
and efficient water quality assessment that overcomes the 
limitations of previous studies. The summary of the lit -
erature review is presented in Table 2.
Proposed methodology
The proposed methodology involves an elaborate 
description on the dataset acquisition, data pre-process -
ing, feature selection based on POA, and the ensemble 
approach.
Dataset acquisition
The proposed research utilized a comprehensive water 
quality database sourced from the Tamil Nadu Pollution 
Control Board (TNPCB), which contained monitoring 
data collected from the Cauvery River basin between 
2018 and 2022. This dataset comprised 26 distinct param-
eters encompassing physical, chemical, and biological 
characteristics of water, as detailed in Table  3. The target 
variable, designated as "class," was structured as a binary 
indicator representing water quality status, specifically 
categorizing samples as either suitable for consumption 
(potable) or unsuitable (non-potable). To maintain data -
set integrity and minimize potential bias during model 
development, stratified random sampling technique has 
been utilized.
Data pre-processing
The data pre-processing involves steps such as data 
cleaning and data normalization. The detailed descrip -
tion of each of these techniques is described as follows:
i) Data cleaning: To perform data cleaning, the miss -
ing values are carefully handled to ensure reliability 
during data training. The proposed work employs 
K-nearest neighbour (KNN) with a neighbourhood 
value of 3. This provides superior ability thereby 
maintaining the local data patterns and relationships. 
The algorithm computes the Euclidean distance 
between the incomplete instance and all complete 
instances, identifies the three nearest neighbours, 
and computes the average of their values for the miss-
ing parameter. This approach is particularly effective 
for water quality data as it preserves the inherent cor-
relations between different parameters that might be 
lost with simpler imputation methods.

Experimental validation
To ensure the robustness, reproducibility, and general -
izability of our proposed model, we employed a multi-
faceted validation strategy:
1. Holdout Validation: The dataset was partitioned into 
training (70%) and testing (30%) subsets using strati -
fied random sampling to maintain class distribution.
2. K-Fold Cross-Validation: We implemented 5-fold 
cross-validation for performance estimation, where 
the dataset was divided into 5 equal parts.
3. Nested Cross-Validation: For unbiased hyperparam -
eter tuning, we employed nested cross-validation 
with an outer fivefold loop for model evaluation and 
an inner 3-fold loop for parameter optimization.
4. Statistical Validation: We performed paired t-tests to 
compare the performance of our proposed method 
with existing approaches, ensuring the statistical sig -
nificance of the reported improvements.

Performance analysis
The predictive capabilities of the individual machine 
learning models, namely Artificial Neural Network 
(ANN), Random Forest (RF), and XGBoost, were sys -
tematically benchmarked against two ensemble strate -
gies, Stacking and Voting, using the reserved test dataset. 
The empirical results, detailed in Table  7, provide t-test 
results comparing the Voting Ensemble with individual 
models. Table  7 unequivocally demonstrates that the 
ensemble approaches consistently surpassed the perfor -
mance of the individual constituent models. This under -
scores the principle that combining diverse algorithms 
can mitigate individual weaknesses and leverage comple -
mentary strengths to produce a more robust and accu -
rate predictive framework. These results confirm that the 
ensemble’s superior accuracy is not due to random varia -
tion but reflects a statistically robust improvement.
To ensure that the observed performance enhance -
ments were not due to random chance, a statistical vali -
dation was conducted. Paired t-tests were performed 
comparing the results of the Voting ensemble against 
each individual model. The analysis yielded p-values less 
than 0.05, confirming that the superior performance of 
the ensemble approach is statistically significant. This 
comprehensive evaluation, combining numerical metrics, 
visual analysis, and statistical testing, provides robust 
evidence that the Voting ensemble represents the most 
effective and reliable model for water quality prediction 
in this study.
From Table  7 and Table  8 the results reveal a clear

by Random Forest and then ANN. The integration of

Voting ensembles showed substantial improvements, 
the Voting ensemble achieved the highest scores across 
all evaluation metrics, with an accuracy of 85.5% and an

KNN performance on water quality prediction
Environmental monitoring data, particularly from 
dynamic river systems like the Cauvery, frequently con -
tain gaps due to equipment malfunction, logistical chal -
lenges, or inconsistent sampling schedules. The integrity 
of predictive models is highly dependent on the quality 
of the input data, making the handling of missing values 
a critical preprocessing step. In this study, we employed 
the K-Nearest Neighbour (KNN) imputation technique 
with k = 3, chosen for its ability to preserve the natural 
variance and distribution patterns inherent in water qual-
ity parameters. Unlike simpler methods such as mean 
or median imputation, which can artificially flatten data 
and mask important environmental events like pollu -
tion spikes, KNN identifies samples with similar multi-
parameter characteristics. This approach maintains the 
complex, non-linear interrelationships between physi -
cal, chemical, and biological indicators that define a riv -
er’s health. As shown in Table  10, this methodologically 
sound approach resulted in superior model performance 
compared to alternative imputation methods.
The KNN imputation method demonstrated a significant 
improvement in all performance metrics, with an increase 
of 7.2% in accuracy compared to mean imputation. This 
enhancement can be attributed to the preservation of local 
data structures and correlations between water quality 
parameters, which are critical for accurate prediction of 
water quality status.

Comparative analysis with existing methods
A comparative analysis of research efforts in water qual -
ity prediction reveals a clear evolutionary trajectory in 
both methodology and performance. Early investiga -
tions, such as Barzegar et al. [7], established a baseline of 
around 80% accuracy by combining wavelet transforms 
with an Extreme Learning Machine on river water data. 
Subsequent studies shifted toward more intricate hybrid 
and ensemble architectures: Chen et  al. [19] applied a 
Deep Convolutional Feed-Forward Network to multi-
source water data, reaching 81.2%. Despite this increase 
in complexity, these sophisticated approaches yielded 
only incremental gains, with accuracy largely plateauing 
in the low-to-mid 80% range. In marked contrast, the 
proposed method, which integrates the Parrot Optimiza -
tion Algorithm (POA) with an Ensemble framework on 
the Cauvery River dataset, achieves a superior accuracy 
of 85.8%, surpassing the performance ceiling established 
by recent state-of-the-art models. Table  12 provides the 
comparative analysis of the proposed methodology Vs 
existing literature.
Environmental interpretation of results
The predictions generated by our POA-ensemble model 
have direct implications for environmental manage -
ment and public health. Water classified as non-potable 
by our model indicates the presence of contaminants 
that exceed WHO guidelines, potentially posing health 
risks to consumers. The feature selection results provide 
additional insights into the key indicators of water qual -
ity in the Cauvery River. The high importance assigned 
to DO, BOD, and COD suggests that organic pollution is 
a significant concern in this water body. This aligns with 
the industrial and agricultural activities along the river, 
which often discharge organic waste that depletes oxy -
gen levels. The inclusion of Fecal Coliforms (FC) among 
the top features highlights microbiological contamina -
tion as a critical issue, likely stemming from inadequate 
sanitation facilities and untreated sewage discharge. This 
finding is particularly concerning given the direct link 
between FC contamination and waterborne diseases. 
The chemical parameters selected by POA (pH, nitrates, 
phosphates, chlorides, etc.) reflect the impact of agri -
cultural runoff, industrial discharge, and natural geo -
logical processes on water quality. For instance, elevated 
nitrate and phosphate levels often indicate fertilizer run -
off, which can lead to eutrophication and harmful algal 
blooms. These insights can guide targeted interventions 
by water resource managers. For example, addressing 
organic pollution might require improved wastewater 
treatment, while reducing microbiological contamination 
could involve enhancing sanitation infrastructure. The 
model’s ability to provide real-time predictions enables 
proactive management, allowing authorities to imple -
ment mitigation measures before water quality deterio -
rates to hazardous levels.

Conclusion
In conclusion, this work pioneered the integration of 
the Parrot Optimization Algorithm (POA) with ensem -
ble learning techniques to create a highly effective model 
for water quality prediction, validated on the Cauvery 
River dataset. The POA demonstrated its superiority 
by identifying 13 critical parameters from an initial 26, 
achieving a 32% faster convergence rate than traditional 
algorithms. This optimized feature set, when used with a 
stacked ensemble of ANN and RF base learners and an 
XGBoost metamodel, yielded exceptional performance 
with 85.5% accuracy, 86.3% precision, 86.2% recall, and 
an 87.3% F1 score. The primary contributions of this 
work are multifaceted, encompassing the first application 
of POA for feature selection in this domain, the develop -
ment of a computationally efficient and robust predictive 
framework, comprehensive validation to ensure general -
izability, and the provision of actionable environmental 
insights for Water resource management. The proposed 
system stands as a practical tool for real-time monitoring, 
enabling stakeholders to implement timely interventions 
and manage resources more effectively. Future research 
will aim to enhance this model by integrating spatial and 
temporal analysis, developing it into a cloud-based plat -
form for wider accessibility, extending its applicability 
to other water bodies, and incorporating explainable AI 
techniques to further improve model transparency and 
user trust, ultimately advancing the field of environmen -
tal monitoring systems.
