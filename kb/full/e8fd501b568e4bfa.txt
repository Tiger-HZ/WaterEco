Abstract Reliable environmental attribution of coastal dissolved organic carbon (DOC) variability is important for interpreting carbon processing and fate under climate change. Yet attribution commonly relies on contemporaneous relationships or a single lag structure, leaving uncertain whether variable rankings remain robust across different spans of prior system history. We analyzed quarterly observations (total nitrogen, total phosphorus, chlorophyll a , pheophytin, water temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, salinity, and water clarity) from 1981 to 2021 across Tokyo Bay. Regional vector autoregressive models and forecast‐error variance decomposition quantified DOC forecast uncertainty attributed to DOC itself and the measured variables.
