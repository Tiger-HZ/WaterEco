Abstract Pseudocapacitive electrode-based capacitive deionization (CDI) is a competitive water purification technology, yet organic electrodes are limited by insufficient redox activity and sluggish adsorption kinetics in practical use. A novel sulfur-bridged polymer (SPTCDA) was developed as the electrode for a hybrid CDI (HCDI) system to address efficient ammonium ion (NH4+) removal and recovery from industrial wastewater. Experimental characterizations and density functional theory calculations confirm that SPTCDA’s ladder-type conjugated structure, delocalized π-electron system, and structural flexibility synergistically boost conductivity, ion diffusion, and NH4+ affinity.
