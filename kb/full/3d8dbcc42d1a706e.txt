The cultivation of freshwater crayfish generates organic waste that can be reused through bioconversion processes. This study aimed to evaluate the N:P ratio present in treated Cherax quadricarinatus waste and characterize the growth performance of Ipomoea aquatica cultivated under different aquaponic systems. The experiment was conducted using a completely randomized design consisting of one control and three treatments with different waste treatment technologies namely control (without additional filtration treatment), treatment 1 (T1, biological filtration (biofilm reactor), treatment 2 (T2, physical filtration (activated carbon adsorption), and treatment 3 (T3, chemical filtration (CaO₂@Fe-alginate material). The results showed that water quality parameters remained within optimal ranges, with temperatures ranging from 26.5–29°C, dissolved oxygen 5.6–7.4 mg/L, and pH 7.0–7.45. The N:P ratio was consistently low, remaining below 0.35 across all treatments. Two cultivation systems were evaluated: a dynamic system with continuous water circulation and a static system without water exchange. In general, the relative growth of Ipomoea aquatica was higher in the dynamic system than in the static system. Based on these findings, it can be concluded that the low N:P ratio observed in all treatments resulted in significant differences between the aquaculture systems applied.
