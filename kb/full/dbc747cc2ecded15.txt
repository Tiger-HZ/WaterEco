Abstract Synechococcus inhabit both nutrient‐rich estuaries and oligotrophic waters, yet exhibit distinct niche segregation across phylogenetic clades. Previous studies indicated clade III predominated in the ultraoligotrophic Mediterranean Sea. However, the mechanisms underlying clade III's dominance over other clades in ultraoligotrophic waters remain unclear. Here, we measured bulk and clade‐specific growth rates of Synechococcus using a dilution approach during a mesocosm experiment in the Eastern Mediterranean Sea. Clade III exhibited higher growth rates than competing clades and competitive superiority for limited nutrients in mesocosms amended with ammonium and phosphate.
