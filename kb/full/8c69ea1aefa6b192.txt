Abstract There has been evidence of increasing fire activity in the United States since approximately the 1980s, now estimated to cause tens to hundreds of billions of dollars of damage per year. Weather is one of the key drivers of fire activity, with hot, dry, and windy weather commonly associated with high-risk conditions. We are still, however, developing an understanding of how these weather variables have contributed to historical trends in fire weather and whether these trends will continue. In this work, we investigate the impact of declining humidity in arid regions of the United States.
