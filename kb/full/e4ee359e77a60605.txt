Managing disruptive behavior remains a significant challenge in contemporary classrooms, particularly among learners with behavioral disorders. This study explored the lived experiences of teachers managing disruptive behaviors among children with behavioral disorders using reinforcement strategies, aiming to understand the challenges they encounter, the strategies they employ, and the insights they gain from classroom implementation. A qualitative descriptive research design was used, with data gathered through in-depth interviews and focus group discussions, which enabled teachers to share their experiences, practices, and reflections on behavior management. Findings revealed that teachers frequently deal with recurring and unpredictable disruptive behaviors that require consistent monitoring, patience, and immediate intervention. They commonly employ relationship-based approaches, positive reinforcement techniques, and improvised strategies tailored to learners’ individual needs and classroom contexts. Teachers also emphasized that disruptive behavior is often a form of communication, prompting them to adjust their responses to encourage positive behavioral change. Over time, participants reported gaining greater confidence, adaptability, and reflective practice in applying reinforcement strategies effectively. The study underscores the importance of strengthening teacher preparation and continuous professional development in behavior management.
