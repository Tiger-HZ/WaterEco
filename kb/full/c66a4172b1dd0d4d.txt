Abstract Trifluoroacetate (TFA) is a member of the PFAS group and shares their persistence. Its very small molecular size and anionic character makes it, in contrast to many other PFAS, highly water-soluble and mobile in the aquatic environment. Wastewater shows elevated TFA concentrations. This TFA may stem from background sources such as drinking water, infiltrating groundwater, or rainwater. It may also come from human water use itself. To quantify a potential TFA surplus, we built a mass balance at three rural sewer sites in Freiburg, Germany. End-member mixing analysis yielded TFA loads from groundwater and drinking water.
