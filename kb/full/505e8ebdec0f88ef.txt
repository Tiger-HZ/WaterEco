We assessed the genetic relationships among landlocked populations and parapatric anadromous populations of Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) from the Saguenay River system to infer the alternative scenarios of single versus double origin of salmon in Lake Saint-Jean, Québec, Canada. We predicted that the hypothesis of double origin would be supported by the closer relationship of some landlocked populations to anadromous ones than to other landlocked populations. Alternatively, the single-origin scenario would be supported if landlocked populations were genetically closer to each other than to anadromous populations.
