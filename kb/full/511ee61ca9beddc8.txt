Wastewater treatment is an important part of environmental management because it aims to eliminate toxins and pollutants from water before it is discharged into the environment. Biodegradation is one of the most environmentally friendly and cost-effective wastewater treatment processes. Biodegradation is a naturally occurring process in which microorganisms break down organic and inorganic chemicals and convert them to less hazardous molecules. It is critical in the removal of contaminants such as organic debris, fertilizers, and numerous industrial compounds in wastewater treatment. This chapter delves into the underlying concepts of biodegradation, focusing on the roles of bacteria, fungus, and other microorganisms in the process. In order to promote the growth and activity of specific microorganisms, it is necessary to optimize environmental parameters such temperature, pH, and oxygen levels for biodegradation to be effective in relating to wastewater. We address numerous tactics for increasing the effectiveness of biodegradation, such as the use of specific microbial cultures and bioaugmentation approaches. This chapter of this book dives into the mechanism and uses of biodegradation in wastewater treatment. Also, include case studies and examples of biodegradation applications in various wastewater treatment systems. It underlines the need of studying biodegradations microbial ecology in order to customize remedies to specific wastewater characteristics. In conclusion, this book chapter provides a thorough review of biodegradation in wastewater treatment, providing light on its processes and many uses.
