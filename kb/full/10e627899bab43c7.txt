 Article 
 Teaching Vulnerability Assessment Through Open-Source Case Studies: A Group-Project Pedagogy and a Cautionary Lesson on Vendor Marketing 
 
 
 
 Authors
 
 
 Jackson N. Stanley Megan Whittle Ricky L. Summitt Kyndal Biladeau Mark Nguyen Howard L. Hall 
 
 
 
 
 Abstract 
 This paper reports a graduate-level pedagogical approach to teaching physical security vulnerability assessment for high-consequence nuclear facilities and a secondary observation arising from the student exercise that has implications for the nuclear industry, vendors, and host states. In the fall semester of 2024, the University of Tennessee, Knoxville (UTK) graduate course Physical Security for High-Consequence Facilities was redesigned around a semester-long group project: students conducted an open-source-only vulnerability assessment of a real high-consequence nuclear facility, developed adversary sequence diagrams (ASDs) for credible attack pathways, and proposed engineering-based mitigations, using professional vulnerability-assessment tools including Sandia National Laboratories’ Pathtrace pathway-analysis software. We report two observations. First, the group-project format was associated with high levels of engagement, depth of analysis, and integration across the detect-delay-respond (3D) framework in this cohort. Relative to our prior lecture-based offerings of the course, these outcomes appeared qualitatively stronger. However, the comparison is observational, and additional iterations of the redesigned course across future cohorts will be required before causal conclusions can be drawn. Second, the open-source corpus available to the students was substantially shaped by the facility vendor’s own promotional materials. The richest single category of source material—including reactor cross-sections, fuel-handling details, and high-resolution interior and exterior imagery—was published by the vendor or its contractors as part of an international marketing campaign for the platform. We describe the pedagogy, summarize the categories of vulnerability the student exercise surfaced, present the defensive mitigations proposed, and discuss the implications of vendor-driven open-source disclosure for high-consequence nuclear platforms. We propose vendor adversarial review (VAR)—periodic evaluation of the cumulative open-source profile of a marketed platform from the perspective of a hostile adversary—as a response rooted in the intellectual lineage of U.S. operations security (OPSEC). 
 
 Keywords: nuclear security education , vulnerability assessment , physical protection systems , adversary sequence diagram , open-source intelligence , small modular reactors , floating nuclear power plant , workforce development , operations security , vendor adversarial review
