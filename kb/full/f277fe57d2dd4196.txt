An eco-friendly and efficient synthetic strategy has been developed for a series of 2,4,5-trichloro-N-arylbenzamide derivatives using a microwave-assisted protocol.The methodology employs bio-derived MnO2 nanoparticles, stabilized by Bunium persicum seed extract and supported on MnO2, as a reusable green catalyst.The approach proceeds under mild conditions and enables rapid formation of the target compounds with excellent efficiency.Structural elucidation of the synthesized derivatives was achieved through comprehensive spectroscopic and analytical techniques, including FTIR, NMR, HRMS, and elemental analysis, confirming the proposed molecular frameworks.The synthesized compounds were evaluated for their antibacterial, antifungal, and cytotoxic properties.Several derivatives demonstrated notable antibacterial activity against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative strains, in some cases surpassing the reference drug.Moderate to good antifungal activity was also observed against selected fungal pathogens.Preliminary cytotoxic screening using a brine shrimp lethality assay indicated appreciable cytotoxic potential for certain derivatives.This study highlights the utility of the 2,4,5-trichlorobenzamide scaffold as a promising platform for biologically active molecules and underscores the advantages of sustainable, plant-mediated nanoparticle catalysis in medicinal chemistry.
