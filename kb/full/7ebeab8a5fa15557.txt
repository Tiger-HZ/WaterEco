ABSTRACT Charophytes (stoneworts) often dominate the submerged vegetation in nutrient‐poor hardwater lakes, where they support high benthic phosphorus (P) retention. As a consequence, epilimnion P concentrations remain low and water clarity high, resulting in a positive feedback. When perennial charophytes, retaining P all year round, are replaced by vascular macrophytes, P is retained only during summer but rapidly released during decomposition in the autumn and winter. Epilimnion P thus becomes available to phytoplankton in the following growing season, reducing lake water clarity and further shifting plant dominance from bottom‐dwelling charophytes to taller vascular species.
