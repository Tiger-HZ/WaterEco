Agriculture is entering a period in which food demand, freshwater scarcity, climate volatility, soil degradation, input costs and labour availability are converging into a single systems problem. The earlier model of adding more water, land, chemicals and human effort is no longer sufficient. This paper proposes a federated agricultural digital twin architecture that links plot-level precision agriculture with village and Farmer Producer Organization services, regional logistics, river-basin water management and national food-system planning. The architecture is designed as a cyber-physical-social system: sensors, satellites, unmanned aerial vehicles, machines and farmer records generate evidence; artificial intelligence and simulation models convert evidence into forecasts; decision-intelligence systems recommend actions; automated irrigation, fertigation, targeted spraying, custom hiring, storage and market-linkage systems execute actions; and outcomes are verified to improve the next decision cycle. The paper updates the original holistic agriculture framework with recent concepts such as agricultural digital twins, generative AI advisory, remote-sensing foundation models, digital public infrastructure, edge AI, precision irrigation, robotics, carbon and sustainability MRV, and India’s Digital Agriculture Mission. It argues that the central aim of smart agriculture should not be data collection alone, but auditable, explainable and economically viable decision-making that improves yield per unit of water, net farmer income, soil health and supply-chain efficiency. A phased implementation pathway and measurable key performance indicators are proposed for crop-cluster pilots and scale-up in India and other Global South contexts.
