Abstract Ensiling fresh forage to produce silage is an effective strategy to ensure a stable, long-term feed supply. Maize (Zea mays) silage is generally preferred because of its high nutritional quality and palatability for cattle; however, its productivity and nutritional quality are drastically reduced under water-limited conditions. Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor) and pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum) are promising alternative forages for silage because of their low-input requirements under sub-optimal conditions.
