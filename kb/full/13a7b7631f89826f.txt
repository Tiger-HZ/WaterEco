Mismatch between quality and engagement in 
myasthenia gravis–related videos: a cross-sectional 
study on Chinese social media platforms
Yiheng Zhou · Ronghui Xia · Xinyi Xu · Chong Pang · Ping Wang · Xiaohua Kou
Received: 14 May 2026 / Accepted: 31 August 2026
© The Author(s) 2026
Abstract
As short-video platforms increasingly play a prominent role in disseminating public health information in 
China, evaluating the quality of their health-related content is crucial. This study aims to systematically assess 
the quality, reliability, and content characteristics of videos about myasthenia gravis (MG) on China’s main -
stream short-video platforms (Douyin and Red Note). Using “myasthenia gravis” as the keyword, the top 
100 videos returned by the default ranking on each platform were retrieved. After excluding duplicate and 
irrelevant videos, 174 videos, including 98 from Douyin and 76 from Red Note, were included in this cross-
sectional content analysis. Transparency, overall informational quality, and general reliability were assessed 
using the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) Benchmark criteria, Global Quality Score 
(GQS), and modified DISCERN instrument (mDISCERN), respectively. Video characteristics, uploader identi -
ties, content themes, and user engagement indicators were analyzed using nonparametric statistical methods. 
Physicians were the primary content source, accounting for 74.1% of the included videos. The most frequently 
addressed topics were treatment and management (47.1%) and clinical symptoms and presentation (40.2%), 
whereas diagnostic criteria and methods were addressed less frequently (8.0%). The median JAMA, GQS, and 
mDISCERN scores were 3.0 (interquartile range [IQR], 3.0–3.0), 3.0 (IQR, 3.0–4.0), and 2.0 (IQR, 2.0–3.0), 
respectively. Douyin videos had significantly higher raw numbers of likes, comments, saves, and shares than 
Red Note videos (all P < 0.001). Douyin showed a more favorable distribution of JAMA scores, whereas the 
median GQS and mDISCERN scores were identical between platforms, despite statistically significant differ -
ences in their score distributions. Across uploader groups, significant differences were observed in JAMA and 
GQS scores, but not in mDISCERN scores ( P = 0.091). Hospitals and public health organizations had the high -
est median numbers of likes, saves, and shares, whereas videos posted by patients and their families had the 
highest median number of comments. Correlations between assessment scores and user engagement indicators 
were generally weak (Spearman’s r ranging from − 0.15 to 0.37). Content related to MG on Chinese short-
video platforms generally exhibits moderate quality, with relatively favorable transparency but limited general 
reliability, and less frequent coverage of diagnostic criteria and examination methods. Content from profes -
sional medical sources tends to be better structured and more transparent, while patient-perspective content

Introduction
Myasthenia gravis (MG) is a chronic autoimmune disorder of the neuromuscular junction, in which pathogenic 
mechanisms involve antibody-mediated impairment of neuromuscular transmission. Clinically, MG is character-
ized by fluctuating and fatigable skeletal muscle weakness 1. The disease commonly presents with ocular symp -
toms such as ptosis and diplopia, but may also involve bulbar, limb, and respiratory muscles, leading to dysarthria, 
dysphagia, limb weakness, or respiratory compromise. Myasthenic crisis is generally defined as worsening MG 
that results in respiratory failure requiring ventilatory support, often accompanied by severe bulbar weakness, 
and represents one of the most serious complications in MG management2. Because early symptoms of MG may 
be subtle, fluctuating, and easily misattributed to other conditions or age-related changes, diagnostic delay and 
misdiagnosis remain common. In one previous study, diagnostic delays of 2 and 5 years were reported in 26% 
and 13% of patients with MG, respectively3. Delayed diagnosis may postpone the initiation of appropriate treat-
ment and increase the risk of disease progression, crisis, and impaired quality of life. Therefore, clear, reliable, 
and accessible patient education on early recognition, timely referral, long-term treatment, and self-management 
is important for both clinical care and public health.
Although MG remains a relatively uncommon disease, accumulating epidemiological evidence has drawn 
increasing attention to its disease burden and evolving population profile. Previous studies have reported annual 
MG incidence rates of approximately 0.3–2.8 per 100,000 person-years and prevalence estimates of about 25 per 
100,000 population4,5. A systematic review further showed substantial geographic heterogeneity in MG incidence 
and prevalence, suggesting that population structure, diagnostic capacity, and healthcare accessibility may influ-
ence observed epidemiological patterns6. In the context of population aging, late-onset MG has become increas-
ingly relevant. Recent national registry data from Turkey also suggest a shift toward late-onset MG and indicate 
that the classic bimodal age-sex distribution may be attenuated in some populations, supporting the need to 
interpret MG epidemiology in the context of aging societies7. In China, a nationwide study estimated an age- and 
sex-standardized MG incidence of 0.68 per 100,000 person-years 8. Cross-regional data have shown geographic 
differences in MG prevalence and treatment response 9, while mortality analyses have revealed regional dispari -
ties and an increasing mortality burden between 2013 and 202010. Chinese cohort studies have also suggested that 
late-onset MG is relatively common11,12. Together, these findings indicate that MG should not be viewed solely as 
a specialist-managed rare disease, but also as a condition with important implications for long-term care, follow-
up, regional healthcare equity, and patient education.

The information needs of patients with MG extend beyond pharmacological treatment. Patients and caregiv -
ers also require information on symptom recognition, diagnostic pathways, prognosis, daily living, treatment-
related adverse effects, and the psychosocial burden of long-term disease management. International studies have 
increasingly examined MG from the perspectives of patient experience and digital health. The MyRealWorld MG 
study, an international digital observational study using patient-reported outcomes, highlights the value of real-
world patient perspectives in evaluating treatment experience, symptom burden, quality of life, and psychosocial 
impact13. In addition, Legg et al. used cross-platform content analysis to identify unmet needs among patients 
with MG and caregivers, showing that online discussions involve treatment, symptoms, diagnosis, investigations, 
and daily living14. These studies suggest that digital platforms are not only channels for health-information seek-
ing, but also spaces where patients’ uncertainties and unmet informational needs become visible. For a disease 
such as MG, which is characterized by symptom fluctuation, diagnostic delay, and long-term management needs, 
it is important to examine whether online health information adequately covers early recognition, diagnostic 
evaluation, and evidence-based management.
Short-video platforms have become important entry points for health-information seeking in China. Compared 
with traditional text-based websites or long-form video platforms, short videos are visually intuitive, rapidly dis-
seminated, narrative-driven, and shaped by algorithmic recommendation. These features may expand the reach of 
health education, but may also increase the visibility of emotionally appealing, experience-based, or highly inter-
active content. Previous studies across different diseases and platforms have reported incomplete information, 
insufficient source disclosure, and variable quality in online health videos 15–18. In the field of MG, Meral et al. 
evaluated the educational value and content quality of MG-related videos on YouTube, providing important evi-
dence on English-language MG video content 19. However, Chinese short-video platforms differ from YouTube 
in language environment, user demographics, content-production patterns, recommendation mechanisms, and 
healthcare communication context. Douyin is one of the leading short-video platforms in China and is character-
ized by broad user reach and algorithm-driven dissemination. Red Note, also known as Xiaohongshu, combines 
social networking, experience sharing, and lifestyle-oriented content, where patients and caregivers may express 
illness experiences and healthcare-seeking narratives through note-style posts and videos. To date, little is known 
about how MG-related information is presented on major Chinese short-video platforms or how content quality 
relates to audience engagement.
Therefore, this cross-sectional study systematically assessed the content and presentation characteristics of 
MG-related short videos on Douyin and Red Note. Specifically, we evaluated the distribution of videos across 
platforms and uploader identities; the coverage of themes including symptoms, diagnostic evaluation, treatment 
and management, prognosis, and daily living; the transparency, overall quality, and general reliability reflected by 
JAMA, GQS, and mDISCERN scores; and the alignment between quality metrics and user engagement indica -
tors. By characterizing these patterns, this study may provide a basis for future MG-specific content validation, 
clinician-patient co-created education, and platform-level strategies for improving the visibility, presentation, and 
accessibility of reliable MG-related educational content.
Methods
Study design
This was a cross-sectional content analysis of publicly available myasthenia gravis (MG)-related short videos on 
Douyin and Red Note. The study was designed to evaluate the presentation characteristics, thematic coverage, 
structural quality, general reliability, and user engagement of MG-related content on two major Chinese short-
video platforms. The unit of analysis was an individual video20–23.

Search strategy and data collection
Video searches were conducted on August 31, 2025, in Tianjin, China, using a Matebook D14 [with Intel(R) 
Core(TM) i5-10210U CPU @ 1.60 GHz (2.11 GHz)] and the Microsoft Edge browser (Version 140.0.3485.81). 
To minimize the influence of personalized recommendations, browser cache, cookies, and search history were 
cleared before the searches. A newly registered Red Note account linked to an unused phone number was used for 
the search on Red Note, whereas Douyin videos were accessed without logging into a personal account because 
public viewing was available.
The keyword “重症肌无力” (myasthenia gravis) was entered into each platform. The top 100 videos returned 
by the default platform ranking were collected from Douyin and Red Note, respectively. This strategy was used 
to approximate the videos that ordinary users would be most likely to encounter when actively searching for MG-
related information within these platforms15,24.
Videos were excluded if they were: (1) purely advertisements; (2) unrelated to MG; (3) not in Mandarin Chi -
nese; or (4) duplicated on the same platform. A total of 200 videos were initially identified. Among them, 26 were 
excluded, including 2 purely advertising videos, 17 videos unrelated to MG, 3 videos not in Mandarin Chinese, 
and 4 duplicate videos. Finally, 174 videos were included in the analysis, comprising 98 Douyin videos and 76 
Red Note videos. The video screening and selection process is shown in Fig. 1.
Data extraction
For each included video, the following information was extracted: platform, video link, video duration, uploader 
identity, communication format, background environment, visual information delivery, subtitle status, content 
themes, therapeutic interventions mentioned, and user engagement indicators. Video duration was recorded and 
analyzed in seconds.
User engagement indicators included the numbers of likes, comments, saves, and shares recorded on the date 
of data collection. These indicators were analyzed as raw platform interaction counts.
Uploader and content classification
Uploaders were classified according to their account identity and identifiable professional background. Medically 
affiliated accounts included hospitals and public health organizations, physicians, and medical students. Non-
medically affiliated accounts included other public service organizations, science communicators, and patients or 
their families. Detailed classification criteria are provided in Supplementary Table 2.
Video presentation characteristics were classified according to communication format, background environ -
ment, visual information delivery, and subtitle status. Communication format was categorized as physician mono-
logue, ward round, outpatient consultation, or other format. Background environment was categorized as hospital 
background, virtual background, or other background. Visual information delivery was categorized as animated 
graphics, static schematics, or text-only presentation. Subtitle status was recorded as subtitles present or absent. 
Detailed classification criteria are provided in Supplementary Table 3.
MG-related content themes were coded according to eight core topics: (1) etiology and pathophysiology; (2) 
disease examination methods; (3) diagnostic criteria and methods; (4) differential diagnosis; (5) treatment and 
management; (6) disease prognosis; (7) clinical symptoms and presentation; and (8) daily living status and qual-
ity of life. The category of clinical symptoms and presentation included MG-related symptoms explained by 
medical professionals as well as symptom descriptions based on patient or caregiver experience. Content themes 
were coded as non-mutually exclusive multilabel variables; therefore, a single video could involve multiple 
themes, such as clinical symptoms, diagnostic evaluation, and treatment and management.

Therapeutic interventions mentioned in the videos were also recorded, including acetylcholinesterase inhibi -
tors, corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, biologic or targeted therapies, thymectomy, thymic radiotherapy, 
rehabilitation therapy, herbal decoctions, and acupuncture or moxibustion.
Quality and reliability assessment
Three commonly used instruments for evaluating online health information were applied to the included videos: 
the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) Benchmark criteria, the Global Quality Score (GQS), 
and the modified DISCERN instrument (mDISCERN).
The JAMA Benchmark criteria were used to assess transparency and basic credibility of online health informa-
tion across four domains: authorship, attribution, disclosure, and currency. Each item was scored as 1 for “yes” 
and 0 for “no,” giving a total score ranging from 0 to 4, with higher scores indicating greater transparency.
The GQS was used to assess the overall informational quality, flow, and potential usefulness of the videos for 
patient education. The GQS is scored on a 1–5 scale, with higher scores indicating better overall informational 
quality and usefulness.
The mDISCERN instrument was used to assess the general reliability and balance of health information. It 
included five binary items, assessing whether the video was clear, concise, and understandable; whether valid 
sources were cited; whether the content was presented in a balanced and unbiased manner; whether additional 
sources of information were listed for patient reference; and whether areas of uncertainty were mentioned. Each 
item was scored as 1 for “yes” and 0 for “no”, giving a total score ranging from 1 to 5, with higher scores indicat-
ing greater general reliability.
These instruments were used to assess transparency, overall informational quality, and general reliability. They 
were not designed to directly determine the clinical accuracy of individual MG-related statements, assess guide-
line concordance, identify MG-specific misinformation, or evaluate viewer knowledge acquisition, health-seek -
ing behavior, or clinical outcomes. Detailed scoring criteria are provided in Supplementary Tables 4–7.
Evaluation process and inter-rater agreement
Two licensed physicians with clinical experience in MG independently evaluated all included videos. Before 
formal assessment, the two evaluators received standardized training on the JAMA, GQS, and mDISCERN scor-
ing criteria and completed a calibration exercise using 10 sample videos to ensure consistent interpretation of the 
scoring standards25–28.
During formal evaluation, each evaluator independently recorded the JAMA, GQS, and mDISCERN scores 
for each video. When discrepancies occurred, the two evaluators first discussed the source of disagreement. If 
consensus could not be reached, a third senior evaluator reviewed the video and made the final decision accord -
ing to the predefined scoring criteria. Inter-rater agreement before consensus was assessed using Cohen’s kappa 
coefficient.
Statistical analysis
Categorical variables were summarized as numbers and percentages. Because video duration and user engage -
ment indicators were skewed and the score variables were ordinal or non-normally distributed, continuous vari -
ables were summarized as medians and interquartile ranges.
Comparisons of continuous variables between two independent groups were performed using the Mann-Whit-
ney U test. For mutually exclusive grouping variables, such as platform and uploader identity, comparisons 
of continuous variables across multiple groups were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis test, with adjustment 
for multiple comparisons when appropriate. For non-mutually exclusive content-theme variables, descriptive 
summaries were provided, and comparisons across content categories were interpreted cautiously. Categorical

variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Spearman correlation analysis was used

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 27.0.1.
Ethical considerations
This study analyzed only publicly available videos from Douyin and Red Note. No non-public user information 
was accessed, and the researchers did not interact with video uploaders or viewers. All data were analyzed in 
aggregate, and no personally identifiable information was collected. According to institutional policy, formal eth-
ics approval and individual informed consent were not required.
Results
Video characteristics
As shown in Table 1, a total of 174 videos were analyzed. The median (IQR) values for video duration, likes, 
comments, saves, and shares were 84.0 (57.0–131.5), 92.5 (21.3–319.8), 12.5 (2.0–48.8), 29.5 (8.0–130.8), and 
21.5 (7.0–106.8), respectively. In terms of uploader identity, physicians contributed the largest proportion of vid-
eos (74.1%, 129/174), followed by patients and their families (12.6%, 22/174), science communicators (4.6%, 
8/174), other public service organizations (4.0%, 7/174), hospitals and public health organizations (3.4%, 6/174), 
and medical students (1.1%, 2/174).
Because content themes were coded as non-mutually exclusive variables, a single video could be assigned to 
more than one content category. Treatment and management was the most frequently addressed topic (47.1%, 
82/174), followed by clinical symptoms and presentation (40.2%, 70/174), patients’ daily living status and quality 
of life (21.8%, 38/174), etiology and pathophysiology (16.7%, 29/174), differential diagnosis (16.7%, 29/174), 
disease prognosis (15.5%, 27/174), disease examination methods (10.3%, 18/174), while diagnostic criteria and 
methods were addressed less frequently (8.0%, 14/174). The median scores for JAMA, GQS, and mDISCERN 
were 3.0 (3.0–3.0), 3.0 (3.0–4.0), and 2.0 (2.0–3.0), respectively.
Furthermore, we compared video characteristics between the two platforms. Inter-rater agreement between 
the two evaluators was excellent, with kappa coefficients of 0.889 for GQS, 0.843 for mDISCERN, and 0.968 
for JAMA. As shown in Table 2, video duration did not differ significantly between Red Note and Douyin. Dou-
yin videos had significantly higher raw engagement counts than Red Note videos in terms of likes, comments, 
saves, and shares (all P < 0.001). For assessment scores, JAMA, GQS, and mDISCERN differed significantly 
between platforms (P < 0.001, P = 0.025, and P = 0.012, respectively). Douyin videos showed a higher distribu -
tion of JAMA scores than Red Note videos, although both platforms had a median JAMA score of 3.0. The two 
platforms also had the same median GQS and mDISCERN scores, despite statistically significant differences in 
their overall score distributions (Fig. 2).
To gain deeper insights, we analyzed video characteristics according to uploader identity (Table 3). Hospitals 
and public health organizations received the highest median numbers of likes, saves, and shares (181.0, 90.5, 
and 82.0, respectively), while videos uploaded by patients and their families received the highest median num -
ber of comments (91.5). Medical students produced the longest videos (median duration: 929.0 s), although this 
subgroup included only two videos. Further comparisons of assessment scores showed significant differences 
in JAMA and GQS scores across uploader groups, but not in mDISCERN scores. Physicians and hospitals and 
public health organizations had the highest JAMA scores (3.0 [3.0–3.0]). Regarding GQS scores, hospitals and 
public health organizations achieved the highest ratings (4.0 [3.3–4.0]). In contrast, mDISCERN scores did not

Discussion
MG, a complex autoimmune disorder, presents growing diagnostic and therapeutic challenges in an aging popu-
lation29—a concern particularly relevant in China, which is undergoing rapid demographic aging. Within China’s 
distinctive healthcare landscape, MG management is further complicated by an uneven distribution of special -
ized medical resources and regional disparities in diagnostic capacity8. Older adults often experience symptoms 
that overlap with age-related physiological decline, which can be easily misattributed to normal aging, thereby 
delaying diagnosis9. Meanwhile, the ongoing digital transformation in health communication has opened new 
pathways for patient education and disease management. Short-video platforms are increasingly serving as a 
vital bridge between professional expertise and public health literacy, especially in regions with limited access 
to conventional health communication channels. According to the latest data from the China Internet Network 
Information Center (CNNIC), as of June 2025, China had approximately 161 million Internet users aged 60 and 
above, representing an Internet penetration rate of about 52% in this age group 30. Older adults are also increas -
ingly turning to digital devices to seek health-related information 31. This study provides a systematic analysis 
of MG-related short-video content on Chinese platforms. In response to the opportunities and heightened need 
for reliable health content, this research aims to characterize how MG is represented online and to establish an 
evidence-based foundation for enhancing digital health education. Overall, MG-related videos showed moderate 
informational quality and relatively favorable transparency, but limited general reliability, with comparatively 
infrequent coverage of diagnostic criteria and examination methods. Notably, quality assessment scores and user

Overall video evaluation
Our analysis showed that MG-related short videos on Chinese platforms demonstrated generally acceptable 
informational quality but with important weaknesses in reliability. Across the 174 included videos, the median 
GQS, JAMA, and mDISCERN scores were 3.0 (IQR, 3.0–4.0), 3.0 (IQR, 3.0–3.0), and 2.0 (IQR, 2.0–3.0), 
respectively, indicating moderate overall informational quality, relatively favorable transparency, and limited 
general reliability. This pattern of limited general reliability is consistent with prior evaluations of medical con -
tent on open video platforms, where clinically themed videos often appear authoritative yet omit key elements 
such as evidence citation, adverse-effect warnings, and criteria for urgent medical review 27. Importantly, the 
medically affiliated group accounted for 78.7% (137/174) of our sample, and physicians were the most prominent 
content source within this group (94.2%, 129/137). However, professional affiliation was not associated with sig-
nificant differences in mDISCERN scores. This suggests that clinical expertise does not automatically translate 
into comprehensive patient education in the short video format. These findings support the need for structured

communication standards for MG-related short videos—not only to improve clarity and perceived quality, but 
also to ensure that safety-critical information is delivered in a balanced and reproducible way. From a public 
health perspective, insufficiently reliable information may increase the risk of misunderstanding disease manage-
ment and delay appropriate care-seeking behaviors, particularly in populations with limited access to specialized 
neurological services.
The Spearman correlation analysis demonstrated that the three quality evaluation tools—GQS, mDISCERN, 
and JAMA—showed weak to moderate intercorrelations ( r = 0.08–0.43), indicating that they capture distinct 
dimensions of video quality and reliability rather than overlapping constructs. Among audience-interaction indi-
cators, the strongest associations were observed between likes, comments, saves, and shares ( r = 0.53–0.92), 
reflecting coherent user behavior patterns in engagement. However, correlations between assessment scores and 
user engagement indicators ranged from − 0.15 to 0.37. For example, mDISCERN and JAMA scores—which 
emphasize general reliability and transparency, respectively—showed little to no correlation with engagement 
variables, whereas GQS exhibited only modest positive associations (likes r = 0.31, saves r = 0.37, and shares 
r = 0.35). These findings indicate that content popularity should not be regarded as a proxy for better informa -
tional quality or general reliability.
This decoupling between information quality and audience engagement may stem from the inherently special-
ized nature of MG. As a rare autoimmune neuromuscular disease, its clinical terminology, symptom heteroge -
neity, and complex management strategies pose barriers to lay understanding. High-quality educational videos 
often prioritize completeness and accuracy, but this technical density can reduce accessibility for general viewers, 
given the generally limited health literacy among the public 32,33—a phenomenon observed in other conditions 
with similar diagnostic complexity, such as thyroid eye disease34. Moreover, MG’s fluctuating clinical course and 
chronic fatigue symptoms may drive patient and caregiver interest toward experiential or coping-related content, 
leading to engagement preferences not aligned with formal quality metrics. Overall, these findings highlight the 
need to improve medical communication design rather than solely pursuing content accuracy. Similar discrepan-
cies between information quality and audience engagement have been reported in studies of health-related con -
tent on platforms such as YouTube and TikTok, suggesting that this challenge may be common across different 
digital health ecosystems35,36.
Our analysis revealed significant heterogeneity in video quality and engagement outcomes depending on 
uploader identity. Among the six subgroups analyzed, significant differences were observed in JAMA and GQS 
scores (P < 0.001 and P = 0.008, respectively), but not in mDISCERN scores ( P = 0.091). Physicians and hospi -
tals/public health organizations had the highest median JAMA scores (3.0 [3.0–3.0]), whereas hospitals/public 
health organizations had the highest median GQS score (4.0 [3.3–4.0]). These findings are consistent with pre -
vious evaluations on TikTok, Kwai, and Bilibili, in which institutional or physician-produced content achieved 
higher transparency or overall quality scores 25,37,38. Overall differences across uploader groups were also sig -
nificant for likes, comments, saves, and shares (all P < 0.01). Hospitals and public health organizations had the 
highest median numbers of likes, saves, and shares, whereas videos posted by patients and their families had 
the highest median number of comments. The particularly high comment engagement observed for patient- and 
family-posted videos may reflect the conversational value of lived experience and experiential narratives. How -
ever, greater comment engagement should not be interpreted as evidence of higher informational quality, clinical 
accuracy, or educational effectiveness.
Video content
Thematic analysis revealed that treatment and management dominated MG-related video content (47.1%), fol -
lowed by clinical symptoms and presentation (40.2%) and patients’ daily living status and quality of life (21.8%). 
In contrast, diagnostic criteria and methods (8.0%) and disease examination methods (10.3%) were among the 
least frequently addressed topics. This pattern suggests a creator emphasis on symptom control and therapeu -
tic options—areas of immediate concern to patients and caregivers—at the expense of early recognition and

diagnostic guidance. While this focus aligns with the demand for actionable knowledge, the limited coverage of 
diagnostic criteria and examination methods may constrain the usefulness of short videos as an entry point for 
early recognition and appropriate referral. Such delays are well-documented: in one international survey 30.9% 
of patients waited three years or more for accurate diagnosis 39. Another retrospective analysis also indicated 
that, due to the diverse manifestations of MG, it is often misdiagnosed as other diseases40. Enhancing messaging 
around early diagnosis—particularly through clear descriptions of hallmark fluctuating symptoms such as fati -
gable ptosis, dysarthria, and dysphagia 41,42—could raise disease awareness and facilitate timely referral to neu -
rology specialists. From a public health perspective, improving awareness of early diagnostic features through 
accessible digital content may support earlier symptom recognition and timely referral to neurological services.
Given the prominence of treatment and management content in the MG-related short-video landscape, we 
further analyzed the specific therapeutic approaches represented. Our findings show that immunosuppressants 
(19.5%), acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (17.2%), and thymectomy (16.7%) were the most frequently discussed 
interventions. This distribution reflects a balanced emphasis on both pharmacological and surgical strategies, 
which aligns with established clinical practices in China43. In these practices, pharmacotherapy forms the corner-
stone of long-term management, primarily through the use of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors and corticosteroids, 
while thymectomy is considered a key disease-modifying procedure—particularly in patients with acetylcholine 
receptor antibody-positive generalized MG 44. The notable emphasis on thymectomy in the videos may also be 
influenced by the substantial proportion of content created by hospitals and physicians, who often highlight 
standard interventional treatments available in tertiary care settings. In contrast, novel biologic and targeted 
therapies—including complement inhibitors (eculizumab, ravulizumab) and neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) antago-
nists (e.g., efgartigimod)—were scarcely represented (2.3%). This gap likely reflects their limited availability, 
significant cost, and relatively recent introduction in China 45–47. These findings indicate that recent advances in 
MG immunotherapy were less frequently represented in the sampled videos.
Interestingly, our analysis revealed a notable presence of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) in MG-related 
short videos, with herbal decoctions appearing in 13.8% and acupuncture or moxibustion in 3.4% of all included 
videos. This reflects the ongoing integration of TCM perspectives into digital patient education—a trend consis-
tent with national policies advocating the incorporation of digital technologies into the preservation and innova-
tion of TCM48. The potential of these TCM modalities is supported by growing research 49. Herbal formulas are 
noted for their multi-component and multi-target nature, with reported mechanisms relevant to MG including 
modulation of the gut microbiota and regulation of immune-inflammatory responses50–52. Additionally, the anti-
inflammatory role of acupuncture in autoimmune conditions is well-established53; a recent meta-analysis further 
found that adjunctive acupuncture was associated with improved treatment response and reductions in MG-
related clinical scores, although the quality of the available evidence remains limited54. These findings provide a 
scientific context for the presence of TCM-related content on Chinese short-video platforms; however, the pres-
ent study did not assess the clinical accuracy, evidentiary basis, or therapeutic value of individual TCM-related 
statements in the included videos.
Limitations and future directions
Several limitations should be considered. First, this was a cross-sectional content analysis conducted at a single 
time point. Because short-video platforms are highly dynamic, video ranking, visibility, and engagement may 
change with platform policies, recommendation algorithms, and trending topics. The findings therefore reflect 
MG-related videos available on the search date rather than long-term trends.
Second, only Douyin and Red Note were included. The findings may not be generalizable to other Chinese or 
international platforms, such as Bilibili, WeChat Channels, Weibo, and YouTube. Although browser cache, cook-
ies, and search history were cleared and a newly registered Red Note account was used, search rankings may still 
have been influenced by geographic location, account status, platform algorithms, and real-time popularity. The 
retrieved videos may therefore differ from those encountered by individual users.

Third, user engagement indicators in this study, including likes, comments, saves, and shares, were analyzed 
as raw platform interaction counts. We were unable to obtain information on video views, account verification 
status, recommendation exposure, or platform-specific interaction mechanisms. Therefore, engagement metrics 
could not be fully standardized and should not be used to compare reach-adjusted dissemination performance 
across platforms. Higher engagement may reflect user interest, emotional resonance, experiential value, or plat -
form recommendation mechanisms, rather than higher informational quality, better educational effectiveness, or 
greater clinical value.
Fourth, the JAMA Benchmark criteria, GQS, and mDISCERN assess information transparency, overall qual -
ity, and general reliability, but do not directly evaluate the clinical accuracy, guideline concordance, MG-spe -
cific completeness, or misinformation contained in individual statements. Accordingly, higher scores cannot be 
equated with clinical accuracy, and lower scores do not necessarily indicate incorrect or harmful information. 
Future studies should incorporate specialist review and MG-specific, guideline-based assessment criteria.
Fifth, viewer-level outcomes were not evaluated. Platform interactions do not indicate whether viewers 
improved their knowledge, recognized warning signs, changed care-seeking behavior, or adhered more closely 
to treatment. Surveys, experimental viewing studies, and pre-post assessments are needed to examine the educa-
tional and behavioral effects of MG-related short videos.
Sixth, subgroup findings should be interpreted cautiously because some uploader categories contained few 
videos and group sizes were imbalanced. In addition, content themes were non-mutually exclusive; therefore, 
theme-related results represent descriptive comparisons of overlapping content features rather than comparisons 
among independent groups.
Future research could further develop MG-specific evaluation frameworks for online health information. Such 
frameworks should cover early symptom recognition, diagnostic pathways, examination methods, differential 
diagnosis, crisis warning signs, long-term treatment, and self-management. In addition, expert review by neurolo-
gists, neuroimmunologists, or neuromuscular specialists should be incorporated to complement the limitations 
of JAMA, GQS, and mDISCERN in assessing clinical accuracy and guideline concordance. Future MG-related 
health communication could also explore clinician-patient co-created short videos that combine professional 
medical knowledge, patient experience, and accessible communication. At the platform level, future efforts could 
explore verified labels, topic collections, or expert-reviewed content recommendation mechanisms to improve 
the visibility, comprehensibility, and accessibility of reliable MG-related educational content. Longitudinal stud-
ies are also needed to examine how content quality, thematic coverage, and user engagement change over time.
Conclusion
This cross-sectional content analysis evaluated the information presentation, thematic coverage, structural qual -
ity, general reliability, and user engagement of MG-related short videos on Douyin and Red Note. MG-related 
content on Chinese short-video platforms generally showed moderate overall informational quality and relatively 
favorable transparency, but limited general reliability. Treatment and management and clinical symptoms and 
presentation were the most frequently addressed topics, whereas diagnostic criteria and methods and disease 
examination methods were less frequently represented. Quality assessment scores were not consistently aligned 
with user engagement, indicating that higher engagement should not be regarded as a proxy for better infor -
mational quality. Because JAMA, GQS, and mDISCERN do not directly assess clinical accuracy, guideline 
concordance, or educational impact, these findings should be interpreted as a characterization of information pre-
sentation, thematic coverage, structural quality, general reliability, and user engagement rather than as evidence 
of clinical correctness or viewer benefit. Future studies should incorporate MG-specific expert validation and 
explore clinician-patient co-created educational content to improve the visibility, accessibility, and comprehensi-
bility of clinically reviewed MG-related information on short-video platforms.
