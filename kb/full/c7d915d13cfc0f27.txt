Anthocyanins are important phenolic compounds responsible for the antioxidant capacity and characteristic purple color of açaí. Despite their nutritional and economic importance, environmental factors influencing their accumulation in fruits remain insufficiently understood. This study evaluated the relationship between edaphic conditions and anthocyanin concentration in açaí fruits collected from different regions of Pará State, Brazil, including mainland municipalities, riverine islands, and the Marajó Archipelago. Anthocyanins were extracted under controlled light conditions and quantified by ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometry. Soil parameters, including pH, organic matter content, and electrical conductivity, were determined using standardized protocols. Statistical evaluation included analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s test, principal component analysis, and multiple linear regression. Anthocyanin concentrations ranged from 43.97 ± 20.34 to 294.48 ± 9.70 µg mL-1, showing significant differences among regions. Multivariate analysis indicated that soil pH, organic matter, and electrical conductivity were the main variables structuring the environmental variability of the dataset. Regression analysis suggested that these parameters collectively explain part of the observed variation in anthocyanin concentration. Overall, the results demonstrate that edaphic conditions influence anthocyanin accumulation indirectly by modulating plant physiological responses associated with environmental stress, contributing to understanding environmental determinants of açaí chemical composition and supporting sustainable valorization of Amazonian bioresources.
