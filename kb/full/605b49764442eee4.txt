The mucilage of forage cactus, a biomaterial derived from cacti such as forage cactus, particularly in the Brazilian semi-arid region, faces obstacles to its use in industry. In a global context that values sustainable chemistry and the reuse of waste, mucilage stands out as a hydrocolloid with multiple applications. Owing to its intrinsic characteristics, such as high viscosity, elasticity and water-holding capacity, it is suitable for various industrial applications. Although the physiological adaptations of forage cactus ensure high productivity even under water-stress conditions, genetic variability and environmental conditions directly affect the quality of the polymer produced, the mucilage. Despite many studies demonstrating its potential use, obstacles not yet addressed hinder its implementation in industrial processes and products. Thus, this study aims to describe the physicochemical properties of mucilage and to highlight the critical need to validate its storage in order to enable its commercial scaling-up.
