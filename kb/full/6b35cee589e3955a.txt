Ammonia-rich agricultural wastewaters challenge reliable biological treatment because treatment
failure can leave reactive nitrogen in the dissolved phase. Duckweed-based systems offer a low-
energy approach for polishing nutrient-rich wastewaters, but their stability under ammonia stress
remains poorly resolved. Here, we integrated physiological, physicochemical, microbiome, nitrogen-
cycling gene, and 15N-tracing analyses with bacterial – microalgal intervention to examine treatment
responses in a duckweed-based swine digestate system. Duckweed responses showed a threshold-
like transition, with an apparent pH – TAN operational breakpoint corresponding to a calculated FA
concentration of approximately 8.5 mg NH₃-N/L under the tested conditions. Beyond this operational
breakpoint, duckweed performance and the associated O₂/pH conditions declined, bacterial diversity
and community composition shifted, and selected nitrogen-cycling genes decreased in DNA-level
abundance. High-FA conditions were associated with slower NH ₄⁺-N removal, reduced NO ₃⁻-N
formation, and redistribution of tracer N from plant- and microbial-associated pools toward the
residual dissolved pool. The combined bacterial-microalgal intervention produced the strongest
partial recovery of treatment performance and biological nitrogen retention, although microalgal
effects cannot be separated from the associated aeration. These ﬁndings identify a condition-speciﬁc
pH– TAN operational breakpoint and highlight pH, ammonia speciation, and aeration as important co-
determining factors in ammonia-rich wastewater treatment.
Anaerobic digestion is widely used for swine manure management because
it reduces organic load and enables energy recovery, but the liquid digestate
after digestion still contains high concentrations of ammoniacal nitrogen
and other residual pollutants, requiring further polishing before discharge or
reuse. Duckweed-based treatment has therefore attracted increasing
attention as a low-energy post-tre atment strategy because duckweeds
combine rapid vegetative propagation, high nutrient uptake capacity, and
the ability to convert wastewater-derived nutrients into harvestable biomass.
Previous studies have demonstrated that duckweed can effectively recover
nitrogen and phosphorus from swine wastewater and anaerobically treated
swine ef ﬂuent, supporting its potential as a nature-based platform for
nutrient recovery and polishing. However, for clean-water applications, the
key challenge is not only nutrient removal ef ﬁciency, but also whether
treatment remains stable enough to prevent residual reactive nitrogen from
persisting in the treated water. Treatment performance remains highly
variable across digestate types and operating conditions, indicating that the
underlying stressors and system constraints are not yet fully resolved1–3.

In this study, we used a duckweed-based swine digestate polishing
system to examine treatment stability and nitrogen fate across an
experimentally imposed pH–TAN regime in which calculated FA covaried
with pH. We ﬁrst identiﬁed an apparent pH–TAN operational breakpoint
and then compared predeﬁned low-FA and high-FA conditions to char-
acterize associated changes in duckweed growth and physiology, bulk-water
physicochemical conditions, bacterial community diversity and composi-
tion, selected nitrogen-cycling gene abundance, and ¹⁵N-resolved nitrogen
partitioning. We further tested whether a pre-constructed SynCom,
Chlorella sp., or their combination could p artially restore treatment per-
formance under the high-FA operatingcondition. We hypothesized that the
transition across the pH–TAN regime would be associated with coordinated
changes in duckweed physiological performance, bulk-water physico-
chemical conditions, bacterial community composition, selected nitrogen-
cycling gene abundance, and nitrogen partitioning, whereas the interven-
tion treatments would partially shift these responses toward those observed
under the low-FA condition. By integrating threshold analysis, host phy-
siological responses, microbiome ecology, functional gene quantiﬁcation,
isotope-based nitrogen partitioning, and path-based construct analysis, this
work aims to move beyond conventional nutrient-removal reporting and
establish an operational framework for improving the reliability of
duckweed-based treatment of ammon ia-rich wastewater. Although the
present study was conducted in swine digestate, the framework may also be
relevant to other high-FA wastewaters in which treatment performance
depends on the coupling among host stress, microbiome stability, and
nitrogen transformation. Understanding how FA regulates treatment sta-
bility and nitrogen fate is therefore critical for advancing duckweed-based
technologies toward reliableclean-water applications.

High free ammonia destabilizes treatment-associated micro-
biome organization
High free ammonia markedly shifted the duckweed-associated bacterial
community toward a less diverse and less densely colonized state. Under H-
FA, the Shannon index declined from 4.17 to 3.46, and Chao1 richness
decreased from 258 to 198 (Fig.4a, b). In the PCoA, L-FA and H-FA samples
formed two clearly separated clusters with no overlap among replicates, and
the ﬁrst two axes explained 42.3% and 18.7% of the community variation,
respectively (Fig.4c). The clear separation indicates that high FA generated a
distinct microbiome state rather t han a weakened version of the L-FA
community. In plant-associated micr obiomes, comparable stress-driven
divergence has been reported under drought and salt stress, where altered
host traits and niche conditions drive selective enrichment of a new set of
root-associated bacteria rather than uniform taxon loss22–24.

Bacterial– microalgal bioaugmentation improves treatment resi-
lience under H-FA
Functional bacterial and microalgae-c ontaining interventions partially
improved the HN-impaired duckweed treatment system, with the com-
bined HN + B + M treatment consistently showing the strongest recovery
under the present operating conﬁguration. Under HN, duckweed biomass
remained at only 0.15 g DW/reactor, whereas HN + Ba n dH N + M
increased it to 0.19 g DW/reactor, and HN+ B + M further restored it to
0.23 g DW/reactor (Fig. 6a). A similar trend was observed for photo-
synthetic performance and oxidative stress, with Fv/Fm rising from 0.67
under HN to 0.79 in HN + B + M and MDA declining from 11.7 to
5.9 nmol/g FW (Fig. 6b, c). In parallel, bulk-water DO increased, and pH
shifted downward toward the LN range in the algal treatments (Fig. 6d).
Because the Chlorella-containing reactors were gently aerated, these phy-
sicochemical improvements should be interpreted as the combined out-
come of algal presence and the associated aerated operating condition rather
than as a microalgae-only effect. These changes indicate that the interven-
tions did not simply stimulate stressed fronds; they rebuilt the physico-
chemical envelope in which duckweed and its associated microbiota
interact. In duckweed, host beneﬁt can emerge from interactions among
microbiome members rather than from single strains in isolation, making
community reassembly itself a functional trait of the rescue process32,33.

Taken together, the results can be summarized as three experimentally
observed response patterns: a low-F A reference condition, an H-FA-

tracer N remained in the dissolved phase. HN was associated with impaired
duckweed physiology, altered bulk-water DO and pH, bacterial community
restructuring, lower abundance of several selected nitrogen-cycling genes,
and greater persistence of tracer N in the residual dissolved pool. The
intervention treatments partially shifted several of these endpoints toward
the LN pattern. This synthesis integrates independently measured host,
physicochemical, microbial, and nitrogen-partitioning responses without
assigning a statistically demonstrated causal order among them. Previous

microbial communities and that plant-, algal-, and bacterial-mediated

an ecological basis for considering these responses together 25,32,b u tt h e

present experiment.
From an engineering and clean-water treatment perspective, the
apparent pH –TAN operational breakpoint identi ﬁed here provides a
practical indicator of declining duckweed-system performance under
comparable digestate conditions. Maintaining the system below the
apparent destabilization range through approaches such as pH manage-
ment, dilution, staged loading, or upstream ammonia recovery may help
preserve duckweed performance and limit the accumulation of residual
dissolved nitrogen. This operational interpretation is consistent with the
broader sensitivity of ammonia-richbiological treatment processes to the
FA fraction, particularly where nitrifying microorganisms are involved27.
When elevated-FA episodes occur, biological intervention may provide an
additional stabilization option, although the individual contributions of
bacterial inoculation, microalgae, and aeration require further separation.
Accordingly, the main practical implication of this study is not that FA alone
determines treatment outcome, but that the combined pH–TAN operating
regime, together with calculated FA as an ammonia-speciation descriptor,
can provide useful warning informatio n for declining biological perfor-
mance and altered nitrogen partitioning in ammonia-rich digestate
polishing.
Several limitations de ﬁne the scope of this interpretation. First,
calculated FA covaried strongly with pH across the experimental
gradient; therefore, the observe d transition is referred to as an
apparent pH –TAN operational breakpoint rather than a pH-
independent FA toxicity threshold . The corresponding calculated
FA concentration should be interpr eted only as the location of this
operational transition along th e experimentally imposed ammonia-
speciation gradient. Factorial expe riments independently varying
TAN and pH will be required to distinguish their individual and
interactive effects. Second, bulk-water DO and pH characterize
reactor-scale aqueous conditions and were not direct measurements
of physicochemical gradients at the duckweed root surface. Root-
scale microsensor measurements will therefore be necessary to
determine whether and how local O 2 and pH conditions contribute
to the observed host and microbial responses. Third, because
Chlorella -containing treatments were gently aerated whereas non-
algal treatments were manually mixed, the present experiment cannot
fully separate microalgal effects from aeration-associated changes in
DO, pH, ammonia volatilization, or nitrogen transformation. Future
experiments should include aerate d non-algal controls and identical
mixing regimes across tre atments. Finally, the ¹ ⁵N experiment
directly quanti ﬁed tracer N only in the plant-associated, microbial-
associated, and residual dissolved pools. Because gaseous ¹ ⁵N species
were not measured, the isotope-based interpretation was restricted to
treatment differences among these three measured compartments
and does not resolve speci ﬁc gaseous or other unmeasured nitrogen-
loss pathways.

Methods
Digestate collection and pretreatment
Solid-liquid separated swine digestate efﬂuent was collected from a com-
mercial pig farm in Yantai, Shandong Province, China. The manure treat-
ment process at the farm consisted of pig house ﬂushing collection,
mechanical solid-liquid separation, anaerobic digestion, and digestate sto-
rage. The liquid fraction after anaerobic digestion was used in this study.
Solid-liquid separation was conducted using a screw-press separator (LX-
180, China). Digestate samples were collected from the outlet storage tank,
transported to the laboratory in sterile polyethylene containers within 4 h,
and stored at 4 °C. All experiments were initiated within 48 h of sampling.
Upon arrival, the digestate was allowed to settle for 24 h at room
temperature, and the supernatant was passed through a 100-mesh nylon
ﬁlter to remove residual particles. The pH was adjusted to 7.0 ± 0.1 with
1 mol/L HCl or NaOH before use. For sterile treatments, the pretreated
digestate was autoclaved at 121 °C for 20 min, followed by sterility ver-
iﬁcation on nutrient agar plates.
The raw digestate was characterized prior to preparation of the
experiment-speciﬁc working media. The measured characteristics of the
raw digestate were pH 7.84 ± 0.06, EC 6.72 ± 0.18 mS/cm, NH 4

Because total duckweed-associat ed bacterial abundance differed
among treatments, normalization to bacterial 16S rRNA gene copy number
was used to express the abundance of each target nitrogen-cycling gene
relative to the size of the bacterial DNA pool in the same sample. This
normalization was selected speciﬁcally to reduce the inﬂuence of treatment-
associated differences in total bacte rial abundance and to distinguish
changes in the relative representation of functional genes within the bac-
terial community from changes driv en simply by differences in overall
bacterial biomass. It was therefore used for a study-speciﬁca n a l y t i c a lp u r -
pose rather than solely because 16S rRNA gene normalization is commonly
applied in the literature. Target-gene abundances are reported as copies per
10⁶ bacterial 16S rRNA gene copies, with the target gene and 16S rRNA gene
quantiﬁed from the same DNA extract. Because bacterial taxa differ in 16S
rRNA operon copy number, these normalized values are interpreted as
relative representation within the bacterial DNA pool rather than exact cell-
normalized abundances. Furthermore, because the measurements were
DNA-based rather than RNA-based, they represent DNA-level genetic
potential and should not be interpret ed as transcriptional activity or
process rates.

Statistical analysis
All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates, and results
are presented as mean ± standard deviation unless otherwise stated. One-
way ANOVA followed by Tukey ’s post hoc test was used to compare
treatment means at individual time points. Two-way ANOVA was used for
repeated measurements involving treatment and time.
Threshold responses in Experiment I were evaluated using one-
breakpoint segmented linear regression, with the initial calculated FA
concentration of each gradient treatment used as the predictor variable. For

pH across the experimental gradient, the estimated breakpoint was inter-
preted as a condition-speciﬁc FA-associated transition rather than a pH-

Microbial beta diversity was analyzed by Bray–Curtis-based PCoA and
tested using PERMANOVA, and LEfSe was used to identify discriminative
taxa. All remaining conventional statistical analyses were conducted in SPSS
25.0 or R as speci ﬁed above. Differences were considered signi ﬁcant
at P < 0.05.
