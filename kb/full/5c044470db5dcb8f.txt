The Middle East is one of the most water-stressed regions globally, facing acute and structurally linked resource pressures across the water, energy, and food (WEF) nexus. This study employs a cross-case approach based on systematic secondary data synthesis and sector-specific SWOT analyses of eight countries — Egypt, Iraq, Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Palestine, Saudi Arabia, and Syria — to Investigate country’s current resource strengths, structural resource complementarities, and propose conceptual cross-border trade scenarios illustrated through a conceptual map. The analysis identifies a set of cooperation pairing clusters grounded in documented resource asymmetries: Jordan's growing renewable electricity capacity paired with Gulf desalinated water and food supply chain infrastructure resources; Egypt's agricultural and gas export base linked to regional energy finance; and the Levant countries' agricultural diversity and freshwater resources connected to the energy-importing sub-region. These complementarities are synthesized into a cooperative WEF-Nexus trade framework supported by a proposed regional governance institution, the WEF-Nexus Cooperation Forum (WEF-NCF). The paper argues that, when grounded in inclusive institutional design and cognizant of political feasibility conditions, WEF resource cooperation can contribute to sustainable interdependence and long-term regional stability. The study's limitations as the geopolitical, finance, investment and infrastructure constraints on implementation are explicitly acknowledged.
