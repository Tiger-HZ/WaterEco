Life Cycle Assessment analysis is used to evaluate the environmental impact of Refuse-Derived Fuel (RDF) production in MSW management systems, preceded by a comparative review study of WtE treatment technologies based on international publications reviewed over the past decade. This study aims to identify the most strategic WtE potential by integrating environmental performance, ecological impacts, and economic calculations. Comparative analysis confirms RDF as a technically feasible and environmentally competitive option when optimized in an integrated waste management system. Meanwhile, LCA was conducted using the ReCiPe Midpoint (I) method through characterization, normalization, and monetization based on environmental price factors adjusted to the local economic context. The results of the study show that the impacts of fossil resource scarcity, terrestrial acidification, and global warming potential dominate the environmental burden in RDF production, which is mainly triggered by transportation activities and processing stages using fossil fuel-based mechanical machines, with a GWP impact contribution of 927kg CO2 eq for the production of 1 ton of RDF/day. The comparison results show that RDF has superior performance compared to incineration, pyrolysis, and landfill-based energy recovery, both in terms of economic feasibility and environmental sustainability when system efficiency is optimized. By combining environmental, economic, and technological dimensions into a single analytical framework, this study offers a new perspective on the application of LCA and confirms the role of RDF as a strategic pathway in supporting energy transition and reducing dependence on fossil fuels in urban waste management.
