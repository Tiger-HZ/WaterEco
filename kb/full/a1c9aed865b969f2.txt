Abstract Objectives/Scope Objectives/Scope: The Gulf of Suez (GOS) is a mature oil-producing region characterized by a tilted fault block system with high range of dip angles. The region's reservoirs, ranging from Miocene to Pre-Miocene, are stacked, often containing multiple pay zones within single wellbores. Historically, the production strategy in the GOS has involved completing one zone until depletion or being water-flooded, followed by recompletion in other zones. After decades of production, many wells have been shut-in due to high water cuts. However, reopening these wells after extended shut-in periods offers a unique opportunity to recover additional reserves.
