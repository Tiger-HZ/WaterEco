This paper presents a study of Soːrɑːŋɡ Sompeŋɡ a indigenous orthography as a tool for cultural sovereignty of the Savara tribe in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha. It is also called by different names like ‘Madi Brahma’, 'Mattar Banoom' and Akṣara Brahma'. Its acts as the cultural guardian of the Savara language through spiritual invocation is the primary goal. It also narrates how they struggle against the external missionary conversion forces. This research highlights the work of Savara Pandita Mangayya Gomango. He presented this script in between 1935-36 as a sacred alphabet. This alphabet of 25 letters engraved on a heart-shaped stone found at Mirchuguda, Orissa. In this he intrinsically attached a specific deity or festival. This script by imbuing with a cultural flavour got attraction of the uneducated Savara people. This study demonstrates how this ritualistic approach has attracted the Savara people to remain rooted in their ancestral tongue, effectively discouraging a shift toward dominant regional or global languages. Drawing on field observations from the Savara regions across the Andhra-Odisha border, the paper analyses how the continued practice of this script in daily and ceremonial life functions as a form of "Cultural Guarding." It concludes that the Soːrɑːŋɡ Sompeŋɡ script is a primary factor in the ongoing resilience of the Savara culture, serving as a successful model of indigenous resistance and linguistic preservation through the fusion of scriptural identity and religious practice.
