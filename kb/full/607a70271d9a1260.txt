The assessment of career transitions among vocational college students in China has long relied upon episodic outcome indicators such as employment status, salary, and study–job match. For a population whose outcomes are substantially conditioned by credential-based labour market discrimination and whose post-graduation destinations are highly heterogeneous, such an evaluative approach conflates educational quality with structural constraint and fails to capture the quality of the transition itself. This conceptual study, situated within the context of Chinese higher vocational education, develops an assessment framework that is both theoretically grounded and oriented towards evaluative practice.
