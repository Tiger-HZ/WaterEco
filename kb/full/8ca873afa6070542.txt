Freshwater mussels (Elliptio complanata) were transplanted into the Sudbury River, Massachusetts, to evaluate the bioavailability of total Hg and methyl mercury (MeHg) and the potential impacts to resident species. The principal Hg source is the Nyanza Superfund site, a former textile dye production facility. Mussels (initial tissue concentrations = 640 ng Hg·g dry weight -1 and 120 ng MeHg·g dry weight -1 ) were transplanted to eight locations in the Sudbury River watershed for 12 weeks. Tissue total Hg concentration increased significantly in mussels at the station closest to the Nyanza site (950 ng Hg·g dry weight -1 ).
