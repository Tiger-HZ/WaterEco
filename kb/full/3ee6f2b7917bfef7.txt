Soil quality in mining-affected regions is often altered due to anthropogenic disturbances. The present study evaluates the physicochemical characteristics and nutrient status of soils surrounding the Ketaki Coal Mine (KCM) in the Ketki region of Surajpur District, Chhattisgarh, India. Soil samples collected at different radial distances (2–6 km) and depths (15 and 30 cm) were analyzed for pH, electrical conductivity, organic carbon, macronutrients (N, P, K, S), and micronutrients (Zn, B, Fe, Mn, Cu). Multivariate statistical techniques including correlation analysis, principal component analysis (PCA), and analysis of variance (ANOVA) were employed to examine spatial variability.
