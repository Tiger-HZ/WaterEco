Under major conservation policies like the Yangtze River Protection Initiative, understanding the
shifting balance between climate drivers and anthropogenic activities as drivers of biodiversity is
critical. Yet, their relative impacts following the Yangtze River Protection Initiative, a catchment-scale
ecological conservation and green-development strategy ﬁrst proposed in 2016, remain poorly
quantiﬁed. Here, we investigated planktonic and benthic biodiversity patterns along the 2800 km
Yangtze River following China ’s largest river protection initiative. Comprehensive sampling of
phytoplankton, zooplankton, and macrozoobenthos was conducted at 117 sites, revealing distinct
longitudinal zonation patterns delineated by the Three Gorges Dam and the Datong tidal limit, with
species turnover dominating community spatial heterogeneity. Our analyses identi ﬁed climatic
factors, speciﬁcally air temperature, precipitation, and potential evapotranspiration (PET) during the
sampling period, as key predictors of aquatic biodiversity across all three taxonomic groups,
collectively outweighing the measured anthropogenic indicators. Machine-learning and mediation
analyses revealed that climatic factors were associated with biodiversity through both direct paths and
indirect pathways mediated by water quality, whereas the measured anthropogenic effects were
dominated by direct pathways. Notably, the Yangtze River Protection Initiative signi ﬁcantly improved
water quality and mitigated anthropogenic pressures, thus amplifying the relative importance of
climate factors. Our ﬁndings highlight the critical role of climate in shaping river ecosystems at
macroscale under the protection initiative and offer valuable insights for adaptive management
strategies aimed at conserving freshwater biodiversity and enhancing ecosystem resilience in large
rivers facing global environmental change.
Riverine ecosystems, global biodiversity hotspots, play pivotal roles in bio-
geochemical cycling, energyﬂow, and ecosystem service provision1– 3.T h e s e
dynamic systems support disproportionately high biodiversity relative to
their spatial extent while functioni ng as critical corridors for material
transport between terrestrial and marine environments 4,5. The Yangtze
River, China’s largest and the world’s third-largest river with a basin area of
approximately 1.8 million km2, serves fundamental functions in clean water
provision, biodiversity maintenance6,7, and economic development8,c o n -
stituting a critical ecological safeguard and genetic reservoir for China 9.
Aquatic communities, including phytoplankton, zooplankton, and mac-
rozoobenthos, are fundamental to riv er ecosystems. Their distribution,
community composition, and diversity respond sensitively to

Macrozoobenthos function as consumers and detritivores, contributing to

Recent global climate change and intensiﬁed anthropogenic activities
have accelerated transformations in aquatic ecosystems24,25, fundamentally
inﬂuencing species composition and distribution patterns across large river
basins26– 28. Climate warming has signiﬁcantly modiﬁed thermal conditions,
with growing evidence indicating that river temperatures are rising
globally22. These changes have affected phytoplankton community structure
and primary productivity29, triggering cascading effects throughout riverine
food webs 30,31. Simultaneously, anthropogenic disturbances including
hydrological alterations from dam construction10,32, habitat fragmentation
from channelization, deforestation and catchment degradation 33,34,a n d
water quality deterioration from industrial, urban, and agricultural runoff
have compounded pressures on aquatic biota 35, threatening ecosystem
stability and resilience10,36. Over the past decades, the Yangtze River and its
catchment have faced severe challenges, including water quality
deterioration9, sharp biodiversity decline 6,37, and ecological function
degradation38. The construction of the Three Gorges Dam, while providing
ﬂood control and hydropower bene ﬁts, has profoundly impacted down-
stream ecosystems through altered ﬂow regimes, modi ﬁed sediment
transport, and created novel selection pressures on aquatic
communities39– 41.
To enhance the ecological health of the Yangtze River, China has
launched one of the largest and highest-investment basin-scale ecological
protection initiatives globally42,43. Its policy foundation can be traced to the
2016 strategic principle of “Prioritizing Protection Over Large-Scale
Development”, and the initiative has been further implemented since
2018. Major measures include strengthening industrial and domestic was-
tewater treatment; protecting river habitats by regulating sand mining,
strictly controlling riverfront utilization, and demolition and relocation of
riverside chemical enterprises; addressing navigation-related pollution; and
optimizing reservoir and dam operations to maintain ecological ﬂows44,45.
These measures have effectively controlled industrial and municipal point-
source pollution. Meanwhile, diffuse runoff has also been effectively reduced
through strengthened catchment an d riparian management, including
reduced fertilizer and pesticide use, the construction of ecological ditches
and ponds, riparian reforestation or revegetation, the establishment of
vegetated buffer strips, and improved management of livestock and poultry
manure46,47. During the implementation period of the Yangtze River Pro-
tection Initiative, national monito ring records indicated remarkable
improvements in surface water quality45,48: sections in the Yangtze River
Basin meeting Class I– III standards (suitable for drinking water sources and
ﬁsheries) increased from 87.5% in 2018 to 98.9% in 2025, a relative
improvement of 13.0%, while Class V (lowest quality) sections decreased by
77.8%, from 1.8% to 0.4%, over the same period. Furthermore, a ten-year
ﬁshing ban has been implemented since 1 January 2021 in the Yangtze
River, seven major tributaries, and two connected lakes, making it one of the
largest and most in ﬂuential inland water ecological protection measures
globally, aimed at promoting the recovery of aquatic biological resources6,42.
The Yangtze River Protection Law of the People’sR e p u b l i co fC h i n ac a m e
into effect on 1 March 2021, providing legal safeguards for these measures
and institutionalizing the principles of ecological priority and green
development49.
Previous studies have documented aquatic biodiversity and environ-
mental conditions before and during the implementation of this initiative,
covering ﬁsh, phytoplankton, zooplankton, macrozoobenthos, and micro-
bial communities 11,37,42,50. Molecular monitoring approaches, such as
environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding, have also been used to assess
biodiversity ranging from microorganisms toﬁsh and other vertebrates50– 52.
However, these studies have often focused on speciﬁc taxonomic groups,
regions, or stressors, such as ﬁsh assemblages, selected river sections, or
changes in water quality and habitat11,53. Systematic surveys from 2017 to
2021, together with monitoring at sites along the Yangtze River from 2018 to
2023, revealed preliminary recovery trends inﬁsh species richness, biomass,
individual condition, and some threa tened species following the imple-
mentation of theﬁshing ban37,42. Despite these advances, large-scale, multi-
site comparisons that integrate mult iple aquatic taxa and evaluate the
relative effects of climate change and human pressures remain limited.
Recent evidence indicates that humanactivities in the Yangtze River Basin
have changed substantially in recent years, including strengthened man-
agement of chemical enterprises and riverfront utilization within 1 km of
the river54, water quality improvement44,45, regulation or removal of some
small hydropower facilities 55, and reduced navigation and bank dis-
turbances in certain river sections. These changes are supported by multiple
lines of evidence, including long-term monitoring data11,42,p o l i c yi m p l e -
mentation records44, and remote sensing analyses54. Nevertheless, anthro-
pogenic pressures remain, particul arly those associated with land-use
change, population concentration, and intensi ﬁed socioeconomic
activity54,56– 58, yet the relative importance of these broad anthropogenic
inﬂuences compared with climatic factors and their associations with dif-
ferent aquatic taxa remain insufﬁciently assessed at large spatial scales.
In this study, we conducted a comprehensive investigation of aquatic
biodiversity along the entire Yangtze River from upstream to the estuary,
integrating analyses across three ke yb i o l o g i c a lg r o u ps: phytoplankton,
zooplankton, and macrozoobenthos . We hypothesized that under the
Yangtze River Protection Initiative , climatic factors have superseded
anthropogenic activities in shapi ng aquatic biodiversity patterns. This
hypothesis rests on two fundamental premises: ﬁrst, with point-source
pollution effectively controlled and diffuse runoff substantially reduced
through strengthened catchment and riparian management, water quality

machine learning approaches to identify dominant drivers, and (3) eluci-

Divergent longitudinal diversity patterns across
taxonomic groups
Despite pronounced regional differentiation in species composition, the
spatial patterns of diversity indices varied substantially among taxonomic
groups. Building upon the established s pecies-level differentiation, long-
itudinal trends in alpha diversity varied markedly across taxonomic groups
(Fig. 2). Phytoplankton Shannon diversity index decreased signi ﬁcantly
from 1.92 in Region I to 1.39 in Region III ( P < 0.05), though Margalef
richness indices remained statistically invariant across regions, suggesting
that while species evenness declin ed, overall taxonomic richness was
maintained (Fig. 2a-c). In contrast, zooplankton diversity and richness
increased signiﬁcantly from 0.76 in Region I to 1.85 in Region II (P <0 . 0 5 )
before stabilizing in Region III (Fig. 2d-f). Macrozoobenthos diversity
showed a non-monotonic longitudinal pattern, decreasing ﬁrst and then
recovering downstream. Region B, the transitional reach immediately
upstream of the Three Gorges Dam, had the lowest Shannon index (0.285),
and differences among the three regions were signiﬁcant (P < 0.05; Fig.2g-i).
This differentiation pattern was statistically con ﬁrmed by MANOVA of
diversity indices (S, H, J): only zooplankton ( R2 = 0.450, P < 0.01) and
macrozoobenthos ( R2 = 0.109, P < 0.05) exhibited signi ﬁcant regional
differentiation, while phytoplankton diversity metrics remained statistically
invariant (R2 = 0.008, P > 0.05), indicating species turnover without sub-
stantial shifts in overall diversity levels.
To disentangle the roles of spatial structure and environmental gra-
dients in shaping these biodiversity patterns, we conducted spatial analyses.
PCNM-based variance partitioning showed that spatially structured
environmental variation explained 10.7% and 24.4% of the variation in
phytoplankton and zooplankton, respectively, whereas this shared fraction
was much smaller for macrozoobenthos (1.1%; Fig. S12a). Pure spatial
effects explained 3.9%, 6.8%, and 17.5% of the variation in phytoplankton,
zooplankton, and macrozoobenthos, respectively, indicating that spatial
structure was most pronounced for macrozoobenthos.
To examine whether directionalﬂow connectivity affected the spatial
interpretation, we compared AEM wi th the original PCNM framework.
When spatial variables were considered alone, AEM explained slightly more
variation in zooplankton communities than PCNM, with adjustedR2 values
of 32.7% and 31.2%, respectively. For phytoplankton and macrozoobenthos,
however, AEM explained less variation than PCNM, with adjustedR2 values
of 11.1% compared with 14.6% for phy toplankton and 12.6% compared
with 18.6% for macrozoobenthos. A si milar pattern was observed after
controlling for water-quality variables. For zooplankton, the pure spatial
fraction estimated by AEM remained higher than that estimated by PCNM,
increasing from 6.8% under the PCNM framework to 11.5% under the AEM
framework. This suggests that downstream directional connectivity may be
particularly relevant to zooplankton community organization. In contrast,
PCNM retained larger pure spatial fractions for phytoplankton and mac-
rozoobenthos, indicating that their spatial patterns were better represented
by broader distance-based spatial structure than by ﬂow direction alone
(Fig. S13).
Mantel tests identi ﬁed six environmental variables with signi ﬁcant
spatial autocorrelation (r =0 . 1 0– 0.58, all P < 0.01), particularly water tem-
perature (r = 0.58) and dissolved oxygen ( r = 0.51), which vary system-
atically along the river’s longitudinal gradient (Fig. S12b). Partial Mantel
tests, controlling for all environmental variables, showed that pure spatial
effects decreased by 5– 17% relative to full Mantel correlations (Fig. S12c),
indicating that while pure spatial effects persist independently of measured
environmental variables, their magnitude is modest compared to spatially
structured environmental variation identi ﬁed in variance partitioning.
Distance-decay analysis further demonstrated that community similarity
for phytoplankton and zooplankton showed weak or negative relationships

Together, these results suggest that longitudinal biodiversity patterns were
shaped primarily by environmental heterogeneity along the river con-
tinuum, with additional taxon-speciﬁc spatial effects.
Community differentiation driven by species turnover rather than
nestedness
Multivariate analysis revealed taxonomically differentiated spatial clustering
patterns. Despite signiﬁcant species turnover detected by PERMANOVA
(R2 = 0.067, P < 0.001), phytoplankton PCoA ordination showed gradual
convergence in community similarity from Region I to Region III (Fig.3a),
reﬂecting species replacement without drastic compositional shifts. Zoo-
plankton PCoA analysis (Fig.3b) demonstrated progressive convergence in
community composition from Region II to Region III, though both
remained distinctly separated from Region I communities. Macro-
zoobenthos PCoA analysis revealed a non-linear pattern in community
similarity (Fig. 3c), with assemblages ﬁrst converging between Regions A
and B before diverging again in Region C.

Threshold effects and machine learning validation of climate
dominance
To further validate the relative importance of key drivers and identify
potential non-linear relationships, we employed machine-learning
approaches. SHAP analysis indicated that aquatic biodiversity patterns
were driven mainly by natural factors, such as topography, water quality,
and climate, while anthropogenic factors played a relatively minor role
(Fig.5). Visualization of SHAP value distributions showed that climatic and
topographic variables were the most inﬂuential predictors across taxa, while
the effects of the HAI and land-use indicators were comparatively weak.
Phytoplankton diversity was markedly more sensitive to land-use change
than zooplankton and benthic commu nities, suggesting taxon-speci ﬁc
response characteristics to human disturbance. Notably, climatic and
topographic variables, particularly air temperature, elevation, and PET,
consistently ranked among the top predictors. With the exception of zoo-
plankton, for which HAI ranked sixth, HAI did not enter the top 10 pre-
dictors for either phytoplankton o r macrozoobenthos. The cumulative
contribution of the top three climate variables exceeded that of anthro-
pogenic factors, with ratios ranging from 3.0 to 34.9:1.
Beyond ranking variable importance, SHAP analysis further revealed
non-linear relationships between climatic factors and aquatic biodiversity
(Figs. S16, S17). Air temperature exhibited pronounced threshold effects,
with inﬂection points at 27.0 °C for phytoplankton diversity, 29.0 °C for
zooplankton diversity, and 26.6 °C for benthic diversity. These thresholds
delineated an optimal range that promoted community development,
whereas SHAP values became negative once the critical thresholds were
exceeded. PET also exhibited nonlinear relationships, with inﬂection points
at 158.9 mm for phytoplankton, 159.1 mm for zooplankton, and 144.0 mm
for macrozoobenthos. Precipitation also exhibited threshold-dependent
effects, with inﬂection points at 188.6 mm for zooplankton diversity and
255.2 mm for macrozoobenthos diversity, while the non-linear effect of
precipitation on phytoplankton diversity (280.8 mm) was comparatively
weaker.

Discussion
Overall, our results support the hypothesis that, under the Yangtze River
Protection Initiative, climatic factors have become more important than
anthropogenic activities in structuring aquatic biodiversity patterns in the
Yangtze River. This conclusion is supported by variance partitioning and
machine-learning analyses, which consistently showed that climatic and
related natural variables explained more variation than anthropogenic
indicators across the three biological groups. However, the strength of
support differed among taxa, being strongest for zooplankton and relatively
weaker for phytoplankton and macrozoobenthos. Thus, climate has
emerged as the dominant driver under improved water-quality conditions,
although anthropogenic activities still exert non-negligible effects and may
interact with climatic gradients.

Our results support the hypothesis that under the Yangtze River
Protection Initiative, climate factors have emerged as stronger drivers of
aquatic biodiversity than anthropogenic activities. Phytoplankton and

Cyanophyta in Region III shows high sensitivity to temperature changes,

their reliance on local habitats such as substrate types and riparian vegeta-

to climate ﬂuctuations81. Meanwhile, climate warming alters food web
structure and energy ﬂow by affecting interactions between primary pro-
ducers and consumers31,82. In warm conditions, accelerated phytoplankton
growth provides abundant food resources for zooplankton, although
increased predation pressure may simultaneously decrease phytoplankton
diversity, triggering cascading ecological effects83,84.
The Three Gorges Dam has had profound impacts on aquatic biota by
affecting downstream drift and upstream migration of fauna, trapping
sediment and pollutants from upstream,and altering seasonal hydrological
dynamics. Our spatial analyses further clarify how environmental hetero-
geneity, rather than geographic distance alone, structures these biodiversity
patterns. Contrary to expectations thatlarge rivers exhibit strong distance-
decay relationships due to longitudinal isolation, we found that spatially
structured environmental gradientsare the dominant driver of community
turnover in the Yangtze River. The strong spatial autocorrelation in water
temperature (r = 0.58) and dissolved oxygen ( r = 0.51) reﬂects the river’s
natural upstream-downstream gradient, creating environmental hetero-
geneity that structures plankton communities more strongly than distance-
based dispersal limitation. The weak o r negative distance-decay patterns
(mean Pearson r < 0.03 for plankton) and the modest reduction in spatial
correlations after controlling for environment (5– 17% decrease in Mantelr)
further support this interpretation, though signiﬁcant residual spatial effects
indicate that dispersal-related processes also contribute independently to
community structure. This ﬁnding has important implications for con-
servation: management strategies should prioritize maintaining environ-
mental gradients rather than solely focusing on spatial connectivity.
Under the Yangtze River Protection Initiative, anthropogenic activities
have relatively weak impacts on aquatic biodiversity, explaining approxi-
mately 12% of variation across biological communities. Climate has become
the dominant driving factor, possibly bene ﬁting from the Yangtze River

Our results indicate that climate-related drivers can outweigh residual
anthropogenic impacts once severe pollution pressures have been sub-
stantially alleviated. A comparable ecological transition has been docu-
mented in the heavily managed Rhine River, where long-term pollution
abatement and ecological rehabilitation were associated with partial
recovery of ﬁsh and macroinvertebrate assemblages, while increasing
temperature and precipitation, toge ther with biological invasions, con-
tributed to long-term ﬁsh-community reorganization87,88. However, the
Yangtze River case is unique in the scale and rapid implementation of its
protection initiative. Unlike the incremental improvements observed else-
where, the “all-out-protection” approach in the Yangtze provides a clearer
basin-wide context in which climate-related signals may become more
apparent after severe anthropogenic pressures have been reduced. Although
anthropogenic activities continue to exert direct pressures, the portion of
their effects mediated through water-quality degradation appears to have
weakened under the current protection initiative.
At the catchment scale, gridded potential evapotranspiration (PET) is
interpreted as an indicator of atmospheric evaporative demand and coupled
regional water– energy conditions. Higher PET indicates a greater potential
for water loss under non-water-limited conditions, but does not directly
quantify actual catchmen t evapotranspiration89. When precipitation is
limited relative to atmospheric evaporative demand, variation in PET can
alter catchment water partitioning and the amount of water available for
runoff and baseﬂow contribution90,91. Associated changes in river discharge
and ﬂow velocity may modify hydraulicresidence time and the transport,
retention, dilution, and concentration of nutrients and dissolved substances,
thereby inﬂuencing plankton communities through changes in resource
availability and downstream transport or retention 92. Macrozoobenthos
may respond to associated changes in stream ﬂow regimes, water depth,
near-bed hydraulic conditions, and habitat stability93. Because actual eva-
potranspiration, discharge, baseﬂow contribution, and hydraulic residence
time were not directly measured, these processes remain hypothesized
hydro-ecological pathways rather than demonstrated causal mechanisms.

Methods
Study area
The Yangtze River mainstem (28°42 ′N-32°19′N, 104°37 ′E-122°30′E)
extends approximately 2800 km from Yibin to its estuary,ﬂowing through
eight provinces and municipalities65. The river is divided into three sections:
upstream from Yibin to Three Gorges Dam in Yichang spanning 1030 km,
traversing mountainous gorges with signi ﬁcant elevation changes, mid-
stream from Yichang to Hukou County in Jiujiang covering 955 km,
including the Jingjiang meander and water-sediment regulation interfaces
with Dongting and Poyang Lakes, anddownstream from Hukou to estuary
stretching 938 km, where the channel gradually widens, forming a complex
delta deposition system inﬂuenced by tides. The region has a subtropical
monsoon climate with annual precipitation of 1100– 1400 mm, over 60%
occurring from May to September, creating distinctﬂood-drought hydro-
logical patterns.
Sampling

Yangtze River from Yibin to the estu ary, covering Sichuan (7 sites),
Chongqing (24 sites), Hubei (29 sites), Hunan (5 sites), Jiangxi (6 sites),
Anhui (19 sites), and Jiangsu (27 sites) (Fig.1a). June– July represents a stable
wet season window with suitable water quality for observation. Site selection
considered riverfront utilization typ es, hydrological characteristics, and
anthropogenic activities gradients, including land-use change, population
concentration, and intensiﬁed socioeconomic activity.
At each site, one accessible bank was selected for biological sampling.

Environmental variables and data collection
Variable classiﬁcation. To explicitly disentangle the speciﬁc contributions of
distinct drivers, we classi ﬁed predictor variables into three hierarchical
categories: (1) Spatial factors: geographic distance matrices and PCNM
eigenvectors representing pure spatial structure independent of environ-
mental conditions; (2) Environmental factors: encompassing in-situ water
quality (14 parameters, see below), to pography (elevation, slope), and
regional climate (precipitation, air temperature, PET). The regional climate
variables were extracted from 1-km resolution raster datasets according to
the geographic coordinates of each sampling site. We checked the extracted
site-level climate data and found no identical climate-variable combinations
among sampling sites; therefore, no averaging, merging, interpolation, or
removal of repeated climate records wasrequired. These variables represent
contemporary climatic conditions rather than temporal climate change
processes. While our space-for-time substitution approach provides insights
into how aquatic communities respond to climate gradients 116,117,c a u s a l
inference regarding long-term climate change impacts requires temporal
monitoring data; (3) Anthropogenic factors: land-use change, population
concentration, and intensiﬁed socioeconomic activity, quanti ﬁed by the
Human Activity Intensity (HAI) index, which integrates land use, popu-
lation density, and nighttime light intensity to characterize anthropogenic
pressure.

The study employed two buffer calculation methods: climate factors
used complete 3 km radius buffer data around sampling points, while
topographic factors, land use types, andanthropogenic activities indicators
used only the terrestrial portion of the3 km buffer, excluding water areas, to
accurately reﬂect land environment impacts on water quality. This 3 km
buffer width was determined based on the integration of river-lake con-
nectivity, biodiversity response thresholds, and nutrient transport dynam-
ics. This scale effectively covers majorecologically sensitive zones and aligns
with methodologies commonly used in recent large-scale river studies71,81,
balancing conservation coverage with land use analysis. All data were uni-
formly resampled to 30 m resolution to ensure spatial analysis consistency.

Statistical analysis
Community structure analysis. Community zonation was determined using
a hierarchical validation approach. Initial zones were deﬁned by two major
hydrological nodes (Three Gorges Dam at Site 39 and tidal limit at Site 78).
For phytoplankton and zooplankton, this framework was validated via
MANOVA (P < 0.001 for both groups) and retained. For macrozoobenthos,
which showed no signiﬁcant zonation under this framework (MANOVA
P > 0.05), we employed exhaustive search optimization to identify the
conﬁguration maximizing joint signi ﬁcance across Margalef, Shannon-
Wiener, and Pielou indices. This organism-speciﬁc approach is supported
by freshwater metacommunity theory , which predicts that sedentary
benthic taxa respond to different envi ronmental thresholds than pelagic
organisms due to their limited dispe rsal capacity and direct substrate
dependence122,123.
All data analyses were conducted in R 4.4.3. One-way ANOVA and
MANOVA were employed to assess signi ﬁcant differences in α diversity
indices among the three river sections (P < 0.05). Community composition
differences were evaluated through principal coordinate analysis (PCoA)
based on Bray – Curtis dissimilarity matrices calculated from community
abundance data using the vegan package, with PERMANOVA and ANO-
SIM tests (999 permutations) used to verify the signi ﬁcance of regional
differentiation. Sequential Segmentation Clustering determined optimal
river section divisions that maximizedcommunity diversity differentiation.
Network analyses visualized relatio nships between phytoplankton, zoo-
plankton, and macrozoobenthos using Gephi 0.10.1, including only
signiﬁcant correlations (P < 0.05). Node effects were assessed by comparing
environmental parameters (Mann-Whitney U) and community differ-
entiation (PERMANOVA) across Dam/tidal-separated regions, testing
whether nodes coincide with ecologi cal thresholds rather than gradual
gradients.
Spatial versus environmental effects. To explicitly test whether biodi-
versity patterns are driven by spatial structure or environmental hetero-
geneity, we conducted complementary analyses: (1) Variance partitioning
using Principal Coordinates of Neighbor Matrices (PCNM)124 to separate
pure environmental effects [a], spatially structured environmental variation
[b], pure spatial effects [c], and residua l variation [d]. Spatial predictors
comprised the ﬁrst ﬁve PCNM eigenvectors with positive spatial auto-
correlation, while environmental predictors included the 14 in-situ water
quality parameters listed above. Analysis used the varpart function in
vegan125. To test whether the spatial results were sensitive to the choice of
spatial eigenfunction method, we additionally performed an asymmetric
eigenvector map (AEM) analysis126. AEM eigenvectors were constructed
according to the upstream-to-downstream order of sampling sites to
represent directional spatial connectivity along the Yangtze River. The
explanatory power of AEM was compared with that of PCNM using the
same variance partitioning framework and the same set of water-quality
covariates. (2) Mantel tests (999 permutations) identiﬁed which environ-
mental variables exhibited signiﬁcant spatial autocorrelation by correlating
each variable with the geographic distance matrix. (3) Partial Mantel tests
isolated pure spatial effects by controlling for all environmental variables
simultaneously, quantifying the pro portion of spatial signal mediated
through environment. (4) Distance-decay analysis partitioned all pairwise
site comparisons (n = 6786 pairs) into 10 equal-width geographic distance
classes, calculating Pearson correlations between geographic distance and
community dissimilarity (Bray-Curtis) for each class (≥10 pairs), with sig-
niﬁcance assessed via two-tailed t-tests.
Environment-biodiversity relationships. Prior to multivariate analyses
involving environmental variables, environmental data were standardized
to remove the effects of different units and scales. Principal component
analysis (PCA, factoextra package) reduced environmental data
dimensionality127. Detrended correspondence analysis (DCA) determined
appropriate response models based on maximum ordination axis length.
Redundancy analysis (RDA, vegan package) was conducted using stan-
dardized environmental variables toassess water environment-community
relationships125. Spearman correlation analysis evaluated relationships
between anthropogenic activities, environment, and biodiversity indices.
Machine learning and mediation analysis. Prior to modeling, the initial
candidate predictor pools consistedof 29 variables for phytoplankton, 29
variables for zooplankton, and 39 variables for macrozoobenthos; after
screening based on correlation analysis and variable redundancy, 16, 18, and
19 taxon-speciﬁc predictors were retained for phytoplankton, zooplankton,
and macrozoobenthos, respectively, and were used for machine-learning
modeling and SHAP interpretation. SHapley Additive exPlanations
(SHAP) analysis quanti ﬁed different factors ’ contributions to aquatic
biodiversity128. Machine learning models we re optimized using Bayesian
hyperparameter tuning with 5-fold cross-validation across eight candidate
algorithms (MLP, GBDT, SVR, CatBoost, Ridge, RF, LightGBM, Lasso).
Model selection was based solely on the lowest mean squared error (MSE).
GBDT was selected for phytoplankton (CV MSE = 0.248) and macro-
zoobenthos (CV MSE = 0.315), while SVR was selected for zooplankton
(CV MSE = 0.439). Complete hyperparameter search spaces are provided in
Table S3. Path analysis constructed a mediation effect model with HAI (X1)
and climate factors (X2) as independent variables, water environmental
parameters (M) as mediator, and biodiversity indices (Y) as a dependent
variable. Bootstrap resampling (1500 re petitions) determined statistical
signiﬁcance of path coef ﬁcients through 95% bias-corrected con ﬁdence
intervals.
