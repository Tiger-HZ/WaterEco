Abstract Anomalously high sea surface temperatures (SSTs) are becoming more frequent under the ongoing ocean warming, but their effects on midlatitude extreme rainfall remain poorly understood. This study aims to clarify how anomalously high North Atlantic SSTs during a prominent marine heatwave in 2023 influenced record-breaking torrential rainfall over Scotland in early October. Using a convection-permitting atmospheric model, we compare a control experiment forced by the observed SST with a sensitivity experiment in which the basin-scale warm SST anomaly in October 2023 has been artificially removed.
