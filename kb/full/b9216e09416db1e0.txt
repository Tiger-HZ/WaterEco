ABSTRACT The protection of urban water‐supply systems against deliberate harm sits at the intersection of two policy debates: how states designate, finance, and protect critical infrastructure, and how the cybersecurity agenda should extend to the operational technology running everyday public services. Water policy nonetheless still regulates utilities through instruments written for accidental failure rather than adaptive adversaries, and this study addresses that mismatch. Focusing on urban water‐supply systems in Iran, it identifies, assesses, and prioritizes the intentional threats against them, emphasizing intentional contamination of water resources, and proposes passive‐defense mitigation.
