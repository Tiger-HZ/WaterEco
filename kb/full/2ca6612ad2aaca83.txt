Fish meal is widely used as a major ingredient and protein source in animal feed production, including that of aquaculture. Fish meal is produced from fish or fish by-products, typically first cooked, pressed to remove oils and water, and then ground into a fine powder. There are sustainability concerns with fishmeal, especially when wild fish stocks are caught indiscriminately to produce it. The price of fish meal continues to rise due to the increased demand in the aquaculture industry. Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify sustainable alternative protein sources for aquafeed formulations. Artemia , a small omnivorous branchiopod crustacean and non-selective filter feeder, has emerged as a promising candidate. Artemia biomass is rich in nutrients, highly palatable, amenable to nutrient enrichment, and suitable for mass production in land-based or hatchery systems using agricultural and aquaculture waste substrates. While Artemia has long been used as live feed in larviculture, its application as a protein source in formulated aquafeeds remains underexplored. To date, only a limited number of studies have investigated the replacement of fish meal with Artemia biomass in species such as white-leg shrimp ( Penaeus vannamei ), giant freshwater prawn ( Macrobrachium rosenbergii ), and tiger prawn ( Penaeus monodon ). These studies have yielded promising results, with improvements observed in survival rate, weight gain, feed conversion ratio, and specific growth rate. However, further research is needed to evaluate the suitability of Artemia biomass across a broader range of aquaculture species and life stages. This chapter provides an overview of the nutritional potential, practical applications, and sustainability prospects of Artemia biomass as an alternative protein source to fish meal. Expanding research in this area will be essential for advancing more resilient and eco-friendlier aquafeed strategies in the future of aquaculture.
