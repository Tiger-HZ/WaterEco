This study analyzes the impacts of water scarcity for human consumption in the indigenous community of La Cruz Pinta, in the municipality of El Fuerte, Sinaloa, Mexico, within a context marked by severe drought, social inequality, and limited water infrastructure. The study begins by recognizing access to drinking water as a fundamental human right and examines the gap between the legal framework and the actual conditions faced by the indigenous population. The methodology employed is qualitative, with an ethnographic and descriptive approach, grounded in grounded theory. Data was collected through in-depth interviews with representative members of the community, as well as through direct observation and a review of documentary sources.
