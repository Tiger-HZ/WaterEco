ABSTRACT Groundwater discharge plays a key role in maintaining habitat suitability for cold‐water fish by generating streamflow and modulating temperatures. However, the thermal influence depends on groundwater source depth and how far groundwater travels from the point of discharge to a receiving stream. In the unglaciated Kenai Peninsula Lowlands (KPL), Alaska, groundwater originates from shallow hillslope groundwater (HGW) or deeper aquifer‐outcrop groundwater (AOGW) sources. We combined 4 years of field observations with a physical model to evaluate the capacity of these groundwater sources to modulate stream temperatures.
