Effect of date of sowing and nutrient management on 
mustard (Brassica juncea L.) 
 
Chetan Sharma, Hansraj Shivran, Vaishali Chaturvedi and LS Dhayal 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/26174693.2026.v10.i9St.10055 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment entitled “Effect of Date of Sowing and Nutrient Management on Mustard ( Brassica 
juncea L.)” conducted during Rabi 2025-26 at Agriculture farm, Suresh Gyan Vihar University, Jaipur 
on sandy loam soil . The treatments in wheat during rabi seasons comprising fifteen treatment 
combinations having main factor (20th October, 30th October and 10th November) and sub plot (control, 
100% RDF, 75% RDF + one nano urea spray at 30 DAS, 50 % RDF + Two nano urea sprays at 30 and 
60 DAS and 75 % RDF + Two nano urea sprays at 30 and 60 DAS). The sowing on 30 th October 
significantly increased seed, stover and biological yield compared to 20th October sowing and remained 
statistically at par with 10 th November, respectively. The maximum net return of ₹74,710 ha-1 was 
recorded with 30 th October sowing, followed by 10 th November sowing (₹70,129 ha-1), whereas the 
minimum net return (₹54,572 ha⁻¹) was recorded with 20th October sowing. The maximum seed, stover 
and biological yield were recorded with application of 75% RDF + two nano urea sprays at 30 and 60 
DAS, followed by 75% RDF + one nano urea spray at 30 DAS, 50% RDF + two nano urea sprays at 30 
and 60 DAS and 100% RDF. The maximum net return (₹75,393 ha⁻¹) was recorded with the 
application of 75% RDF + two nano urea sprays at 30 and 60 DAS, followed by 50% RDF + two nano 
urea sprays (₹70,898 ha⁻¹), 75% RDF + one nano urea spray at 30 DAS (₹69,214 ha⁻¹) and 100% RDF 
(₹63,803 ha-1). 
 
Keywords: Mustard, sowing date, nutrient management, nano urea, yield, economics 
 
Introduction 
Indian mustard ( Brassica juncea L.) is an important oil -yielding crop that belongs to the 
family Brassicaceae, having chromosome number 2n = 36, and is a natural amphidiploid 
derived from an interspecific cross between B. Nigra and B. campestris. Different regions 
have different names for it, including Sarson, Rai, Raya, Toria, and Lahi. Rai or Laha is 
frequently called m ustard, and Sarson and Toria are commonly called rapeseed. The 
occupied area, production, and yield of mustard in India were 10.47 million hectares, 13.2 
million tons, and 1443 kg/ha, respectively, during 2023 -24. DA&FW (Agricultural -
Statistics-at-a-Glance-2023 
Among the different agro nomic practices, optimum sowing time is very important for 
mustard production. Delayed sowing of mustard crop can face unfavorable weather 
conditions at flowering and siliquae formation which shorten maturity period, decreased 
number of siliquae/plant as well as infestation of aphids that finally lead to reduction in seed 
yield. Uniform distribution of plants over an area results in efficient use of nutrients, 
moisture, sun light and suppression of weeds leading to high yield (Patel and Patel 2022) [8]. 
The concept behind integrated nutrient management is Maintaining the fertility of the soil 
and providing vital plant nutrients at the ideal level for the sustained growth of the targeted 
crop and crop yield. Sridhara et al. (2022) [10] found that foliar application of nitrogen 
enhances the economic yield of chickpea. It is used in place of conventional urea and other 
nitrogenous fertilizers for better environment, soil health and farmers profitability. Nano urea 
contains 4% nitrogen by weight in its nano form (Kantwa and Yadav, 2022) [4]. Nano urea is 
a potential component of 4 R nutrient stewardship as it promotes precision and sustainable 
agriculture. It promotes clean and green technology as its industrial production is neither 
energy intensive or resource consuming. Nano urea reduces the requirement of conventional 
urea by 50% or more (Baboo, 2021) [1].

Methods and Materials 
A field experiment entitled “Effect of Date of Sowing and 
Nutrient Management on Mustard ( Brassica juncea L.)” 
conducted during Rabi 2025-26 at Agriculture farm, Suresh 
Gyan Vihar University, Jaipur on sandy loam soil. 
Geographically, the study area is located at 75 o48’84” E 
longitude and 26 o82’47” N latitude and this region falls 
under agro -climatic zone III A (Semi -arid Eastern Plain 
Zone) of Rajasthan. The treatments in wheat during rabi 
seasons comprising fifteen treatment combinations having 
main factor (20 th October, 30th October and 10 th November) 
and s ub plot (control, 100 % RDF, 75 % RDF + one nano 
urea spray at 30 DAS, 50 % RDF + Two nano urea sprays at 
30 and 60 DAS and 75% RDF + Two nano urea sprays at 30 
and 60 DAS) treatments was laid out in a Spilt plot design 
with three replications. Mustard was sown at 45 cm row 
spacing using seed rate of 05 kg ha-1. 
 
Results and Discussion 
Date of sowing 
The data presented in the table indicate that seed, stover and 
biological yield of mustard were significantly influenced by 
date of sowing. Among the different dates, 30 th October 
sowing recorded the maximum seed yield (1477 kg ha-1), 
stover yield (4490 kg ha-1) and biological yield (5967 kg ha-
1), followed by 10th November sowing, while the minimum 
values were recorded with 20 th October sowing. The higher 
yield under 30th October sowing may be attributed to better 
synchronization of crop growth and reproductive stages with 
favourable temperature and radiation conditions, resulting in 
greater dry -matter accumulation and improved yield 
attributes such as siliquae plant⁻¹ and seeds siliqua⁻¹. Recent 
studies have confirmed that optimum sowing time enhances 
growth, yield attributes and biomass production in mustard. 
The present results are also supported by Keerthi et al . 
(2025) [5] and Meena et al . (2026) [7]. The maximum net 
return (₹74,710 ha-1) and B:C ratio (1.96) were recorded 
with 30 th October sowing, followed by 10 th November 
sowing (₹70,129 ha-1 and 1.84), whereas the minimum net 
return (₹54,572 ha-1) and B:C ratio (1.43) were obtained 
with 20 th October sowing. Thus, 30 th October sowing 
resulted in 36.90 and 6.53% higher net return and 36.79 and 
6.50% higher B:C ratio than 20 th October and 10 th 
November sowing, respectively. The higher profitability 
under 30th October sowing was mainly due to its superior 
seed and stover y ields, which increased gross returns 
without a proportionate increase in cultivation cost. The 
present findings are supported by Singh and Singh (2020) [9] 
and Chaudhary et al. (2026) [2]. 
 
Effect of nitrogen management 
The maximum seed yield (1483 kg ha-1), stover yield (4564 
kg ha-1) and biological yield (6047 kg ha-1) were recorded 
with 75% RDF + two nano-urea sprays at 30 and 60 DAS, 
followed by 50% RDF + two nano-urea sprays, 75% RDF + 
one nano -urea spray and 100% RDF, while the minimum 
values were recorded under control. The higher productivity 
under 75% RDF + two nano-urea sprays may be attributed 
to better synchronization of nitrogen supply with crop 
demand, particularly during active vegetative and 
reproductive stages. The resulting increase in br anches, 
siliquae plant⁻¹ and seeds siliqua⁻¹ ultimately contributed to 
greater seed yield, while enhanced vegetative growth 
increased stover and total biological yield. The findings are 
supported by Kumar et al . (2025) [6] and Thakur et al . 
(2026) [11]. 
The maximum net return (₹75,393 ha-1) and B:C ratio (1.93) 
were obtained with 75% RDF + two nano-urea sprays at 30 
and 60 DAS, followed by 50% RDF + two nano-urea sprays 
and 75% RDF + one nano -urea spray. Similarly, the B:C 
ratio was 29.59% higher than control and 24.25, 20.82 and 
10.85% higher than 100% RDF, 75% RDF + one spray and 
50% RDF + two sprays, respectively. The higher 
profitability was primarily due to greater seed and stover 
yields obtained with the integrated treatment, while the 
partial rep lacement of conventional N with nano -urea 
helped maintain input costs. The present findings are 
supported by Tripathi et al . (2025) [12] and Thakur et al . 
(2026) [11].
