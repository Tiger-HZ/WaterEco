Access to safe drinking water is essential for public health, yet evidence integrating household drinking water practices, source-specific expenditure patterns, and consumer perceptions in Saudi Arabia remains limited. This cross-sectional survey examined drinking water sources, consumption practices, expenditures, and perceptions among a non-probability convenience sample of 265 adults residing in the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia. Comparisons were conducted between water-sector specialists and the general public and across primary drinking water sources, while expenditure measures were characterized separately according to source.
