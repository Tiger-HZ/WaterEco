Retail competition is increasingly a competition between supply chains rather than between individual products or stores. This paper analyzes the supply chain of Sam's Club in China and argues that its competitive advantage rests on four capabilities, namely a limited stock-keeping-unit (SKU) strategy, membership-driven demand visibility, centralized procurement, and data-driven supply chain management. Drawing on established concepts from operations and economics, including economies of scale, information sharing, and inventory theory, the paper explains how each capability lowers cost or reduces uncertainty and how the four capabilities reinforce one another.
