Abstract: Coumarins (2H-chromen-2-ones) are an important group of benzopyrone derivatives used in pharmaceuticals, agriculture, and materials. Although many “green” methods for making coumarins have been reported, few critical comparative analyses have assessed how sustainable these methods really are. This mini-review examines recent progress (2018 to 2025) in environmentally friendly methods for the synthesis of coumarins, focusing on solvent choice, catalysts, activation methods such as microwave, ultrasound, and mechanochemistry, and bio-based techniques. The review pays special attention to how these methods align with green chemistry principles, such as saving energy, reducing waste, reusing catalysts, lowering solvent toxicity, and scaling up. It compares solvent-free systems, water-based methods, deep eutectic solvents, ionic liquids, micellar catalysis, nanoparticle-based systems, plant-based catalysts, and enzyme methods, highlighting the pros and cons of each. Ongoing challenges such as recovering catalysts, batch-to-batch differences in bio-based systems, viscosity and mass-transfer issues in deep eutectic solvents, nanoparticle toxicity, and scaling up for industry are also discussed. The review aims to provide a fair overview of truly sustainable advances and to identify the main obstacles that need to be addressed for real-world use.
