We investigated the mechanisms behind the negative effects of cyanobacteria on zooplankton by comparing the effects of Microcystis aeruginosa single cells, colonies, and toxins on the feeding behavior of Daphnia pulicaria in three independent experiments. The animals were fed a mixture of Scenedesmus supplemented by increasing proportions (0, 20, 50, 80, 100%) of Microcystis or concentrations (0, 50, 500, 5,000 ng ml −1 ) of purified microcystin‐LR. The changes in feeding behavior, as indicated by the appendage beat, mandible or labrum movement rates were evaluated by a direct observation method that coupled video recording and computerized image analysis.
