Abstract Background. Microplastics (MPs) and nanoplastics (NPs), collectively referred to as MNPs, are widespread environmental pollutants detected in numerous human tissues, including blood, placenta, liver, lungs, and brain. Their potential impact on cardiovascular health has recently become an important research focus, particularly given that cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading cause of death worldwide. Aim. This narrative review summarises current evidence regarding the presence of MNPs in the human cardiovascular system, their proposed mechanisms of toxicity, and their possible association with adverse cardiovascular outcomes.
