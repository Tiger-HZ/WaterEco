Coastal waters worldwide are increasingly affected by oxygen loss due to human-induced eutrophication and global warming. This coastal deoxygenation has dramatically altered biogeochemical processes with major consequences for marine life. Prominent examples of large anthropogenic coastal &amp;#8220;dead zones&amp;#8221; include the Gulf of Mexico, Baltic Sea and Chesapeake Bay but numerous small coastal systems are also strongly affected. Many efforts are currently underway to restore the water quality of these coastal waters, but these are not always effective. In this presentation, I will discuss how the interplay of biogeochemical processes and hydrodynamics may affect present-day restoration efforts in coastal systems.
