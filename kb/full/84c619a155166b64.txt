This study addresses the critical need for effective environmental education by developing extracurricular programs and environmental communication for primary school students. It evaluated the current state of EE implementation through a quantitative survey of 281 primary teachers and qualitative interviews with teachers and students. Three diverse communication activities—a school-wide waste exchange program, recycled materials art, and coffee grounds crafting—were piloted at two primary schools. Results showed exceptionally high student interest and satisfaction (98%-100%). Teachers expressed a strong demand (99.2%) for supporting materials, citing significant challenges including time constraints (M=4.36), heavy workload (M=4.30), and lack of appropriate training (M=4.31). While current activities are often basic, EE is highly valued for fostering knowledge, attitudes, and habits. In response, the study developed a guidebook containing 11 comprehensive extracurricular plans to provide a systematic framework. This initiative will foster sustainable environmental protection behaviors among young learners by bridging implementation gaps.
