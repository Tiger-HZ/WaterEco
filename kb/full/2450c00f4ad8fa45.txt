Abstract Iron–ozone catalytic oxidation (CatOx) shows promise in addressing challenging wastewater pollutants. This study investigates a CatOx reactive filtration (Fe‐CatOx‐RF) approach with two 0.4 L/s field pilot studies and an 18‐month, 18 L/s full‐scale municipal wastewater deployment. We apply ozone to leverage common sand filtration and iron metal salts used in water treatment into a next‐generation technology. The process combines micropollutant and pathogen destructive removal with high‐efficiency phosphorus removal and recycling as a soil amendment, clean water recovery, and the potential for carbon‐negative operation with integrated biochar water treatment.
