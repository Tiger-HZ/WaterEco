Reliable prediction of streamflow in ungauged basins remains a major challenge in hydrology, particularly in regions with sparse monitoring networks. This study applies the NAM rainfall–runoff model to 94 hydrological catchments of a water district, where systematic discharge measurements are largely unavailable. Model parameters were derived from soil moisture properties, rooting depth, and infiltration coefficients associated with soil type and hydro-lithological formation classes, ensuring physical consistency across sub-basins. Model validation against exceptionally limited discharge observations at two monitoring sites demonstrated satisfactory performance, with water balance errors below 2% and correlation metrics within acceptable ranges (R = 0.65–0.75; RNS = 0.45–0.50). These results confirm that the proposed approach provides a robust and practical solution for rainfall–runoff modelling in environments with almost no data, enabling long-term runoff estimation and water balance analysis to support sustainable water resources management and planning in the absence of hydrological data.
