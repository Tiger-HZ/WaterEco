Metal corrosion is known to be associated with microorganisms' sulfate reduction metabolism. However, little research has been done on how the metabolism of bacteria that have both sulfate reduction and sulfur oxidation capabilities affects steel corrosion. In this work, we examined the corrosion process of Ectothiorhodospira shaposhnikovii on carbon steel and regulated its sulfur metabolism by modifying the carbon source. E. shaposhnikovii corrodes carbon steel more deeply under mixed carbon sources than under organic carbon sources, according to surface and electrochemical research. The pitting can grow to 91 microns after three weeks of corrosion. The consumption of sulfur by E. shaposhnikovii under mixed carbon sources experienced sulfate reduction, sulfide oxidation, and sulfate re-reduction processes, according to transcriptome and qPCR study. It only goes through the sulfate reduction process when exposed to organic carbon sources. The oxidation of sulfides is facilitated by the expression of the genes por, fumAB, and accB. Sulfate re-reduction is encouraged by the expression of the genes cysH and cysI. This study explains a novel MIC mechanism that helps us comprehend how bacteria with sulfate reduction and sulfur oxidation functions corrode carbon steel.
