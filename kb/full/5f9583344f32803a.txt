The Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) represents a grayscale image as multiple residue shadow images and enables progressive reconstruction by introducing moduli step by step. When the available moduli are insufficient and their product is less than 256, the CRT result determines only each pixel’s congruence class rather than a unique grayscale value. Thus, one residue may correspond to several candidates within the range of 0 to 255, and directly rendering the residue can cause structural artifacts. To address this underdetermined reconstruction problem, we propose a pixel-domain neighborhood prediction method. For each pixel, feasible candidates are enumerated from the current CRT residue using the current modulus product as the step size. A lightweight predictor based on the already reconstructed left and upper neighbors then estimates a plausible pixel value, and the candidate closest to this prediction is selected as the output. Experiments using PSNR and SSIM compare conventional CRT progressive reconstruction with the proposed method at different reconstruction levels. Results show that when the modulus product is below 256, the proposed method consistently improves objective quality and visual structural consistency without additional transmitted information. As more moduli are introduced, reconstruction ambiguity decreases and the recovered image becomes more accurate.
