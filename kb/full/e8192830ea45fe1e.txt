Abstract: China is undergoing rapidly urbanization process, enlarging the continuous optimization of 
infrastructure and increasing relevant construction activities sharply, releasing abundant carbon 
emissions (CEs) subsequently. Understanding the spatial-temporal patterns and changing characteristics 
is thus a fundamental prerequisite to assess and sustain energy saving and CEs reduction of construction 
industry (CI) level. Previous studies on carbon emission (CE) of CI however, are often limited to the 
quantitative level, provincial or local administrative unit scales, lacking relevant st udies at the spatial 
raster resolution scale, due to data gaps. Here, using the energy consumption , social economic data and 
a series of remote sensing data from EU EDGAR, this study explored the spatial-temporal distribution 
and changing characteristics of CEs from CI in typical years of 2007, 2010, and 2012. This study found, 
from 2007 to 2010, then 2012, in addition to subtle differences, the direct, indirect, and total CEs of CI 
all showed an increasing trend overall. All provincial units except Tianjin and Guangdong, indirect CEs 
took up more than 50% of the total CEs, which can clearly indicate the “dominant low carbon, recessive 
high carbon” characteristics of CI. The direct, indirect, and total CEs of the CI in 2007, 2010, and 2012, 
all showed a positive spatial clustering. Specifically, hot spots were mainly distributed in Beijing-Tianjin-
Hebei, Yangtze River Delta, and cold spots were mainly focused in the west and northeast of China, 
presenting a similar distribution pattern with population-economy characteristics. 
Key words: carbon emission; construction industry; spatial-temporal dis tribution; heterogeneity 
characteristic; grid scale

1. Introduction 20 
Concerns about climate change are increasing worldwide. Carbon emissions (CEs) have exacerbated 21 
the global warming process and serious environmental problem. Therefore, how to formulate carbon 22 
reduction strategies effectively is urgent. ( Pandey et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2020). As the biggest 23 
carbon emitters, China’s total carbon emissions (CEs) account for over 25% the total in the w orld 24 
(Zabalza et al., 2009; Cui et al., 2019). In 2020, China has stated that China will strive to reach the 25 
goal of carbon peak in 2030, carbon neutrality by 2060 with more effective measures (Mi et al., 26 
2017; Li et al., 2020). Among them, CEs from construction industry ( CI) contribute significantly, 27 
for it can also drive considerable amount of CEs from associated industries. It is reported that more 28 
3 
 
than 90% of the CEs from CI are generated indirectly, mainly from upstream industry in the 29 
production stage of building materials (Huang et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2017). Statistically, the CI 30 
consumes approximately 30% of global raw material usage, 25% of solid waste, and 25% of water 31 
resource (Zhang et al., 2013), and it also consumes a lot of sand, wood, gravel ( Kokoni and Skea, 32 
2014). Moreover, steel, cement, plastics and other materials are all needed for the CI production 33 
(Wang and Zhang, 2008). These materials will produce a large amount of energy consumption 34 
(Chang et al., 2010; Huang and Bohne, 2012; Khamraev et al., 2021). Therefore, by considering the 35 
direct and indirect CEs from CI comprehensively, it seems much critical to carbon reduction. 36 
Scholars abroad started studying the CI early (Nassen et al., 2007; Marzia et al., 2010). During their 37 
earlier studies, the relationship among associated industries were analyzed, which both calculated 38 
the direct CEs from CI but also the associated CEs from associated industry sectors. And, with the 39 
deepening of research gradually, the concept of CEs from CI domestic is constantly being developed 40 
and improved. It has been extended to the indirect CEs caused by the building materials, as well as 41 
other industries. Indirect CEs accounted for total from CI highly (Hendrickson and Horvath, 2000). 42 
For example, Acquaye and Duffy (2010) found that the indirect CEs of CI in Irish took a proportion 43 
of 91%. It is noteworthy that most of existing studies focus on the CEs from CI in a country or 44 
region; And scholars generally believe that the CEs from CI mainly come from two parts: direct and 45 
indirect CEs. Various studies have been carried to analyze the characteristics of direct and indirect 46 
CEs from time series, which play fundamental effects in the in-depth understanding of CEs from CI, 47 
but this is not enough, carbon reduction also should be clear from the spatial grid scale, which can 48 
provide value references for the implementation of emission reduction policies. 49 
A variety of methods have been applied in the existing studies involving CE from CI. 50 
Specifically, the emission coefficient method is the common method for direct CE (Chuai et al., 51 
2015). The application of life cycle method and IOA is primarily in indirect CE investigation (Zhang 52 
and Wang, 2016). IOA can calculate indirect CE through different connections between various 53 
departments and industries (Meng et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2018). While, life cycle method exists 54 
the error during the boundary truncation, which cannot fully quantify the indirect CE (Nassasset et 55 
al., 2007; Liu et al., 2019). Therefore, the IOA method has a great advantage in measuring the 56 
indirect CE of a certain industry, and, it has been widely employed (Chuai et al., 2015; Guo et al ., 57 
2018; Huang and Bohne, 2012; Zhang and Liu, 2013; Chuai et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2017; Li et 58 
4 
 
al., 2017; Wang et al., 2019 ). However, most studies were conducted on the characteristics of the 59 
total, or direct, indirect CEs, lacking the contribution characteristics exploration from other 60 
industries to indirect CEs from CI. Exploring the contribution of other industries to indirect CEs 61 
from CI is beneficial for the policy maker of carbon reduction. 62 
CI is a pillar industry in China, which occupies an important function in the national economic 63 
system, it accounted for 7.20% of China’s GDP in 2020. In recent years, with the development of 64 
China's infrastructure, CI has developed rapidly, generating considerable energy-related CEs (Wu 65 
et al., 2019). The energy consumed by the CI accounts for about 30% (Wang et al., 2019; Hong et 66 
al., 2016). In 2010, CEs related to energy consumption reached 79.72Mt (Lin and Liu, 2015). 67 
Existing studies have conducted the CEs from CI in whole China (Shi et al., 2017) or certain region 68 
or city (Li et al., 2020). However, these studies mainly show the total amount of China or a certain 69 
region under the premise that the emission intensity of the entire study area is uniform without 70 
considering the spatial heterogeneity of CEs. We must be aware that because of the vast territory, 71 
geographical conditions and large differences in resource endowments between areas, there are huge 72 
gaps in economic development and industrial structure among areas (Wang et al., 2020). Recently, 73 
some studies have analyzed the spatial heterogeneity of CEs from CI. However, most of them are 74 
based on administrative units (Li et al., 2017; Du et al., 2017), showing differences through 75 
statistical data. They cannot accurately describe the emission distribution, and freely find the exact 76 
emission-intensive areas and consistent hot spots in the physical space. All in all, they are powerless 77 
to reveal the spatial differences in CE from CI. In summary, they cannot help to reveal the spatial 78 
differences in CEs from CI, and there is an urgent need for more detailed spatial CE simulations on 79 
the grid scale. 80 
Overall, there are still many questions that are still unclear. How to help shape CEs from CI in 81 
different areas? What are the similarities and differences in the spatial distributi on of direct, and 82 
indirect CEs from CI? Where are the hotspots for CEs from CI? What is the spatial distribution and 83 
change in grid scale? All these issues are still uncertain, but they are very important for the 84 
government to take targeted mitigation measures to carbon reduction. Accordingly, this study 85 
calculated the direct, indirect, and total CEs of CI in 2007, 2010, and 2012, conducted the analysis 86 
of industry pulls, and explored the innovative integrated simulation based on the spatial distribution 87 
data of the EDGAR database. Compared with previous studies, the spatial distribution of CEs of CI 88 
5 
 
is simulated at the grid level, and long-series hot spot analysis is beneficial to track the consistency 89 
of emission intensive points change. Finally, the responsibility mechanism for carbon reduction 90 
considering local conditions can be proposed innovatively. 91 
2 Data sources & methods 92 
2.1 Data sources 93 
All data employed in this study includes remote sensing data, statistics, energy consumption and 94 
socio-economic data. Specifically, energy consumption is mainly acquired from the energy balance 95 
sheets of 31 provincial units in 2007, 2010, and 2012. The total output value and the complete 96 
consumption coefficient are mainly based on Chinese multi-regional input-output table of 31 97 
provincial units in 2007, 2010, and 2012. Socio-economic data mainly derives from the "China 98 
Statistical Yearbook" and the "China Energy Statistical Yearbook" in 2008, 2011, and 2013. In order 99 
to ensure the consistency of the industry sectors division, this study classifies the ind ustry system 100 
into five industries: agriculture, industry, CI, transportation, storage and postal industry, and, others. 101 
The spatial CI data comes from the Global Atmospheric Research Emissions Database (EDGAR) 102 
dataset (https://edgar.jrc.ec.com) (Crippa et al., 2018), it not only includes emissions intensity value 103 
(which refers to the CEs per grid cell), but also the spatial distribution data with a resolution of 104 
0.1°×0.1°. After data pre-processing in this study, the spatial simulation of CEs of the CI has been 105 
transformed into spatial raster data with a resolution of 10km×10km. The grid cell in t his study 106 
refers to the grid of 10km×10km. 107

2.2.2 Spatial simulation of the CEs from CI 137 
Although the emission coefficient method and IOA method can be used to calculate the CEs of CI, 138 
the spatial distribution of the emission sources cannot be clearly depicted. The EU EDGAR database 139 
provides global grid carbon emission maps of different industries, and the spatial consumption data 140

3.1 Changes of CEs from the CI 185 
In order to better investigate the changing trends of CEs from CI, this study produced the line chart 186 
of CEs. As Fig. 1 shows, direct, indirect, and total CEs all present an overall increasing trend in the 187 
period of 2007-2012 (Fig. 1). The direct CEs showed obvious regional differences, areas with high 188 
direct CEs are generally those with larger economies and better development of the CI, except Inner 189 
Mongolia. Areas with the smallest direct CEs are Hainan and Guangxi, respectively. Areas with 190 
relatively low direct CEs are concentrated in Qinghai, Heilongjiang, Ningxia, Guizhou, Jiangxi, 191

less energy consumption. The indirect and total carbon emission have a similar trend. Areas w ith 194 
high indirect CEs mainly concludes Yunnan, Sichuan, Shandong and Liaoning. And, the total CEs 195 
in these areas were also higher. Overall, the CI has relatively low direct CEs and high indirect CEs 196 
due to the closely connections between CI and other industries, it is a typical “demand” industry. 197

4. Discussion 328 
Under the successive releases of new infrastructure policies, the CI is facing a new wave of rapid 329 
development. This study calculates the direct and indirect CEs from CI in typical years, and 330 
innovatively simulates the spatial variation in characteristics of CEs from CI. It is helpful to 331 
determine emission reduction targets according to the local development of the CI (Wen and Wang, 332 
2020). 333 
In China, the CI has been developed boomingly with the rapid urbanization. The production 334 
18 
 
process of CI drives a large amount of resource consumption all over the world, and generates a lot 335 
of CEs and other air pollutants (Chuai et al., 2020). In this study, direct CEs are those caused by on-336 
site activities in CI (Onat et al., 2014). Indirect CEs refer to emissions caused by products related to 337 
the CI in the upstream activities, such as bricks and cements (Kokoni and Skea, 2014). It has been 338 
demonstrated that CI is typical “dominant low carbon, recessive high carbon” (Qi and Zhang, 2013). 339 
According to our research, on average, indirect carbon emission accounted for more than 77% of 340 
the total in all provincial units. Heilongjiang, Hainan and Guangxi accounted for the highest 341 
proportion in 2007, 2010 and 2012, which were 97%, 98% and 97%, respectively. This is because 342 
the CI is a typical demand-oriented industry. And existing relevant study demonstrated the 343 
production phase of building materials was the period with the largest energy (Bin and Park, 2012), 344 
then followed by building operation phase (Cui et al., 2019). The CEs of materials and energy 345 
consumption during the production, manufacturing and transportation of construction materials, as 346 
well as the CEs generated by maintaining the energy consumption (electricity, water, heating, etc.) 347 
during the construction process, so the indirect CEs were so high. Here, the calculation in this study 348 
does not consider emissions produced during the operation phase, such as building use and 349 
demolition. According to You et al. (2011), in 50 years of building use, the CEs of the operation 350 
stage are 6-8 times of the production phase of building materials. Therefore, strengthening th e 351 
selection of green building materials should be paid more attention. 352 
As the results show, in the period of 2007-2012, CEs from CI presented an increasing trend 353 
overall. The indirect CEs were mainly gathered in transportation, storage and postal industry, which 354 
is consistent with the research of others (Yan et al., 2010 ). According to the study of Chuai et al. 355 
(2015), the industry was the sector with the highest indirect CEs, indicating there is no substantially 356 
change in traditional construction pattern of China, energy structure mainly relies on fossi l fuels, 357 
especially coal. Specifically, the sectors with significant changes in indirect CEs caused by the CI 358 
are mainly concentrated in the production and supply of electric power, heat power, and water, the 359 
manufacturing and processing metals and metal products, the coking, gas, and processing of 360 
petroleum, the manufacturing of nonmetallic goods, the mining, the transport, storage, postal, and 361 
information industry, and the chemical industry. These sectors accounted for 94.92%, 96.2%, 96.59 % 362 
and 96.43% of total indirect CEs in 1995, 2000, 2005 and 2010. , and their contribution to indirect 363 
CEs remained unchanged between 1995 and 2010. Thus, the development of new clean energy 364 
19 
 
should be paid more attention. And, for the transport, storage, and postal industry, short-distance 365 
transport should be promoted and recycle intermediate production products during the production 366 
phase of products (Huang, 2012). In the future, carbon reduction in CI can be implemented through 367 
strengthening the selection of green building materials, reducing high-carbon building materials use 368 
(Li et al., 2021). 369 
High resolution map and long-term sequence hotspot analysis revealed the temporal-spatial 370 
variation characteristics of CEs from CI in China, which is helpful for us to track carbon emission 371 
concentration points and take targeted mitigation measures, which has been not studied in similar 372 
literature. The spatial distribution of direct and indirect CEs from CI in China was similar: low in 373 
the northwest and high in the southeast, which means that CEs from CI have a strong correlation 374 
with population density and economic level (Huang et al., 2019). In addition, CEs from CI also 375 
showed a trend of agglomeration in the same direction. Liang et al. (2019) found that socio-376 
economic and geographical conditions can significantly affect air pollution emissions. According to 377 
the study of Zhang et al. (2018), most of China 's heavy industries were located in the North China 378 
Plain, with construction CEs accounting for a large proportion. For coastal areas, long coastline, 379 
large estuaries, ports, these convenient traffic conditions also promote the development of foreign 380 
trade. Therefore, it is not surprising that the Yangtze River Delta, and Pearl River Delta become high 381 
emission areas. In addition, there has been polarization in these areas and in many other provincial 382 
capital cities. These regions are usually relatively developed, so they can attract more enterprises, 383 
labor and capital. All these reasons have promoted their new infrastructure construction 384 
development, resulting in more intensive CEs. 385 
There is still some uncertainties in this study. First, the research period of this study i s only 386 
updated to 2012. Due to the long compilation cycle of the input-output table, it does not keep up 387 
with the latest time limit. In the future, it should be updated according to the latest input-output table 388 
to further study the latest conditions in CI. Second, this study applies the EU EDGAR database to 389 
simulate the spatial distribution of CEs from CI. The resolution of this raster data is 0.1°  0.1°, 390 
with a low resolution and certain errors. More refined spatial data should be explored for further 391 
study. Third, the direct CEs was obtained using the coefficient method, which has certain errors, but 392 
the existing related research of our research group can prove the effectiveness and scientifici ty of 393 
this method (Yuan et al., 2019). On the basis of data availability, it is hoped that from the aspect of 394 
20 
 
the industrial chain, we will study the inter-industry spatial transfer characteristics of the CI in the 395 
latest years. 396 
5. Conclusions & policy implication 397 
5.1 Conclusions 398 
The exploration and simulation of CEs in CI at the provincial level play fundamental effects in 399 
improving the emission reduction responsibility mechanism and promoting green development. 400 
This study found that, from 2007-2010-2012, in addition to subtle differences, the direct, indirect, 401 
and total CEs all showed an increasing trend overall. By calculating the contribution ratio of indirect 402 
CEs in 2007, 2010, and 2012, we found indirect CE in China averagely accounted for more than 403 
77% of total, which can clearly indicate the “dominant low carbon, recessive high carbon” 404 
characteristics of CI. The direct and indirect CEs from CI in 2007, 2010, and 2012, all showed a 405 
positive spatial clustering. All show ed a gradually significant and tightly concentrated pattern 406 
changes, that is, hot spots were mainly distributed in Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei, Yangtze R iver Delta, 407 
and cold spots were mainly distributed in the west and northeast, showing that the CE intensity of 408 
the CI has a significant correlation with economic development and population density. Hence, 409 
policy maker should formulate corresponding carbon reduction target adapt to local conditions. 410 
5.2 Policy implication 411 
Based on the research results, the following policy recommendations can be put forward. Firstly, it 412 
is necessary to strengthen the recycling intensity and effective use of intermediate product s in 413 
relevant industries. Specifically, construction units should be actively guided to obtain raw materials 414 
by the principle of proximity to reduce emissions during transportation. Secondly, in order to ensure 415 
the carbon reduction efforts n, the government should also allocate emission reduction tasks 416 
differentially based on local conditions. Thirdly, for carbon reduction effectively, there should adopt 417 
different carbon reduction measures, for the developed area, they should develop advanced 418 
technology to reduce intermediate CEs. For western areas, there is better vigorously develop clean 419 
energy such as wind energy and solar energy. 420
