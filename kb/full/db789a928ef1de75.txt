Abstract Efficient pressure surge control is essential for maintaining hydraulic infrastructure safety and preventing catastrophic pipeline failures. Air vessels are widely deployed for surge protection, yet traditional evaluations often conflate geometric air-water ratios with total volume scale. This study presents an experimental investigation into small-scale air vessel performance, isolating the Volume Fraction Ratio (VFR) from initial air volume (V int ) alongside the orifice-to-pipe diameter ratio (β). Synchronized high-frequency pressure and optical water level tracking revealed that varying VFR by a factor of 2.16 produces negligible changes in transient peaks, yielding maximum variations under 2.77% in peak water pressure and 1.97% in peak air pressure.
