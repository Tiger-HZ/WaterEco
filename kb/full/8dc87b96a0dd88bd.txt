Greenwashing refers to the act of misleading stakeholders with false environmental claims, which distorts market signals and hampers sustainable development. Existing research mainly evaluates single policy instruments, while the synergistic effects of multiple policies on corporate greenwashing remain insufficiently understood, especially in countries with market conditions similar to China. This study identifies key synergies among major environmental policies in China—Low-Carbon City Pilot (LCCP), Carbon Emission Trading (CET), Environmental Courts (EC), Atmospheric Ten Articles (ATA), Green Credit Subsidy (GCS), and New Energy Cities (NEC)—using association rule mining. We then estimate the causal impacts of prominent policy combinations on corporate greenwashing with panel data for Chinese A-share listed firms (2010–2023) and a double/debiased machine learning framework. Results show two dominant synergies: LCCP–CET and LCCP–CET–EC. The LCCP–CET mix is associated with the largest reduction in corporate greenwashing, while incorporating EC modestly attenuates this effect. These findings contribute to the environmental policy literature and provide actionable insights for governments to design effective environmental governance systems.
