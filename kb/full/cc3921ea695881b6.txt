The Experience Sampling Method (ESM) requires participants to repeatedly complete assessments in daily life, typically without direct researcher supervision. Participant briefing is therefore an important opportunity to prepare participants to follow the protocol independently, but the effects of different characteristics of briefings are poorly understood. This quasi-experimental study examined whether online versus in-person briefing influenced data quantity (compliance), data quality (careless responding), and participant experiences (participant burden, perceived briefing sufficiency). First-year psychology students (N=249) were assigned to an online (n=209) or in-person (n=40) briefing condition based on recruitment phase and completed a seven-day ESM protocol with 10 daily prompts.
