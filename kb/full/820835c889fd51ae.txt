We propose a Bayesian evidence-based inference framework built on relative belief ratios and apply it to discriminating between one and two incoherent optical point sources using spatial-mode demultiplexing (SPADE). Unlike the Helstrom measurement, SPADE requires no collective detection and is optimal for asymptotically large samples. Our method avoids statistical constructs and draws inference directly from the information contained in the data, with all assumptions entering only through the likelihood model and prior beliefs. Using experimental evidence, we demonstrate the superior resolving performance of SPADE over direct imaging from a perspective that generalizes to multiple sources and offers a practical, robust approach to analyzing quantum-enhanced superresolution.
