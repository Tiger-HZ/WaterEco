Abstract Surface architecture plays a fundamental role in governing the interaction between biological interfaces and their surrounding environment. In this study, we present a comprehensive multiscale surface metrology analysis of the adaxial and abaxial leaf interfaces of Couepia bracteosa a combination of optical microscopy, scanning electron microscopy, and three-dimensional confocal profilometry. Quantitative characterization was performed according to ISO 25178–2 areal surface texture parameters complemented by power spectral density (PSD) analysis, Hurst exponent estimation, fractal dimension evaluation, and Hough transform-based structural mapping.
