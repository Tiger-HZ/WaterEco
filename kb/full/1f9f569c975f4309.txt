Given the presumed importance of benthic and epibenthic estuarine habitats in Chinook salmon ( Oncorhynchus tshawytscha ) smolt growth and survival, resource managers would be well served by an improved understanding of how smolts use such habitats. A cabled acoustic positioning system was used to precisely track (&lt;1 m resolution) the movement of seventeen 0-aged hatchery-reared fall Chinook smolts in a large (~4000 m 2 ) enclosure over a period of 10 days in Willapa Bay, Washington, USA. A hierarchical Bayesian state–space model of movement was subsequently developed to associate the behaviors of tagged salmon with characteristics of benthic habitat in the enclosure.
