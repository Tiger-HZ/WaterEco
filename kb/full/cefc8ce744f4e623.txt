ABSTRACT Drawing on the Affect‐Behavior‐Cognition (ABC) model, this study examines how greenwashing perception shapes the job‐seeking intentions of final‐year business students in Ghana. Specifically, the study theorizes and tests the moderating role of perceived green corporate image and recruitment buzz. Data from 179 final‐year business students at the University of Ghana Business School were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS‐SEM). The findings reveal that green‐washing perception positively and significantly influences the intention to apply for jobs. Importantly, perceived green corporate image amplifies this effect, indicating that a strong green reputation heightens applicants' sensitivity to greenwashing signals.
