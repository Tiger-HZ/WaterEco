ABSTRACT The Colorado River downstream of Glen Canyon Dam (GCD) in the southwest USA demonstrates anthropogenic modification including altered flow and temperature regimes and established nonnative species in upstream reservoirs. Water overallocation and megadrought are contributing to new potential invasion pathways for nonnative fishes through dam releases. In this study, we quantified factors that influence survival of fish entrained at GCD, a pathway for nonnative fishes to leave Lake Powell and enter the Grand Canyon as reservoir levels remain near historic lows since the reservoir initially filled.
