Remediation of soils contaminated with heavy metals and organic pollutants using Black Sea macroalgae remains insufficiently investigated. War-caused soil destruction in Ukraine has created an urgent need for cost-effective recovery, making locally available seaweed biomass a highly relevant resource. This review evaluates the potential of marine macroalgae for soil fertility restoration and remediation based on recent literature. Studies from other regions have extensively investigated the use of genera Ulva and Cladophora as biofertilizers and biosorbents; however, their biomass from the Black Sea has received little attention, representing a promising research direction. Compost, extracts, and dried biomass are most commonly applied to enhance soil properties and plant growth, whereas algal-derived biochar appears most effective for contaminant immobilization. The high polysaccharide and mineral content of seaweeds supports their use in soil applications. Black Sea macroalgae represent an underexplored yet promising resource, combining strong biotechnological potential with the low cost of storm wrack harvest.
