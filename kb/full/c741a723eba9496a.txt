The integration of green facade systems as passive cooling strategies has gained global attention; however, their adoption within the Nigerian hospitality sector remains limited and fragmented. This study systematically investigates the barriers constraining the implementation of green facade systems in this context. Adopting a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) approach guided by Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) protocols, the study synthesizes evidence from 53 peer-reviewed publications published between 2020 and 2026. A thematic analysis framework was employed to categorize identified barriers into economic, technical, institutional, environmental, and socio-cultural domains. The findings reveal that economic constraints represent the most dominant barrier, particularly high initial capital costs, long payback periods, and dependence on imported materials. Technical challenges, including limited local expertise and absence of climate-specific performance data, further restrict adoption. Institutional barriers are reflected in weak regulatory frameworks and lack of fiscal incentives, while environmental concerns such as irrigation demands and plant suitability present context-specific limitations. Critically, socio-cultural factors, especially the misalignment between passive design strategies and guest expectations for mechanical cooling, significantly hinder implementation. The study identifies a persistent knowledge-action gap and a broader socio-technical misalignment that collectively impede mainstream adoption. It concludes that current implementation is largely driven by individual initiatives rather than systemic support. To address this, the paper recommends policy reform, financial incentives, capacity development, and the promotion of pilot demonstration projects to generate localized empirical evidence. This research contributes to knowledge by providing a comprehensive, context-specific synthesis that informs policy, practice, and future research in sustainable hotel development in Nigeria.
