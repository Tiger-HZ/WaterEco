Abstract Climate change threatens Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services globally, with post‑conflict regions facing compounded legacies of infrastructure damage and institutional erosion. Syria exemplifies this challenge, combining extreme climate vulnerability with the enduring consequences of past conflict and economic collapse. This study assesses climate risks to Syria’s WASH sector using the GWP–UNICEF Strategic Framework for Climate‑Resilient WASH Development. We integrate climate hazard analysis, infrastructure exposure assessment, and multidimensional vulnerability evaluation using IPCC risk definitions (Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability).
