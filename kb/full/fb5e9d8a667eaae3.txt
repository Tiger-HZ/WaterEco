ABSTRACT Plant‐insect interactions regulate ecosystem processes and productivity. Most studies have focused on the effect of plant diversity on insect herbivory in grasslands, croplands, and natural forests. However, the direction of this effect remains controversial. Unlike these ecosystems, island ecosystems possess distinct geographical features and provide natural experimental settings for studying trophic interactions. Nevertheless, the effects of plant diversity, island characteristics, and their interactions on insect herbivory in such environments have scarcely been explored.
