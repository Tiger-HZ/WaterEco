Academic burnout is associated with impaired student wellbeing and achievement, yet less is known about how distinct teacher-level resources relate to burnout across classroom and student levels. Drawing on the Job Demands–Resources model and Social Cognitive Theory, this study used hierarchical linear modeling of data from 84 homeroom teachers and 2,979 students in China to examine teacher organizational citizenship behavior and teacher–student relationship quality as classroom-level resources, with self-esteem and academic self-efficacy as parallel personal-resource pathways. Teacher organizational citizenship behavior was negatively associated with student academic burnout, whereas the direct association between teacher–student relationship quality and burnout was not statistically significant. Both teacher resources showed significant indirect associations with burnout through self-esteem and academic self-efficacy. Academic burnout was also negatively associated with academic performance. These findings indicate that relational and discretionary teacher resources may be linked to student burnout partly through distinct student self-beliefs.
