Oyster farming provides crucial ecosystem services by mitigating coastal eutrophication. However, intensive farming frequently leads to benthic organic overloading through biodeposition, which impacts ecosystem functioning. Yet, the exact microbial feedback mechanisms and ecological thresholds remain unclear. Here, we conducted a large-scale benthic environmental and metagenomic investigation across five intensive oyster (Crassostrea gigas) farming areas in the Shandong Peninsula, China. Our results revealed that biodeposit-driven organic loading significantly promoted total sulfur (TS) accumulation, triggering a non-linear functional regime shift in the benthic nitrogen cycle.
