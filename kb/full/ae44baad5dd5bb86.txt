Abstract Plant-pollinator interactions are shaped not only by environmental conditions but also by biotic interactions such as predation. Predators influence prey through direct consumption and non-consumptive effects (NCEs) arising from perceived predation risk altering prey traits such as foraging behaviour. Emergent freshwater macrophytes such as the yellow water lily ( Nuphar lutea) create a shared interface where predators and pollinators co-occur. Yet, NCEs of predation risk on pollinators and their consequences for plant-pollinator interactions remain severely understudied, especially at aquatic-terrestrial transition zones.
