Abstract Water shortage risk management strategies, such as demand curtailment and/or temporary water supply augmentation, are vital, but their effectiveness depends on accurate monitoring and forecasting of water shortage indicators, robust water restrictions enforcement, and timely public engagement. A key question following a water shortage declaration is, “how long will it persist, and how severe might it become?” The answer is essential for determining whether water shortage measures should be sustained or adjusted. This study fills the gap of integrating the impact of Central Pacific-El Niño on local climate into the operational forecasts of water shortage indicators and provides guidance for regional water shortage management.
