Co-granulated urea–polyhalite improves yield and 
nutrient use efficiency of rainfed maize
V. Visha Kumari  · K. A. Gopinath · V. K. Singh · A. K. Shanker · S. Suvana · 
J. V. N. S. Prasad · B. Sunitha · G. Subramanyam · Vipin Mishra · Neeraj Awasthi

Abstract
Balanced nutrient management using multi-nutrient fertilizers has gained increasing attention for improving 
crop productivity and nutrient use efficiency in rainfed areas. Multi-nutrient fertilizers have received consider -
able attention in recent years, particularly for fields with multi nutrient deficiencies. A two-year field experi -
ment was conducted during rainy season of 2023 and 2024 on acidic sandy loam soil to evaluate the agronomic 
performance of co-granulated urea–polyhalite (Urea-POLY) fertilizer in comparison with conventional farmer 
fertilizer practices under rainfed maize cultivation. The experiment included six nutrient management treat -
ments comprising Farmer Practice (NP), Farmer Practice (NPK), Urea-POLY bag-for-bag, Urea-POLY recom -
mended N, DAP + MOP and SSP + MOP treatments in randomized block design with three replications. Pooled 
analysis showed that Urea-POLY recommended N recorded the highest plant height (149.1 cm), leaf area 
index (3.14) and biomass accumulation (114.4 g plant-1), this was statistically comparable with Farmer Prac -
tice (NPK) (p ≤ 0.05). Similarly, Urea-POLY recommended N produced the highest grain yield (4669 kg ha-1), 
representing a 6.3% grain yield increase over Farmer Practice (NPK) and stover yield (7978 kg ha-1), Yield 
improvements were associated with enhanced cob length, grain number and test weight. Urea-POLY treatments 
also improved post-harvest soil available N, P, K and S along with exchangeable Ca and Mg compared to 
conventional control treatments, and the difference was statistically significant (p ≤ 0.05). Furthermore, in terms 
of nitrogen use efficiency (NUE); application of Urea-POLY rec. N resulted in more pronounced agronomic 
use efficiency and partial factor productivity of N, P, K and S compared with the Farmer Practice (NPK) treat -
ment. This nutrient management strategy, which particularly emphasized the importance of N, S and K levels, 
optimized maize productivity and quality. However, the study was conducted at Gungal, Telangana region and 
the findings should be validated further across different soil types, agro climatic seasons and crops. Overall, 
this study showed the potential of cogranulated Urea-POLY as an efficient multi-nutrient commercial fertilizer 
to supply N, K, S, Ca and Mg nutrients under dryland agriculture.

Introduction
Maize is a significant crop in India, with an expanding role in domestic food systems as well as industrial and 
export markets. Globally, maize ranks among the leading cereals due to its high productivity and diverse applica-
tions, including food, animal feed and industrial raw material. In India, maize is the third most important cereal 
after rice and wheat, with production reaching approximately 33 million metric tons (MMT) in 2023. Despite 
this, nutrient deficiencies resulting from unbalanced fertilization and variable rainfall patterns in rainfed areas 
are the present major challenges for maize productivity 1, (Gopinath et al.2022). In these regions, suboptimal 
plant growth is frequently attributed to nutrient limitations and water scarcity, both of which reduce yields 2,3. 
Excessive application of nitrogen fertilizers, such as urea, often leads to nutrient losses from the soil and reduced 
nitrogen-use efficiency, particularly under water-limited conditions. Urea is less efficient because its hydrolysed 
nature, resulting in substantial nitrogen loss. Consequently, approximately 30–50% of the nitrogen supplied by 
urea is utilized by crops. Acidic soils commonly exhibit severe deficiencies of phosphorus, calcium, magnesium 
and molybdenum and may also contain toxic concentrations of aluminium and iron, further restricting crop 
growth4 (Swami and Yadav, 2020). In these soils, calcium and magnesium deficiencies may arise due to leaching 
or low soil retention capacity 5,6. These nutrient limitations adversely affect a range of crops, including peanuts, 
sunflowers, rainfed rice, fruits and vegetables7,8. So, multi-nutrient fertilizers can address these challenges more 
effectively than conventional fertilizers by enhancing yields and nutrient use efficiency. They achieve this by 
releasing nutrients gradually to align with crop demand, thereby improving application timing and reducing the 
frequency of applications.
Maize is characterized by high nutrient demand, necessitating strategic fertilizer management. The crop exhib-
its a pronounced response to substantial nitrogen fertilization. Under rainfed conditions, deficiencies of macro, 
micro and secondary nutrients are prevalent. Nitrogen deficiency is particularly widespread, accounting for 89% 
of observed deficiencies (63% low and 26% medium), while potassium and sulphur deficiencies account for 50% 
(13% low and 37% medium) and 41%, respectively 9,10. There remains a critical need for multi-nutrient sources 
that not only provide high nutrient content but also ensure synchronized nutrient release in accordance with crop 
requirements11. Besides conventional fertilizers, multi-nutrient fertilizers such as Urea-POLY (30–00-05 + 7S) 
represent promising options for nutrient management in maize and for maintaining soil fertility. Urea-POLY 
(30–00-05 + 7S) supplies a balanced combination of nitrogen, potassium, calcium, magnesium and sulphur. Its 
high nitrogen content is particularly beneficial for maize, given the crop’s substantial nitrogen requirement. Each 
nutrient contributes to essential plant metabolic processes and supports crop growth and development. The avail-
ability of these nutrients in granulated, powdered, or chipped forms allows for flexible application across various 
soil conditions. Additionally, the polyhalite in Urea-POLY exhibits lower water solubility compared to conven -
tional sources, suggesting the potential for gradual nutrient release and improved suitability as a fertilizer12,13.
Although studies on the use of polyhalite fertiliser in rainfed maize systems is limited, several studies have 
demonstrated its efficacy as a fertiliser for corn (Wang et al., 2018), wheat14, oats (Ola et al., 2023), peanut (Tan 
et al., 2022), mustard (Tiwari et al., 2015) and sugarcane (Herrera et al., 2022). Mello et al. (2018) found that 
soils treated with polyhalite fertiliser contained higher levels of calcium and magnesium than those treated with 
conventional fertilisers. Urea-POLY provides slow and sustained nutrient release due to the polyhalite component 
and its content of nitrogen, potassium, sulphur, calcium and magnesium, which together enhance nutrient use 
efficiency14–16. Accordingly, this study aimed to evaluate the effect of Urea-POLY (30–00-05 + 7S) as an alterna-
tive to urea on the productivity and nutrient use efficiency of rainfed maize andhypothesises that co-granulated 
Urea-POLY can achieve maize yields comparable to those obtained with conventional fertilisers under rainfed 
conditions because of its multi-nutrient availability and slow release nature. The objectives are to assess the effi-
cacy of Urea-POLY on maize growth, productivity, nutrient uptake, post-harvest soil nutrient status and nutrient 
use efficiency under rainfed conditions.

Materials and methods
Site description and treatment details
Rainfed maize (cv DHM 117) experiments were conducted during rainy season of 2023 and 2024 at Gungal 
Research Farm, Telangana (Latitude 17°40’ 40.4" N, Longitude 78°39’, 55.7’’ E, Altitude at 626 m).
The experiment was arranged in a randomized complete block design with three replications and 6 treatments 
(Table 1; Table 2). Each plot was made with size of 5 m × 5 m. The experimental plots were tilled to a depth of 
15–20 cm using tractor-drawn cultivator twice before sowing of the crop. The crop was sown on 7th July in 2023 
and 1st July 2024. Weeds were controlled both by pre-emergence and post-emergence herbicides. Sowing was 
done with a tractor-drawn seed drill at a spacing of 60 cm × 25 cm in the first week of July. Thinning and gap fill-
ing were done 10–15 days after sowing, as required. Pre-application of Atrazine@ 0.75 kg a.i. ha-1, one day after 
sowing. The crop was harvested at 10th October in 2023 and 3rd October in 2024.
All products except urea and Urea-POLY were applied as a basal dose at the time of sowing. Urea and Urea-
POLY were applied in three equal splits i.e., basal, knee-high stage (V8, 35 days after sowing (DAS) and tasseling 
stage of crop (VT 55 DAS). During the crop growing period, the weekly mean minimum and maximum tempera-
tures ranged from 22.3 to 31.4 °C during 2023 and 22.6 to 30.7 °C during 2024. Fig. 1A and B shows the amount 
of rainfall recorded during the two growing seasons of maize. In 2023, maize crop received only 396.0 mm dur-
ing the crop period, whereas 687.3 mm was received in 2024 year during the crop growth stages significantly 
influenced the crop production potential. During the crop growth period, two major drought stress periods were 
observed: one at the vegetative stage and one at tasselling stage in the year 2023; one at the vegetative stage and 
one at the grain development stage in the year 2024.

Soil analysis
Soil samples were collected from three different points within each plot at a depth of 0–15 cm both before the 
commencement of the field experiments in 2023, as well as post-harvest. The three samples were then bulked 
together and thoroughly mixed to create a single sample for each plot.
A sub-sample from each plot was then crushed using a ceramic pestle and mortar and passed through a 2-mm 
sieve. These samples were then analysed for Available N using the Alkaline KMnO 4 method17, extractable P 
using the (0.025 N HCl and 0.03 N NH 4F, Bray and Kurtz P1 method 18, exchangeable K using the ammonium 
acetate method (1 M NH4OAc, pH 7.0 extraction19 and extractable S using the calcium chloride method (0.15% 
CaCl2 extraction20, Available Ca and Mg using EDTA-Versenate method19.
Initial soil analysis indicated that the soil texture of the experimental field was red sandy loam, specifically 
belonging to the Alfisols, which were slightly acidic, with a pH of 5.73. The electrical conductivity (EC) was 0.13 
dS m-1 and the cation exchange capacity (CEC) was within the normal range at 16.1 cmol + kg-1. The soils were 
low in organic carbon (0.42%) with medium levels of available N (210 kg ha-1), low available P (17.2 kg ha-1) 
andmedium available K (196 kg ha-1), Ca (5.09 meq/100 g) and Mg ((0.68 meq/100 g) and S (13.9 kg ha-1). Based 
on the soil analysis and the crop requirements the recommended dose of NPKS was 90:60:45:12.5 kg N, P2O5, 
K2O and S ha-1 applied to the crop.

Growth attributes
Biomass accumulation, plant height and leaf area index (LAI) of the maize crop were measured at the flowering 
stage. To determine biomass accumulation, five maize plants were collected at random and dried at 65 ºC. When 
a constant weight was attained, the samples were weighed. For plant height five random plants were measured 
from the base of each plant to the apex of growing point. Similarly, for LAI, the leaf areas of all leaves of five 
random plants were measured using LICOR (LI-3100C) and averaged value was presented. LAI was calculated 
by dividing the leaf area by the ground area21.
Yield and yield attributes
At harvest, five randomly selected cobs were assessed from each plot for cob length, cob dry weight and number 
of grains per cob. The estimation of grain yield was conducted by harvesting the cobs from all plants by hand 
picking them from the net plot area of 4.5 m × 4.5 m which was dried and threshed with a maize sheller. The sepa-
rated grain was then weighed to calculate grain yield. Additionally, the weight of one hundred randomly selected 
kernels was determined from each plot to calculate hundred grain weight. After separating the cobs from the 
plants, the plants were sun-dried to a constant weight and then weighed to calculate the stover yield.
Plant nutrient analysis
At harvest, the stover and grain samples collected for analysis were air dried under shade, followed by hot air 
oven drying at 60 °C for 48 h until a constant weight was attained. The samples were powdered in a Willey mill 
and sieved (0.5 mm) for nutrient analysis. The N content was determined by the Kjeldahl method using an auto-
analyzer, while P, K and S content were determined after digesting the samples with diacid mixture of 9:4 of 
HNO3: HClO4
22 using ICP –OES22.

was determined by subtracting the yield without nitrogen application from the yield with nitrogen application and

Fertilizer details
Urea-POLY (30–00-05 + 7S) is a complex fertilizer formulated from polyhalite and urea, containing 30% nitrogen 
(N), 5% potassium oxide (K2O), 6% calcium oxide (CaO), 2% magnesium oxide (MgO) and 7% sulphur (S). In 
this study, the fertilizer was made by co-granulating urea with polyhalite. The polyhalite used in this formulation 
was sourced from mineral ore through a natural process and contains 14% K2O, 17% CaO, 6% MgO and 19% S.

Statistical analysis
We analysed the experimental data collected over two years using OPSTAT software23 and R software (version 
4.5.2). Analysis of variance (ANOV A) appropriate for a randomized block design was performed to evaluate 
treatment effects on different parameters following the procedures described by Gomez and Gomez24. Treatment 
means were compared using the least significant difference (LSD) test at the 5% significance level. Year-wise 
analyses were conducted separately for each experimental year, followed by pooled analysis across the two years. 
Prior to pooled analysis, Bartlett’s test was performed to examine the homogeneity of error variances between 
years. When variances were found to be homogeneous, combined ANOV A was carried out for the pooled data. 
For pairwise treatment comparisons in the pooled analysis, Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) test 
was used wherever appropriate. Anova table for the treatments are given in S1.
Results
Growth
Urea-POLY application significantly influenced rainfed maize growth, with notable differences across years and 
treatments (Fig. 2 (A)). Pooled analysis revealed that plant height, LAI and biomass varied significantly among 
treatments, whereas only LAI varied significantly over years. Urea-POLY rec. N resulted in the tallest plants 
(149.1 cm) and highest LAI (3.14), which were statistically similar to Farmer Practice (NPK) (147.1 cm, 2.85). 
The lowest plant height and LAI were observed in the SSP + MOP (74.1 cm, 1.82) and DAP + MOP (115.0 cm, 
2.02) controls. The highest biomass was recorded in the Urea-POLY rec. N treatment (114.4 g plant-1), fol -
lowed by Farmer Practice (NPK) at 99.7 g plant-1. The lowest biomass values were found in SSP + MOP and 
DAP + MOP controls, at 40.2 g plant-1 and 42.8 g plant-1, respectively (Fig. 2(B)).

Yield attributes
The combined analysis of yield attributes of maize revealed that the interaction between year and treatment 
was significant (p ≤ 0.05) with the application of Urea-POLY , indicating that the performance of treatments var-
ied across years (Table 3). Across the years studied, cob weight and the number of grains per cob did not vary 
significantly. In contrast, both cob length and test weight showed significant variation in both years. Among 
the treatments, Urea-POLY rec. N produced the longest cobs (19.6 cm), which did not differ statistically from 
Farmer Practice (NP), Urea-POLY bag-for-bag and Farmer Practice (NPK), with cob lengths of 18.0, 18.5 and 
19.1 cm, respectively. Farmer Practice (NPK) achieved the highest cob weight (187.7 g), which was statistically 
comparable to Farmer Practice (NP), Urea-POLY bag-for-bag and Urea-POLY rec. N, with respective values of 
168.5 g, 173.1 g and 175.2 g. Conversely, SSP + MOP and DAP + MOP treatments resulted in shorter cobs (15.1 
and 16.1 cm) and lower cob weights (85.0 and 130.4 g, respectively).
A similar pattern was observed for both test weight and the number of grains per cob (Table 3). Urea-POLY 
rec. N produced the highest number of grains per cob (390) and the greatest test weight (19.4 g), which were 
statistically comparable to Farmer Practice (NPK) and Urea-POLY bag-for-bag, with 371 grains and 19.3 g and 
369 grains and 18.7 g, respectively. In contrast, SSP + MOP and DAP + MOP controls exhibited the lowest test 
weights (14.8 and 16.4 g) and the fewest grains per cob (162 and 302, respectively).
Grain yield and stover yield (kg ha-1)
Urea-POLY application significantly influenced maize yield, with notable differences among treatments (Fig. 3). 
Grain and stover yields were consistent across years. Urea-POLY rec. N produced the highest grain yield 
(4669 kg ha⁻1), statistically similar to Farmer Practice (NP), Urea-POLY bag-for-bag and Farmer Practice (NPK), 
which yielded 3958, 4093 and 4372 kg ha⁻1, respectively. Stover yield was also highest with Urea-POLY rec. 
N (7978 kg ha⁻1), which was at par with Farmer Practice (NP), Urea-POLY bag-for-bag and Farmer Practice 
(NPK), with yields of 7514, 7675 and 7794 kg ha⁻1, respectively. The lowest grain and stover yields occurred in 
the SSP + MOP and DAP + MOP control treatments (Fig. 4).

Discussion
The maize plant requires a robust fertilizer program for higher productivity due to widespread nutrient deficien -
cies under rainfed conditions in India. Multi-nutrient fertilizers like Urea-POLY are useful for maintaining soil 
fertility and supporting crop growth. One benefit of Urea-POLY is that polyhalite provides a prolonged release of 
nutrients when compared to other fertilisers16. Zhao et al.25 reported that controlled release fertilizers could sig-
nificantly increase aboveground dry matter accumulation in maize compared to common compound fertilizer. Li 
et al.26 highlighted that controlled-release urea enhances nutrient remobilization from vegetative parts to grains, 
thus increasing grain number and weight. Similarly, Ciampitti et al. 27 demonstrated that nitrogen supplied via 
continuous-release formulations significantly improves the conversion of biomass into grain during the reproduc-
tive stages.
In our experiment the benefits of Urea-POLY were seen particularly in the yield, which was significantly 
improved with the Urea-POLY rec. N treatment compared to non-Urea-POLY treatments and was also consis -
tently seen through numerical increases across the measured variables including plant height, leaf area index, cob 
size and hundred grain weight. Although non-significant, the overall improved growth and development trans -
lated into higher yields across both growing seasons.
The improvements in yield may be attributed to the more balanced plant nutrition delivered by the multi-nutri-
ent composition of Urea-POLY , particularly the inclusion of K and S, supply of Ca and Mg present in polyhalite 
in addition to N. This balanced nutrition, along with a continuous availability of nutrients throughout the crop’s 
development, promotes vegetative vigour and reproductive growth, leading to improved grain filling. In previ -
ous studies13,28,29 have shown that application of sulphur and potassium alongside nitrogen and phosphorus can 
significantly improve seed yield and agronomic performance while enhancing sustainability. Gopinath et al. 10 
also reported that application of polyhalite-based fertilizer at rates, in combination with NP fertilizers, resulted in 
a 5.3–12.8% increase in pod yield over NPK + gypsum and control plots in groundnut, which supports the find -
ings of the present study in maize. The rainfall distribution during the crop period has influenced the crop growth 
the yield significantly due to the crop experienced two dry spells at the critical stages which limited the nutrient 
uptake, absorption and availability. Application of Urea-Poly improved the K and S availability which might have 
improved the growth and productivity of maize under limited moisture conditions.
In addition, the application of Urea-POLY enhanced post-harvest nutrient availability in the topsoil layer 
(0–15 cm), retaining higher levels of key macronutrients i.e. N, P, K and S compared to the SSP Control and 
DAP + MOP Control treatments. While both NPK and Urea-POLY applications did not significantly affect 
soil NPK levels, only Urea-POLY led to a notable improvement in sulphur content. However, Ca and Mg was 
retained in Urea-POLY applied treatments and surprisingly SSP + MOP Control treatments showed increase in the

In line with our results, Li et al. 31 reported that the application of polyhalite-containing fertilizers increased the 
availability of Ca, Mg and S in the soil, which was attributed to its multi-nutrient composition and the release of 
CaSO4 and MgSO4. The study further indicated that polyhalite improved soil chemical properties by enhancing

tural production. In contrast the lower levels of Ca and Mg in the soil in DAP + MOP control treatment might 
be due to replacement of fused calcium magnesium phosphate in the soil with Di-Ammonium Phosphate which 
might have decreased soil pH. The decrease in soil pH is closely related to the increase in soil exchangeable alu-
minum and the decrease in soil exchangeable calcium and magnesium32. In this study, we found that the nutrients 
are released in the soil solution immediately after application of Urea-POLY , they are immediately available for 
plants as shown by higher nutrient uptake of N and K, which is as effective as NPK fertilizers whereas P and S 
uptake was only improved by Urea-POLY than other treatments. Several works have supported the strong inter-
actions between S and N metabolism leading to enhanced productivity and nutrient uptake 33. The application of 
co-granulated urea-polyhalite increased the growth and nutrient uptake might be partly in association with the 
complementary roles of N and S in plant metabolism. Thus, adequate S availability may improve the utiliza -
tion of applied N and contribute to greater biomass production and yield. These results are supported by Kumar 
et al. (2022), who demonstrated that application of 100% potassium through polyhalite significantly increased 
nitrogen and phosphorus uptake in grain and straw compared to other potassium sources in wheat 34 studied that 
Potassium supplementation enhanced root growth, acts as osmo-regulator by regulating stomata under dry spells 
and its application improved the yield and quality parameters of cotton and pigeon pea under rainfed conditions. 
Similarly, Li et al. (2020) demonstrated that application of polyhalite resulted in the gradual release of potassium, 
sulphur, magnesium and calcium, which interacted with the soil exchange complex and were efficiently dis -
tributed in the soil solution, thereby maintaining continuous nutrient availability for plant uptake.The increased 
nutrient uptake observed in the present study can be attributed to the balanced supply of N, P, K and S, Ca and 
Mg through Urea-POLY , which are essential for maintaining cell membrane permeability, chlorophyll synthesis, 
improved crop growth and biomass production, enhanced root activity, balanced nutrient supply, and efficient 
translocation, absorption and assimilation of photosynthates. Partial Factor Productivity (PFP) measures the total 
grain yield per unit of nutrient applied which reflects the overall productivity of the nutrient input. Karthikeyan 
et al.35 reported that application of polyhalite which has multinutrient composition had significantly increased 
the uptake of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, sulfur, calcium and magnesium. Greater mobility of Ca and Mg 
in soil and efficient absorption by roots and grain translocation was attributed to the increased uptake of calcium 
and magnesium in their sulphate forms 36. The balanced nutrient supply from polyhalite synchronized nutrient 
availability, reduced nutrient antagonism and thereby improving overall nutrient use efficiency and distribu -
tion within plant tissues. Magnesium, as a critical component of chlorophyll and energy transfer and calcium, 
which is essential for cell wall formation and nutrient transport, further supported physiological activity and 
nutrient accumulation37–39. The slow-release nature of polyhalite ensured a sustained nutrient supply, minimized 
leaching and maintained nutrient availability throughout crop growth, ultimately enhancing nutrient uptake and 
grain nutrient concentration. Agronomic Efficiency (AE) measures the increase in crop yield obtained per unit of 
nutrient applied. It reflects how effectively the plant responds to the added nutrient. In this study, application of 
Urea-POLY as a nutrient source in maize resulted in higher partial factor productivity of P, K and S whereas N

Conclusion
The two-year field study demonstrated that co-granulated urea-polyhalite fertilizer improved maize growth, yield 
attributes, nutrient uptake and post-harvest soil nutrient status under acidic rainfed conditions. Urea-POLY rec -
ommended N and Urea-POLY bag-for-bag treatments performed comparably with conventional farmer fertil -
izer practices for grain and stover yield while also improving nutrient use efficiency parameters. The study also 
indicated improved availability and uptake of sulphur, calcium and magnesium under Urea-POLY treatments. 
However, due to the multi-nutrient nature of the fertilizer source and the single-site experimental conditions, the 
present study does not conclusively establish the individual role of these nutrients or fully confirm the replace -
ment potential of conventional urea across different production environments. Therefore, further long-term and 
multi-location studies along with detailed economic evaluations are required to validate the broader applicability 
of co-granulated urea-polyhalite fertilizer in maize-based production systems.
Limitation of the study
The study has certain limitations that should be considered while interpreting the results. The experiment was 
conducted at a single location for two consecutive years under acidic rainfed soil conditions; therefore, the find-
ings may not fully represent the performance of the co-granulated urea–polyhalite fertilizer under diverse agro-
climatic conditions and soil types. The multi-nutrient nature of the fertilizer source supplied N, K, S, Ca and 
Mg simultaneously, making it difficult to completely isolate the individual effects of specific nutrients on crop 
performance and nutrient use efficiency. NUE was evaluated using PFP and AE rather than a full suite of NUE 
indicators and nutrient-release pattern was not evaluated which provides further research scope. In addition, the 
study primarily focused on agronomic performance and nutrient dynamics, while a detailed economic cost–ben-
efit analysis could not be performed because the product is currently not commercialized. Therefore, further long-
term and multi-location studies with more refined nutrient balancing approaches and economic evaluations are 
required to validate the broader applicability and replacement potential of the co-granulated fertilizer in different 
cropping systems and soil types.
