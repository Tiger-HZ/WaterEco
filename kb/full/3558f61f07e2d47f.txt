Abstract Photosymbioses—associations between heterotrophs and photoautotrophs—are widespread and indispensable in today’s ecosystems. The chloroplasts of algae and land plants, which are at the heart of most of earth’s primary production, stem from ancient photosymbioses. Photosymbioses often combine heterotrophy and autotrophy and must thus efficiently allocate resources between these two costly cellular processes. We currently lack a clear picture of how photosymbioses allocate their valuable cellular resources in response to environmental change.
