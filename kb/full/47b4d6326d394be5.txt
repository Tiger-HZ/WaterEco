The excessive input of nitrogen and phosphorus pollutants into surface water bodies poses a serious threat to the aquatic ecosystem. As an efficient porous adsorbent material, ceramsite shows remarkable potential in the field of simultaneous nitrogen and phosphorus removal. In this study, Fe2O3 catalyzed the decomposition of K2CO3 to generate CO and CO2 gases, leading to the formation of a large number of pore structures in the composite ceramsite. Subsequently, adsorption experiments were conducted on the obtained ceramsite. The regulatory mechanisms of the ceramsite dosage and solution pH on its adsorption performance were revealed.
