The proximity of groundwater sources to cemeteries poses a significant public health risk due to the potential leaching of contaminants from decomposing human remains. This review synthesizes literature on the physicochemical quality of water from wells and boreholes near Nigerian cemeteries, aiming to evaluate contamination levels against WHO and NSDWQ standards. Findings consistently reveal deteriorated water quality, with parameters such as pH (5.8–8.9), electrical conductivity (450–2800 µS/cm), total dissolved solids (210–1950 mg/L), chloride (85–650 mg/L), nitrate (5–120 mg/L), sulphate (40–400 mg/L), and heavy metals like lead (0.02–0.45 mg/L) and iron (0.35–2.10 mg/L) frequently exceeding permissible limits.
