Abstract
The Lower Jurassic sedimentary rocks of the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation in northern Sonora, Mexico has been 
deposited in a forearc basin and its age has been assigned based on the fossil assemblages and detrital zircon U–Pb geo -
chronology data. Neverthless, their geochemical characteristics has not undertaken yet. In this study, the whole rock geo -
chemistry (including major oxides, trace, and rare earth elements) of 23 sandstone samples from the Sierra de Santa Rosa 
Formation is investigated to infer the provenance and paleoweathering conditions of the basin, within the Lower Jurassic 
paleogeographic framework. The concentration of total rare earth elements (ΣREEs) range between 46.5 and 166.6 ppm 
(ave. 118.7 ppm), which is lower than the average Upper Continental Crust (UCC) value (147.80 ppm). Chemical index 
of weathering (CIA), weathering index of Parker (WIP), and A-CN-K diagrams signify the low to moderate chemical 
weathering in the source region. It suggests that the source regions experienced arid to semi-arid climatic conditions dur -
ing the deposition of these detritus. The chondrite normalized REE patterns, negative Eu anomaly, bivariate diagrams and 
elemental ratios like, Eu/Eu*, La/Sc, La/Co, Th/Sc, Th/Co, and Cr/Th indicate that majority of the detritus were derived 
from the felsic rocks with minor contribution from intermediate source rocks.

The Lower Jurassic sedimentary strata are well exposed 
in the Sierra de Santa Rosa locality (Hardy, 1981). These 
sedimentary rocks include conglomerate, coarse- to fine-
grained sandstone, fossiliferous limestone, siltstone, and 
mudstone. The stratigraphy, biostratigraphy, and U–Pb geo-
chronological studies were carried out on these strata by 
several researchers (Damborenea & González-León, 1997; 
González-León et al., 2005; Hardy, 1981; Hodges et al., 
2015, 2017; Pálfy & González-León, 2000; Scholz et al., 
2008). An early Sinemurian to middle Toarcian age assigned 
for the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation that exposed in the 
Sierra de Santa Rosa locality (Hodges et al., 2017; Pálfy 
& González-León, 2000). Previous studies have mainly 
focused on stratigraphy, paleontology, and geochronology 
of the sedimentary rocks that exposed in the Sierra de Santa 
Rosa locality. However, there are still several gaps exist that 
should be bridged by undertaking geochemical study on 
these sedimentary rocks. For the present study, geochemical 
analyses (major, trace, and rare earth elements) were car -
ried out on the sandstone samples collected from the Sierra 
de Santa Rosa locality to determine the geochemical varia -
tions, paleoweathering conditions, and provenance signa -
tures. Also, the results of this study will be compared with 
the geochemical signatures of the nearby Sierra del Álamo 
locality to infer regional variations in paleoweathering con-
ditions and source rocks.
2 Geology of the study area
The basement rocks of northwestern Sonora indicate the 
union of the Mojave, Yavapai, and Mazatzal crustal prov -
inces of southwestern Laurentia (Iriondo et al., 2004; 
Nourse et al., 2005). The Caborca block in Sonora consists 
of late Paleoproterozoic rocks of the Mojave and Yavapai 
provinces, which are intruded by Mesoproterozoic plutons 
(Iriondo & Premo, 2010). The Permo-Triassic and Lower 
Jurassic sedimentary sequence of the El Antimonio Group 
(~ 3.4 km-thick) is well exposed in the Sierra del Alamo, 
~40 km west of Caborca in northwestern Sonora, México 
(González-León, 1997; González-León et al., 2000; Lucas 
et al., 1999) (Fig. 1). El Antimonio Group has been depos -
ited in a forearc basin on the southwestern margin of Lau -
rentia that accumulated marine sediments, and also the 
sediments were derived from the contemporaneous Permo-
Triassic and Jurassic magmatic arcs of North America 
(González-León et al., 2005; Lindner, 2013). The El Anti -
monio Group is considered as one of the most complete 
early Mesozoic sedimentary successions in northwestern 
México (González-León et al., 2000, 2005) that unconform-
ably overlies the Monos Formation of middle Permian age 
(Cooper & Arellano, 1946; Dobbs et al., 2021). This group 
is divided into 16 sequences (sequences I to XVI) (Galindo-
Ruiz, 2019; Galindo-Ruiz et al., 2025; González-León et 
al., 2000, 2005). The age of this group is ranging from the 
late Permian to early Jurassic (Estep et al., 1997; Galindo-
Ruiz et al., 2025; González-León, 1997; González-León et 
al., 2005; Lucas & Estep, 1999; Lucas et al., 1997; Pálfy 
& González-León, 2000; Scholz et al., 2008; Szücs et al., 
2019; Taylor et al., 2001).
The Lower Jurassic sedimentary rocks of the Sierra de 
Santa Rosa Formation show a northwest-to-southeast trend. 
The two most complete sections of this unit are found in the 
Sierra de Santa Rosa and Sierra del Álamo localities (Hardy, 
1981; Hodges et al., 2017; Galindo-Ruíz et al., 2025; 
González-León et al., 2005, 2009). Hardy (1973), who first 
studied the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation from the local -
ity of the Sierra de Santa Rosa that is located ~100 km to 
the southeast of the Sierra del Álamo, and he divided it into 
lower, middle, and upper members (1450 m thick). Based 
on the ammonoid assemblages (Szűcs et al., 2019; Taylor et 
al., 2001), the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation ranges from 
Upper Hettangian to Upper Sinemurian in the Sierra del 
Alamo, and from Upper Sinemurian to Lower Pliensbachian 
in the Sierra de Santa Rosa. However, the detrital zircon 
U–Pb geochronology reveals Sinemurian to early Toarcian 
maximum depositional ages for the Sierra del Álamo local-
ity (Galindo-Ruiz et al., 2025) whereas early Sinemurian, 
Pliensbachian, and middle Toarcian, for the lower, middle, 
and upper members respectively for the Sierra de Santa 
Rosa locality (Hodges et al., 2017).
In the study area, the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation has 
been divided into lower, middle, and upper members (Hardy, 
1981). Subsequently, paleontological and stratigraphic stud-
ies were carried out by Damborenea and González-León 
(1997). They were reported detailed stratigraphy based 
on the three measured sections, with total thickness of 
843 m (Fig. 2). The lower member includes conglomerate,

3.2 Geochemical proxies
The Chemical Index of Alteration (CIA) serves as an impor-
tant parameter for assessing weathering intensity, defined as 
CIA = Al2O3/(Al2O3 +CaO* +Na 2O +K2O) ×100 (Nesbitt 
& Young, 1982). Weathering Index of Parker (WIP: Parker, 
1970) is another proxy to estimate the chemical weather -
ing intensity of the source rocks. WIP index is calculated as 
follows: WIP = [(CaO*/0.7 + 2 Na 2O/0.35 + 2 K2O/0.25 + 
MgO/0.9] x 100. Both CIA and WIP índices are calculated 
using molar percentages of oxides, with CaO* referring as 
CaO present in silicate fractions alone. The CaO* correction 
method is detailed by McLennan et al. ( 1993) and Bock et 
al. (1998) in which CaO values were accepted only if CaO 
<Na2O, when CaO > Na 2O, it was assumed that the con -
centration of CaO is equal to Na 2O. In addition, the Al 2O3 
- (CaO*+Na2O) – K 2O (A-CN- K) ternary plot is consid -
ered as powerful tool to evaluate the weathering trends than 
a simple way of comparing numerical values (Nesbitt & 
Young, 1984).

4.1 Elemental variations
The major oxides concentrations of sandstone samples of 
the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation are given in Supplemen-

higher in the sandstone samples (46.6 to 84.5 wt.%, 5.9 to 
15.3 wt.%, and 0.37 to 22.6 wt.%, respectively) than other 
oxides. The higher content of CaO at certain intervals of 
the studied section is related to the enrichment of carbon -

concentrations of Fe 2O3 and MgO (0.79 to 7.17%; 0.40 to 
3.71%; respectively) than other elements. Large variations 
are observed in K2O and Na2O contents (0.69 to 4.67%; 0.73 
to 5.19%; respectively). Some samples are enriched in Na2O

and other Na-bearing minerals. The concentrations of MnO,

5 Discussion
5.1 Paleoweathering
Composition of sedimentary rocks reflects the intensity of 
source-area weathering, because major elements exhibit 
quite different geochemical behaviors during weather -
ing processes (McLennan, 1989). The intensity of chemi -
cal weathering is calculated by using several índices like 
chemical index of alteration (CIA: Nesbitt & Young, 1982), 
weathering index of Parker (WIP: Parker, 1970), chemical 
index of weathering (CIW: Harnois, 1988), and plagioclase 
index of alteration (PIA: Fedo et al., 1995). Among them, 
the widely used index to understand the paleoweathering 
conditions of source area is CIA. The fresh rocks have CIA 
values around 50 whereas the intensively weathered prod -
ucts such as kaolinite and gibbsite have values up to 100 
(Nesbitt & Young, 1982). The CIA values for the sandstone 
range between 43 and 67 (54±6.8, n=23). Two sandstone 
samples from the lower part of the section (SSRF-04: 46; 
SSRF-06: 43) and one sample from the upper part of the 
section (SSRF-37: 43) have lower CIA values suggesting 
that they contributed by unweathered source rocks. How -
ever, the remaining sandstone samples have significant vari-
ations in CIA values (48 to 67) suggesting that they were 
received sediments from low to moderately weathered rocks 
in the source areas.
The CIA values are plotted on the A-CN-K diagram to 
infer the weathering trend and K-metasomatism (Fedo et al., 
1995; Nesbitt & Young, 1982, 1984), where A, CN, and K 
represent the molar fractions of Al 2O3, CaO + Na 2O, and 
K2O, respectively. The weathering trend of parent rocks is 
subparallel to the A-CN apex on A-CN-K diagram (Nesbitt 
& Young, 1984) whereas sediments affected by K-metaso -
matism mainly plot towards K-axis (Fedo et al., 1995). On 
the A-CN-K diagram (Fig. 4), most of the sandstone sam -
ples plot near and above the feldspar join line and also plot 
subparallel to A-CN axis with moderate dispersion suggest-
ing a low to moderate degree of chemical weathering. This 
is consistent with the CIA values (Supplementary Table 1). 
However, few samples plot just below the feldspar join line 
which is consistent with their low CIA values ((SSRF-04: 
46; SSRF-06: 43; SSRF-37: 43). The CIA values of these 
samples are lower than the average UCC value (UCC: 50) 
that may likely due to unweathered nature of the source 
rocks or enrichment of feldspar in the coarse fractions due 
to sedimentary sorting process.

The CIA and WIP indices are mostly used by various 
researchers to evaluate the weathering history and climatic 
conditions of the source rocks (Armstrong-Altrin et al., 
2013; Galindo-Ruíz et al., 2025; Madhavaraju et al., 2016a, 
2016b, 2019; Olivas et al., 2025; Ramírez-Montoya et al., 
2021; Wani & Mondal, 2010). The usage of CIA vs WIP 
plot readily discriminate between first cycle sediments and 
polycyclic sediments (Garzanti & Resentini, 2016; Gar -
zanti et al., 2013, 2014) and also discriminates the sort -
ing and recycling effects (Guo et al., 2018). The first cycle 
sediments have CIA/WIP ratios between 1 and 2 and show 
negative correlation between CIA and WIP (Garzanti et 
al., 2014). On the CIA vs WIP plot (Fig. 6), most of the 
sandstone samples show inverse relationship with limited 
dispersion indicates that they were largely contributed by 
first-cycle weathering products. However, few samples plot 
near and below the carbonate dilution line suggesting that 
these samples were some extent affected by carbonate dilu-
tion process.
5.2 Sedimentary sorting and recycling
The immobile elements like Al, Ti, and Zr are generally 
enriched during chemical weathering and are considered 
as good proxies for sorting and recycling (Kairouani et al., 
2023; Perri et al., 2021). During sedimentary sorting, SiO 2 
and Al 2O3 may fractionate from quartz and feldspar and 
clay minerals, respectively, whereas TiO2 is present within 
Fe/Ti hydroxides or clay minerals. Similarly, the enrichment 
of Zr in the sedimentary rocks is mainly due to the sorting 
and recycling effects (Caracciolo et al., 2011; Garcia et al., 
1994; Ramos-Vázquez et al., 2018). The sorting and recy -
cling processes can be inferred by using Al2O3-Zr-TiO2 ter-
nary diagrams (Caracciolo et al., 2011; Garcia et al., 1994). 
On this diagram (Fig. 7), sandstone samples plot close to the 
granodiorite suggesting the immature nature of the studied 
samples with poor sorting and recycling effects. In addition, 
the recycling process can be defined by using Th/Sc vs Zr/
Sc plot (McLennan et al., 1993). On this disgram, sand -
stone samples plot along the compositional variation line

5.3 Provenance
The provenance of the sedimentary rocks can be assessed 
using major oxides concentrations, particularly Al 2O3 and 
TiO2 are more useful because Al and Ti are immobile dur -
ing transportation and weathering (McLennan et al., 1990). 
Hence, Al2O3/TiO2 ratio is commonly used to estimate the 
nature of the source rocks (Garcia et al., 1994; Hayashi et 
al., 1997; Olivas et al., 2025; Ramírez-Montoya et al., 2021). 
Al2O3/TiO2 ratios significantly vary in mafic, intermediate, 
and felsic rocks (3 to 8, 8 to 21, 21 to 70, respectively; Sup-
plementary Table 1). The sandstone samples have a wide 
range of Al 2O3/TiO2 ratio (~ 11–31), which indicates their 
derivationfrom both felsic and intermediate source rocks. 
Major oxides-based discriminant function diagram was 
proposed by Roser and Korsch (1988) to infer source rocks 
characteristics. The diagram includes four groups: mafic 
igneous, intermediate igneous, felsic igneous, and quartz -
ose. On this diagram (Fig. 8), most of the sandstone samples 
plotted in the felsic and intermediate fields. However, some 
samples fall in the quartzose region indicating loss of feld -
spar and increase in quartz due to weathering.
The geochemical elements in the sedimentary rocks 
provide lithological characteristics of the source rock, 
particularly REE, becuase they are least soluble, making 
them relatively stable and unaffected by transportation and 
depositional processes (Taylor & Mclennan, 1985). The 
sandstone samples of the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation 
have LREE enriched and virtually flat HREE in the chon -
drite-normalized REE patterns and this characteristic fea -
ture indicate that the sediments were probably derived from 
the felsic source rocks. La/Th against Hf diagram is useful 
to dicriminate between various source rocks such as felsic, 
mixed felsic/mafic, andesitic arc, tholeitic oceanic arc, and 
recycled sources (Floyd & Leveridge, 1987). Low La/Th 
ratio signifies felsic source whereas higher ratio indicates 
tholeitic oceanic arc source. Similarly, lower content of Hf 
suggests the felsic source, whereas increasing concentra -
tion of Hf indicates the recycled nature (Floyd & Leveridge, 
1987). On this diagram (Fig. 9), majority of the sandstone 
samples plot in the felsic source region and also plot closer 
to the Precambrian granites of the Caborca and North Amer-
ican blocks (Iriondo et al., 2004). However, few samples 
fall away from the felsic source and plot along the recycled 
source. In addition, the source rocks characteristics of the 
siliciclastic rocks can also be differentiated using the La/
Sc vs Co/Th diagram (McLennan et al., 1993). The cross 
plot reveals that the relationship between La/Sc and Co/Th 
ratios show higher values of La/Sc compared to Co/Th indi-
cating felsic source (Fig. 10). Further, most of the samples 
plot near to lueco and melanocratic Permo-Triassic gran -
ites (Arvizu et al., 2009), Pinal Schist (Madhavaraju et al., 
2017), Carborca block granite, and North American block 
granite (Iriondo et al., 2004).

Y , Th, U, and La are preferentially separted into melts during 
crystallization (Feng & Kerrich, 1990) and these elements

5.4 Regional variations in paleoweathering and 
provenance
The siliciclastic rocks of the Sierra de Santa Rosa Forma -
tion are well exposed in the localities of Sierra del Alamo 
and Sierra de Santa Rosa. The geochemical data of the 
present study (Sierra de Santa Rosa section) is compared 
with Sierra de Álamo section (Galindo-Ruiz et al., 2025) to 
infer the regional variations in paleoweathering condition 
and provenance signatures. The geochronological studies 
confirm their contemporaneous sedimentation occurred in 
these localities during the Early Jurassic (Galindo-Ruiz et 
al., 2025; Hodges et al., 2017).
Although there are a wide variety of indices, the CIA 
(Nesbitt & Young, 1982) has proven to be a reliable source 
of information to determine the degree of weathering in 
ancient rocks (Galindo-Ruíz et al., 2025; Pacheco-Olivas et 
al., 2025; Ramírez-Montoya et al., 2021). The sandstones 
of this study show a range of CIA values from 43 to 67 
(n=23), suggesting low to moderate chemical weathering

some samples (Fig. 12). In addition, on the M-F-W diagram

show some variations in weathering trends.

6 Conclusions
The chemical composition of the Lower Jurassic sandstones 
in the Sierra de Santa Rosa Formation, northern Sonora, 
Mexico is studied to infer the provenance and paleoweather-
ing conditions of the basin within the Lower Jurassic paleo-
geographic framework. The concentrations of SiO 2, Al2O3 
and CaO are enriched in the sandstone samples than other 
oxides. Al 2O3 show significant positive correlation with 
other oxides (Fe 2O3, MgO, K 2O, and TiO 2) supports their 
association with aluminosilicates. Among trace elements, 
Ba, Zr, Sr, and Rb have higher concentrations than other
