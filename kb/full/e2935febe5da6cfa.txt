Concerns regarding the toxicity of nanoparticles like ZnO and TiO2 to aquatic ecosystems have been raised by their extensive use in industry and the environment. The indicator organism, the brackish and marine rotifer species Brachionus, is used in this work to assess the acute toxicity of TiO2 and ZnO following a 24 hours exposure. According to the findings, ZnO had the lowest toxicity under filtered circumstances without light (324,14 mg/L) and the maximum toxicity under unfiltered settings with light (less than 6,00 mg/L). On the other hand, in all circumstances, TiO2 did not exhibit any harmful effects at the measured doses (200 mg/L, 400 mg/L, 600 mg/L, 800 mg/L, and 1000 mg/L). This work emphasizes the potential of B. plicatilis as an indicator species for environmental risk assessment and adds to the ecotoxicological data on nanomaterials in Vietnam. As a result, it encourages the quick assessment of novel materials prior to their use in the remediation of environmental contaminants.
