Remediating heavy-metal pollution in flowing groundwater is challenging because powder adsorbents may agglomerate or wash away, while static tests do not represent dynamic transport. To address these limitations, a PET-Fe-BDC composite geotextile was prepared by in situ synthesis following PAM pretreatment and evaluated for Pb(II) and Cd(II) retention in continuous soil-column seepage experiments. Compared with unamended soil, the composite-geotextile-amended system markedly retarded the downward migration of both ions. In the 0–5 cm retardation layer, cumulative removal efficiencies exceeded 80% up to te′, and the breakthrough times of Pb(II) and Cd(II) increased to approximately 6.9 and 6.4 times those in the unamended system, respectively.
