Divergent microbial functional pathways and
declining redundancy in lake sediments
across eutrophication gradients
John K. Pearman 1 , Jack Sissons 1, Joseph Kanyi Kihika1, Sean Waters2,
Andrew B. H. Rees3,J a m i eD .H o w a r t h3,M a r c u sJ .V a n d e r g o e s4 &
Susanna A. Wood5
Microbial communities underpin nutrient cycling across aquatic ecosystems
and are strongly inﬂuenced by human disturbance, yet the implication for
functional redundancy remains poorly understood. Lake eutrophication is a
global threat to freshwater systems,primarily driven by human activities
within lakes and surrounding catchments. We hypothesize that eutrophication
alters functional redundancy of key metabolic pathways in lake sediments. To
test this, we conducted a national scale metagenomic study of surface sedi-
ments from 144 New Zealand lakes spanning a broad nutrient gradient.
Increasing eutrophication is associated with reduced taxon-based functional
redundancy, the potential of multiple species to perform the same function,
indicating a narrower taxa pool supporting core functions. In contrast,
abundance-based functional redundancy, characterized by the abundance of
organisms that can perform the function, showed pathway-speciﬁcr e s p o n s e s
across trophic gradients. For example, nitriﬁcation and denitriﬁcation have
greater abundance-based functional redundancy under higher nutrient con-
ditions, whereas phosphorus transport shows reduced redundancy. By erod-
ing microbial functional redundancy, eutrophication may increase
susceptibility of microbial communities to environmental perturbations,
potentially compromising their ability to sustain key metabolic processes. As
lakes play key roles in biogeochemical cycles, the lower stability of vital bio-
geochemical pathways in lake sediments is likely to have an impact across
ecosystems globally.
Human activity is leaving an indelible mark on Earth ’se c o s y s t e m s ,
rapidly restructuring the compo sition and functioning of ecolo-
gical communities 1,2. Freshwater lakes are among the most sen-
sitive sentinels of this transformation 3. They respond not only to
stressors that directly impact them, but also integrate dis-
turbances from their catchments, making them early warning
systems for anthropogenically induced change at local to
regional scales 4,5.

ecosystems, yet they are profoundly altered by human disturbance6,7.

The capacity of ecosystems to withstand such recon ﬁguration
depends on functional redundancy, the coexistence of species that
have similar functional roles but different ecological niches12– 14.H i g h e r
redundancy can stabilize ecosystem functioning by maintaining key
processes even when individual taxa/organisms decline or are lost12,15.
Recent work by Fässler et al. 16 used information-theoretic oper-
ationalizations to investigate functional redundancy at the single-trait
level. They de ﬁned functional redundancy as the capacity of a com-
munity to maintain a given function despite the depletion or loss of
individual species, enabling the investigation of trends in different
functions across environmental pressure gradients. This deﬁnition of
functional redundancy can be characterized in different ways. Firstly,
taxon-based functional redundancy assesses whether the community
contains multiple species capable of performing a certain function16.
Secondly, abundance-based functional redundancy takes into account
the abundance of organisms that can perform a certain function in the
community16. While higher functional redundancy generally increases
ecosystem resilience and stability13, its effect depends on the type of
environmental change. Changes that fundamentally restructure
metabolic niches, for example, shifts in redox conditions or electron
donor/acceptor availability, may alter the abundance or composition
of entire functional groups, even when redundancy within those
groups is high. Therefore, functional redundancy provides robustness
to many disturbances, but extreme or transformative environmental
changes may still impact key ecosystem processes.
Investigations into the prevalence of microbial functional redun-
dancy have produced contrasting results. Some studies report strong
correlations between taxonomic changes and function, suggesting low
functional redundancy in surface water samples in marine 17 and
freshwater environments18, whereas others have found high levels of
functional redundancy at global scales 19, including marine
environments20. Despite the central role of sediment microbes in lake
ecosystem functioning, the extent of their functional redundancy
remains poorly understood. Studies from Poyang Lake, China, have
reported limited functional redundancy21, while a paleolimnological
investigation in Aotearoa, New Zealand, indicated low microbial resi-
lience to natural perturbations22. The extent to which these commu-
nities can maintain functional stability under increasing anthropogenic
pressure is a critical but unresolved question.
Lakes across Aotearoa New Zealand provide a natural experiment
for testing how anthropogenic pressures shape microbial function
across multiple environmental gradients. A national-scale lake study8,23
sampled lake sediments across Aotearoa New Zealand, of which 144
lakes were selected for investigation in the current study (Fig. 1A).
Spanning 12° of latitude and encompassing pronounced gradients
(Fig. 1B) in trophic state (sediment bacterial trophic index (SBTI),
sediment nutrient concentrations (phosphorus (TP), nitrogen (TN),
TN:TP ratio and sulfur (S)),, elevation (Elev) and catchment land-use
(high productivity grassland (HPG)), these systems provide a unique
opportunity to examine how lake and catchment degradation in ﬂu-
ence microbial community function. The microbial communities in
these lake sediments vary taxonomically across a trophic gradient 8,
providing an opportunity to focus on this as a measure of lake
degradation and investigate its relationship with the functional
potential of microbial communities in lake sediments. To assess this,
we applied metagenomics to our nationwide lake surface sediment
sample set to test two hypotheses: (1) The functional potential of
sediment microbiomes varies across trophic states, with key biogeo-
chemical pathways (e.g., carbon cycling, nutrient transformations)
responding differently to the speciﬁc pressures associated with each
trophic state; and (2) Functional redundancy buffers key biogeo-
chemical processes across trophic gradients; however, redundancy
declines under increasing anthropogenic pressure.
In this work, we show that eutrophication leads to a reduction in
taxon-based functional redundancy, with metabolic pathways
becoming increasingly reliant on fewer taxa to function. The decline in
taxa-based functional redundancy in eutrophic lake sediments sug-
gests heightened vulnerability to both natural and anthropogenic
disturbances. Abundance-based functional redundancy showed
pathway-speciﬁc responses to eutrophication, with increases in
redundancy evident for key metabolic pathways (especially nitrogen-
related). The increase in abundance-based functional redundancy in
key pathways alongside shifts in community composition could

Results
The non-redundant database constructed for bacteria in lake surface
sediments resulted in a total of 156,537,568 open reading frames
(ORF), of which 60,876,927 were annotated against the KEGG (Kyoto
Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) database. A total of 14,469 KEGG
Orthologs (KO) terms had a class-level taxonomic classiﬁcation.
To assess if there was a relationship between the taxonomic
composition of the lake sediments and the functional potential, a
Mantel correlation test was undertaken between the current functional
data and previously published 16S rRNA bacterial metabarcoding8.T h e
correlation revealed a weak but signi ﬁcant (Mantel r = 0.146,
p = 0.004) relationship between the microbial taxonomic composition
and their functional potential.
To assess if there were statistical differences in the functional
potential of microbial communities in lake sediments, multivariate
analysis was undertaken. The microbial functional potential (KEGG
KO’s) showed clear differences across trophic state (adonis: F = 4.035,
p < 0.001; Fig. 2A). At a national scale, functional potential of speciﬁc
pathways also varied signi ﬁcantly with environmental factors, most
strongly with trophic state (as determined using a surface bacterial
trophic index [SBTI7]) and the extent of high-productivity grassland in
the catchment (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Data 2).

lakes to 2.7% and 0.2% in supertrophic lakes, respectively.

Discussion
We conducted a national-scale investigation of the functional potential
of lake sediment bacterial communities, examining trends in func-
tional redundancy. Lake sediments contain both organisms that have
settled from the water column and actively living sediment-dwelling
microbes, providing an integrated record that is less sensitive to short-
term temporal variation. Previous work by Pearman et al. 7 demon-
strated that a sediment bacterial trophic index could be developed,
which correlates strongly with the Trophic Level Index, a measure of
eutrophication in the water column. By sampling the surface sediment,
we were able to investigate recent patterns in the lake's history and
understand how functional potential varies across a trophic gradient.
Our results revealed a statistically signiﬁcant, albeit weak, positive
relationship between 16S rRNA taxonomic community differentiation
at the ASV level and functional potential, as determined by annotated
ORFs from metagenomics. The positive correlation suggests that
changes in the taxonomy of the community contribute to variations in
functional potential at a national scale, aligning with observations from
other ecosystems such as prairie soils24 and in the surface seawaters of
the Bay of Banyuls sur Mer (France) in the Mediterranean17. However,
the weak correlation observed in the present study indicates that
functional potential can be shaped by environmental drivers distinct
from those in ﬂuencing taxonomic composition, a ﬁnding which is
consistent with ocean microbiomes 20. Although our study did not
directly measure ecosystem processes, variation in functional
redundancy may inﬂuence the stability and resilience of key ecosystem
functions under environmental stress, highlighting the ecological
relevance of the patterns we observe.
Previous bacterial metabarcoding research on lake sediments of
Aotearoa-New Zealand7,8 and lake planktonic and sediment microbes
globally6,25 highlighted that agricultural land use intensi ﬁcation and
subsequent eutrophication were substantial driver of the taxonomic
composition of lake microbial communities. A similar trend was noted
in the metagenomics in the present study, with signi ﬁcant shifts in
functional potential amongst lake sediments of different trophic
states. Genes indicative of functional pathways showed differential
responses to trophic state. For instance, a positive relationship was
noted between the abundance (copies per million mapped reads) of
indicative genes for methanogenesis and organic matter (Fig. 2B).
Methane production in lakes is primarily undertaken in the sediments
or anaerobic sections of the water column26,27 where anoxic conditions
and high organic matter prevail. In eutrophic lakes, increased primary
productivity often leads to greater deposition of organic matter in the
sediments, creating conditions that favor methanogenesis 28.T h i si s
exempliﬁed in the current dataset with the increasing abundance of
Methanomicrobia in more eutrophic lakes compared with its near
absence in oligotrophic lake surface sediments. In oligotrophic lakes,
where surface sediment is likely still aerobic, it is likely that the
methanogenesis is occurring in d eeper sediment horizons where
anaerobic conditions prevail. In contrast, indicative genes for phos-
phorus transport were negatively correlated with trophic state and
high productivity grassland in the catchment but positively correlated
with TN:TP. This suggests that the potential for active transport of
phosphorus is lower in the eutrophic lakes, where phosphorus is more
readily available, as indicated by the TN:TP correlation. The intensiﬁ-
cation of agriculture in catchments surrounding lakes has led to
increased eutrophication and is a signi ﬁcant issue globally6,29– 31. His-
torically, intensive dairy farming in Aotearoa-New Zealand has used
relatively high levels of phosphorus fertilizer31,32 and leaching of ferti-
lizers has potentially provided additional levels of phosphorus into
lake sediments as indicated by the opposite trends between high
productivity grassland and TN: TP for phosphorus transport. These
results suggest that there is great er functional potential for active
phosphorus transport when phosphorus availability in the sediment
increases, compared to nitrogen availability.
Across the functional pathways investigated, there was, in general,
as i g n iﬁcant decrease in taxon-based functional redundancy as trophic
state increased, except for the rTCA, which had a signi ﬁcant positive
relationship (Table 1). This reduction in taxon-based functional
redundancy agrees with the observation that lakes of higher trophic
states, in general, have few taxonomic classes involved in the func-
tional pathways compared to more oligotrophic lakes. Given that
functional redundancy has been shown to enhance stability and resi-
lience to perturbations13, this suggests that various metabolic path-
ways in eutrophic lake sediments may be more prone to disruption as
they are reliant on fewer taxa, which could be lost during substantial
perturbations. However, alternate stable state theory suggests that
there is a switch from macrophyte dominated clear water states and
de-vegetated turbid states driven by changes in the intensiﬁcation of
the catchment (and subsequent eutrophication)33,34, which is in con-
trast to the reduction in taxon-based redundancy observed in this
study. However, while taxon-based functional redundancy is declining
along the trophic gradient, the signiﬁcant increase in abundance-based
functional redundancy for key metabolic pathways such as nitri ﬁca-
tion, denitriﬁcation and nitrogen ﬁxation was observed. Shifts in the
composition of the sediment community, such as increases in Gam-
maproteobacteria, Desulfobacteria and Syntrophia and decreases in
Alphaproteobacteria and Nitrospiria with increasing trophic state were
also noted. The combination of increased functional redundancy in
important metabolic pathways and shifts in the community

Abundance-based functional redundancy had a positive correla-
tion with trophic state for nitri ﬁcation, denitriﬁcation and nitrogen
ﬁxation, and a negative correlation for P transport, assimilatory nitrate
reduction and assimilatory sulfate reduction. These differences are
likely driven by variations in environmental conditions that selectively
favor abundance changes of speciﬁc organisms, resulting in changes to
the abundance-based functional abundancy. Both nitri ﬁcation and
denitriﬁcation pathways showed an increase in abundance-based
functional redundancy with higher trophic state, while there was a
reduction in taxon-based functional abundance (only signi ﬁcant for
denitriﬁcation). This indicates that in these higher trophic states fewer
classes are contributing to the functional pathway (reduction in taxon-
based functional redundancy) while the abundance of the taxa
undertaking the pathway is more evenly distributed (increase in
abundance-based functional redundancy). This increased evenness in
the contribution of taxa in the community to the functional pathway
can be highlighted by the reduction in the deviation away from the 1:1
line between community abundance and functional abundance of
Gammaproteobacteria for both pathways and Nitrospiria in the nitri-
ﬁcation pathway. Gammaproteobacteria were the dominant compo-
nent in the community for both pathways, and this contribution
increases in lakes with a higher trophic state, which is in agreement
with previous work indicating that Gammaproteobacteria are copio-
trophs that are often prevalent in freshwater sediment
environments7,35 and have preferences for organic-rich sediments,
typical of eutrophic lakes affected by agricultural run-off, in lakes of
the Eastern Plain of China 36. The contribution of Gammaproteo-
bacteria to the functional abundance also increases, which is likely in
part due to the reduction of Nitrospiria in more eutrophic lakes for the

This study demonstrates distinct trends in functional redundancy
across a trophic gradient for multiple pathways, focusing on functional
potential in surface sediments using metagenomics. Future research
could employ metatranscriptomics to target living sediment microbes,
revealing how the active members of the community respond across
the trophic gradient. It should also be noted that the investigation was
undertaken at the class level due to limitations in the taxonomic
classiﬁcation of ORFs. Therefore, there is a potential that at ﬁner
taxonomic levels, such as the species level, different patterns in
functional redundancy might be observed across the trophic gradient.
As the sequencing of microbial genomes increases, the ability to assign
taxonomy to ORFs will increase, and future studies will have the
potential to investigate functional redundancy at the species level.
Similarly, this study was undertaken at the KEGG Orthologs level and
investigations at different levels (e.g., ORFs or metabolic pathways)
could give a different result with the ﬁner the scale studied the less
likely for redundancy to be present12.
Overall, our ﬁndings indicate that eutrophication drives a reduc-
tion of functional redundancy, especially taxon-based redundancy,
with metabolic pathways increasingly reliant on fewer taxa to function.
Because functional redundancy is a key determinant of ecosystem
stability, its decline in eutrophic lake sediments suggests heightened
vulnerability to both natural and anthropogenic disturbances. How-
ever, increases in abundance-based functional redundancy for key
metabolic pathways (especially nitrogen-related) and shifts in the
community composition could enable the creation and stabilization of
alternative states as the increased abundance-based functional
redundancy counteracts the reduction in taxon-based redundancy.
Given the central role of lakes in global nutrient and carbon cycling,
including greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane,
which can buffer against climate change impacts42 these changes are
likely to have consequences that extend beyond individual lake sys-
tems. Shifts in the functional redundancy of metabolic pathways in
lakes could cascade and in ﬂuence connectivity and feedback across
terrestrial– aquatic– atmospheric interfaces.
Methods
Sampling and extraction
Sediment samples used in this study were collected from 144 lakes
(183 samples; some lakes had triplicate samples taken from the same
point in the lake) across Aotearoa New Zealand between October 2018
and April 2021 (Fig.1 and Supplementary Data 1). Lakes were accessed
with the permission of regional councils, local iwi and landowners. The
lakes were selected to be representative of the various natural and
a n t h r o p o g e n i cc o n d i t i o n se x p e r i e n c e db yl a k e sa c r o s sA o t e a r o aN e w
Zealand. The sampled lakes ranged from sea level (e.g., Lakes Moeraki,
Paringa) to 1799 m (Alta Tarn). Lake depths varied from ~ 1 m (e.g.,
Runanga Lake, Tomahawk Lagoon) to greater than 400 m (e.g., Lakes
Manapouri and Te Anau). In general, sampling was undertaken at the
deepest parts of the lakes except for the lakes deeper than 100 m,
where sampling was undertaken in shallower bays.
Samples of sediment were taken from the top 2 cm of the ponar
grab and collected in 500 mL containers and shipped chilled to the
laboratory. For metagenomics subsamples (~20 g) were taken and
frozen (−20 °C) until processing. DNA was extracted using an Alkaline
Buffer Extraction Protocol 43 in dedicated UV-sterilized laboratories
under sterile conditions. The VAHTS Universal Plus DNA Library Prep
Kit was used for library preparation, and samples were sequenced on
an Illumina (San Diego, CA, USA) NovaSeq 6000 machine by Azenta
Life Science (Suzhou, China).
Lake characteristics are provided in Supplementary Data 1. Sur-
face sediment samples were taken for sediment biogeochemistry
analysis, with nutrient and elemental analysis detailed in Pearman
et al.8.B r i eﬂy, total nitrogen and total organic carbon were analyzed
using catalytic combustion and separated using a thermal conductivity
detector. Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry was used to
assess the elemental concentrations (see Supplementary Data 1).
Catchment characteristics were determined using the Land Cover
Database version 5 (LCDBv5, Landcare Research New Zealand Ltd.,
Lincoln, New Zealand). Trophic state was determined for each lake
using the Sediment Bacterial Trophic Index (SBTI) as detailed by
Pearman et al. 7, The SBTI consists of numeric values, which were
subsequently grouped into four trophic states with Oligotrophic:
SBTI < 3; Mesotrophic: SBTI 3 – 4; Eutrophic: SBTI 4 – 5; Super-
trophic: SBTI > 5.
Bioinformatics
Raw reads from 220 samples were used for the creation of the non-
redundant gene database (only 183 samples, which had a trophic state,
were used for the analysis of the data). The raw data is stored in the
Aotearoa Genomic Data Repository under the accession https://doi.
org/10.57748/3jdy-rc84. These were processed using Trimmomatic v
0.3944 using ILLUMINACLIP and the following options: LEADING:5
TRAILING:5 SLIDINGWINDOW:10:15 MINLEN:30. Cleaned reads were
then assembled using Megahit v1.2.945 for each individual sample, and
contigs shorter than 500 bp were removed with seqmagick v0.8.6 46.
Using the software Prodigal v2.6.3 47 ORFs were predicted for each
sample. The ORFs from each sample were concatenated together, and
am m s e q sv 1 4 . 7 e 2 8 448 database was created. A non-redundant gene
database was constructed, adapting the approach of Feng et al.49.T h i s
database was then clustered at 95% identity with 90% of the shorter
gene being aligned (--cluster-mode 2 --min-seq-id 0.95 -c 0.9 --cov-
mode 1) using linclust from mmseqs, creating a non-redundant gene
database of New Zealand lake sediments and ﬁltered to remove
sequences shorter than 100 bp. Reference sequences for each cluster
were retrieved using mmseqs and annotated using EggNOG-mapper
v2.1.1250,51 using DIAMOND v2.1.8.16252.
For read mapping, the non-redundant gene database wasﬁltered
to those sequences that were annotated against the KEGG database
and reads for each sample were annotated using bwa mem53 and then

ﬁrst ﬁnding the reads per kilobase (RPK) and then dividing by the total
RPK and scaling to a million reads.
Taxonomic classiﬁcation of the raw reads to give an assessment of
the community composition was undertaken by sylph v0.9.054 against
the GTDB55 v232 database. Taxonomic classiﬁcation of the ORFs was
undertaken using mmseqs against the GDTB55 ORF database for bac-
teria using the default parameters. Taxonomical classi ﬁcation of the
coding sequences against the NCBI non-redundant protein database,
based on Xiong et al. 56, although for this study we were interested in
eukaryotic sequences, was undertaken using blastp within DIAMOND
(max target seqs 10). The lowest common ancestor was found for each
coding sequence based on the hits that had scores with greater than
50% similarity and were within 10% of the highest bitscore for each
ORF. Where the blastp results were attributed to eukaryotes, they were
added to the bacterial taxonomy from GDTB.
Statistical analysis
The script for analysis can be found at https://github.com/
jkpearmanbioinf/Lake-Metagenomic-Redundancywith ﬁles to run
the script available at https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.ﬁgshare.30881612.
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was undertaken on the
environmental dataset within R v4.5.1 57 to give an indication of the
environmental gradients present. The environmental data (nutrients,
catchment characteristics, depth and elevation) were scaled and
Article https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77595-w
Nature Communications Article in Press
centered using the R package vegan v2.7-558 with the PCA assessed and
visualized using ggfortify v0.4.1459 and ggplot2 v3.3.660.
Multivariate analysis was undertaken at the KEGG Orthology level
(KO) on a Hellinger-transformed data table with a distance matrix
constructed using Bray-Curtis. Visualization was undertaken in ggplot
after dimensionality reduction using t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor
Embedding (t-SNE) in the Rtsne package v0.17 61. Mantel correlations
were undertaken using the functional data in the current paper and
compared against a subset of the 16S rRNA metabarcoding at the ASV
level data published in Pearman et al.8.
Indicative genes (KEGG Orthologs; KO numbers) were determined
for speciﬁc pathways based on the literature (Supplementary Table 3)
and ORFs that were annotated with these KO numbers were subset for
further investigation. For lakes with triplicate samples, the mean
copies per million mapped reads for the lake was calculated.
Correlation analysis was undertaken in R using cor.test with the
Spearman rank algorithm, with p-value adjustments undertaken using
the Benjamini-Hochberg equation. Heatmap plots were constructed
based on ORFs that were classiﬁed at the class level. For the heatmap
analysis, ORFs with only higher-level classiﬁcations (e.g., phylum) were
removed from the dataset.
Taxon-based and abundance-based functional redundancy were
determined using the equations of Fässler et al. 16. For lakes with tri-
plicate samples, mean values of functional redundancy were calculated
before further analysis. For the abundance vector, the relative con-
tribution of each taxonomic class (only genes classi ﬁed at the class
level were included) was calculated across the whole dataset, then
subset for each functional pathway to the classes that could perform
the function. The contribution of these taxa was then normalized, and
the n_reference was determined as the total number of classes that
could undertake the function across the whole dataset for comparison
between samples, as suggested by Fässler et al. 16. For the function
vector, the counts per million of the class to the speciﬁc function were
determined and normalized. Differences in functional redundancy
amongst different functional pathways and within functional pathways
across trophic state were tested using the Kruskall-Wallis test and
pairwise comparisons undertaken using the Wilcox test in R. Spear-
man’s correlations were undertaken between the functional redun-
dancy results and the SBTI 7 to assess the effect of trophic state on
functional redundancy.
