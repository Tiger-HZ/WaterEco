Vachellia tortilis is an important multipurpose tree species widely distributed in arid and semi-arid regions of Africa, where it contributes to rangeland restoration, agroforestry development and ecosystem stability. Despite its ecological and socio-economic importance, large-scale propagation of this species is often constrained by low and irregular germination due to physical dormancy imposed by a hard and impermeable seed coat. This study assessed the effects of different seed-preparation treatments on the early viability and germination performance of Vachellia tortilis seedlings under uniform nursery soil conditions. Seeds were collected from mature trees in Tingatinga Village, Longido District, Tanzania, and the experiment was conducted at the Tanzania Livestock Research Institute (TALIRI) nursery in Siha District. A Completely Randomised Design (CRD) with seven treatments was used, including untreated seeds (control), cold-water soaking, hot-water treatment, mechanical scarification, roasting, burning, and acid scarification with sulfuric acid. Each treatment was replicated three times with 100 seeds per experimental unit. Germination was monitored weekly for four weeks, and cumulative and final germination percentages were recorded. Data were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test and for homogeneity of variance using Levene’s test. Analysis of Variance (ANOVA). The results showed clear differences in germination performance among the treatments. Mechanical scarification produced the highest germination percentage and the fastest seedling emergence, followed by hot-water treatment and acid scarification. Seeds soaked in cold water showed moderate germination, while untreated seeds exhibited relatively low germination due to persistent seed coat dormancy. No germination was observed in seeds subjected to roasting and burning treatments, suggesting that excessive heat exposure likely damaged the seed embryo. The study concludes that appropriate seed-preparation treatments are essential for overcoming physical dormancy and improving germination success of Vachellia tortilis. Mechanical scarification is recommended as the most effective and practical method for enhancing seed germination in nursery production, while hot-water treatment and acid scarification may serve as alternative approaches where appropriate. These findings provide useful guidance for improving propagation techniques to support dryland afforestation, agroforestry and ecological restoration initiatives.
