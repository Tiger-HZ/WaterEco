ABSTRACT A high level of nutrient removal in onsite wastewater treatment systems is critical for avoiding eutrophication and groundwater contamination, especially in phosphorus- and nitrogen-sensitive areas. This study evaluates modifications to a pilot-scale Multi-module Biochar Filter (MmBF) system aimed at enhancing nitrogen and phosphorus removal through material amendments. A preliminary column experiment identified sawdust as an efficient carbon source for supporting denitrification, motivating its use in anoxic modules containing biochar and 10 wt% and 30 wt% sawdust. Polonite, a calcium-silicate material, was also tested at the same dosages in the final aerobic module for phosphate removal.
