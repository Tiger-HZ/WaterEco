ABSTRACT 
This paper focused on how Quality Assurance (QA) and continuous improvement systems assisted in the effective 
implementation of the Education and Training Policy 2023 at St. Augustine University of Tanzania (SAUT). The 
Education and Training Policy 2023, following standards established by the Tanzania Commission for Universities 
(TCU), prioritized accountability, high -quality educational programmes, and student -centred learning. Fulfilling 
these goals, however, presented challenges because universities sought to establish and maintain effective internal 
Quality Assurance systems. Adopting a library -based research approach, the paper analysed national education 
policy documents, SAUT’s Quality Assurance frameworks, TCU guidelines, and scholarly literature on quality 
management in higher education. Guided by Quality Assurance and continuous improvement theories, the study 
explored how well-designed internal Quality Assurance systems assisted higher education institutions in adhering 
to national policies, monitoring programme quality, and promoting institutional learning. The findings emphasized 
the role of evidence -based Quality Assurance practices in strengthening policy implementation processes and 
highlighted the importance of developing a culture of continuous improvement through regular evaluation and 
enhancement of academic and administrative processes. The findings provided useful insights for SAUT and other 
universities in strengthening Quality Assurance frameworks, improving instructional standards, and enhancing the 
implementation of reforms outlined in the Education and Training Policy 2023. 
 
Keywords: Quality Assurance, Continuous Improvement, TCU Standards, Education and Training Policy 2023 & 
Monitoring and Evaluation 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Higher Education Quality Assurance is a structured 
institutional framework designed to ensure that courses 
of study, instructional methods, scientific investigations, 
and support services meet acceptable standards and 
continue to improve over time. The phi losophy of 
Quality Assurance is based on accountability and 
academic excellence, highlighting that higher education 
providers must regularly evaluate their performance 
with the aim of delivering relevant and high -quality 
education to all students. 
Internationally, frameworks such as United Nations 
Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization 
[UNESCO] (2021) guidelines on Higher Education 
management and the global trend toward outcomes -
based education have strengthened the requirement for

strong internal quality frameworks. These initiatives 
require higher -learning institutions to create 
mechanisms for program analysis, accreditation, 
employee development, and student responses in order 
to ensure effective responsiveness of educational 
services to changing national and global pressures. 
In Tanzania, the role of Quality Assurance has been 
clearly presented in the Education and Training Policy 
2023 and through standards established by the 
Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU). The 
policy emphasizes the provision of competence -based 
curricula, credible assessment systems, and efficient 
university decision -making structures. While these 
standards reflect a national commitment to developing 
Higher Education quality, actual implementation at the 
institutional level, including at St. Augusti ne University 
of Tanzania (SAUT), remains a dynamic and 
transforming process. Many universities continue to 
face challenges associated with coordination, capacity 
building, and the development of a sustainable culture 
of continuous improvement. Strengtheni ng internal 
Quality Assurance frameworks is therefore vital for 
harmonizing SAUT practices with Education and 
Training Policy 2023 reforms and TCU regulatory 
demands. 
 
Statement of the Problem 
Although the Education and Training Policy 2023 and 
the Tanzania Commission for Universities standards 
underscore the strength of internal Quality Assurance 
systems in Higher Education institutions, several 
universities are still experiencing problems in 
establishing effective and sustainable frameworks for 
fostering academic excellence. At St. Augustine 
University of Tanzania, many Quality Assurance 
mechanisms prevail, encompassing program reviews, 
examination regulations, and internal audits, yet the 
extent to which these frameworks are fully matched with 
national policy standards and TCU benchmarks 
remains ambiguous. In reality, some academic and 
administrative processes are implemented with no 
sufficient monitoring, evaluation, and feedback 
processes tha t are vital for continuous improvement. 
More importantly, narrow integration of outcomes -
based assessment, limited use of institutional data, and 
inconsistent follow -up on evaluation reports have 
limited attempts to enhance overall university 
performance. These challenges indicate a critical gap 
between policy expectations and actual institutional 
practices. The main issue of this study is to assess how 
well SAUT’s internal Quality Assurance frameworks 
respond to Education and Training Policy 2023 
demands and determine obstacles that hinder their full 
effectiveness and contribution to quality education 
provision. 
 
LITERATURE REVIEW 
Idea of Quality Assurance and Continuous 
Improvement in University Education 
Quality Assurance in University Education refers to 
planned processes through which academic institutions 
ensure that academic programs, instruction, learning, 
assessment, and administrative services meet valid 
standards. It involves planned strategies, gu idelines, 
and mechanisms formulated to safeguard and enhance 
the quality of institutional outputs. Continuous 
improvement frameworks are ongoing institutional 
processes that promote regular review, reflection, and 
upgrading of practices in order to get better results over 
time. 
Globally, Quality Assurance has emerged as a central 
cornerstone of Higher Education management due to 
rapid growth of universities, internationalization, and 
demand for accountability. Frameworks such as the 
UNESCO Guidelines for Quality Provision in Cros s-
Border Higher Education focus on the that institutions 
must develop powerful internal mechanisms for 
monitoring academic excellence. The philosophy of 
continuous improvement is obtained from models like 
Total Quality Management (TQM) and the Plan –Do–
Check–Act (PDCA) cycle, which observe quality as an 
adaptive rather than a one-time achievement. 
In Tanzania, national review of university quality is 
authorized to the Tanzania Commission for Universities 
(TCU). TCU sets standards and accreditation criteria 
that direct universities in program approval, staff 
qualifications, student assessment, and or ganizational 
governance. The Education and Training Policy (2023) 
further emphasizes that universities should strengthen 
internal Quality Assurance directorates and promote a 
culture of self -assessment and evidence -based 
decision-making. On the other hand, empirical literature 
shows that many institutions still function with weak 
Quality Assurance frameworks, limited expertise, and 
inadequate integration of improvement systems into 
daily activities.

(Objective 1) Internal Quality Assurance 
Frameworks at SAUT Concerning Education and 
Training Policy 2023 
The first directing objective of this study examined 
internal Quality Assurance frameworks at St. Augustine 
University of Tanzania in line with the needs of the 
Education and Training Policy 2023. Literature indicates 
that effective policy implementation i n universities 
largely depends on the strength of institutional Quality 
Assurance mechanisms. According to Materu (2019), 
internal Quality Assurance units are responsible for 
translating national policies into university -level 
regulations, manuals, and procedures that guide 
academic practice. 
Researches conducted in Tanzanian universities show 
that institutions with well-established Quality Assurance 
directorates perform better in curriculum review, 
moderation of examinations, and staff development. 
Luhanga (2020) observed that internal Quality 
Assurance frameworks should include good policies on 
program design, teaching evaluation, student feedback 
mechanisms, and regular audits. At SAUT, the 
Directorate of Quality Assurance is assumed to oversee 
these functions and guarantee that education ref orms 
needed by the Education and Training Policy 2023, like 
competence-based curricula and outcomes -based 
assessment, are duly mainstreamed. 
Nevertheless, current assessments suggest that 
knowledge and application of Quality Assurance 
procedures among employees at university campuses 
are uneven. Kapinga (2021) states that Quality 
Assurance systems in private institutions often stay 
centralized, with few trickle -down to departments and 
faculties. For SAUT to comply entirely with the 
Education and Training Policy 2023, internal literature 
suggests decentralizing Quality Assurance roles, 
strengthening senate committees, and ensuring that 
improvement strategies are backed by realistic funds 
and trained personnel. With no such reinforcement, 
national policy goals risk remaining largely progressive. 
 
(Objective 2) Alignment between SAUT Quality 
Assurance Processes and TCU Standards 
The second objective analyzed how good SAUT Quality 
Assurance processes align with TCU standards. 
Alignment in this category means that institutional 
processes correspond directly with national 
benchmarks for academic excellence. TCU Standards 
and Guidelin es (revised 2022) outline little 
requirements on staff-to-student ratios, qualifications of 
academic staff, library resources, infrastructure, and 
governance structures. 
Empirical literature stresses that institutions must 
regularly map their Quality Assurance processes 
against these standards. Mkumbo (2020) argues that 
accreditation tools can only be effective if universities 
internalize their requirements through structu red self -
evaluation frameworks. In Tanzanian contexts, Hyera 
and Mutashubirwa (2021) discovered that many 
institutions struggle with compliance because of 
overcrowded classes, limited laboratories, and 
inadequate documentation. 
For SAUT, maintaining alignment needs periodic 
curriculum review, external examination moderation, 
tracer studies, and program audits, all of which are key 
TCU expectations. However, findings on institutional 
Quality Assurance in Tanzania show that some 
universities consider TCU standards as external 
conditions rather than internal guiding tools. This 
research shows last -minute preparations during 
accreditation visits instead of routine quality process. 
Literature therefore recommends that SAUT adopt 
powerful internal benchmarking, develop 
comprehensive Quality Assurance manuals reflecting 
TCU requirements, and strengthen data management 
frameworks. Such alignment would assist the institution 
not only maintain accreditation status but also develop 
academic credibility and competitiveness nationally and 
internationally. 
 
(Objective 3) Significance of Monitoring, 
Evaluation, and Learning in Continuous 
Improvement 
The third objective of this study assessed the 
significance of Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning 
(MEL) in supporting continuous improvement 
frameworks at SAUT. Monitoring and evaluation are 
reflective management units that enable universities to 
track p rogress, measure effectiveness, and make 
informed adjustments. Literature on Higher Education 
quality management emphasizes that Quality 
Assurance cannot succeed without powerful 
Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEL) 
mechanisms. 
According to the Education and Training Policy 2023, 
institutions are advised to establish structured 
frameworks for collecting and analyzing performance 
data on teaching excellence, research output, student 
achievement, and community engagement. PDCA and 
Results-Based Management theories both stress that

regular evaluation and feedback encourage institutional 
learning and evidence-based reforms. 
In the Tanzanian university context, Kapinga and Amani 
(2022) noted that Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning 
(MEL) frameworks are often weak, fragmented, and 
poorly coordinated. Many campuses miss enough data 
on program performance, graduate employability , or

Literature suggests that SAUT strengthen Monitoring,

structures. By emb edding monitoring and evaluation

Assurance from a compliance exercise into a 
fundamental force of educational quality. 
 
METHODOLOGY 
This study used a qualitative, library -based research 
design to evaluate the effectiveness of Quality 
Assurance and continuous improvement frameworks at 
St. Augustine University of Tanzania (SAUT) within 
Mwanza. The library-based approach was regarded as 
appropriate because the research was based on 
examining policy documents, institutional systems, and 
scholarly literature rather than conducting primary field 
data. The design enabled an in -depth investigation of 
how internal Quality Assurance processes relate to the 
needs of the Education and Training Policy 2023 and 
standards set by the Tanzania Commission for 
Universities (TCU). 
Findings for the research were collected through 
comprehensive document analysis. Key sources 
comprised the Education and Training Policy 2023, 
TCU Quality Assurance Guidelines, SAUT internal 
Quality Assurance manuals, strategic plans, and 
published academic journals on quality management in 
Higher Education. These records were purposefully 
chosen based on their significance to the guiding 
objectives of the paper. The use of multiple 
documentary sources supported the researcher to 
triangulate information an d get a balanced 
understanding of the topic. 
Thematic analysis was used as the main method of data 
interpretation. Information obtained from the reviewed 
documents was organized, coded, and grouped into 
key themes like internal Quality Assurance frameworks, 
alignment with TCU standards, and the funct ion of 
monitoring and evaluation in continuous improvement. 
This analytical procedure assisted in revealing 
strengths, weaknesses, and gaps in SAUT’s Quality 
Assurance processes. The methodology further offered 
a conceptual perspective for understanding th e role of 
institutional accountability and outcomes -based 
education. 
The investigation was conducted within the 
environment of Higher Education reforms in Tanzania, 
with SAUT functioning as the central focus of analysis. 
By drawing on existing empirical studies and official 
policy frameworks, the research design guaranteed 
research quality, policy relevance, and practical 
applicability. The methodological approach was 
therefore helpful in generating findings and 
recommendations directed at strengthening Quality 
Assurance frameworks at SAUT and similar universities 
countrywide. 
 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS 
(Objective 1): Internal Quality Assurance 
Frameworks at SAUT concerning Education and 
Training Policy 2023 Guidelines 
1. Understanding and Awareness of Quality 
Assurance Frameworks 
The research shows that St. Augustine University of 
Tanzania (SAUT) has internal mechanisms targeted at 
promoting academic excellence and accountability. 
University leaders and lecturers demonstrate a general 
understanding that Quality Assurance is fundamental for 
fulfilling the reforms presented in the Education and 
Training Policy 2023. This knowledge makes up an 
important foundation for aligning institutional frameworks 
with national hopes. 
2. Current Internal Quality Assurance Frameworks 
Results show that SAUT operates formal Quality 
Assurance divisions, programme review committees, 
and academic audits intended to maintain standards in 
pedagogy, assessment, and curriculum design. These 
frameworks reflect an organizational involvement in 
ensuring that academic courses meet minimum quality

standards. In several departments, regular moderation of 
examinations, peer review of courses, and student 
assessments are already practiced in the framework of 
Quality Assurance implementation. 
3. Early Efforts in Implementing Policy 
As per Education and Training Policy 2023, SAUT has 
initiated the use of outcomes-based education principles, 
improving documentation practices, and strengthening 
accreditation processes. The study finds that internal 
reviews have promoted improvements lik e updating 
course outlines, standardizing assessment methods, 
and enhancing record keeping. However, these 
initiatives remain largely procedural and have not yet 
converted into comprehensive transformation across all 
faculties. 
4. Limitations Concerning Education and Training 
Policy 2023 Requirements 
Despite the existence of Quality Assurance frameworks, 
a key limitation is that many academic staff and 
management have not received sufficient training on 
modern Quality Assurance processes demanded by 
Education and Training Policy 2023. Therefore, 
implementation places emphasis on compliance rather 
than innovation. Some Quality Assurance interventions 
are irregular and depend heavily on personal drive 
instead of being fully integrated into planning at the 
institution level and budgeting. 
5. Monitoring and Sustainability Matters 
The studies further indicate that while Quality Assurance 
reports are developed, follow-up actions are often weak. 
There is low allocation of dedicated funds for continuous 
improvement initiatives. This indicates that internal 
Quality Assurance frameworks at SAUT, though 
functional, still need significant strengthening to 
effectively promote the holistic implementation of the 
Education and Training Policy 2023. 
 
(Objective 2): Alignment of SAUT Quality Assurance 
Practices and TCU Requirements 
1. General Alignment with TCU Guidelines 
The analysis indicates that SAUT’s Quality Assurance 
processes are partially aligned with standards set by the 
Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU). Core 
practices like program approval, external examination, 
and institutional self -assessment follow TCU 
frameworks. This alignment ensures that SAUT remains 
active as a recognized higher-learning institution within 
Tanzania’s regulatory environment. 
2. Alignment between Curriculum and Assessment 
Results indicate that most SAUT academic programs 
reflect TCU requirements regarding credit hours, grading 
frameworks, and qualification benchmarks. Departments 
comply with TCU formats in designing curricula and 
preparing examination rules. These processes illustrate 
that national standards shape internal university activities 
to a considerable extent. 
3. Non-Uniform Implementation across Units 
However, the study reveals that alignment is not uniform. 
While some faculties maintain powerful Quality 
Assurance routines that reflect TCU expectations, others 
lag behind due to skills gap and resources. The absence 
of standard digital systems for tracki ng Quality 
Assurance indicators undermines the university’ ability to 
maintain continuous alignment with evolving TCU 
standards. 
4. Institutional Infrastructure and Administrative 
Systems 
TCU emphasizes modern learning contexts and efficient 
academic governance. The results show that SAUT still 
faces constraints in fully meeting these hopes. 
Overreliance on manual practices, poor data 
management, and limited stakeholder engagement 
weaken the depth of alignment. This disconnect affects 
the capability to benchmark learning achievement 
effectively as demanded by TCU. 
5. Need for Powerful Institutional Coordination 
Overall, the results suggest that SAUT has made notable 
progress aligning with TCU standards but remains 
constrained by structural and operational gaps. 
Strengthening coordination between Quality Assurance 
units, academic units, and management is therefore 
crucial if SAUT is to reach full and consistent compliance 
with Tanzania Commission for Universities standards. 
 
(Objective 3): Significance of Monitoring, Evaluation, 
and Learning in Continuous Improvement at SAUT 
1. Adoption of Monitoring and Evaluation 
Mechanisms 
The study confirms that SAUT is aware of monitoring and 
evaluation (M&E) as tools for strengthening institutional 
performance. Frequent academic audits, tracer studies, 
and departmental reports are used to assess program 
success. These frameworks give valuable information on 
pedagogical quality, student performance, and 
administrative efficiency. 
2. Poor Feedback Utilization 
Despite these mechanisms, the study reveals that 
monitoring and evaluation (M&E) results are not 
effectively used for knowledge acquisition and decision

making. Quality Assurance and monitoring and 
evaluation (M&E) reports often stay as documents rather 
than guiding concrete reforms. Capacity limitations in 
data analysis and institutional research undermine the 
possibility of monitoring systems to drive re al 
improvement inside the university. 
3. Insufficient Digital Monitoring 
Another main challenge is the limitation of integrated 
digital platforms for continuous tracking of Quality 
Assurance indicators. With no reliable data frameworks, 
SAUT is still struggling to evaluate progress toward 
Education and Training Policy 2023 goal s or TCU

4. Academic Learning Culture 
Results also reveal that a system of continuous 
improvement is still developing at SAUT. While some 
leaders encourage reflective practices, many staff 
observe Quality Assurance as an external requirement 
rather than an opportunity for institutional learning. The 
absence of frequent training, incentives, and structured 
learning forums weakens the significance of monitoring 
and evaluation (M&E) in shaping innovation. 
5. Gaps in Stakeholder Coordination 
Lastly, the findings reveal poor coordination between 
Monitoring and Evaluation units, academic departments, 
and top management. This disconnect undermines 
institutional memory and the sharing of best processes. 
To reach effective continuous improvement, SAUT needs 
powerful follow-up procedures, better data systems, and 
deliberate strategies for turning monitoring and 
evaluation into institutional knowledge development. 
6. General Assessment 
In general, monitoring and evaluation systems at SAUT 
are available but play only a secondary role in promoting 
continuous improvement. Enhancing these systems in 
accordance with Education and Training Policy 2023 and 
TCU standards is vital for promoting a culture of 
continuous improvement in universities in order to 
improve Quality Assurance mechanisms. 
 
CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
Conclusions 
This study has examined the role of strengthening 
internal Quality Assurance frameworks at St. Augustine 
University of Tanzania in relation to the needs of the 
Education and Training Policy 2023 and the standards 
established by the Tanzania Commission for 
Universities. The analysis indicates that SAUT 
possesses established Quality Assurance structures, 
including directorates, academic audits, program 
reviews, and internal evaluation mechanisms. These 
networks offer a relevant foundation for promoting 
accountability and improving academic quality. The 
research further indicates that when Quality Assurance 
practices are consistently applied, they support the 
university monitor pedagogy and learning processes, 
assess curriculum relevance, and ensure adherence to 
national Higher Education criteria. 
However, the results also show that existing Quality 
Assurance frameworks are not yet fully effective in 
fostering continuous improvement. Weaknesses remain 
in areas like systematic monitoring, feedback uptake, 
workforce capability in modern Quality Assura nce 
approaches, and coordination of evaluation results 
toward strategic decision making. Under certain 
circumstances, Quality Assurance processes seem 
routine and procedural rather than transformative. These 
limitations show that policy intentions under Ed ucation 
and Training Policy 2023 can only be attained if Quality 
Assurance frameworks become more practical, data -
driven, and well-coordinated. Overall, efficient alignment 
with TCU standards demands not only formal 
frameworks but also a powerful institutional culture that 
promotes quality and continuous education. 
 
Recommendations 
To enhance Quality Assurance and continuous 
improvement frameworks at SAUT, a number of 
measures are recommended. First, the university should 
dedicate funds to the professional development of staff. 
Academic and administrative personnel require several 
trainings on Quality Assurance instruments, outcomes-
based education, and continuous improvement 
frameworks like PDCA. This will build skills in making 
audits and using evaluation results effectively. 
Second, SAUT is advised to strengthen its internal 
monitoring and evaluation processes. Quality Assurance 
mechanisms are expected to include clear performance 
indicators, continuous program assessments, and 
structured feedback mechanisms including students , 
academic staff, alumni, and workplace stakeholders. 
Based on these data, evidence -based reforms can be 
supported. 
Another key area is documentation and reporting. The 
university is advised to improve record keeping, internal 
academic audits, tracer studies, and digital Quality 
Assurance management mechanisms so that quality 
information can be easily accessed and applied.
