The instability of holed limestone in water-rich karst areas is controlled by both hole-induced stress concentration and water-induced weakening. Its damage evolution is reflected not only by mechanical degradation, but also by changes in acoustic emission event structures and energy release paths. To clarify the damage accumulation mechanism of holed limestone under different water saturation degrees, limestone specimens containing a central through-going circular hole were prepared and tested at five target saturation degrees of 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%. Uniaxial compression tests were carried out with synchronous AE monitoring.
