Thematic cartography is a fundamental tool for quantitative analysis of ecological dynamics, environmental monitoring, and regional planning. The present contribution explores the increasing availability of geo-cartographic information and discusses its relevance in spatial policy analysis. By illustrating their structure, objectives, and applications, we specifically focused on European CORINE Land Cover (CLC) datasets and the Italian “Carta 48 della Natura” project, taken as representative of continental and national geo-spatial databases. The CLC initiative provides harmonized information on land-use and land cover at the continental level, allowing for a comprehensive analysis of territorial dynamics over time − including urbanization, loss of agricultural land, and changes in vegetation cover, among others. The “Carta della Natura” project – developed by a national authority involved in empirical research on environmental monitoring and surveillance – inte grates remote sensing methodologies, image interpretation, field surveys, and geographical analysis with the aim at producing multiscale maps of physiographic units and habitats. The chapter specifically highlights the central role of these operational tools in ecological planning, biodiversity protection, and the definition of spatial policies, through their use in defining environmental quality, landscape fragmentation, ecological sensitivity, and anthropogenic pressure. Finally, the latest innovations and developments in thematic cartography have been illustrated, being regarded not only as cartographic products, but also as a constantly evolving cognitive process.
