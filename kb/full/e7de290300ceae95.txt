Abstract Background Land-use intensification has enhanced impacts on water quality of South American rivers. A long-term database was compiled from public monitoring data to evaluate the temporal variation in water quality in the mid-lower Uruguay River from 1987 to 2019. Forty-two parameters (physicochemical, nutrients, metals, microbiological, pesticides, persistent, bioaccumulative and toxic substances and hydrological) in three sites across 440 km of the river were selected. Bayesian Generalized Linear Models were applied to understand the temporal and discharge-related drivers of each water quality parameter.
