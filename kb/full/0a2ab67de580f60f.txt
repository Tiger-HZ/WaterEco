Thermophilic cyanobacteria successfully colonize thermal gradients within hot springs, implying evolved mechanisms to cope with temperature-induced oxidative stress. Although polyphosphate (polyP) is known to contribute to oxidative stress resistance, its specific role in thermophilic cyanobacteria remains poorly understood. To address this, this study established a temperature gradient (30–70 °C) and used phloretin (polyP synthesis inhibitor) plus exogenous polyP to investigate polyP metabolism, redox homeostasis, photosynthetic function, and growth of Thermosynechococcus sp. FJSJ-1 from hot spring.
