Sacred forests contribute to biodiversity conservation and forest carbon storage, yet quantitative information on their carbon and nutrient characteristics remains limited. This study quantified standing aboveground biomass (AGB) and aboveground carbon (AGC) stocks and characterized soil carbon–nutrient concentrations across Edge, Intermediate, and Core spatial zones in Kaya Kauma Sacred Forest, coastal Kenya. Forest inventories from ten permanent 20 × 20 m plots containing 384 trees representing 42 indigenous species were used to estimate AGB and AGC. Soil samples collected at four depths (0–10, 10–20, 20–50, and 50–100 cm) were analysed for total organic carbon (TOC), total nitrogen (TN), soil organic matter (SOM), soil inorganic carbon (SIC), and calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
