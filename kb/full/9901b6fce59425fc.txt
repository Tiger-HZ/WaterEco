Most salmonids, as well as many other freshwater fish species in the Northern Hemisphere, have been reported to show some form of daytime sheltering behaviour over the winter. Previous work has shown that temperatures around 6-8°C trigger the onset of this sheltering behaviour. However, fish from colder environments would be expected to respond differently to temperature than fish from warmer environments. The incidence of sheltering at different temperatures between two Icelandic populations of both juvenile Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) and juvenile Arctic char (Salvelinus alpinus) was examined by quantifying the use of refuges in identical controlled laboratory conditions.
