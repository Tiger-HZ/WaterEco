Abstract Reliable prediction of groundwater recharge from rainfall is crucial to water management but is scientifically challenging. Previous studies have demonstrated that caves, mines, and tunnels provide useful “windows” for observing rainfall recharge timing and thresholds (i.e., amount of rainfall in a given time period causing a change in dripping from the void ceiling). Using drip loggers at sites across the Australian continent, we find that annual median rainfall recharge thresholds are statistically similar across 16 sites. However, thresholds vary by season. In climates dominated by winter rainfall, recharge is highly unlikely to occur during summer and autumn, even for high volume rain events.
