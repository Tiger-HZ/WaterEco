Permanent sedimentation ( 210 Pb and 137 Cs), sediment mixed layer thicknesses, and mixing coefficients ( 7 Be) were measured in the St. Lawrence River in order to evaluate the importance of sediment retention in the particulate matter budget and to characterize the system's resilience to changing contaminant loads. Net sediment accumulation (1 to &gt; 18 kg·m -2 ·year -1 ) is observed at most sites deeper than 4.5 m located outside the main channels. Annual sediment retention in the lakes ranges from 1.5% (Lake St. Pierre) to 17% (Lake St. Francis) of their total load of suspended solids. 7 Be profiles indicate that the average mixed layer thickness, mixed layer mass, and mixing coefficient are 3.3 ± 0.2 cm, 17.8 ± 1.7 kg·m -2 , and 14.9 ± 2.8 cm 2 ·year -1 , respectively.
