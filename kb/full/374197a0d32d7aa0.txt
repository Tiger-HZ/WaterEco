Spain represents the most extensively developed and best-documented case of formal water markets within the European Union. However, three decades of experience show that, although the legal framework has progressively expanded, actual market activity has remained mainly confined to drought periods. Between 1994 and 2025, five market-based instruments operated across six river basin districts (temporary transfer contracts, inter-sectoral permanent transfers, water banks, buy-backs, and quality swaps), constituting a broader institutional repertoire than previously recognised in the literature.
