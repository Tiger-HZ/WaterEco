Study regions The wandering reaches of the upper Tarim River. Study focus Under the combined effects of mountain meltwater recharge, changing flow and sediment conditions, and human activities, planform adjustment in the upper Tarim River poses risks to bank stability and water conveyance. Using multi-temporal remote sensing images from 2005 to 2023, this study delineated channel water boundaries and centerlines, and quantified channel width, sinuosity, lateral migration rate, and water-boundary expansion and contraction. Hydrological and sediment records were further used to construct high-flow metrics, and hierarchical clustering and correlation analysis were applied to identify interannual adjustment types and their associated flow and sediment conditions. New hydrological insights for the region Planform evolution showed clear downstream differences. Reach R1 exhibited slowly increasing sinuosity and persistent bend-scale centerline oscillation. Reach R2 was wide, shallow, and relatively stable, with limited sinuosity variation. Reach R3 showed increasing sinuosity, bend development, and stronger local main-channel oscillation. Interannual adjustment was classified into three types, including strong migration with main-channel oscillation, expansion and widening with bank retreat, and contraction adjustment with bend development. These types corresponded to different high-flow and sediment conditions. Multi-stage water-boundary overlay analysis identified persistently expanding and contracting active bank sections, which were further assigned to the three adjustment types. The results provide a basis for risk identification and segmented, type-specific management of wandering reaches in arid inland rivers.
