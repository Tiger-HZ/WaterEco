Evaluate the effect of integrated nutrient management 
practices on growth, productivity, nutrient uptake and 
economics of wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) 
 
Sumit Kumar Singh, Balkrishna Namdeo, Brijkishor Rajput, Munesh 
Kumar, Ashok Kumar Verma, Udham Singh Gautam, Ram Batuk Singh, 
Ram Pyare, Tej Bahadur Singh, Bhayankar and Anshuman Singh 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9d.6513 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment was conducted during the rabi seasons of 2022 –23 and 2023 –24 at the Agricultural 
Research Farm, Rabindranath Tagore University, Raisen, Madhya Pradesh, to evaluate the effect of 
integrated nutrient management practices on growth, productivity, quality, nutrient uptake and economics of 
wheat. The experiment was laid out in a Randomized Block Design with eleven nutrient management 
treatments and three replications. The treatments comprised different levels of recommended dose of 
fertilizers (RDF) applied alone or in combination with farmyard manure (FYM), zinc sulphate (ZnSO₄) and 
biofertilizers (Azotobacter + phosphate-solubilizing bacteria, PSB). The results revealed that application of 
100% RDF along with Azotobacter + PSB inoculation recorded the highest grain yield (43.62 q ha⁻¹), straw 
yield (66.41 q ha⁻¹), gross monetary return (₹97,021 ha⁻¹), net monetary return (₹66,552 ha⁻¹) and benefit: 
cost ratio (3.18). The same treatment also recorded the highest leaf area index, chlorophyll content (at 
60DAS), crop growth rate, relative grow th rate nutrient concentration and total uptake of nitrogen, 
phosphorus and potassium. Application of 100% RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ and 100% RDF + ZnSO₄ @ 25 kg 
ha⁻¹ also produced significantly higher growth, biological yield, nutrient uptake and economic retu rns 
compared with the recommended dose of fertilizers alone. The study indicated that application of 100% RDF 
in combination with Azotobacter and PSB inoculation is the most efficient and economically viable 
integrated nutrient management practice for achieving higher wheat productivity, improved nutrient uptake, 
superior grain quality and sustainable wheat production under the agro -climatic conditions of Madhya 
Pradesh. 
 
Keywords: Wheat, integrated nutrient management, Azotobacter, phosphate-solubilizing bacteria, farmyard 
manure, zinc sulphate, nutrient uptake, productivity. 
 
Introduction 
Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is one of the most important cereal crops and serves as a major 
source of calories and protein for nearly one -third of the world's population. Globally, wheat 
occupies about 220 million hectares with an annual production exceeding 790 million tonnes, 
making it one of the most widely cultivated food crops (FAOSTAT, 2023) [5]. India is the second-
largest producer of wheat after China, contributing significantly to global wheat production. The 
crop plays a pivotal role in ensuring food and nutritional security and contributes substantially to 
the country's agricultural economy (Government of India, 2024) [7]. The continuous increase in 
food demand due to rapid population growth has necessitated higher agricultural productivity. 
During the last few decades, increased wheat production has largely been achieved through the 
intensive use of chemical fertilizers. However, continuous and imbalanced application of 
inorganic fertilizers has resulted in declining soil organic carbon, nutrient imbalance, reduced 
fertilizer uses efficiency and deterioration of soil physical, chemical and biological properties 
(Ladha et al., 2022; Bhattacharyya et al., 2021) [10, 2]. Such practices have also affected long-term 
soil fertility and sustainability of crop production systems . Integrated Nutrient Management 
(INM) has emerged as an effective approach for sustaining crop productivity while maintaining 
soil health. The concept of INM involves the combined use of inorganic fertilizers, organic

manures and biofertilizers to ensure balanced nutrient supply and 
improve nutrient use efficiency (Mahajan et al ., 2021) [11]. 
Organic sources such as farmyard manure (FYM) improve soil 
structure, water-holding capacity, microbial activity and nutrient 
mineralization, while biofertilizers like Azotobacter and 
phosphate-solubilizing bacteria (PSB) enhance biological 
nitrogen fixation and phosphorus availability, thereby improving 
nutrient uptake and crop growth (Meena et al., 2020) [12]. 
The integration of organic and inorganic nutrient sources has been 
reported to enhance photosynthetic efficiency, root growth, dry 
matter accumulation and yield attributes, ultimately leading to 
higher grain yield and improved grain quality (Kumar et al ., 
2022). Furthermore, micronutrients such as zinc play a vital role 
in enzyme activation, chlorophyll synthesis, protein metabolism 
and carbohydrate translocation, which directly influence crop 
productivity and nutritional quality (Alloway, 2008; Cakmak, 
2008) [1, 3]. 
Madhya Pradesh is one of the leading wheat -producing states of 
India. However, continuous cultivation and inadequate 
replenishment of nutrients have resulted in declining soil fertility 
and nutrient -use efficiency in many wheat -growing regions. 
Under these conditions, integrated nutrient management offers a 
promising strategy for improving crop productivity, maintaining 
soil fertility and enhancing economic returns. Although several 
studies have demonstrated the beneficial effects of integrated 
nutrient management in cereal crops, information on the 
comparative performance of different nutrient management 
strategies under the agro -climatic conditions of central Madhya 
Pradesh remains limited. Therefore, the present investigation to 
evaluate the ef fect of different integrated nutrient management 
practices on growth, physiological parameters, yield, grain 
quality, nutrient uptake and economics of wheat, and to identify 
an efficient and sustainable nutrient management strategy for 
wheat cultivation under the prevailing agro-climatic conditions of 
Madhya Pradesh. 
 
Materials and Methods 
A field experiment was conducted during two consecutive rabi 
seasons (2022 –23 and 2023 –24) at the Agricultural Research 
Farm, Rabindranath Tagore University, Raisen, Madhya Pradesh, 
India, to evaluate the effect of integrated nutrient management 
(INM) practices on the growth, productivity, quality, nutrient 
uptake and economics of wheat ( Triticum aestivum L.). The 
experiment was laid out in a Randomized Block Design (RBD) 
with three replications comprising eleven nutrient management 
treatments. The treatments included: T₁ –Control, T₂–50% RDF 
(60:30:20 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹), T₃ –75% RDF (90:45:30 kg 
N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹), T₄ –100% RDF (120:60:40 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O 
ha⁻¹), T₅–125% RDF (150:75:50 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹), T₆ –150% 
RDF (180:90:60 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹), T₇–100% RDF + FYM @ 
5 t ha⁻¹, T₈–100% RDF + ZnSO₄ @ 25 kg ha⁻¹, T₉–100% RDF + 
Azotobacter + phosphate -solubilizing bacteria (PSB), T₁₀ –75% 
RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹ and T₁₁–75% RDF + ZnSO₄ @ 25 kg ha⁻¹. 
The experimental soil was sandy clay loam in texture with nearly 
neutral reaction (pH 7.78 and 7.82), medium in organic carbon 
(0.52 and 0.56%), low in available nitrogen (245.60 and 252.40 
kg ha⁻¹), medium in available phosphorus (16.80 and 17.25 kg 
ha⁻¹) and available potassium (315.40 and 322.80 kg ha⁻¹) during 
2022–23 and 2023 –24, respectively. The recommended dose of 
fertilizers wa s supplied through urea, diammonium phosphate 
(DAP) and muriate of potash (MOP). Full doses of phosphorus 
and potassium along with half of the nitrogen were applied as 
basal at the time of sowing, while the remaining nitrogen was top-
dressed in two equal s plits at the first irrigation (Crown Root 
Initiation stage) and before the second irrigation. Farmyard 
manure was incorporated into the soil approximately two weeks 
before sowing in the respective treatments. Zinc sulphate and 
biofertilizers (Azotobacter a nd PSB) were applied as per 
treatment details, with wheat seeds inoculated with the respective 
biofertilizer cultures immediately before sowing. The wheat crop 
was raised following the recommended package of agronomic 
practices for the region. Observations on growth parameters, 
physiological traits, yield attributes, grain and straw yield, 
chlorophyll content, crop growth rate, relative growth rate, 
nutrient content, nutrient uptake and economics were recorded 
using standard procedures.

Nitrogen content in grain and straw was estimated by the micro -
Kjeldahl method, phosphorus by the vanadomolybdate yellow 
colour method and potassium using a flame photometer. Nutrient 
uptake was calculated by multiplying nutrient concentration with 
the respective grain and straw yields.The data obtained during 
both years were subjected to analysis of variance (ANOVA) 
appropriate for a Randomized Block Design as described by 
Gomez and Gomez (1984) [6]. Treatment means were compared 
using the critical difference (CD) test at the 5% level of 
significance. Since the error variance s were homogeneous over 
the years, pooled analysis was performed and the pooled results 
are presented and discussed. 
 
Results and Discussion 
Growth and physiological attributes 
Integrated nutrient management (INM) treatments significantly 
influenced the physiological growth parameters of wheat, viz., 
Leaf Area Index (LAI), chlorophyll content (SPAD value), Crop 
Growth Rate (CGR) and Relative Growth Rate (RGR) (Table 1). 
The combi ned application of inorganic fertilizers with organic 
and biological nutrient sources consistently enhanced crop growth 
over the sole application of chemical fertilizers and the control. 
The highest LAI (3.45) was recorded under T₉ (100% RDF +

Azotobacter + PSB inoculation), which was significantly superior 
to all other treatments. This was closely followed by T₇ (100% 
RDF + FYM @ 5 t ha⁻¹) and T₈ (100% RDF + ZnSO₄ @ 25 kg 
ha⁻¹), recording LAI values of 3.33 and 3.29, respectively, 
whereas the lowest LAI (2 .88) was observed under the control 
(T₁). The increase in LAI under integrated nutrient management 
treatments may be attributed to balanced nutrient availability, 
enhanced cell division and expansion, and improved canopy 
development resulting from better s oil fertility and microbial 
activity. A similar trend was observed for chlorophyll content 
(SPAD value). Treatment T₉ recorded the highest SPAD value 
(42.15), followed by T₇ (41.55) and T₈ (40.60), while the control 
treatment recorded the lowest value (32. 85). The higher 
chlorophyll content under integrated nutrient management 
treatments may be due to improved nitrogen availability through 
the combined application of fertilizers and biofertilizers. 
Azotobacter enhances biological nitrogen fixation, whereas PSB 
improves phosphorus availability, thereby promoting chlorophyll 
synthesis, delaying leaf senescence and improving photosynthetic 
efficiency. Zinc supplementation further contributes to 
chlorophyll formation and enzyme activation. Crop Growth Rate 
(CGR) also differed significantly among the treatments. The 
maximum CGR (10.50 g m⁻² day⁻¹) was recorded under T₉, 
followed by T₇ (10.25 g m⁻² day⁻¹) and T₈ (9.75 g m⁻² day⁻¹), 
whereas the lowest CGR (7.00 g m⁻² day⁻¹) was recorded under 
the control treatment. The enhanced CGR under integrated 
nutrient management may be attributed to sustained nutrient 
availability throughout the crop growth period, resulting in 
greater dry matter production and efficient utilization of solar 
radiation. A similar response was ob served for Relative Growth 
Rate (RGR). Treatment T₉ registered the highest RGR (0.062 g 
g⁻¹ day⁻¹), followed by T₇ (0.060 g g⁻¹ day⁻¹) and T₈ (0.058 g g⁻¹ 
day⁻¹), whereas the minimum RGR (0.044 g g⁻¹ day⁻¹) was 
recorded under the control. The improvement i n RGR indicates 
enhanced physiological efficiency and biomass accumulation 
under integrated nutrient management. The combined application 
of FYM, ZnSO₄ and biofertilizers improved soil physical, 
chemical and biological properties, resulting in better root 
growth, higher nutrient uptake and increased photosynthetic 
activity. Similar findings have also been reported by Meena et al. 
(2020) [12], Mahajan et al. (2021) [11], Kumar et al. (2022) [9] and 
Ladha et al . (2022) [10], who observed that integrated nutrient 
management significantly improved physiological growth traits 
through enhanced nutrient availability and soil health. 
 
Yield studies 
Integrated nutrient management significantly improved grain 
yield, straw yield and biological yield of wheat, wher eas harvest 
index remained statistically non-significant (Table 1). Among the 
treatments, T₉ (100% RDF + Azotobacter + PSB inoculation) 
recorded the highest grain yield (43.62 q ha⁻¹), straw yield (66.41 
q ha⁻¹) and biological yield (107.65 q ha⁻¹), followed by T₇ (42.42, 
65.33 and 107.74 q ha⁻¹, respectively) and T₈ (41.78, 64.12 and 
107.74 q ha⁻¹, respectively). The lowest grain, straw and 
biological yields (34.84, 57.85 and 92.69 q ha⁻¹, respectively) 
were recorded under the control (T₁). It might be due to the higher 
yield under integrated nutrient management treatments may be 
attributed to balanced nutrient supply, improved nutrient 
availability and enhanced nutrient use efficiency, which promoted 
better crop growth, higher dry matter accumulation and e fficient 
translocation of photosynthates to developing grains. The 
combined application of Azotobacter, PSB, FYM and ZnSO₄ also 
improved soil biological activity and nutrient uptake, thereby 
increasing crop productivity. Similar results have also been 
reported by Jat et al . (2018) [8], Patel et al . (2020) [13] and 
Choudhary et al. (2021) [4]. Harvest index was not significantly 
influenced by the treatments; however, the highest value 
(40.49%) was recorded under T₈, followed by T₁₀ (40.00%) and 
T₇ (39.37%), while the lowest (37.52%) was observed under T₂. 
The non-significant variation in harvest index indicates that both 
grain yield and biomass increased proportionately under 
integrated nutrient management practices. Comparable results 
were reported by Verma et al. (2021) [17] and yadav et al. (2017) 
[18]. 
 
Nutrient uptake 
Integrated nutrient management (INM) significantly influenced 
nutrient uptake by grain and straw as well as the economics of 
wheat cultivation (Table 2). Among the different treatments, T₉ 
(100% RDF + Azotobacter + PSB inoculation) recorded the 
highest nutrient uptake by grain, with 65.43 kg N ha⁻¹, 13.09 kg 
P ha⁻¹ and 17.45 kg K ha⁻¹, followed by T₇ (63.63, 12.73 and 
16.97 kg ha⁻¹, respectively) and T₈ (62.67, 12.53 and 16.71 kg 
ha⁻¹, respectively). Similarly, the highest nutrient uptake by straw 
was also observed under T₉, recording 33.34 kg N ha⁻¹, 7.37 kg P 
ha⁻¹ and 87.20 kg K ha⁻¹, whereas the lowest uptake by grain 
(52.26, 10.45 and 13.94 kg ha⁻¹ of N, P and K, respectively) and 
straw (23.72, 4.39 and 68.26 kg ha⁻¹, respectively) was recorded 
under the control treatment (T₁). The increased nutrient uptake 
under integrated nutrient management treatments may be 
attributed to balanced nutrient supply, improved root growth, 
enhanced microbial activity and greater nutrient use efficiency. 
The combined application of Azotobacter, PSB, FYM and ZnSO₄ 
improved nitrogen fixation, phosphorus solubilization and 
nutrient availability throughout the crop growth period, resulting 
in greater nutrient absorption and accumulation in both grain and 
straw. Similar findings have been reported by Singh et al. (2006) 
[16], Roy et al. (2014) [14] and Sharma et al. (2019) [15]. 
 
Economics 
The economic analysis further revealed significant differences 
among the nutrient management treatments. Treatment T₉ proved 
to be the most profitable, recording the highest net return (₹66,552 
ha⁻¹) and benefit:cost ratio (3.18), followed by T₇ (₹62,632 h a⁻¹ 
and 2.97) and T₁₀ (₹61,369 ha⁻¹ and 3.02). The control treatment 
recorded the lowest net return (₹42,217 ha⁻¹) and B:C ratio (2.64). 
The higher profitability under integrated nutrient management 
treatments was mainly due to higher grain yield, improved 
nutrient use efficiency and better economic utilization of applied 
inputs, which compensated for the additional cost of FYM, 
biofertilizers and zinc application. These findings are in close 
agreement with those reported by Roy et al. (2014) [14], Kumar et 
al. (2022) [9] and Mahajan et al. (2021) [11].
