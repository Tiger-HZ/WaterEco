Uromodulin (UMOD) is the most abundant glycoprotein in human urine, and its high-mannose content acts as a flushing ligand for uropathogenic Escherichia coli (UPEC). Nevertheless, many commensal bladder microbes, including Bifidobacterium longum, have the genetic capacity to digest N-glycans. Uromodulin could also serve as a nutrient reservoir for urinary bacteria capable of digesting N-glycans, as it has been demonstrated for other host glycoproteins and bacteria at other body sites. To test this hypothesis, we collected voided urogenital bacteria from male and female donors and assessed their survival aerobically on agar plates supplemented with 2% UMOD as the sole carbon source.
