Abstract Conservation of instream flows and water levels (instream flows) are vital to sustaining healthy aquatic, riparian, and terrestrial ecosystems. Natural instream flow regimes provide habitat and life history cues, sustain ecological communities, influence water temperatures and other water quality attributes, and affect geomorphic processes. Changes in flow and water level timing, duration, rate of change, magnitude, and frequency can affect the ecological characteristics of water bodies by altering habitat and changing temperature and sediment regimes, ultimately leading to changes in faunal biomass and diversity that may be viewed as positive or negative depending on resource management objectives.
