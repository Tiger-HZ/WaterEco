Abstract In environments with shallow water tables, vegetation may use groundwater to support transpiration (T G ). This process has been carefully studied in some arid climates but rarely in humid climates—even those with severe droughts and seasonal water deficits. As such, the role of T G in humid‐catchment hydrology is poorly constrained. We analysed water table fluctuations from nine monitoring wells along three transects in a second‐order forested catchment to estimate T G at plot and whole‐riparian zone scales. Average T G estimated around all well locations ranged from 1.06 to 4.95 mm d −1 and did not change systematically as a function of distance from stream channel or with plot‐scale tree basal area.
