Abstract: The Selenga River basin, located in southern Siberia, is an important component of the
Lake Baikal ecosystem, and comprises approximately 80% of the Baikal watershed.
The Selenga Delta, is one of the largest inland freshwater floodplains in the world, and
plays an important role in the ecosystem functioning of Baikal. The Lake Gusinoye
region, is southwest of Lake Baikal and the Selenga Delta, is a more heavily
industrialized region within the Selenga River basin. We assessed possible drivers of
changes of sedimentary organic matter (OM) composition within two shallow lakes. We
focused on individual n-alkanes, one of the most abundant lipids used to provide
information on past vegetation. We used multivariate statistics to disentangle changes
in the sources of sedimentary OM over time. The depositional OM history of SLNG04B
core can be divided in four zones: (i) major influence of non-emergent vascular plants,
typically found in transitional environments (ca. 1835 to ca. 1875); (ii) increased
influence of grasses/herbs (ca. 1880 to ca. 1910); (iii) transition from non-emergent
vascular plants and grasses/herbs to submerged and floating macrophytes and
phytoplankton (ca. 1915 to ca. 1945); (iv) maintenance of autochthonous OM from
submerged and floating macrophytes and phytoplankton (ca. 1945 to ca. 2014). The
depositional OM history of the Black Lake core can be divided in two main zones: (i)
major influence of non-emergent vascular plants and submerged and floating
macrophytes (ca. 1915 to ca. 1980); (ii) increased influence of grasses/herbs and
phytoplankton (ca. 1980 to ca. 2010). Natural events (e.g., an earthquake in 1862
caused flooding and subsidence of much of the land surrounding SLNG04 lake and a
further catastrophic flood event in 1897) and anthropogenic activities (e.g., nutrient
pollution from expansion of agricultural ) changed the composition of OM resulting in
ecological shifts across trophic levels in the Selenga River basin.

1. Introduction 54 
Freshwater floodplain wetlands are some of the most dynamic, diverse, productive and 55 
often complex ecosystems on Earth, occurring most extensively in regions where precip itation 56 
exceeds evapotranspiration (Mitsch and Gosselink, 2007). They are characterized by regularly 57 
flooded land, a highly connective landscape, and shallow lakes of varying degrees of connectivity 58 
(Junk et al., 2013). More than 80% of known inland wetland systems have been historically lost, 59 
especially during the late 20th century (Davidson et al., 2018; Wasserman and Dalu, 2022), due to, 60 
e.g., nutrient enrichment from urban and agricultural sources , the construction of dams , stream 61 
channelization and dredging for flood control (Hobbs et al., 2016; Sievers et al., 2018). 62 
Climate-induced changes in the hydrological regime are often very important drivers of 63 
organic matter (OM) production and ecological variability within freshwater wetlands, and 64 
consequently, in the type of sedimentary OM accumulated in shallow lakes. They are also often 65 
attributed to changes in precipitation levels , which can result in complete shifts in the vegetation 66 
and ecological structure with in the wetland resulting in different types of sedimentary OM 67 
(Cobbaert et al., 2014; Levi et al., 2016). Therefore, the OM content of lake sediments provides a 68 
variety of indicators that can be used to infer histories of environmental conditions of lakes and 69 
their watersheds (Rullkötter, 2000; Pancost and Boot, 2004) . The primary source of OM to lake 70 
sediments is from plants in and around the lake. Plants can be divided into two geochemically 71 
distinctive groups on the basis of their biochemical compositions: (1) non-vascular plants such as 72 
phytoplankton (e.g., cyanobacteria, algae, diatoms, dinoflagellates) that contain little or no carbon-73 
rich cellulose and lignin, and (2) vascular plants, such as grasses, herbs, shrubs, trees on land, and 74 
macrophytes in lakes, that contain large proportions of these tissues (Meyers and Teranes, 2001). 75 
Biomarkers are a group of widespread geochemical compounds present in sedimentary OM 76 
and applied in the (paleo)reconstruction of regional climate changes (Freeman and Pancost, 2014). 77 
Biomarkers can distinguish between allochthonous and autochthonous carbon sources, and also 78 
anthropogenic inputs based on differences in molecular types, functional group positions, and 79 
carbon chain length (Derrien et al., 2017; Inglis et al., 2022). Biomarkers can also identify specific 80 
types of OM sources over time (Volkman et al., 2008). Amongst biomarkers, n-alkanes are one of 81 
the most common lipids used to provide information on past vegetation. They are biosynthesized 82 
by terrestrial and aquatic higher plants and phytoplankton (Eglinton and Hamilton, 1967; Ficken 83 
et al., 2000), and have odd-to-even dominance in the number of carbon atoms. Short carbon chain-84 
length n-alkanes (< C20) are biosynthesized mainly by bacteria and phytoplankton (Derrien et al., 85 
2017; Liu et al., 2020 ) while submerged and floating macrophytes (classified as non-emergent 86 
vascular plants) are generally dominated by medium-carbon chain-length n-alkanes (i.e., C20-C25). 87 
Terrestrial sources are dominated by long-carbon chain-length n-alkanes (> C25) and have a strong 88 
odd-to-even carbon preference (Chen et al., 2016). As a component of vascular plant leaf wax, n-89 
alkane distribution is generally dominated by n-C27 to n-C35, being more abundant in deciduous 90 
trees/shrubs and biosynthesized by grasses/herbs (Schefuß et al., 2003). 91 
The aim of this study was to assess the variability of sedimentary OM composition within 92 
shallow water bodies of the Selenga Delta, a designated Ramsar site (No 682), for the conservation 93 
and sustainable development of wetlands throughout the world (Ramsar, 2022), and nearby region 94 
of the Selenga River basin . Anthropogenic activities within the Selenga River basin, including 95 
industry, agriculture, aquaculture, growing urban settlements, mining , and deforestation , pose 96 
threats to water quality and ecology in the Delta and other nearby catchment lakes through both 97 
chemical pollution (Adams et al., 2018) and increased erosion of sediments into the Selenga river 98 
(Bazhenova and Kohylkin, 2013) . Two shallow lakes in the region were selected for biomarker 99 
analysis (n-alkanes and their relate d ratios) to evaluate the temporal variations in the input and 100 
sources of n-alkanes through the last decades . We also tried to answer a specific question: can 101 
sedimentary n-alkanes be used to indicate changes in the ecological structure of Selenga River 102 
basin shallow lakes? 103 
2. Study area 104 
2.1. The Selenga River basin 105 
The Selenga River basin is located in the continental subarctic of southeast Siberia (Fig. 106 
1), and covers an area of almost 450,000 km 2 in Siberia and Mongolia, compris ing over 80% of 107 
the Lake Baikal watershed (Nadmitov et al., 2015) . The Selenga River flows approximately 950 108 
km from the head waters in northern Mongolia before it reaches the Selenga Delta and Lake Baikal. 109 
The upstream portions of the Selenga River basin, primarily tributaries and headwaters within 110 
Mongolia, run through mountainous and forested terrain, while further downstream, the southern 111 
Siberian portion, is dominated by steppe vegetation, underlain by permafrost . Part of th is steppe 112 
has been converted to pasture or crop land (Chalov et al., 2016). Peak river discharge occurs during 113 
the summer, when 98% of total sediment loads are transported through the Selenga River basin 114 
(Chalov and Romanchenko, 2016). The region is characterized by an ultracontinental climate, with 115 
dry, intensely cold winters and short, mild summers, subjected to westerly and northwesterly winds 116 
all year-round (Plyusnin et al., 2008). 117 
The Selenga Delta itself (52°14’N; 106°30’E), has formed where the Selenga River enters 118 
Lake Baikal, the oldest and deepest lake in the world and classified as a UNESCO World Heritage 119 
since 1996. The delta is heavily influenced by tectonic processes with the oldest portions hav ing 120 
been formed over two to three million years ago (Scholz and Hutchinson, 2000). 121 
 122 
2.2. Sampled study sites 123 
Two shallow lakes were selected for study. The first is located within the Selenga Delta 124 
itself (‘SLNG04’; 52°16’N, 106°40’E), and the second, located outside of the Selenga Delta, but 125 
within the Selenga River basin ( named ‘Black Lake’; 51°24’N, 106°29’E) (Fig. 2). SLNG 04 is 126 
an expansive shallow lake, with surface flow connections to both the Selenga River and Lake 127 
Baikal. It is partially surrounded by agricultural lands, consisting primarily of livestock rearing . 128 
Black Lake is one in a chain of several shallow freshwater lakes located approximately 200 km 129 
upstream of the Selenga Delta along the Selenga River, and 80 km direct distance southeast of 130 
Lake Baikal (Pisarsky et al., 2005). Black Lake is situated in the more heavily industrialized area 131 
of Gusinoye, and provides therefore a useful comparison to SLNG04. 132

3. Material and Methods 134 
3.1. Extraction of sediment cores 135 
In March 2014, sediment cores were extracted from SLNG04 and Black Lake (‘SLNG04B’ 136 
and ‘BRYT02B’, respectively), for organic compound analyses. Further sediment cores 137 
(‘SLNG04C’ and ‘BRYT02C’) were extracted at the same sites for radiometric dating analyses. 138 
An Uwitec piston corer (UWITEC Ltd., Austria) fitted with a 6.3 cm in ternal diameter perspex 139 
tube ( n-hexane-cleaned to eliminate organic contamina tion) was used . Sediment cores were 140 
collected while lakes were ice -covered, through an auger -drilled hole in the ice. Sediment from 141 
these cores w as vertically extruded and sliced at 0.5 cm intervals in the field, using n-hexane-142 
cleaned equipment and placed in n-hexane cleaned aluminum foil before being stored in a plastic 143 
bag and sealed. Sediments were stored at -20°C and then freeze-dried in preparation for analysis. 144 
Dried sediment from each sample depth was ground to a fine powder using an agate mortar and 145 
pestle. 146 
 147 
3.2. Methods for radioisotope dating of sediment cores 148 
Radiometric dating techniques were used to date the SLNG04C and BRYT02C sediment 149 
cores. The detailed metho ds were published in Adams et al. (201 8). Briefly, w et sediment was 150 
subsampled from sediment cores at 3.0 -cm intervals and freeze -dried. Dried sediment samples 151 
were analyzed for 210Pb, 226Ra, 137Cs and 241Am by direct gamma assay using ORTEC HPGe GWL 152 
series well-type coaxial low background intrinsic germanium detectors. 210Pb was determined via 153 
its gamma emissions at 46.5keV, and 226Ra by the 295keV and 352keV gamma rays emitted by its 154 
daughter isotope 214Pb. 210Pb chronologies for both sediment cores were constructed using the 155 
constant rate of supply (CRS) dating model (Appleby and Oldfield, 1978; Appleby, 2001). 156 
Artificial radionuclides 137Cs and 241Am were measured by their emissions at 662keV and 157 
59.5keV, respectively (Appleby et al., 1986). To obtain dates for the undated cores (‘B’) loss-on-158 
ignition at 550 °C (LOI550) was conducted on all collected sediment cores (Heiri et al., 2001). 159 
LOI550 profiles from both ‘B’ and ‘C’ cores were examined for distinct features (i.e., tie -points) 160 
present in profiles from both cores. The radiometric dates for the tie -points were then cross -161 
correlated between the dated and undated profiles as described by Adams et al. (2018). 162 
 163 
3.3. n-alkanes: extraction, fractionation, instrumental analyses and quality assuranc es 164 
The analytical method for hydrocarbons was described in detail by Wisnieski et al. (2016). 165 
Approximately 10 g of sediments were Soxhlet extracted for 8 hours with 80 mL of a mixture of 166 
dichloromethane (DCM) and n-hexane (1:1, v:v) and 100 µL of a surrogate standard mixture 167 
containing 1-hexadecene and 1-eicosene (50 ng µL-1) was added to each sample and blanks prior 168 
extraction. This organic extract was reduced to ca. 2 mL by rotoevaporation , purified and 169 
fractionated using 3.2 g of silica (silica gel 60, 0.063 – 0.200 mm; 5% deactivated with Milli-Q® 170 
water) and 1.8 g of alumina (aluminium oxide 90 active, 0.063 – 0.200 mm; 5% deactivated with 171 
Milli-Q® water) column chromatography. The samples were eluted with 10 mL of n-hexane to 172 
obtain the aliphatic hydrocarbon fraction, containing n-alkanes and the isoprenoids pristane and 173 
phytane. Sample extracts were concentrated to 500 µL and 100 µL of the internal standard 1 -174 
tetradecene solution (50 ng µL-1) was added prior to instrumental analysis. 175 
The hydrocarbons were determined by the injection of 2 µL of the extract into a gas 176 
chromatograph Agilent GC (model 7890A) equipped with a flame ionization detector and an 177 
Agilent 19091J-413 capillary fused silica column coated with 5% diphenyl/dimethylsiloxane (30 178 
m length, 0.32 mm ID, and 0.25 μm film thickness). Hydrogen was used as the carrier gas. The 179 
injector and detector temperatures were adjusted to 280 °C and 325 °C, respectively. Splitless 180 
injection mode was adopted. The following oven temperature program was employed for analyses: 181 
40–60 ºC at 20 ºC min-1, 60 ºC for 2 min, 60–290 ºC at 5 ºC min-1, 290 ºC for 5 min, 290–300 ºC 182 
at 5 ºC min-1, and 300 ºC for 9 min. The individual compounds were identified by matching their 183 
retention times with those obtained for exte rnal standard mixture (n-C10 to n-C40, pristane and 184 
phytane) and quantified using a calibration curve ranging from 0.25 to 10.0 ng L-1. The detection 185 
limits (DL) were 0.01 µg g -1 for individual compounds. These data are based on the lowest 186 
instrumental sensitivity of n-alkanes concentration (0.02 ng µL -1, respectively) multiplied by the 187 
final extracted volume (500 µL) and divided by the sediment weight (10 g) before extraction. 188 
The procedural blanks showed that no peaks interfered with the analyses of the target 189 
compounds. Mean recoveries of the surrogate standards, 1 -hexadecene and 1 -eicosene, ranged 190 
from 50 to 99 % (mean = 66  10, N = 61) and from 56 to 106 % (mean = 78  12, N = 63), for at 191 
least 90 and 93 % of samples analyzed, respectively. The precision, expressed as the coefficient 192 
of variation between triplicates of the standard reference material (SRM) provided by International 193 
Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA-408) was lower than 15 % for at least 85 % of compounds 194 
analyzed. The measured concentrations of target parameters available in the IAEA-408 reference 195 
sheet (e.g., total n-alkanes, n-C17, n-C18, pristane and phytane) in the SRM were in good agreement 196 
with the certified values (lower than 15 %). 197

3.4. Data analysis 199 
To explore temporal trends in the individual odd n-alkanes data ( n-C15 to n-C35), a 200 
constrained hierarchical cluster analysis was applied to a Euclidian distance of normalized values, 201 
i.e., based on each individual n-alkane concentration divided by the sum of odd total n-alkanes (n-202 
C15 to n-C35). The clustering of the individual odd n-alkane (n-C15 to n-C35) data were used to 203 
visualize stepwise changes in the n-alkane contribution s along the cores. The clustered core 204 
sections appear to represent environmental changes reflected by different n-alkane distributions 205 
and were therefore used as ‘zones’ to be evaluated in detail in each sediment core. These zones 206 
could be representative of different predominance of n-alkanes, and consequently, sources of 207 
sedimentary OM. Broken stick analysis was performed to visualize the residual dispersion of the 208 
hierarchical classification , and to determine the number of significant zones within the 209 
sedimentary n-alkanes profile. The constrained hierarchical cluster and broken stick analysis were 210 
performed in R using the rioja package (Juggins, 2020) 211 
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was also adopted as a multivariate approach to verify 212 
which individual odd n-alkanes (n-C15 to n-C35) may best explain the core sections through each 213 
core, thereby helping to identify the main sources of OM and their poss ible variations over the 214 
period. PCA was performed using these same normalized data sets (individual n-alkanes) in order 215 
to indicate which variable(s) may explain the zones formed in the cluster analyses. The PCA was 216 
applied and plotted using R and the packages factoMineR and factextra, respectively (Husson et 217 
al., 2016). 218 
Box- and whisker plots were constructed to display the trends of n-alkanes and ratios across 219 
depth zones for each sediment core, with median, first and third quartile, outlier and extreme values 220 
indicated. Box-plots were created in the Statistica TIBCO® Software (v.14.0.0). 221

4. Results and Discussion 230 
4.1. Core chronologies 231 
The radiometric dating and loss-on-ignition profiles for both SLNG04C and BRYT02C 232 
cores are shown in Supplementary Information ( Fig. S1 and S2 ). The LOI550 profiles for the 233 
sediment cores ‘B’ and ‘C’ from each lake show good agreement (Fig. S1 and S2) and were used 234 
to apply dates to undated SLNG04B and BRYT02B cores (Adams et al., 2018 ). The cross-235 
correlation of LOI550 records from cores SLNG04C and BRYT02C with those from SLNG04B 236 
and BRYT02B allow chronologies to be allocated extending to ca. 1835 (179 years) and ca. 1915 237 
(99 years), respectively. 238 
 239 
4.2. The n-alkane levels and distributions in the studied shallow lakes 240 
Tables S1–S2 display the n-alkanes and isoprenoids concentrations. The sum of individual 241 
n-alkanes from n-C14 to n-C35 (Total n-Alk (C14-C35)) concentrations ranged from 2.02 to 61.5 μg 242 
g−1 (median = 28.2 ± 12.0) in SLNG04B and from 35.9 to 106.9 μg g−1 (median = 60.1 ± 13.8) in 243 
BRYT02B (Fig. S 3). These levels are considered high when compared to sediments from a 244 
paleothermokarst lake on the Bykovsky Peninsula, northeastern Siberia (0.6 - 20.8 μg g −1; 245 
Jongejans et al., 2020) and surficial sediments from Lake Baikal collected in summer 2001 (4 - 55 246 
μg g−1; Russell and Rosell-Melé, 2005). 247 
In general, the analyzed samples for both cores presented significant levels of long -chain 248 
n-alkanes (LC n-Alk; n-C27 to n-C32) followed by mid-chain (MC n-Alk; n-C21 to n-C26) and short-249 
chain n-alkanes (SC n-Alk; n-C15 to n-C20) (Tables S1 and S2 ; Fig. S3). The contribution of the 250 
SC n-Alk in the Total n-Alk is low in the lower core sections, likely due to high lability of the low 251 
molecular weight hydrocarbons. A unimodal distribution of n-alkanes is observed in all samples 252 
analyzed, reaching a maximum between n-C27 to n-C33. The n-C29 was the predominant n-alkane 253 
in SLNG04B, while n-C27 was dominant in BRYT02B (Fig. S4). This n-alkane distribution is 254 
typical for Holocene age sediment in both Lake Baikal (Brincat et al., 2000 ; Russell and Rosell -255 
Melé, 2005) and in Lake Billyakh, northern Siberia (Tarasov et al., 2013). 256

4.3. Changes in the sedimentary OM composition and ecological structure based on n-alkane and 258 
isoprenoids proxies in the studied shallow lakes 259 
4.3.1. The SLNG04 lake 260 
The SLNG04B record can be divided in 4 zones from the bottom to the top (Fig. 4) based 261 
on cluster and broken stick analyses (Fig. S 5 and S6). These four distinct zones correspond to 262 
variations in the PCA scores (Fig. S7) and along depth (Fig. S8). 263 
 264 
 Zone 1 (ca. 1835 to ca. 1875; 63 – 50 cm) 265 
Zone 1 is primarily influenced by grasses/herbs (high relative percentages of n-C31, n-C33 266 
and n-C35; Fig. S9) and non-emergent vascular plants (n-C25 and n-C27; Fig. S9). Zone 1 presented 267

wax values (< 0.35) ( Fig. 5) associated with a mixed source. This particular individual n-alkanes 275 
contribution is reflected in the lower values of ACL found in Zone 1 (Fig. 5). Low concentrations 276 
of SC and MC n-Alk in Zone 1 indicate a low contribution from autochthonous OM sources (Fig. 277 
3). The absence of submerged aquatic macrophytes and aquatic animal remains, an a bsence of 278 
preserved algal pigments, and very low diatom concentrations were also verified in this part of the 279 
core (Adams, 2017), and are consistent with the individual n-alkanes distribution. 280 
The low preservation of OM in the Zone 1 is corroborated by the relatively low values of 281 
O/E and CPI diagnostic ratios in this stage (outliers to CPI 1, Fig. S10 and S11), and the high values 282 
to TAR (outliers, Fig. S12) compared to middle and top core sections (Fig. 5). The higher exposure 283 
to light and air at SLNG04 lake prior to the mid -19th century increased the degradation of labile 284 
components of sedimentary OM (Reuss et al., 2005), and consequently, isoprenoids (pristane and 285 
phytane) (Fig. 6) and pigments (Adams, 2017). Low levels of preserved OM content and low n-286 
alkanes concentrations may be tied to depositionary oxic conditions, as indicated by the maximum 287 
Fe/Mn and minimum Ca/Ti ratios that occurred in this period ( Adams, 2017). On the other hand, 288 
the LC n-Alk that are more resistant to degradation were also pr esent in relatively low 289 
concentrations, and may be related to low values of LOI550 in the bottom of the core. 290

 Zone 2 (ca. 1880 to ca. 1910; 47 – 38 cm) 292 
Zone 2 is influenced by grasses/herbs and higher plants (Fig. S9). Both Zones 1 and 2 are 293 
under strong influence of terrestrial sources of organic matter. Low concentrations of SC and MC 294 
n-Alk concentrations suggests the low contribution of aquatic sources; however, this may also be 295 
due to higher degradation rates of these compounds. 296 
Zone 2 presented a slight increase in concentrations of LC n-Alk (and consequently in Total 297 
n-Alk) compared to the Zone 1 (Fig. 3); it was consistent with the increase of LOI550 values found 298 
in this section of the core, which reached the maximum OM content of the core at 38 cm (ca. 299 
1910). 300 
The main distinction between this zone and the rest of the core is related to the major 301 
influence of n-C31, n-C33 and n-C35 in the Total n-Alk concentration, that are related to grasses and 302 
herbs (Fig. S9). This contribution is well marked due the higher ACL values (outliers, Fig. S12) 303 
found in Zone 2. The environmental changes in Zone 2, indicated by the n-alkanes and related 304 
diagnostic ratios (TAR and ACL; Fig. 5), reflected changes in the allochthonous OM input. The 305 
deposition of allochthonous OM from vascular plants, grasses and herbs in the Selenga Delta 306 
created a deeper open water zone, now hospitable to diverse communities of submerged and 307 
floating macrophytes. The low preservation of OM observed during Zone 1 continued into Zone 308 
2, indicated by the relatively low values of O/E and CPI diagnostic ratios in this stage (Fig. 5). 309 
The low levels of n-C17 and n-C19, primarily related to phytoplankton , is consistent with 310 
the absence of preserved diatoms frustules during this period ( Adams, 2017). Shallow depths at 311 
this time potentially led to increased turbulence, increased mixing, and increased wave-action, and 312 
consequently, the degradation of sedimentary OM. Biological parameters reinforced the 313 
environmental changes in Zone 2 due to the slight increase in diatom concentrations, consist ing 314 
mainly of epiphytic and small benthic diatoms (Adams, 2017). These geochemical and biological 315 
features suggest the transition period t o the permanent inundation of Selenga Delta (Gell et al., 316 
2007), characterized by a small, shallow -water, high -closure system with mostly littoral and 317 
epiphytic habitats (Hay et al., 2000; Summers et al., 2017). 318

 Zone 3 (ca. 1915 to ca. 1945; 35 – 25 cm) 320 
Zone 3 marks a transition on the main components of sedimentary OM, from non-emergent 321 
vascular plants ( n-C25 and n-C27) and grasses/herbs ( n-C31, n-C33) to submerged and floating 322 
macrophytes (n-C23 and n-C25) and phytoplankton (n-C17, n-C19) (Fig. S9). This is primarily shown 323 
by the increase of P aq wax index and, also by a decrease in TAR values (Fig. 5). In addition, the 324 
CPI2 values are typical of terrestrial sources associated with higher plants ( n-C29), that are an 325 
important OM source throughout the core (Fig. 5). 326 
Zone 3 showed a clear increase in concentrations of different groups of n-alkanes compared 327 
to the previous zones and is consistent with the concurrent variation in LOI550 (Fig. 3). Similar 328 
concentrations of SC and LC n-Alk compared to Zone 2 were detected. An increase in MC n-Alk 329 
was noted in the lower sections of this zone ( ca. 1915 to ca. 1925; 35 – 32 cm), while a fast and 330 
prominent increase in n-alkane concentrations in the upper sections occurred (ca. 1925 to ca. 1945; 331 
32 – 25 cm) (Fig. 3). The decrease in ACL in this zone may represent an increase in sedimentary 332 
n-C29 as a consequence of deforestation (Meyers, 2003; Aichner et al., 2010). 333 
There was an increase in the preservation of OM in Zone 3, indicated by increased values 334 
of O/E and CPI diagnostic ratios compared to previous zones (Fig. 5). This zone contained the first 335 
detectable levels of isoprenoids. Higher values of Pri/Phy, Pri/ n-C17 and Phy/n-C18 ratios were 336 
obtained compar ed to the rest of the core ( Fig. 6 and S13 ) suggest ing an increase in anoxic 337 
conditions and corroborated by minimum Fe/Mn ratios ca. 1920 (Adams, 2017). 338

 Zone 4 (ca. 1945 to 2014; 25 – 0 cm) 340 
Zone 4 showed the highest concentrations of different groups of n-alkanes and LOI550 (Fig. 341 
3). Fresh aquatic OM from phytoplankton (n-C17 and n-C19) and submerged and floating 342 
macrophytes (n-C21 and n-C23) play an important role in the sedimentary OM in this most recent 343 
zone. Notably, we observed an increase followed by a slight decrease in Total n-Alk concentration 344 
and LOI550 between ca. 1945 to ca. 1980 (25 – 11.5 cm) (Fig. 3), after which levels remained 345 
slightly higher through to the top of the core. 346 
Highest levels of Total n-Alk, particularly SC and MC n-Alk, are expected to be found in 347 
the more surficial section s of sediment cores due the recent input of fresh autochthonous OM 348 
(Hedges et al., 1997). Also, the individual n-alkanes distribution within the PCA confirmed the 349 
establishment of current connectivity and sedimentary environmental, with a greater contribution 350 
of autochthonous OM from submerged and floating macrophytes ( n-C23 and n-C25) and 351 
phytoplankton (n-C17, n-C19). This is also evidenced by the lowest TAR values of the entire core 352 
and the constant P aq wax values (Fig. 5). However, the main contribution continue d to be from 353 
higher plants (n-C29) due to the connection to the Selenga River, and the influx of river -derived 354 
terrestrial matter. The slightly higher values of ACL, when compared to Zone 3, confirm this (Fig. 355 
5). 356 
Trends in total n-Alk are consistent with biological parameters that indicate maximum lake 357 
productivity levels in the late-1950s, notably pigment concentrations in the SLNG04 lake (Adams, 358 
2017). Increasing SC n-Alk up to ca. 1965, are in agreement with greater diatom concentrations 359 
at ca. 1960 (Adams, 2017) as phytoplankton are an important source of n-C17 and n-C19. Changes 360 
in macrophyte assemblages can influence changes in other biological communities, including 361 
diatoms (Sokal et al., 2010), and may be reflected in the distribution of individual n-alkanes, and 362 
related indices such as O/E (< C24), CPI 1 and Paq wax (Fig. 5). 363 
Changes in delta morphology in the late -20th and early -21st centuries caused a shift in 364 
discharge from the southwestern to the northeastern branches of the Selenga River Delta (Chalov 365 
et al., 2016), with severe implications for sediment and chemical transport and river water flow. 366 
Connectivity to the river dictated the abundance and diversity of the diatoms present (as indicated 367 
by the high contribution of SC n-Alk to the sediment) with a higher diversity reflecting higher 368 
connectivity sites and the presence of planktonic species in lakes connected to the Selenga River. 369 
Therefeore, it is also possible that decreased macroph yte biomass (indicated by low contribution 370 
of MC n-Alk) at high connectivity sites is related to increased connectivity with the Selenga River. 371 
The regular and relatively high values of O/E and CPI found in this zone are related to fresh 372 
and/or preserved OM, and despite the recent increase in human activities in the Selenga River 373 
basin, the hydrocarbons found may be primarily related to biogenic sources. The isoprenoids ratios 374 
(Pri/Phy, Pri/n-C17 and Phy/n-C18) showed decreasing values in this zone reaching a minimum at 375 
the top core (Fig. 6) and reflecting the evolution of anoxic conditions in this zone due to the large 376 
amount of deposited OM. 377

Zone 1 is primar ily influenced by submerged and floating macrophytes (high/moderate 387 
percentages of n-C21 and, n-C23, respectively), non -emergent vascular plants (high/moderate 388

Vascular plants (n-C29) and submerged and floating macrophytes ( n-C23 and n-C25) were 391 
the major components of sedimentary OM in mid- (ca. 1945 to ca. 1955; 50 – 40 cm) and lower 392 
(ca. 1915 to ca. 1945; 67.5 – 50 cm) sections of this zone (Fig. S7 and S17), as confirmed by the 393

The upper sections of this zone ( ca. 1955 to ca. 1980; 40 – 23 cm) may represent the transition 395 
from an environment typically influenced by allochthonous sources (higher plants) , with 396 
grasses/herbs appearing as an important fraction of terrestrial OM input , to an increase in 397

5). 400 
Zone 1 showed a general decrease and slight fluctuation in the LOI 550 values and Total n-401 
Alk concentrations (Fig. 3), and contained mainly MC and LC n-Alk until ca. 1955, while SC n-402 
Alk concentration increased until ca. 1960. Between ca. 1955 and ca. 1980 the concentrations of 403 
all classes of n-alkanes and LOI 550 values o scillated between their recorded maximum and 404 
minimum values (Fig. 3). Magnetic susceptibility values were low and decreased through Zone 1, 405 
with low fluctuations throughout this sectio n of the core ( Adams, 2017), suggesting decreased 406 
input of terrestrial materials. 407 
The values of O/E and CPI in this zone indicate preserved OM and the absence of 408 
anthropogenic hydrocarbons, with a marked influence from terrestrial higher plants in the mid - 409 
and bottom sections. A decrease in the O/E (< C24) and CPI1 ratios (both related to SC and MC n-410 
Alk) was observed in the upper sections (Fig. 5), which may indicate the presence of oil 411 
hydrocarbons sources. Indeed, the input of hydrocarbons related to crude oil and by-products has 412 
been previously characterized in Black Lake (Adams et al., 2018), by the increased concentrations 413 
of low molecular weight polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs with 2 -3 rings) after ca. 1970 414 
and low values (< 0.10) o f the anthracene / anthracene + phenanthrene (Ant/178) ratio after ca. 415 
1960, that are typically associate d with petroleum sources and its by-products (Yunker et al., 416 
2002). 417 
The individual n-alkanes distribution and related ratios corroborate the pigment and diatom 418

Macrophyte communities in Black Lake in the early -1900 (Eftesum, 2017), showed the 421

with stable and diverse macrophyte communities (Bazarova and Itigilova, 2006; Sayer et al., 423

4.4. Depositional history at the Selenga River basin 468 
SLNG04 and Black Lake have undergone several major shifts in state, structure, and likely 469 
functioning since the early -19th century reflecting environmental changes in the Selenga River 470 
basin. Biomarkers concur with palaeolimnological records and reveal ed that changes in 471 
sedimentary OM at the mid/end -19th century in the Selenga Delta coincide with the Tsagan 472 
earthquake just off-shore of the Delta in 1862. The earthquake was the strongest ever recorded in 473 
east Siberia (with a magnitude of 7.5) and was integral in forming the contemporary structure of 474 
the right bank of the Selenga Delta (Vologina et al., 2010). This seismic event resulted in 475 
substantial subsidence and flooding of a large area of the northeast corner of the Delta, with a total 476 
area of approximately 200 km2 (Orlov, 1872). Furthermore, a catastrophic flood event in the late-477 
19th century in Transbaikalia, flooding many towns and villages along the Selenga River, caus ed 478 
an increase in the river height by 4 metres within one day (Kadetova and Radziminovich 2014), 479 
was likely responsible for increased input of allochthonous OM, trace and m ajor elements in the 480 
Selenga Delta at this time (Yang et al., 2016). The apparent predominance of allochthonous 481 
sources from vascular plants, grasses and herbs buried in the SLNG04 lake seems be related to the 482 
Tsagan earthquake and the transport of these m aterials from the drainage basin to the Delta due 483 
extreme flooding events. Also, the Tsagan earthquake of 1862 and the flood event in the late-19th 484 
century contributed to high sediment exposure to light and air increasing the degradation of labile 485 
components of sedimentary OM and increasing the oxic potential of the sediments at this time, 486 
due to high levels of sediment influx/erosion to the site (Gell et al., 2007) during this period 487 
(Adams, 2017). 488 
Land-use conversion for agricultural expansion and intensification began in the Selenga 489 
River basin in the early -19th century as a response to increased populations in the region due the 490 
development of the Trans -Siberian Railroad and mining operations in different portions of this 491 
area (Robinson and Anisov a, 2004). Agricultural expansion was the primary cause of massive 492 
deforestation events and land conversion from boreal forest -steppe (higher plants, with 493 
predominance of n-C29) to croplands in the 19 th century in the Selenga Delta and Lake Gusinoye 494 
regions. These land conversions resulted in an increase in the erosion and transport of sediment 495 
and allochthonous OM directly into the Selenga River affecting large areas of the basin (Eimers et 496 
al., 2008; Bazhenova and Kobylkin, 2013). Also, increased agriculture within the Selenga River 497 
basin during this period resulted in increasing eutrophic conditions that was reflected in changes 498 
in the main components of sedimentary OM, from non-emergent vascular plants and grasses/herbs 499 
to submerged and floating macrophytes and phytoplankton in Selenga Delta (Fig. S9). 500 
Further agricultural expansion in the Selenga River basin occurred intensively from the 501 
mid-1950s, with a peak around the 1980s (Robinson and Anisova, 2004), and contributed to an 502 
increase to hypereutrophic conditions in the Selenga Delta , and increasing the contribution of 503 
autochthonous OM to the sediments (Fig. 3) . Increased connectivity to the Selenga River, 504 
increased agricultural activity and anthropogenic disturbances within the Selenga River basin in 505 
the early-20th century likely resulted in increased nutrient flux to the Selenga Delta. A possible 506 
mechanism relates to stimulated plant production (submerged and floating macrophytes), leading 507 
to increased photosynthetic removal of CO 2 from the w ater column, resulting in increasing pH 508 
(Potasznik and Szymczyk, 2015). A higher pH would lead to increased phosphorus release from 509 
sediments (Koski -Vahala et al., 2001; Wu et al., 2014), thereby stimulating plant productivity 510 
during the early- to mid-20th century, as well as slight reducing conditions in the Selenga Delta. 511 
Population growth after the end of World War II, as well as intensive increases in livestock 512 
populations until ca. 1990, contributed to the increase in nutrient flux to soils, and increased 513 
sediment erosion within the Selenga River basin (Bazhenova and Kobylkin, 2013). The enrichment 514 
from increased populations and intensification of agricultural practices drove the observed changes 515 
found in the sedimentary OM in the mid-20th century (ca. 1960) at Black Lake. Differing controls 516 
for OM and mineral supply has been assumed in Lake Gusinoye region post-1950 (Adams, 2017), 517 
indicating a higher influx of mineral sources and co inciding with increased development and 518 
industrialization near Lake Gusinoye in the 1950s (Pisarsky et al., 2005). 519 
The construction of the Irkutsk Hydroelectric Dam along the Angara River (the only 520 
outflow from Lake Baikal) was completed in the mid-1950s. The filling of the associated reservoir 521 
was completed in 1963 and the environments surrounding of the Lake Baikal, and were submerged 522 
(Pinegin et al., 1976), increasing the water depth, surficial area and the connectivity of Selenga 523 
Delta with Selenga River, promoting changes in submerged macrophyte and diatom communities 524 
(Brock et al., 2011). These features may have contributed to increased preservation of sedimentary 525 
OM and substantial changes in sedimentary redox conditions since ca. 1940 and, as well as to the 526 
development of diatom assemblages in the early-1960s (Adams, 2017). Indeed, the sharp, though 527 
temporary, increase in sedimentation rate in the early -1960s (1963 ± 4 yrs) may correspond with 528 
the timing of the flood associated with the construction of the Irkutsk Dam and may contribute to 529 
the evolution of anoxic conditions and preservation of a large amount of deposited OM. 530 
The decades after the end of World War II were characterized by intense economic growth 531 
and industrial expansion in the USSR, and industrial and mining growth in the Lake Gusinoye 532 
region (Pisarsky et al., 2005) . This may have increased petroleum product demand (e.g., diesel 533 
fuel, m otor oil), increasing usage and consequently, indirect input to the environment and the 534 
occurrence of oil hydrocarbons in aquatic systems as indicated by n-alkanes and polycyclic 535 
aromatic hydrocarbons in sediments of the region (Adams et al., 2018). 536

5. Conclusions 538 
The drivers of sedimentary OM composition and sources since the 19 th century in the 539 
Selenga River basin have been both natural and anthropogenically -induced. Local natural 540 
disturbances, regional natural variability in climate, and anthropogenic stressors have resulted in 541 
ecological responses within the Selenga River basin during the last two centuries , including the 542 
composition of lacustrine sedimentary OM. 543 
The greatest period of agricultural expansion (resulting in wide -scale deforestation) and 544 
population growth in the Selenga River basin occurred in the mid -20th century, and was closely 545 
linked with periods of economic development in Russia. Changes in hydrological regimes (flood 546 
events), and nutrient pollution, changed the composition of sedimentary OM in the region. 547 
The SLNG04B record extends back to the early-19th century, while the record from Black 548 
Lake extends only to the late-19th century. Within the period covered by these records, the primary 549 
finding is the biomarker response to the elevated nutrient levels related to increasing regional 550 
anthropogenic development in southeast Siberia, with agricultural intensification in the early-20th 551 
century, and economic and industrial intensification following World War II in the early to mid -552 
20th century. 553 
The individual n-alkanes and the ir related proxies provide valuable information on the 554 
origin, degradation level and deposition conditions of the deposited OM over the last two centuries 555 
in southern Siberia and can be used to corroborate ecological changes in freshwater floodplain 556 
wetlands. 557
