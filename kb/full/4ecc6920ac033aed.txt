This study focuses on a semi-arid area of Chad—the Mangalmé department—where access to drinking water remains a major concern. The population relies primarily on groundwater (wells) and surface water (ponds/reservoirs) for drinking and domestic use. These resources are affected by climate change, notably through declining water table levels, the disappearance of water bodies, drought, and the degradation of vegetation cover. Compounding these issues are anthropogenic pressures linked to agriculture, livestock farming, and sanitation. The aim of this study is to assess the physicochemical quality of these waters, identify critical anomalies, and determine the sources of contamination in order to provide data useful for health and environmental management.
