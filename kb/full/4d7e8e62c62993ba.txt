Abstract In California and other (semi-)arid regions, chronic groundwater overdraft and aquifer salinization limit irrigated agriculture, threaten crop yield, and reduce flexibility in groundwater management. Higher aquifer salinity reduces groundwater’s ability to irrigate in drier years, when fresher water is less available to dilute more saline groundwater. This paper employs a novel, nested hydro-economic optimization framework—a decadal stochastic quadratic optimization model within a 10-stage dynamic program—to analyze century-scale water and crop management for a salinizing aquifer with limited drainage and hydrologic variability, similar to conditions in California’s western San Joaquin Valley.
