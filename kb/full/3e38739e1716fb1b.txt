Assessing environmental sensitivity on a regional scale requires processing and updating of a large amount of data in an integrated way. This aims to evaluate land degradation and specific interactions among different components of the environment. In the present study, selected applications of key indicators-based systems to early desertification monitoring were presented and discussed, namely (i) the Environmental Sensitive Areas (ESA) methodology evaluating environmental sensitivity to desertification, (ii) the recent Desertification Indicator System for Mediterranean Europe (DIS4ME) system and (iii) a quantitative framework for indicators’ weighting in composite 120 indexes. The ESA system, developed within the European MEDALUS project, exemplifies an efficient methodology for assessing desertification vulnerability. It integrates soil, climate, vegetation, and management layers, applying a two-stage calculation to estimate environmental vulnerability, validated across Mediterranean regions. Building on ESA, the DIS4ME system offers a comprehensive, user-oriented tool for monitoring desertification in Mediterranean Europe, integrating over hundreds indicators with interactive evaluation modules, scenario analysis, and socioeconomic impact assessment. Recent methodological advancements include performance evaluation of indicator weights and cost-efficiency analysis, enhancing the practical applicability of ESA and related systems. Overall, key indicators-based systems of environmental monitoring and surveillance applied to early desertification processes represent a standardized, flexible, and reliable approach for ecological assessment and environmental planning of critical and vulnerable areas. They may effectively support scientific research and advanced policymaking efforts in the light of sustainable development and ecosystem resilience to external stress.
