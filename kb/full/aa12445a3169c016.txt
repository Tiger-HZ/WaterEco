Abstract Bangladesh directs substantial expenditure to social safety net (SSN) transfers, yet how governance quality translates into policy effectiveness remains empirically underspecified. Using a mixed-methods design (n = 224 survey, seven key informant interviews) and structural equation modeling with bootstrapped confidence intervals across three SSN programmes in Meherpur Sadar Upazila, this study finds that bivariate correlations between governance quality and patronage, and between patronage and effectiveness, run directionally opposite to the corresponding structural path estimates once administrative and programme-level controls are introduced.
