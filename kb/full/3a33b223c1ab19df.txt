Background/Objectives: Escherichia coli is one of the most problematic bacterial species in hospital and community settings and a leading cause of resistant infections worldwide. This reinforces the need of new antibacterial tools like phage therapy. This study aimed to isolate and characterise lytic bacteriophages (phages) targeting clinical E. coli strains with different resistance profiles and to assess their potential for future therapeutic development. Methods: Environmental and clinical samples were used to isolate lytic phages active against clinical E. coli strains, and to evaluate their potential for future therapeutic applications.
