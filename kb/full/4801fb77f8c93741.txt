ABSTRACT On remote oceanic islands, changes in land uses frequently involve the clearance of native forest, establishment of exotic commercial plantations and intensification of agricultural and livestock activities. These transformations often result in the alteration or removal of native riparian vegetation, with potentially strong effects on stream ecosystem structure and function. Aquatic decomposers and organic matter decomposition are particularly sensitive to land uses changes due to their dependence on terrestrial litter inputs.
