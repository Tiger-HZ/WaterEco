This paper examines whether digitalization promotes export diversification in emerging economies and whether institutional quality conditions that effect. It develops a transaction-cost framework in which digital infrastructure lowers the fixed costs of entering new export lines — buyer search, documentation and border compliance — while institutions determine the share of returns an exporter can appropriate. Three testable propositions are derived from the framework and confronted with a structured synthesis of the empirical literature, assembled through systematic searches of RePEc, Scopus-indexed journals and the working-paper series of the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank.
