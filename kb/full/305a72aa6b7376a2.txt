Cyanobacteria are highly effective microorganisms with strong potential for remediation of a wide range of contaminants, including heavy metals, pharmaceuticals and personal care products (PPCPs), synthetic dyes, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), and other organic pollutants, commonly found across aquatic environments. This review critically discusses cyanobacteria as natural absorbents (“sponges”) and examines related mechanisms of bioremediation: biosorption, bioaccumulation, and biodegradation, while exploring sustainable approaches for resource recovery and biomass valorization.
