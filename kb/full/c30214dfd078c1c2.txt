Increasing energy demand and the environmental impacts associated with conventional thermal drying have intensified interest in renewable and hybrid drying technologies for food processing applications. Among these, hybrid solar and infrared (IR) drying systems have garnered considerable attention because of their potential to enhance drying efficiency, improve process reliability, and preserve product quality while reducing fossil-fuel dependence for thermal energy. This review critically synthesizes the developments reported between 2020 and 2025 in hybrid solar dryers, hybrid IR dryers, and integrated solar–IR drying systems. Emphasis is placed on system configuration, heat and mass transfer characteristics, drying kinetics and modeling, energy and exergy performance, techno-economic considerations, and quality attributes including color, texture, nutrient retention, and microbial stability. Recent progress in sensor-assisted monitoring, IoT-enabled data acquisition, and AI-supported prediction is also examined, particularly in relation to supervisory decision support and process optimization. Comparative analysis indicates that hybrid solar drying systems are advantageous for decentralized, cost-effective applications due to lower energy demand and improved utilization of renewable energy. In contrast, hybrid IR systems generally provide faster drying kinetics and improved quality retention for high-value, heat-sensitive products. The review further identifies major research gaps related to performance standardization, long-term field validation, sensor robustness, and scalable intelligent control strategies. Overall, hybrid solar–IR drying technologies represent a promising pathway toward energy-efficient and climate-resilient food drying with application-specific system design and integrated process management.
