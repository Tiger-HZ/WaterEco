Abstract
Aiming at the problems of poor generalization ability and unstable evaluation of 
current dance movement evaluation systems based on deep learning when facing 
unknown dancers, clothing, and environment, this paper proposes a Causal Invariant 
Dance Evaluation System. The system deeply integrates causal inference and invariant 
learning theory, aiming to strip surface-level correlations from multi-domain data and 
learn stable mappings between action quality and core causal characteristics. The 
core work of this paper is to construct a causal feature decoupling framework that 
separates the input action sequence into domain-invariant core causal features and 
domain-specific style features, and uses the principle of invariant risk minimization to 
ensure that the predictor based on core features is consistent across multiple training 
environments. To verify the effectiveness and robustness of the Causal Invariant 
Dance Evaluation System, this paper conducts a comprehensive experiment on a 
dance dataset comprising 125 dancers across 5 styles and 5 shooting environments. 
The results show that the system’s evaluation accuracy reaches 92.5% on the known 
test set, comparable to that of mainstream deep learning methods. In the more 
challenging cross-domain generalization test, the system is significantly better than 
the baseline model. Its average absolute error is reduced by 38.7% compared with 
the optimal baseline model, and the correlation coefficient remains above 89.1%. 
Ablation experiments further confirmed the necessity of each module, and removing 
the invariant learning module resulted in a cross-domain performance degradation of 
more than 15%. This study provides an effective solution for building a highly robust 
intelligent dance teaching system.

 1 Introduction
The inheritance and development of dance art have long depended on subjective evalu -
ation and the transfer of experience between teachers and apprentices, with limitations 
in efficiency and differing standards. Currently, the field of dance movement evaluation 
has transitioned from subjective expert assessment to automated computer vision-based

Feng Discover Artificial Intelligence (2026) 6:1165 
systems, aiming to provide objective feedback through intelligent means [ 1, 2]. State-
of-the-art methods utilize deep learning to map spatiotemporal action sequences to 
quality scores, achieving remarkable results on specific datasets [ 3]. However, these 
mainstream data-driven approaches primarily capture statistical correlations rather than 
causal mechanisms, leading to significant performance degradation when encountering 
unseen dancers or environments. To bridge this gap, this research aims to construct a 
highly robust evaluation system by introducing a methodological approach that inte -
grates structural causal models and invariant risk minimization to decouple core causal 
features from environmental noise. These data-driven deep learning methods learn 
statistical correlations rather than causal mechanisms, leading to severe generalization 
challenges in practical applications. The model is prone to capturing false associations in 
the data, including misjudging irrelevant features such as specific dancers’ personal style 
and the training environments of training venues as evaluation criteria [ 4, 5]. When sys-
tems are deployed to new environments, facing different dancers, costumes, or lighting 
conditions, performance tends to degrade significantly, lacking the robustness required 
for practical applications [6].
To fundamentally solve this problem, this study introduces causal inference and invari-
ant learning theory. Causal inference provides a new analysis framework. The quality 
of dance movements should be determined by their core causal characteristics, includ -
ing joint angle accuracy, force timing, etc., while dancers’ personal characteristics and 
environmental background are only non-causal factors that affect the observation data. 
Invariance learning provides an implementation path. By allowing the model to learn 
standard prediction rules across multiple training environments, it can strip away envi -
ronmental interference and capture authentic causal relationships.
Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes the Causal Invariant Dance Evalu -
ation System (CIDES). The system aims to construct a robust evaluation system with 
strong generalization to unknown domains by fusing a causal inference framework and 
an invariant learning mechanism. The main contributions of this paper include:
1. The structural causal model is systematically applied to the field of dance evaluation 
for the first time, and a causal diagram including core causal features, domain features, 
and other variables is established, which provides a theoretical basis for robust 
evaluation.
2. Design the CIDES model architecture and integrate feature decoupling and invariance 
learning modules, which can automatically separate feature representations causally 
related to action quality to ensure the stability of cross-domain prediction.
3. Through experiments on multi-domain dance data sets, it is proven that the 
generalization performance of CIDES in an unknown environment is significantly 
better than that of mainstream methods, which provides a reliable solution for 
intelligent dance teaching.

2.2 Causal inference basis
Causal inference is a discipline that studies how to identify causal relationships between 
variables from observed data or experimental data [ 13, 14]. It goes beyond the tradi -
tional correlation analysis and aims to reveal the direct effects of intervention behavior 
[15]. In machine learning, introducing causal ideas can help models overcome statistical 
biases in data and learn more stable, explainable mechanisms [16].
A structural causal model is one of the core frameworks of causal inference, using a 
causal diagram to represent the causal relationships between variables intuitively [ 17, 
18]. The nodes in the graph represent variables, and the directed edges represent the 
causal direction. Through the causal diagram, we can clearly analyze the impact of inter -
vening on one variable on other variables, and identify familiar sources of bias such as 
confounding factors, as shown in Eq. (2).

2.3 Invariant learning theory
Invariant learning is a cutting-edge paradigm in domain generalization, enabling models 
to learn invariant feature representations across diverse training environments [ 22, 23]. 
The characteristics that are truly causally related to the prediction target should remain 
unchanged in different environments. Moreover, those characteristics that change with 
the environment may be unreliable pseudo-correlations. In addition, self-distillation 
techniques based on data augmentation also embody the core ideas of invariant learn -
ing. For instance, self-distillation guided by data distortion [ 24] or self-knowledge distil-
lation using Mixup [ 25] can encourage the model to learn feature representations that 
are robust to input perturbations, thereby essentially enhancing the model’s ability to 
capture cross-domain invariance.
Invariance risk minimization is an important way to realize this idea [ 26]. It forces 
the model to rely on features that are stable across environments by encouraging it to 
maintain good predictive performance across multiple defined or automatically inferred 
training environments [ 27]. In this process, the model will spontaneously ignore false 
features that are useful in some environments but fail in others, thereby improving its 
generalization in new environments, as shown in Eq. (4).

differences and external interference, and capture the most essential mechanical and 
aesthetic laws that determine movement quality.

show that the single-frame inference latency of CIDES is only 12.4ms, which is a slight

system balances robustness with the feasibility of real-time deployment in intelligent 
teaching scenarios.

an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 graphics card, and the software environment is Ubuntu 
20.04. All models are developed and trained using Python 3.8 and PyTorch 1.10, and rely 
on the Scikit-learn library for subsequent data analysis and performance evaluation.

5 Conclusion
The dance movement quality robustness evaluation system based on causal inference 
and invariant learning proposed in this study has achieved significant breakthroughs in 
cross-domain generalization, noise-resilience, and cross-data-set robustness by com -
bining a causal inference module with an invariant learning module. The experimental 
data show that the motion recognition accuracy of the system on the multi-scene dance 
motion data set is 92.5% where the 95% confidence interval ranges from 91.2% to 93.8%. 
Compared with the baseline model, the average absolute error in the cross-domain gen -
eralization test is reduced by 38.7%, and the correlation coefficient remains stable above 
89.1% with a 95% confidence interval between 87.5% and 90.7%. All these performance 
gains are statistically significant because the p-value is less than 0.05, which verifies the 
system’s high robustness evaluation ability in complex dance scenes.
1. Behind the excellent performance of the system in the cross-domain generalization 
test, and the average absolute error is reduced by 38.7%, is the ability of the causal 
inference module to decouple the essential characteristics of the action. In cross-
domain verification across four major motion capture datasets, including Human3.6 
M and CMU MoCap, CIDES achieves the lowest cross-domain mean-squared error, 
resulting in an average reduction of 62.3% compared with traditional deep learning 
methods. Under the interference scenarios where rhythm stability fluctuates ± 15% 
and ambient illumination changes ± 30%, the system still maintains a classification 
accuracy of more than 92%, which proves that it can effectively strip off the interference 
of environmental noise on action quality evaluation.
2. Compared with the traditional Baseline model, CIDES is significantly better than 
Baseline’s 0.8-1.0 interval in the cross-domain MSE peak interval. The comparison 
of module effects shows that the invariant learning module improves cross-domain 
performance by 15%, and that the error of the causal inference module reduces 
by 30–40% under the single-module configuration. Under the complete model 
configuration, the MAE of the system in the Master-level difficulty scenario is as 
low as 0.59, the average deviation between the prediction score and the expert score 
is only 0.32 points, the prediction error in the high-score area is controlled within 
± 0.5 points, and the Spearman correlation coefficient is concentrated in The range of 
0.6–0.8 improves the positive correlation stability by 40% compared with the Baseline 
model.
3. Through noise interference stability test and cross-data set generalization ability 
verification, the system constructs a multi-dimensional index system including 
average absolute error, Spearman correlation coefficient, prediction score deviation, 
etc. In the noise interference scenario, the standard deviation of the system’s quality 
score within the action complexity interval is reduced by 40%, and the trend line 
slope is increased by 18%, indicating that the causal inference feature effectively 
enhances evaluation stability. The cross-dataset generalization verification shows 
that the system achieves the lowest cross-domain MSE across the four major motion 
capture datasets, demonstrating its generalization universality. To sum up, this study 
constructs a highly robust dance movement quality evaluation system by integrating

causal inference and invariant learning, providing a scientific and reliable quantitative 
evaluation method for the development of an intelligent dance teaching system. The

quality evaluation from “experience-driven” to “data-driven + causal reasoning, ” with 
significant theoretical innovation value and practical application prospects.
