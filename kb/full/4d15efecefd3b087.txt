This chapter provides a detailed and systematic overview of membrane-based technology for the efficient separation of heavy metal ions in water and wastewater systems. It has critically discussed key membrane processes, including reverse osmosis, nanofiltration, ultrafiltration, electrodialysis, membrane distillation, and forward osmosis, with a focus on separation mechanisms, performance characteristics, and operational advantages and limitations. The chapter emphasizes the significance of membrane selectivity, charge, and transport processes in controlling the rejection of heavy metals under various feed mixtures and operating conditions. In addition to the fundamentals of the processes, this chapter relates the membrane-based separation strategies to general sustainability objectives through a discussion of their applicability to global water security, resource recovery, and circular economy models. Exemplary case studies demonstrate the practical applications and performance outcomes of real-world treatment settings. The most critical issues, including membrane fouling, energy use, and concentrate handling, are thoroughly evaluated, and new materials and approaches for hybrid systems are emerging. Overall, this chapter provides a balanced perspective that helps readers understand when membrane-based approaches are most effective and where further development is still needed.
