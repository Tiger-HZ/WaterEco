Abstract Ice cover on lakes can have far‐reaching and profound effects on ecosystem structure and function. However, the duration and spatial extent of ice cover is changing rapidly with warming aquatic ecosystems. This loss of ice creates an urgent need to understand how lakes will function without ice cover, both during the ice‐on and ice‐free season, and how this loss of ice will interact with preexisting ecosystem stressors. One major question that remains is how changing winters and ice conditions may affect ice‐free season harmful algal blooms. Given that ice cover can affect nutrient loads and cycling, we suggest that ice will have indirect effects on algal blooms by modulating nutrient dynamics. Here we investigated the relationship between changing ice conditions, phosphorus, nitrogen, chlorophyll, phycocyanin, cyanobacteria, and microcystin by using 12‐yr monitoring datasets from the western basin of Lake Erie. We identified a potential pathway by which ice cover and precipitation affect spring nutrient loads, which then affect algal bloom severity. We also observe an overall positive relationship between variables, suggesting that years with less ice cover have lower nutrient concentrations and less severe harmful algal blooms.
