One Lake, Two Basins – Does Productivity Define Fish 
and Food Web Mercury Dynamics in Human‑impacted 
Lake Vesijärvi, Southern Finland?
A. J. Piro  · J. Weckström  · T. Malinen  · 
K. Kulo  · K. K. Kahilainen 
Received: 27 May 2026 / Accepted: 21 August 2026 / Published online: 2 September 2026 
© The Author(s) 2026
Abstract Productivity in large lakes is often het-
erogenous between basins, with potential effects on 
the fish community and contamination levels in the 
food web. Clean lakes and resilient food webs sup-
port biodiversity and ecological integrity, however, 
pressures on these ecosystems are numerous. Mer -
cury (Hg) is a ubiquitous neurotoxic metal linked to 
productivity, thus highlighting its potential value for 
lake monitoring. However, limited knowledge exists 
regarding the effectiveness of Hg monitoring between 
basins of large lakes with different nutrient concentra-
tions. In this study, primary producers, benthic mac-
roinvertebrates, zooplankton, and the fish community 
were sampled in the less productive Kajaanselkä and 
more productive Enonselkä basins of Lake Vesijärvi, 
southern Finland. Basin differences in total Hg con-
tent [THg] (mg kg−1) and its bioaccumulation in 
common fish species, and biomagnification in the 
food web were assessed. In fish, [THg] was generally 
higher in the Kajaanselkä basin, although not in all 
species. Bioaccumulation regression slopes between 
[THg] and length were also not consistently differ -
ent between basins. Biomagnification was detected 
in both basins through the regression of [THg] with 
nitrogen isotopes (δ 15N). However, the trophic mag-
nification slopes (TMS) and [THg] baselines (inter -
cepts) were similar. Variation in [THg] and bio-
accumulation found in some fish likely indicates 
differences in species-specific habitat and resource 
use and availability between basins. These methods 
revealed that basin differences in fish THg metrics 
did not translate to differences in food web biomag-
nification between basins, although the methods  did 
highlight the potential monitoring value of pelagic 
fish in large boreal lakes. Similar methods may give 
more pronounced results given larger differences 
in morphology and physical and chemical charac-
teristics between lake  basins, and future work may

1 Introduction
Municipal effluents have been identified as a pri-
mary cause of increased lake productivity for decades 
(Hasler, 1947; Rohde, 1969), and remain one of the 
most significant environmental impacts on lakes (Li-
kun et al., 2017; Smith & Schindler, 2009; Vitousek 
et al., 1997). As a limiting nutrient in lakes, excessive 
phosphorus causes eutrophication (Schindler, 1974, 
2012), and external loading from anthropogenic ori-
gins has been an ongoing concern across the boreal 
zone. This phenomenon is typically characterised by 
increased primary productivity, thereby driving the 
growth of algae and increasing turbidity and hypolim-
netic oxygen depletion. Perhaps the most significant 
change due to eutrophication is that cyanobacteria 
tend to eventually become the dominant component 
of phytoplankton blooms (Jeppesen et  al., 2005; 
Schindler, 1977; Smith, 1983). Changes to water 
quality, as well as changes in abundance and com-
munity composition of different species of fish, inver-
tebrates, and macrophytes are typical, and eutrophi-
cation may cause fish kills (Schindler et  al., 2008; 
Vollenweider & Kerekes, 1982). Lake eutrophication 
is commonly a consequence of intensive human set-
tlement, which may reduce the recreational value of 
these lakes and increase water treatment costs and 
health risks from cyanobacteria, therefore necessitat-
ing monitoring and potential remediation.
Monitoring of lakes in the European Union (EU) is 
used to classify lake status and identify where reme-
diation may be warranted. Monitoring of water bodies 
is required under the EU Water Framework Directive 
(2000/60), including the measurement of neurotoxic 
mercury (Hg) (European Commission, 2000). Cur -
rently in Finland, only one fish species, perch (Perca 
fluviatilis, total length 15–20  cm), is monitored for 
Hg content (Kangas, 2018). Hg is a global contami-
nant originating from natural and anthropogenic 
sources, and the northern hemisphere receives a dis-
proportionately higher amount of Hg atmospheric 
deposition than is emitted there (AMAP, 2021). Hg 
contamination in Europe is changing as emissions 
from EU countries have continually declined due to 
improved policy and effluent controls, while emis-
sions from more southerly parts of the world have 
increased (AMAP, 2021; Dastoor et al., 2022; Lode-
nius, 2013). However, the burning of fossil fuels and 
production of cement and non-ferrous metals remain 
strong contributors within the EU (AMAP, 2021). 
Additionally, climate change may result in higher 
levels of Hg in lakes due to increased mobilisation 
caused by more rain than snow in colder months, 
shorter winter seasons, and potentially greater atmos-
pheric deposition of Hg (Douglas et al., 2017; Obrist 
et al., 2018; Pelletier et al., 2021; Steffen et al., 2014). 
As a contaminant of primarily airborne origin in 
northern Europe, Hg contamination in lakes is nota-
bly complex and modified by the physicochemical 
and ecological attributes of these ecosystems (Schin-
dler et  al., 1995), likely necessitating lake-specific 
assessments.
Lake Vesijärvi in southern Finland is the 43rd larg-
est lake in Finland (107.5 km2) with a long history 
of deterioration due to municipal wastewater effluents 
from the City of Lahti. In the 1970s, sewage water 
was discharged into the southern Enonselkä basin, 
resulting in significant eutrophication, dangerous 
cyanobacterial blooms, and loss of recreational and 
fisheries value. Toxic cyanobacterial blooms have 
been associated with such health risks as skin irrita-
tion and algal toxin poisoning in humans, as well as 
fish kills (Igwaran et al., 2024; Lindholm et al., 1989; 
WHO, 2003). Initially, efforts to improve water qual-
ity were taken in the form of improved wastewater 
treatment and diversion of municipal effluents away 
from the lake, thereby significantly decreasing the 
external loading of nutrients (Horppila et  al., 1998; 
Keto & Sammalkorpi, 1988). Despite some level 
of recovery, within 10  years the Enonselkä basin 
returned to hosting large blooms of cyanobacteria 
throughout the year. Additional management and res-
toration efforts were then made, including hypolim-
netic oxygenation in the Enonselkä basin (Keto, 
1982) and biomanipulation by intensive trawling for 
roach (Rutilus rutilus) and smelt (Osmerus eperlanus) 
during 1989–1993, resulting in higher abundance 
of perch and vendace (Coregonus albula) (Peltonen 
et  al., 1999). As a result of biomanipulation, fish 
growth increased (Rask et al., 2020), thereby poten-
tially increasing somatic growth dilution (SGD) of

Hg in tissues (Lavigne et  al., 2010; Trudel & Ras-
mussen, 2006; Ward et al., 2010). At the same time, 
introduction of pikeperch (Sander lucioperca) led to 
the establishment, and later improvement of the fish-
ery (Ruuhijärvi et  al., 2005). However, despite an 
achieved clear-water state in the 1990s, Enonselkä 
eventually reverted to poor water quality, cyano-
bacteria blooms, and deep water hypoxia during the 
summer (Anttila et al., 2013), which even resulted in 
a fish kill during the warm summer of 2002 (Ruuhi-
järvi et al., 2005). Later on, during years of intensive 
aeration (surface water circulation to deep water in 
2010–2018) the smelt stock suffered from the lack 
of the  cool hypolimnetic habitat (Ruuhijärvi et  al., 
2020). However, hypolimnetic aeration was not found 
to increase oxygen flux into sediments and high phos-
phorus releases were still observed, although the peri-
ods of hypoxia and anoxia in Enonselkä were short-
ened (Horppila et  al., 2015; Salonen et  al., 2020), 
and  the aeration of the hypolimnion was terminated 
in 2018. This indicates continued nutrient loading 
beyond the threshold for internal loading from the 
surrounding Enonselkä catchment (Taipale et  al., 
2020). Remediating lake eutrophication requires a 
holistic lake-specific ecosystem approach, and loss 
of recreational and economic value necessitates 
such measures for monitoring the changing sta-
tus of lakes under pressure (Carvalho et  al., 2012). 
While Enonselkä has undergone a history of notable 
human-impact, the northern Kajaanselkä has not been 
exposed to as great an extent, and continually main-
tained higher water quality, less eutrophication, and 
limited hypoxia in the hypolimnion (KVVY, 2023, 
2025). Low elevation, multi-basin lakes are common 
in the boreal region and may have different morphol-
ogies, nutrient profiles, and ecosystem species com-
positions and abundances (Nelligan et al., 2020; Tam-
melin & Kauppila, 2018). This is the case with the 
Enonselkä and Kajaanselkä basins of Lake Vesijärvi 
(KVVY, 2023, 2025).
To assess trophic hierarchy, stable isotope ratios 
of nitrogen (δ 15N) are often used due to δ 15N 
enrichment with uptake through the food web, 
which in combination with Hg content of food web 
organisms can infer Hg biomagnification (Borgå 
et  al., 2012; Cabana & Rasmussen, 1994; Kidd 
et  al., 2012). Increased biodilution of Hg in the 
food web has been linked to eutrophication due to 
longer food chain length and increased number of 
trophic links and food web complexity, whereby Hg 
is more broadly distributed throughout the food web 
(Chen & Folt, 2005; Kozak et al., 2021; Pickhardt 
et al., 2002; Piro et al., 2025a). Lake eutrophication 
affects Hg contamination in the food web through 
food web composition and abundance, as well as 
energy pathways from benthic to pelagic sources 
(Eagles-Smith et  al., 2008; Hayden et  al., 2019). 
Fish Hg content is typically defined by diet, and 
biodilution of Hg in more eutrophic environments 
through the greater biomass of lower trophic levels 
may result from algal blooms, leading to lower Hg 
content in fish (Pickhardt et al., 2002; Razavi et al., 
2015). In Fennoscandia, low Hg content in fish has 
been measured in comparatively more eutrophic 
lakes (Hattula et al., 1978; Johnels et al., 1967). In 
contrast, the opposite has also been observed and 
attributed to longer food chain lengths promot-
ing higher levels of contaminants in upper trophic 
levels (Cabana & Rasmussen, 1996; Kozak et  al., 
2021). However, eutrophication may also increase 
the production and transfer efficiency of methylmer -
cury in the food web to the point of surpassing this 
dilution effect and increasing Hg levels (Fitzgerald 
& McCarty, 2024). Eutrophication resulting from 
sewage or agricultural discharge has also been 
linked to higher δ 15N (Hansson et al., 1997; Hobson 
et al., 2012; Ofukany et al., 2014), although lower 
δ15N in the food web has also been linked to ferti-
lisation (Peterson et al., 1993). The direction of fish 
Hg content and bioaccumulation, and food web Hg 
biomagnification appear to be inconsistent across 
eutrophicated lakes, likely depending on the nutri-
ent enrichment source (Ahonen et al., 2018; Kozak 
et al., 2021; Piro et al., 2025a; Verburg et al., 2014). 
Eutrophication resulting from forestry operations 
and peatland ditching has been linked to increased 
Hg levels, whereas the opposite has been observed 
in the case of eutrophication from agricultural and 
sewage inputs (Keva et  al., 2022; Kozak et  al., 
2021; Piro et al., 2025a). While monitoring Hg con-
tent in biota and in fish muscle as a human dietary 
source is required in the EU (European Commis-
sion, 2000, 2006), the monitoring of Hg biomagni-
fication is not.
In this study, the food webs of two major basins 
of large, human-impacted Lake Vesijärvi were sam-
pled to test for potential differences in the Hg dynam-
ics between the food webs of these basins, which have

different levels of productivity. The total Hg con-

biomagnification of THg in both basins were evalu-
ated. This study assessed two primary questions and 
predictions.
Question 1. Are there differences in the [THg] and 
bioaccumulation in the same fish species between 
basins?
Prediction 1: The [THg] will be higher in all fish 
species residing in the more productive Enonselkä 
basin and will exhibit reduced bioaccumulation 
than those residing in the Kajaanselkä basin, as 
previously observed along climate-productivity 
gradients (Ahonen et al., 2018).
Question 2. Does the biomagnification of THg 
significantly differ between basins?
Prediction 2: The biomagnification of THg will 
be greater in the less productive Kajaanselkä basin 
compared to the more productive Enonselkä basin, 
as detected through significant differences between 
slopes of the linear regression between [THg] and 
δ15N, termed as the trophic magnification slope 
(TMS), and the intercepts, termed as the [THg] 
baseline (Kozak et al., 2021).
2 Methods
2.1 Study Area
Lake Vesijärvi is a large boreal lake (107.5 km2; 
mean depth 6.1  m) (Keva et  al., 2022; Piro et  al., 
2025a) primarily comprising two major basins (Enon-
selkä and Kajaanselkä) in southern Finland with the 
city of Lahti located on the southern shore of Enon-
selkä and the village of Vääksy on the northern 
shore of Kajaanselkä (61°00′ N, 25°30′ E) (Table  1; 
Fig.  1). The average winter (January–March) and 
summer (June–August) air temperatures are − 4.9 °C 
and 15.8  °C, respectively, and average precipita-
tion is 135  mm in winter and 208  mm in summer 
(1990–2020, FMI database, 2026). Ice-cover typi-
cally forms on the lake in November/December and 
lasts until April/May (HERTTA database, 2026). The 
Enonselkä and Kajaanselkä basins are deep (maxi-
mum depth of 33 m and 42 m, respectively), and mes-
otrophic (total phosphorus: 20 µg L−1 and 11 µg L−1) 
with Secchi depths of 2 m.
2.2 Sampling
Primary producers (benthic algae, filamentous green 
algae, and macrophytes) were sampled by hand 
from the shoreline and littoral habitats and manu-
ally cleaned  at the Lammi Biological Station (LBS) 
laboratory. Zooplankton were sampled by vertical 
hauls from the deepest points (Enonselkä: 0–33  m, 
Kajaanselkä: 0–42 m) in each basin (Table  1) with a 
zooplankton net (500 µm mesh size, 0.8 m diameter, 
3 m length) and stored in plastic containers, while lit-
toral benthic macroinvertebrates were sampled from 
the shoreline using kick-nets (500 µm mesh size) and 
handpicking. All samples were stored in cool boxes 
for transport to the laboratory, where they were iden-
tified, sorted, and manually cleaned on the same day 
as sampling. Zooplankton samples were composited, 
benthic macroinvertebrates were sorted to genus or 
family, and all samples were stored in 2  ml plastic 
tubes.
Fish sampling was conducted in July–August 2022 
using Nordic multimesh gillnets (length 30 m, height 
1.5 m, comprising  12 2.5  m  wide panels of mesh 
sizes: 5, 6.25, 8, 10, 12.5, 15.5, 19.5, 24, 29, 35, 43 
and 55 mm) following the Finnish standard for Water 
Framework Directive sampling (described in Appel-
berg et  al., 1995). Briefly, gillnet sampling included

A total of 15 fish species were captured from 
Lake Vesijärvi, including bleak (Alburnus alburnus), 
bream (Abramis brama), roach, rudd (Scardinius

the length distribution in total  catches (Supplemen-

Following laboratory work, all plastic tubes 
were stored in a −20  °C freezer. All samples were 
freeze–dried at −54 °C for 48 h using a Christ Freeze 
Dryer (Alpha 1–2 LDplus) (Martin Christ Gefri-
ertrocknungsanlagen GmbH, Osterode am Harz, 
Germany) and subsequently pulverised for further 
analyses.
2.3 Mercury
Individual samples were measured for [THg] using 
a Direct Mercury Analyzer (DMA-80) (Mile-
stone S.r.l., Sorisole, Italy) (20–30  mg dry weight 
(d.w.)) at LBS. Blank samples (blank mean ± SD: 
0.00328 ± 0.0134  mg  kg−1, n = 31) and certified ref-
erence materials (DORM-4, National Research Coun-
cil Canada, mean ± SD, Hg 0.410 ± 0.055  mg  kg−1 
d.w) (ERM-BB422, European Reference Materi-
als, Hg 0.601 ± 0.030  mg  kg−1 d.w.) were used in 
the beginning and end of every run (DORM-4: 
0.433 ± 0.030 mg  kg−1 d.w., mean recovery = 102.1%, 
n = 29; ERM-BB422: 0.615  mg  kg−1 d.w., mean 
recovery = 102.2%, n = 2). Additionally, where suffi-
cient material was available, samples from 445 of the 
607 fish individuals (Enonselkä: 210, Kajaanselkä: 
235) were selected with representative length distri-
butions and measured for wet weight (w.w.) [THg]. 
The same procedure used for dry weight samples was 
employed in each wet weight run (blank mean ± SD: 
0.00118 ± 0.00352  mg  kg−1, n = 17; DORM-4: 
0.441 ± 0.029 mg  kg−1 d.w., mean recovery = 101.4%, 
n = 17). In all sample runs, every fifth sample was 
duplicated to assess variability.
2.4 Nitrogen Stable Isotopes
Representative subsamples of all freeze-dried mate-
rials were weighed and encapsulated in tin cups 
(animal material: 0.5–0.7  mg; primary producers: 
0.9–1.1 mg) for measurement of δ 15N. A total of 231 
fish, 47 invertebrate, and ten primary producer sam-
ples were measured. Averages were calculated per 
species in fish, per group in invertebrates, and pri-
mary producers were combined (Table 2; Supplemen-
tary Table S1). Samples were measured using a Flash 
EA1112 Elemental Analyzer connected to a Thermo 
Finnigan DELTAplus Advantage Mass Spectrometer 
at the University of Jyväskylä. Internal standards 
with a known reference to Vienna Pee Dee belem-
nite and atmospheric N2 were used after every fifth 
sample, and empty and blank samples were used in 
the beginning of each run. Analytical error for δ 15N 
was ≤ 0.2‰.

3 Results
3.1 THg Content
Of the fish species measured for [THg] between basins, 
the highest values were observed in pike (Fig.  2; 
Table 2; Supplementary Table  S1). The lowest meas-
urements, however, differed between basins. The low-
est in Enonselkä was measured in ruffe, while bream 
was the lowest in Kajaanselkä. Statistical differences in 
species adj[THg] between basins  were found in white 
bream, pikeperch, ruffe and smelt (p < 0.001), and 
near-significant in roach and rudd (Fig. 2; Supplemen-
tary Table S5). With the exception of white bream, fish 
adj[THg] was higher in Kajaanselkä for those species 
where a difference was found. Conversion factors for 
dry weight to wet weight [THg] ranged from 4.58–5.08 
in Enonselkä and 2.96–5.11 in Kajaanselkä, and a 
range of 4.65–5.03 considering species occurring in 
both basins (Table 3). A conversion factor of 5 is typi-
cally used for this purpose (Cresson et al., 2014, 2015; 
Magalhães et al., 2007), however, the values here dem-
onstrate variability (e.g., eel CF = 2.96).

Biomagnification of THg was significant in both

ured in an Enonselkä pike (δ 15N = 17.1).

4 Discussion
4.1 Basin Comparison of [THg] and 
Bioaccumulation in Fish
Six of the ten species examined were found to have 
a significant or near-significant difference in [THg] 
between the basins, and none of the samples exceeded 
the recommended guidelines for consumption (most 
species: 0.5  mg  kg−1, pike 1.0  mg  kg−1 w.w., EU 
Regulation 1881/2006) (European Commission, 
2006). The [THg] measured throughout the food web 
was generally lower in most species compared with 
a  series of  other Finnish boreal and subarctic lakes 
with similar nutrient levels (Ahonen et  al., 2018; 
Piro et al., 2023, 2025a). Additionally, bioaccumula-
tion slopes in fish were similar to those in the same 
species measured elsewhere in Finland, although the 
intercepts in this study were generally lower (Ahonen 
et al., 2018; Piro et al., 2023).

Higher [THg] in roach, rudd, pikeperch, ruffe, and 
smelt were found in Kajaanselkä, and higher [THg] in 
comparatively low productivity lakes and basins has 
been observed elsewhere in Fennoscandia (Hattula 
et al., 1978; Johnels et al., 1967; Piro et al., 2025a). 
This result is generally contrary to the initial predic-
tion of higher [THg] in the more productive Enon-
selkä basin. Although this phenomenon has been pre-
viously linked to comparatively decreased food chain 
length and biodilution (Kozak et al., 2021), the food 
chain lengths here are similar between basins. The 
historical source of increased productivity in Enon-
selkä is municipal sewage, which has been linked to 
Hg dilution in fish muscle, whereas lower produc-
tivity has been previously associated with increased 
[THg] due to greater Hg transfer efficiency (Clayden 
et al., 2013; Hattula et al., 1978; Johnels et al., 1967). 
Additionally, higher algal productivity in Enonselkä 
and different feeding strategies of the same spe-
cies between basins may be drivers of the [THg] dif-
ference. The first possibility is supported by recent 
measurements of lake chlorophyll and phytoplankton 
biomass, where higher concentrations were measured 
in Enonselkä (KVVY, 2023, 2025). The latter possi-
bility aligns with differing bioaccumulation metrics 
between basins in the same species.
In species where a difference in [THg] was iden-
tified, either a significant difference in bioaccumula-
tion slope or intercept, or a lack of significant bioac-
cumulation was found. However, the prediction of 
reduced bioaccumulation in Enonselkä compared 
with Kajaanselkä was rejected. Lower levels of Hg 
are typically found in littoral invertebrates compared 
with their pelagic counterparts (Kahilainen et  al., 
2017; Power et al., 2002), and fish consuming pelagic 
resources tend to be more enriched in Hg (Thomas 
et  al., 2016). Consequently, fish relying on pelagic 
resources have been found to bioaccumulate Hg to 
a greater extent compared with those relying on lit-
toral resources (Kahilainen et  al., 2017). This effect 
cascades up the food web, as pelagic carnivores feed-
ing on prey with higher levels of THg enrichment 
increase bioaccumulation metrics (Ahonen et  al., 
2018), which may be the case in smelt and pikeperch 
in Kajaanselkä compared with Enonselkä. Addi-
tionally, decreased body size and nutritional quality 
in zooplankton have been observed in Enonselkä, 
although this may fluctuate within and across years 
(Ruuhijärvi et al., 2020; Taipale et al., 2020). In white 
bream, however, comparatively higher levels of THg 
measured in Enonselkä individuals are likely linked 
to the lack of bioaccumulation in Kajaanselkä.
A lack of bioaccumulation in rudd (both basins), 
white bream (Kajaanselkä), and ruffe (Enonselkä) 
may result from different habitat use between basins, 
thereby influencing Hg levels through ontogenetic 
shifts with consumption of low trophic level food 
items in littoral areas compared with pelagic areas 
used in earlier life stages (Hayden et  al., 2014; 
Thomas et al., 2016). Ruffe rely on littoral and pro-
fundal resources (Hayden et  al., 2015), and the 
oxygenated profundal of Kajaanselkä facilitate its 
use to a greater extent than the anoxic profundal of 
Enonselkä. This may limit resource availability, thus 
restricting these species to littoral areas in Enonselkä. 
Profundal resources are typically higher in [THg] 
than those in littoral or pelagic habitats (Amundsen 
et  al., 2023; Chételat et  al., 2008; Kahilainen et  al., 
2017), which may explain the difference in ruffe Hg 
metrics. The omnivorous rudd, however, tends to 
feed on invertebrates and zooplankton at smaller size 
categories in littoral habitats, followed by increasing 
herbivory with age (Kapuscinski et  al., 2015; Nur -
minen et  al., 2003). This likely explains the limited 
bioaccumulation in this species in both basins. In 
Kajaanselkä white bream, initial foraging on pelagic 
zooplankton followed by littoral invertebrates with 
age (Wielgosz & Tadajewska, 1988; Yazıcıoğlu 
et  al., 2017) likely explains the lack of bioaccumu-
lation. In Enonselkä, bioaccumulation may indicate 
that white bream  mainly persist in the pelagic. This 
may result from greater competition in littoral habi-
tats due to higher abundance linked to increased 
productivity (Matern et  al., 2021), thus amplifying 
dietary overlaps with other fish (Specziár et al., 1997; 
Vinni et al., 2000) and reduced resource availability 
(Vadeboncoeur et al., 2003). Additionally, pikeperch 
have been introduced in Enonselkä (Kairesalo et al., 
1999), which now has an established naturally repro-
ducing stock. This may limit the use of pelagic areas 
for some fish as pikeperch tend to prefer feeding on 
juveniles and smaller, slender species such as roach, 
perch, and smelt (de Moraes et al., 2025; Keskinen & 
Marjomäki, 2004; Peltonen et al., 1996).

4.2 Biomagnification of THg in the Food Web 
between Basins
From the broader food web perspective, biomagni-
fication was similar with no significant difference in 
TMS or [THg] baselines between basins, contrary 
to the initial prediction. The TMS and [THg] base-
line in both basins are similar to lakes of comparable 
size and productivity previously measured in Canada 
(Kidd et  al., 2012; Lavoie et  al., 2013) and Finland 
(Piro et al., 2025b). Higher TMS has previously been 
linked to higher nutrient concentration in Canada 
(Kidd et  al., 2012), although the opposite has been 
observed in a global study (Lavoie et al., 2013) and 
in subarctic Finland (Kozak et  al., 2021; Piro et  al., 
2025a). Although differences in Hg metrics were 
noted in the same fish species between basins, these 
were not apparent when all species were included for 
assessment of biomagnification. One potential expla-
nation is that when considering the whole food web 
and the lack of expected difference in biomagnifica-
tion, these results may indicate recovery of the Enon-
selkä basin, in conjunction with recent water chem-
istry measurements (KVVY, 2023, 2025). Extensive 
efforts to reduce nutrient loads in Enonselkä have 
taken place, and fewer cyanobacteria blooms cou-
pled with a long-term trend of decreasing phospho-
rus indicate potential re-oligotrophication (Salonen 
et  al., 2020, 2023). However, comparatively higher 
methylation potential in the deep, anoxic areas of 
Enonselkä remains a pressure potentially offsetting 
biodilution (Fitzgerald & McCarty, 2024; Wang et al., 
2023) and equating it with that of the Kajaanselkä 
basin. This would imply higher [THg] measured in 
fish from Enonselkä, and the opposite was generally 
observed. Another potentially confounding factor is 
the movement of fish between basins (Ofukany et al., 
2014). However, based on a  tagging study of roach, 
bream, and pikeperch in Lake Vesijärvi, this seems 
to have limited importance between Enonselkä and 
Kajaanselkä (Kotakorpi et al., 2024).
4.3 Eutrophication and Recovery in Lake Vesijärvi, 
and Monitoring Implications
An extensive history of effluents from the catchment 
and subsequent efforts towards remediation have pro-
duced mixed results. The results of this study in com-
bination with recently measured water chemistry may 
support re-oligotrophication in Enonselkä (Salonen 
et  al., 2020, 2023). The differences observed here 
regarding THg bioaccumulation and biomagnification 
appear limited. This suggests that the baseline Hg and 
its uptake through the food webs of both basins are 
generally similar throughout the whole lake, rather 
than these basins being distinct based on basin-spe-
cific levels of inputs seen elsewhere (Hobson et  al., 
2012; Ofukany et al., 2014). The relationship between 
phosphorus levels and Hg biomagnification encom-
passes notable spatial variability (Kidd et  al., 2012; 
Lavoie et al., 2013; Piro et al., 2025a; Verburg et al., 
2014), and the rejection of the prediction of higher 
biomagnification in the less productive basin high-
lights lake-specific attributes and nutrient sources that 
must be considered rather than relying on assump-
tions based solely on trophic status.
This research has been conducted as part of the 
VesiMon project (https:// www. helsi nki. fi/ fi/ proje ktit/ 
vesis tojen- seura ntame netel mien- kehit tamis hanke), 
which aims to develop and test methods to holistically 
understand aquatic ecosystems, identify the pressures 
influencing them, and make monitoring water bodies 
more cost-effective and accurate. The measurement 
of Hg in aquatic biota is required under EU Direc-
tive 2000/60 (European Commission, 2000), and may 
provide valuable insight into the source of changes 
in lakes (Sonesten, 2003), as seen here where gen-
erally lower [THg] in fish from the more productive 
Enonselkä basin align with municipal effluents as a 
source of eutrophication. While Hg content in perch 
is typically considered for monitoring purposes in 
Finland (Kangas, 2018), Hg metrics in these basins 
were different in pikeperch and smelt. These species 
were found to have higher [THg] in the less produc-
tive basin, coupled with either a higher bioaccumu-
lation intercept or slope than in the more produc-
tive basin, whereas no differences were observed in 
perch. Future monitoring should consider including 
assessments of similar pelagic species, especially as 
pikeperch and smelt are abundant pelagic species in

4.4 Caveats
The two basins in this study are the major areas of 
Lake Vesijärvi, however, there are smaller basins in 
Lake Vesijärvi with higher levels of nutrients and 
eutrophication, as is the area closer to the city of 
Lahti. Despite a significant effort in field sampling, 
ensuring an accurate representation of food webs is a 
common challenge in such studies. In addition, gain-
ing full length distributions of all fish species pre-
sent and representative samples of lower food webs 
remain important pursuits for future efforts (Gozlan 
et al., 2019; Jackson & Füreder, 2006). Measurement 
of methylmercury (MeHg) content would have also 
been of significant benefit, as this is the species of Hg 
that primarily bioaccumulates in organisms and bio-
magnifies in food webs. Lower trophic levels typically 
have lower [MeHg]/[THg] ratios than those higher in

5 Conclusions
This study measured Hg metrics in the food web of 
two basins in Lake Vesijärvi with different nutrient 
status and a history of municipal effluents to assess 
whether Hg could be used to differentiate them. Sig-
nificantly different [THg] in fish dorsal muscle was 
observed in half of the species measured, which were 
typically higher in the less productive Kajaanselkä 
basin than in  the more productive Enonselkä basin. 
Limited differences were observed in THg  bioaccu-
mulation in fish, and in combination with [THg], did 
not translate to a significant difference in THg bio-
magnification between these basins. These findings 
suggest a biodilution effect in the Enonselkä food 
web, in combination with different species-specific 
feeding strategies and habitat use between basins. As 
biomagnification was found to be similar between 
basins, this may suggest re-oligotrophication of Enon-
selkä. Measurement of Hg in the food web may be 
valuable for monitoring purposes in identifying dif-
ferences between eutrophication sources in lakes and 
between basins, however, this  should be combined 
with measurements of physical and chemical charac-
teristics and catchment land use analyses. Addition-
ally, as significantly higher [THg] and bioaccumula-
tion metrics were observed in Kajaanselkä pikeperch 
and smelt, and not in the commonly monitored perch, 
these or similar pelagic species may be considered for 
monitoring purposes in large boreal lakes. Pikeperch 
may be especially valuable for monitoring as this spe-
cies is commonly consumed as a meal fish. Future 
studies should assess different basins of large lakes 
with more pronounced nutrient differences to confirm 
these findings in a broader context.
