The Alabama Cave Shrimp Palaemonias alabamae is an endemic stygobiont restricted to 6 cave systems in northern Alabama, USA, one of which is Bobcat Cave. To facilitate threat characterization, the Geological Survey of Alabama has collected biological and hydrogeological data at Bobcat Cave since 1992. This study’s objectives were to (1) evaluate ecological relationships for the population of P. alabamae in Bobcat Cave, and (2) quantify relationships between water quality constituents and cave water elevation to characterize seasonal runoff impacts on water quality in Bobcat Cave. Generalized additive models were used to evaluate relationships between shrimp counts or presence–absence and hydrogeological characteristics and between water quality and cave water elevation. The best count (R 2 = 18.8%) and presence–absence (R 2 = 60.1%) models described P. alabamae as an outcome of a chloride–cave water elevation interaction. Seasonal, chemodynamic relationships were detected for nitrate, total phosphorus, and lead while non-seasonal chemodynamic relationships were detected for chloride, phosphorus, nitrate, and chromium. Major findings indicate that (1) there is a nonlinear effect of chloride on P. alabamae , (2) chloride concentrations in Bobcat Cave do not appear to vary by season; instead, this solute is becoming more diluted over time due to increases in the system’s cave water elevation, and (3) there was no evidence that any other water quality constituent acted as a stressor on this population during the study period. Future efforts should account for the imperfect detection of this species to validate these results and effectively characterize demographic trends for this population over time.
