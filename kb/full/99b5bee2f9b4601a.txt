Lipids can be valuable sources of energy and nutrition for organisms, but their assimilation into organisms is hampered by the difficulty of transporting these hydrophobic compounds through water. In the digestive tract of metazoans, extracellular digestion of lipids requires transport of insoluble materials from the zone of digestion to the site of absorption, which is facilitated by formation of lipid aggregates in the form of micelles or emulsions. We examined the use of these two transport modes in benthic invertebrate animals via experiments and cross‐phyletic characteristics of gut fluids.
