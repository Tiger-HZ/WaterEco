The escalating pressure on global food security, driven by the decline in per capita arable land area and widespread soil degradation, necessitates a paradigm shift from conventional uniform fertilization to more precise nutrient management strategies. Variable rate fertilization (VRF) has emerged as a key enabler of sustainable intensification, offering the potential to enhance crop yield, optimize fertilizer use efficiency, and mitigate environmental impacts. This paper provides a comprehensive review of the current state of VRF research, focusing on (i) field characterization methods using soil and vegetation indices, (ii) decision-support models for variable-rate nutrient application, and (iii) the design and operation of VRF applicators. By synthesizing recent advances and identifying persistent challenges, this review aims to provide a scientific basis and practical pathways for implementing VRF across diverse cropping systems.
