Environmental management of urban cemeteries in developing countries faces the challenge of overcoming purely hydrogeological assessment models that ignore the complexity of socio-territorial and institutional interactions. This article presents the application of the Integrated and Multiscale Forensic Framework for Diagnosing and Assessing Vulnerabilities Related to Cemetery Activities (FFIMDAVAC) and the Cemetery Environmental Vulnerability Index (IVAC) at the Quinta dos Lázaros Cemetery Complex (CELAZ) in Salvador, Bahia, Brazil. The methodology is grounded in the Theory of Cemetery Environmental Vulnerability (TVAC) and the OD–ID–JT traceability protocol (Direct Observation, Documentary Information, and Technical Judgment), integrating eight analytical dimensions into a multi-criteria index that synthesizes environmental vulnerability on a scale from 1.00 to 5.00. The results revealed an IVAC of 4.61, classifying the complex as Very High Vulnerability, validated by the convergence of 7/7 independent lines of evidence. The analysis demonstrated that vulnerability stems not only from the physical environment's susceptibility but from the convergence of operational weaknesses, governance gaps, and environmental injustice — the socio-territorial, operational, and governance dimensions jointly accounted for 33.68% of the total score, a proportion that would remain invisible in assessments conducted exclusively through GOD and DRASTIC models. The dimension contribution ranking was: D8 (17.17%) &gt; D2 = D5 (14.45% each) &gt; D1 = D4 = D6 (13.73% each) &gt; D3 (9.76%) &gt; D7 (5.78%) . The study documents the phenomenon of Illusory Compliance, in which environmental licensing based exclusively on documentary compliance becomes dissociated from the material reality of the territory, and concludes that groundwater protection and the promotion of environmental justice in urban cemetery areas require the adoption of integrated models that articulate physical, forensic, and institutional evidence.
