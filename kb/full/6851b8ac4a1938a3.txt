Tunnel drainage deterioration reduces groundwater release capacity and may increase local water pressure behind linings, whereas aggregate outlet discharge can conceal different local pressure states after capacity or connectivity changes. This study determines when aggregate discharge remains an informative proxy for local pressure and when that inference fails. A reduced framework coupling groundwater supply, finite component capacity, and network topology was evaluated using cross-head blockage-discharge data, a 6 × 6 blockage length–area dataset, and multi-outlet blockage cases. Increasing blockage from 60% to 80% reduced normalized discharge from 0.73–0.84 to 0.26–0.29.
