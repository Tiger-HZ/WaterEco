Existing analytical models for aquifer thermal energy storage (ATES) system treat aquitards as single porosity and neglect heat convection in aquitards. This study develops two novel analytical models for heat transfer in a double-porosity three-layer ATES system of the upper aquitard, lower aquiclude, and middle leaky aquifer, incorporating heat convection in the aquitard. The standard model adopts traditional governing equations to each layer. The Robin model applies a new double-porosity heat-capacity Robin boundary condition to the aquitard and the governing equations to the other layers. The new Robin boundary condition is derived by the divergence theorem of Gauss. The semi-analytical solutions of both models are derived. The finite element solutions for fully two-dimensional transfer are obtained to release the assumption of only radial transfer in the aquifer and only vertical transfer in the other layers in the analytical models. Results suggest both semi-analytical solutions agree within 5% relative error in temperature when two quantitative conditions are met. Quadruple-humped temporal temperature distributions representing the double-porosity effect are predicted. One finite element solution with the new Robin boundary condition and no aquitard discretization takes 1/149 of the computing time of obtaining the other finite element solution with the governing equations and the fine aquitard discretization. The present solution applies to a field ATES experiment in Alabama, USA, demonstrating the effect of heat convection in aquitards is minor during the injection period but significant after the period. This study may provide a useful approach and a better understanding of ATES systems.
