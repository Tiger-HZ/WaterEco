The development of tourism in regions with high ethnocultural diversity requires effective mechanisms for the spatial organization of tourist activities, ensuring a balance between economic interests, preserving cultural heritage, and protecting the rights of indigenous peoples. This problem is especially acute for Khabarovsk Krai, which is home to eight indigenous peoples of the Russian North that have unique cultural practices and traditional forms of environmental management. The purpose of the study was to identify and provide a spatial characteristic of the tourist clusters of Khabarovsk Krai, as well as to develop a model for their management, considering the ethnocultural features of the territories of residence of the indigenous peoples of the North of Russia. The methodological basis of the study consisted of the cluster approach, spatial analysis, comparative analysis of regulatory documents, and analysis of the tourist and recreational potential of the territories. The information base included strategic documents of the Russian Federation and Khabarovsk Krai, statistical materials, and scientific publications on the problems of tourist clustering and ethnocultural tourism. The study identified four tourist clusters on the territory of Khabarovsk Krai: the Khabarovsk Tourist Cluster, the Southern Tourist Cluster, the Komsomolsk Tourist Cluster, and the Northern Tourist Cluster. Each cluster was characterized in terms of spatial boundaries, functional specialization, transport accessibility, and the involvement of indigenous peoples in tourist activities. The Southern, Komsomolsk, and Northern clusters were found to have a pronounced ethnocultural core formed by the territories of residence of the Nanai, the Ulch, the Nivkh, the Evenki, the Evens, Negidals, Orochs, and the Udege people. A three-level model of tourist cluster management including the strategic, tactical, and operational dimensions was proposed, providing for the participation of representatives of indigenous peoples in decision-making on the tourist development of the territories. The scientific novelty of the study consists in the identification of the ethnocultural dimension of tourist clustering as an independent factor in territorial development and in the developed institutional management model for tourist clusters adapted to the conditions of the Russian Far East.
