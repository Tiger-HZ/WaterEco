 Abstract Migrasomes, vesicular structures formed at the termini of retraction fibers during cell migration, actively sort and release diverse molecular cargoes to mediate intercellular communication. Recently, the involvement of migrasomes in central nervous system (CNS) disorders has garnered increasing attention, with their dual pathogenic and protective functions emerging as a key area of investigation. This review systematically delineates the opposing roles of migrasomes in neurodegenerative diseases, acute brain injury, and brain tumors, and critically analyzes the potential mechanisms underlying their functional polarity. We propose a context-dependent model of functional polarity. In this model, migrasome function is not intrinsically predetermined but is jointly shaped by the cells from which they originate, their cargo composition, and the disease microenvironment. Furthermore, we evaluate the translational potential and key challenges associated with leveraging migrasomes as novel diagnostic biomarkers, therapeutic targets, and engineered delivery vehicles are evaluated. This review aims to establish a theoretical framework for the precise modulation of migrasomes to enable targeted interventions in CNS diseases.
