Water availability influences the distribution, behavior, and interactions of wildlife, especially in arid and semiarid environments. In Argentina’s extra-Andean Patagonia, the scarcity of natural water resources has prompted the establishment of artificial water points, although their ecological role remains poorly understood. This study evaluated species richness, visitation rates, and temporal patterns of use by birds and mammals at artificial water points located in two nature reserves on Península Valdés, Chubut province, Argentina. Camera traps were used to assess species richness and visitation rates during summer period, while seasonal variation in visitation rates was analyzed over a full year.
