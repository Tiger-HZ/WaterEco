Abstract Tappan Lake, a medium-sized shallow reservoir situated in Harrison County, OH, exhibits recurring algal blooms despite having limited external nutrient loading from its predominantly forested watershed. This study examined the role of internal nutrient loading and sediment resuspension as the principal drivers of water quality degradation and increasing algal bloom in the lake using remote sensing, observed data, and hydrodynamic and water quality modeling. Initial analysis was focused on establishing the statistical correlation between Sentinel-2-derived Normalized Difference Chlorophyll Index (NDCI) and in-situ Chl-a measurements to validate the index as a reliable proxy for algal biomass at Tappan Lake.
