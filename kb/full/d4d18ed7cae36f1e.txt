 Effect of iron biofortification on growth, yield and soil nutrient availability of spinach ( Spinacia Oleracea L.) grown in calcareous soil
 
 
 
 Author(s): 
 
 Chaudhari Payal, SL Waikar, HK Kausadikar, SP Zade, GS Pawar, PH vaidya, AL Dhamak 
 
 
 Abstract: 
 
 A field experiment was conducted during Rabi 2025–26 at the Research Farm, Department of Soil Science, Vasantrao Naik Marathwada Krishi Vidyapeeth, Parbhani, Maharashtra, to study the effect of iron biofortification on growth, yield, soil physico-chemical properties and nutrient availability in spinach ( Spinacia oleracea L.) grown in calcareous soil. The experiment was conducted in a Randomized Block Design with three replications and eight treatments comprising soil and foliar application of FeSO 4 , Fe-EDTA and iron-solubilizing bacteria along with recommended dose of fertilizers (RDF). The initial soil was alkaline (pH 7.71) with 51.67 g kg −1 CaCO 3 . Treatment T 7 (RDF + soil Fe-EDTA @ 2.5 kg ha −1 + foliar Fe-EDTA @ 0.25%) recorded the highest plant height (26.51 cm), number of leaves (6.27 plant-1), leaf area (376 cm 2 ), fresh yield (186.00 q ha −1 ) and dry matter yield (33.78 q ha −1 ). Soil pH, EC, organic carbon and CaCO 3 were not significantly influenced by treatments. The highest available N (206.97 kg ha −1 ), P (14.11 kg ha −1 ), K (691.71 kg ha −1 ), DTPA-Fe (4.58 mg kg −1 ), Mn (4.58 mg kg −1 ), Zn (0.71 mg kg −1 ) and Cu (2.51 mg kg −1 ) were recorded under T 7 . Thus, combined soil and foliar application of Fe-EDTA improved spinach growth, yield and nutrient availability in calcareous soil.
