Abstract Scanning tunneling microscopy and spectroscopy were performed on a cleaved n -InAs(110) surface at 78 K in an ultra-high vacuum. An effective-Fermi-level-dependent charge state transition of defects was driven by Fröhlich-scattering-induced tunnel electron doping, manifesting as a donor state transition from D + to D 0 . An approximation formula of the effective Fermi level is proposed to estimate the chemical potential for defect formation. We detected the formation of metastable DX − centers, stimulated by the elevated quasi-Fermi level of secondary electrons, in the near-surface region at energies exceeding the threshold for tunnel electron impact-ionization.
