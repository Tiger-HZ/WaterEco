Abstract Fluoride-rich groundwater threatens drinking-water quality across northern China, yet the hydrochemical controls can differ substantially between geomorphic settings. This study compiled 212 groundwater samples with complete major-ion data from the North China Plain (NCP; n = 109) and the Taihang Mountain region (THM; n = 103) to compare fluoride occurrence and its hydrochemical constraints. Hydrochemical facies, Pearson correlations, Gibbs diagrams, mineral saturation indices, and cation-exchange indicators were integrated. NCP groundwater had higher mean total dissolved solids and greater hydrochemical heterogeneity, whereas THM groundwater had higher mean fluoride concentrations and a higher proportion of samples exceeding 1.5 mg/L.
