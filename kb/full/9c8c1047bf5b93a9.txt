The Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna (GBM) River Basin ranks as one of the largest and most strategically significant transboundary river basins. It covers parts of Bangladesh, Bhutan, China, India, and Nepal. This river basin is fast becoming a crucial geopolitical area where water plays an increasingly influential role in shaping interstate relations, security dynamics, economic development, and foreign policy in South Asia. Asymmetric geographical position of the riparian states where China is upstream in the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra) River, India's pivotal role, the dependency of Bangladesh being located downstream, and Nepal and Bhutan's hydro potential have made this river basin one of the arenas of hydro-politics and water diplomacy.
