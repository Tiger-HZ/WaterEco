Abstract Earth observations enable consistent and spatially contiguous city-wide observations, yet their effective integration into urban planning remains a challenge. Surface thermal patterns reflect the interplay between urban forms (morphology, density) and functions (land use, sociodemographic vulnerability), but most assessment methods rely on spatial units that are not aligned with how architects and planners understand and transform urban space. This limits their usefulness for design practice, which works through plots and blocks that reflect spatial form but overlook functional conditions essential to understanding heat vulnerability. We present a method that integrates forms and functions to support site selection, using a multicriteria ranking based on more than fifty variables
