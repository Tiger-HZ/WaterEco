Abstract Ocean particles are a heterogeneous collection, including living and dead cells, feces, mucus‐rich aggregates, and mineral shells and frustules. In the well‐lit upper ocean, phototrophic processes fuel production of particulate matter; however, only a relatively small percentage (&lt; 10% per day) of this material (mostly detrital) escapes the recycling‐intensive upper ocean, transiting downward by gravitational settling. In the North Pacific Subtropical Gyre, sinking particles are considered the major pathway supporting the vertical export of material to the mesopelagic zone. In the summer of 2021, an expedition explored a phytoplankton bloom, constrained within an anticyclonic eddy, that was detected from satellite chlorophyll products.
