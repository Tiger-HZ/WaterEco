This research analyzes the water crisis in the municipality of Vinhedo, within the PCJ Basin, aiming to understand its causes and identify sustainable solutions for local water security. The objective was to assess whether the city’s water stress results from natural water scarcity or from failures in water resource management. The methodology was based on a literature review, analysis of official data, and comparison of information on water supply, distribution losses, rainfall, sanitation, and consumption. Data from Sanebavi, IBGE, SNIS, and INMET were used, along with an interview with city councilor Luís Vieira. Results indicate that Vinhedo has sufficient water availability to meet population demand, with relevant rivers, reservoirs, and streams. However, distribution losses, urban expansion, planning failures, and infrastructure limitations increase water stress. The study concludes that the crisis is mainly related to poor management rather than natural scarcity, highlighting the need for integrated planning and continuous investment.
