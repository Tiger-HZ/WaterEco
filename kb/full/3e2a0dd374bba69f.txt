Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) serves as a vital indicator of soil health, ecosystem performance, and climate-change mitigation efforts. Soil erosion leads to the loss and redistribution of SOC by stripping away carbon-rich topsoil. This review explores the relationships among soil erosion, SOC dynamics, methods for estimating SOC, and Soil and Water Conservation (SWC) practices across various geographical areas, land uses, soil depths, and landscape positions. The results reveal that erosion typically diminishes SOC levels, while techniques such as contour bunds, contour trenches, vegetative barriers, terraces, conservation tillage, mulching, and integrated watershed management can significantly increase SOC storage.
