Abstract The Nicaraguan lake system, comprising two Great Lakes and several isolated crater lakes, is a natural laboratory for investigating how environmental heterogeneity and isolation shape biodiversity and a classic model for Midas cichlid diversification. We asked whether lake characteristics associated with cichlid diversification are also linked to prokaryotic and eukaryotic diversity patterns. Using environmental DNA metabarcoding of water and sediments, we characterized communities in the large, shallow Great Lakes and smaller, deeper crater lakes. Lakes differed markedly in environmental conditions, and community composition differed between water and sediments, while patterns suggested higher prokaryotic diversity in sediments and greater eukaryotic diversity in the water column
