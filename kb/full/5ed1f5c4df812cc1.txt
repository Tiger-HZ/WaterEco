Abstract Climate warming and legacy phosphorus (P) in sediments are two main factors counteracting current efforts to prevent eutrophication of temperate freshwater lakes and reservoirs. Climate warming impacts lacustrine thermal regimes, accelerates harmful cyanobacterial growth, and increases the probability of periodic anoxic bottom conditions. Legacy P in sediments becomes influential over eutrophication because of its reactivation under anoxic conditions. Lake Stechlin is a deep glacial lake of the Baltic Lake area, Germany in which artificial external P loading has been effectively blocked for more than 3 decades.
