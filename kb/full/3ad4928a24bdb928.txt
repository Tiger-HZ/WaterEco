Abstract Rising atmospheric CO 2 concentrations consistently reduce mineral concentrations in C 3 crops, yet the mechanisms driving this decline – dilution, reduced transpiration-driven mass flow, and altered root nutrient acquisition capacity – have rarely been tested simultaneously under field conditions. Here, we grew two soybean (Glycine max Merr.) varieties with contrasting yield responses to elevated [CO 2 ] (Loda and HS93-4118) at the Soybean Free Air CO 2 Enrichment (SoyFACE) facility and quantified the independent contributions of dilution, transpiration, and root uptake to observed changes in tissue mineral concentration and nutrient yield. Elevated [CO 2 ] increased grain yield by 21% and 6% in Loda and HS, respectively, and reduced transpiration by 13-14% across both varieties.
