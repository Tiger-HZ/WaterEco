Abstract This chapter examines how isotope tracing techniques ushered in a radical shift in thinking regarding the role of groundwater in storm runoff. Early isotope tracing with oxygen and hydrogen isotopes revealed that storm hydrographs were influenced primarily by pre-event, or “old,” water rather than event rainfall or snowmelt. This discovery challenged the long-held assumption that groundwater played a minimal role in stormflow generation. It also led to the development of several new perceptual models to explain the rapid mobilization of stored subsurface water.
