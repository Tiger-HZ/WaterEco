Objectives: The main objective of this study was to evaluate and compare the adsorption performance of zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnO NPs) and iron-doped zinc oxide nanoparticles (Fe-ZnO NPs) for the removal of hexavalent chromium [Cr(VI)] from aqueous solutions. Method: ZnO NPs and Fe-ZnO NPs were synthesized and characterized using SEM and FTIR analyses to investigate their morphology and surface functional groups. Batch adsorption experiments were conducted under different operating conditions to assess Cr(VI) removal efficiency, adsorption isotherms, kinetics, and thermodynamic behavior. Findings: Fe-ZnO NPs exhibited a significantly higher adsorption capacity (166.67 mg g ^{-1} ) than ZnO NPs (66.67 mg g ^{-1} ), demonstrating a 2.5-fold enhancement in Cr(VI) removal performance. Adsorption data were best fitted by the Langmuir isotherm model (R2 = 0.966–0.981), indicating monolayer adsorption, while the pseudo-second-order kinetic model (R2 = 0.987–0.999) suggested chemisorption as the dominant mechanism. Thermodynamic analysis revealed increased interfacial ordering for Fe-ZnO NPs (negative ΔS°) and increased randomness for ZnO NPs (positive ΔS°). Novelty: This study demonstrates that iron doping substantially enhances the Cr(VI) adsorption performance of ZnO NPs and provides a comprehensive understanding of the associated adsorption mechanism. The superior adsorption capacity and favorable adsorption behavior of Fe-ZnO NPs highlight their potential as an efficient adsorbent for the treatment of chromium-contaminated wastewater. Keywords: Doped Nanoparticles, Iron Doped Zinc Oxide Nanoparticles, Adsorption, Kinetics, Heavy Metals
