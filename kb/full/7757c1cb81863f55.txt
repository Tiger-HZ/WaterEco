Objectives: To evaluate an ethanol extract of Senna tora (L.) Roxb. fruit (STEE) in CFA-induced arthritic mice and characterize its phytochemical and antioxidant properties. Materials and Methods: Female Swiss albino mice were assigned to normal, CFA, CFA+meloxicam, and CFA+STEE (100, 200, or 300 mg/kg/day) groups. Arthritis was induced on Day 0; treatments were administered orally on Days 8–28. The hind-paw response was normalized to each group’s Day 0 mean and analyzed descriptively because individual repeated-measures records were unavailable. Joint ROS signal and catalase (CAT), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) activities were measured. Phytochemicals and in vitro DPPH, ABTS, and FRAP responses were assessed. Results: STEE produced concentration-related antioxidant responses in vitro. At 300 mg/kg/day, joint ROS was 1.72 ± 0.03 AU/mg protein versus 6.33 ± 0.04 in CFA controls, with higher CAT, SOD, and GPx activities (p &lt; 0.05). Normalized hind-paw trajectories declined after treatment with meloxicam or STEE. Conclusion: STEE attenuated CFA-associated joint oxidative stress and showed a descriptive reduction in the hind-paw response. Anti-arthritic efficacy requires confirmation using verified individual longitudinal data, joint histopathology, inflammatory biomarkers, and longer-term safety assessment.
