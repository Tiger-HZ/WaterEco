Abstract Water near solid–liquid interfaces is well known to exhibit structural and dynamical properties distinct from those of the bulk, owing to strong interactions with the solid surface. While water dynamics near the interface have been quantified, a direct atomistic link between spatially heterogeneous surface–water hydrogen bonding and local diffusion remains elusive. Here, using classical molecular dynamics simulations of water confined by silica, we examine how surface topology and silanol distributions modulate the local structure and diffusion of water. To characterize the structure of water near the interface, water density profiles and spatial density maps were analyzed.
