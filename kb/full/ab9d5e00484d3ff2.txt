Abstract Efficient trophic transfer of energy and matter underpins ecosystem functionality, service provision, and stability. The efficiency of energy and matter transfer is jeopardized by various environmental stressors, but their limiting factors might be distinct and require mechanistic elucidation. Addressing this gap, this study examined the transfer efficiency of energy and fatty acids (FAs), and consumer energy budget in a highly representative and widely distributed marine tritrophic food chain under nutrient enrichment using in situ enclosure and lab mesocosm experiments, as well as with a comprehensive global‐scale estimation.
