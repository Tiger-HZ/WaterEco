Abstract
Surface acoustic wave (SAW) sensors are extensively employed for temperature/strain measurement. However, precise
temperature/strain sensing remains challenging under complex conditions involving simultaneous mechanical strains
and temperature ﬂuctuations, due to the inherent cross-sensitivity between temperature and strain. To address this
challenge, we developed a single resonator-type SAW sensor operable up to 600 °C and integrated machine learning
techniques for temperature-strain multiphysics decoupling. Six machine learning models are implemented and
evaluated using normalized root mean square error ( NRMSE), root mean square error ( RMSE), mean absolute error
(MAE), coefﬁcient of determination ( R²), and execution time ( ET) metrics. Results demonstrate that the extreme gradient
boosting (XGBoost) model achieves optimal predictive performance from 22 to 160 °C and applied strains of 0 με to
800 με. For temperature/strain predictions respectively, R² values reach 99.97% and 99.23%, simultaneously enabling
high-precision temperature and strain sensing. Our study presents an effective solution for simultaneous temperature
and strain sensing using a single resonator-type SAW sensor operating in a single acoustic wave mode.
Introduction
Temperature/strain sensors are highly required in ﬁelds
such as healthcare, aerospace, infrastructure, and energy/
power systems 1–4. Among various measurement techni-
ques, surface acoustic wave (SAW) sensors are widely
employed for temperature/strain monitoring due to their
advantages of passive wireless sensing capability, fast
response speed, and high stability, etc 5–8. However, under
real-world conditions, SAW sensors not only respond
simultaneously to temperature and strain, but also exhibit
inherent temperature-strain cross-sensitivity 9,10. This
characteristic will further amplify the interference of
coupled parameter changes on measurement results,
which keeps a great challenge for high-precision tem-
perature/strain sensing 6.
In recent years, a variety of schemes for temperature-
strain multiphysics decoupling in SAW sensors are pro-
posed. Guangyao Pei et al. 11 developed a SAW tempera-
ture sensor with a strain-isolation structure, which is
capable of reducing cross-strain sensitivities from 176.1 to
2.5 Hz/με. Within the temperature range of 80 –850 °C,
the temperature detection error remains below 0.5 ‰ ,
achieving high-precision temperature sensing perfor-
mance. However, this kind of decoupling scheme com-
pletely relies on strain-isolation structure design, which
leads to a complex sensor architecture and signi ﬁcant
development challenges. For high-precision strain sen-
sing, common decoupling schemes mainly include dual-

resonator beat frequency and dual-mode beat frequency.
The dual-resonator beat frequency scheme typically
requires integrating two independent resonant units on
the same substrate, and strain measurement is achieved
through frequency difference calculation 12,13. Its structure
needs to strictly control the consistency of the two reso-
nators in the lithography process to avoid introducing
additional frequency drift due to excessive deviation of the
interdigital transducers. In addition, an additional isola-
tion layer needs to be designed to reduce crosstalk
between units, resulting in an increase in sensor size.
More critically, this scheme often ignores the in ﬂuence of
temperature on strain sensitivity, thereby hindering
complete temperature compensation and making it dif ﬁ-
cult to meet the requirements of high-precision scenar-
ios14,15. The dual-mode beat frequency scheme uses only
a single resonator-type SAW sensor, and realizes
temperature-strain decoupling through the frequency
difference of exciting two different propagation modes,
which signi ﬁcantly reduces the structural complexity 16.
However, not all acoustic wave modes are suitable for
achieving ideal signal decoupling through beat frequency
processing. Studies by Zhang et al. 17 have shown that the
effectiveness of dual-mode beat frequency decoupling
highly depends on the consistency of the temperature
response of the two modes. Only when the two modes of
the same sensor have the same temperature coef ﬁcient
and strain transfer ratio can the frequency difference
operation offset the temperature-induced frequency drift
and the strain transfer ratio variation caused by the soft-
ening of the adhesive layer. In practical applications, it is
difﬁcult to accurately match the temperature coef ﬁcients
of different types of acoustic wave modes, and the exci-
tation of multiple acoustic wave modes relies on speci ﬁc
structural designs, which further limits the universality of
the dual-mode beat frequency scheme. In particular, this
processing may usually reduce the strain sensitivity 17,18.
For high-precision temperature-strain composite sen-
sing, general decoupling methods mostly involve inte-
grating temperature-sensitive units into SAW strain
sensors, or utilizing multi-resonator beat frequency. The
former routine requires complete strain isolation in the
temperature-sensitive unit to achieve high-precision
temperature detection. Subsequently, high-precision
strain detection is achieved through beat frequency pro-
cessing with the SAW strain sensor or by identifying the
resonant frequency-strain response curve at the speci ﬁc
operating temperature19,20. While this decoupling method
needs a complex sensor structure, a key technical chal-
lenge in practical applications remains the dif ﬁculty of
achieving complete strain isolation within the
temperature-sensitive unit. The latter requires three SAW
resonators fabricated based on different piezoelectric
materials to achieve high-precision simultaneous
measurement of temperature and strain via pairwise beat
frequency processing 21. The sensor structure is complex
and ignores the in ﬂuence of temperature on strain sen-
sitivity. At present, the single acoustic wave mode-excited
single resonator-type SAW sensor remains the main-
stream approach, so there is an urgent need to develop a
solution for its temperature-strain multiphysics
decoupling.
Recently, researchers proposed to integrate machine
learning techniques with sensors to achieve accurate
detection of physical quantities 22,23. From 2022 to 2025,
the research team at Hunan University conducted
extensive work on the signal decoupling of SAW sensors
using machine learning models. Key contributing work
includes ultraviolet light-temperature decoupling 10, strain
direction-magnitude decoupling 24, precise ultraviolet
detection25 and temperature detection 26 under strain
interference. However, little research was reported on
temperature-strain multiphysics decoupling for SAW
sensors.
In this work, we developed a high-temperature SAW
sensor operable up to 600 °C, demonstrating excellent
temperature sensing characteristics. Temperature/strain
sensing tests were conducted under 22 –160 °C and
0–800 µε loading conditions. We theoretically analyzed the
temperature-strain cross-sensitivity of SAW sensors and
introduced six machine learning algorithms, including
ridge regression (RR), polynomial regression (PR), multiple
linear regression (MLR), extreme gradient boosting
(XGBoost), random forest regression (RFR), and decision
tree regression (DTR), to establish regression models for
temperature-strain multiphysics decoupling. Feature
information was extracted from S11 spectra under varying
temperature and strain loading conditions. Feature
importance was quanti ﬁed, and the impact of different
input feature combinations on predictions of temperature
and strain was evaluated using the normalized root mean
square error ( NRMSE), root mean square error ( RMSE),
mean absolute error ( MAE), coef ﬁcient of determination
(R²) and execution time (ET) metrics. As shown in Table 1,
compared with the methods reported in existing literature
for decoupling SAW sensing signals based on machine
learning models, the temperature-strain decoupling model
constructed in this study can simultaneously achieve high-
precision and accurate evaluation of both temperature and
strain signals. This scheme provides a precise extraction
path for temperature and strain signals for single-resonator
SAW sensors excited by a single acoustic wave mode.

The amplitude variation of S₁₁ is the combined result of
temperature and strain. Higher temperatures attenuate
the S₁₁ amplitude, whereas larger strain levels enhance it,
ultimately yielding ﬂuctuation characteristics that stem
from the superposition of these two factors. Speci ﬁcally,
the resonant frequency decreases with increasing tem-
perature and strain, while the amplitude of | S₁₁| declines
as temperature rises but generally increases with growing
strain. This phenomenon primarily arises from the fact
that both temperature and strain induce alterations in the
IDT period of SAW sensors, thereby modifying the
wavelength of SAW. Additionally, these factors also affect
parameters such as the density and elastic modulus of
piezoelectric materials 31, leading to changes in SAW
propagation velocity. These combined effects ultimately
lead to the changes in the resonant frequency, which
cause shifts in the impedance matching point, ultimately
affecting the S11 values. In summary, both temperature
and strain affect the output frequency signals of SAW
sensors, demonstrating signi ﬁcant coupling effects
between the two parameters.

employed to predict the temperature and strain

sensor, where each input dataset corresponds to a known
output target variable (i.e., the calibration temperature
and strain). The objective of the models is to identify the
underlying relationships between input data and output

Stratiﬁed data splitting strategy
In machine learning models, the training set constitutes
the data collection used for model training, encompassing
both feature labels and corresponding target variables.
Through the training set, models establish mapping
relationships between inputs and outputs by learning the
patterns in the data to adjust internal parameters, thereby
minimizing prediction error. While larger training set
enhances a model’s capacity to capture complex mappings
between input feature labels and output target variables,
but it is also prone to over ﬁtting. The training set typically
comprises 70 –80% of the total data. The validation set is
an intermediate data partition between the training set
and the test set, speci ﬁcally used for hyperparameter
tuning and model complexity optimization, it neither
participates in the update of model parameters during the
training process nor serves as unseen data for the ﬁnal
generalization evaluation. Its core function is to prevent
overﬁtting caused by improper hyperparameter settings
and help select the optimal model structure. The test set
serves as the data collection for evaluating model per-
formance and this data has never been used at all during
the model training process. Its function is to assess gen-
eralization capability of the model, that is, to determine
how the model performs on unencountered data.
To address the adjacent sample leakage bias that may
occur when randomly splitting data from the same device
and batch, and to enhance the rationality of data dis-
tribution and generalization reliability, we divided the
data into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio.
Considering that temperature has a more signi ﬁcant
impact on the sensing performance of SAW sensors, we
performed strati ﬁed sampling based on temperature
intervals. Speci ﬁcally, we divided the temperature range
into 3 equal-frequency intervals, ensuring that the pro-
portion of samples in each temperature interval within the
training, validation, and test sets remains consistent. This
avoids data distribution imbalance caused by excessive
concentration of samples in speci ﬁc temperature ranges.
Finally, we ﬁxed the random seed during the splitting

process to ensure the consistency and reproducibility of
data division, eliminating result ﬂuctuations caused by
random splitting.
Results and discussion
We employed six machine learning models: RR, PR,
MLR, XGBoost, RFR, and DTR, for predicting both
temperature and strain. Following the feature importance
ranking illustrated in Fig. 8, features were sequentially
incorporated into model training in descending order of
importance. Predictive performance was evaluated using
the NRMSE, MAE and RMSE evaluation metrics con-
currently. Notably, models demonstrated generally infer-
ior performance with fewer than four input features.
Consequently, only results with four or more input fea-
tures are presented in this study.
Temperature prediction performance
Figure 8 shows the prediction performance of six
machine learning models on the temperature signals of
SAW sensors. It can be seen from the ﬁgure that there are
signiﬁcant differences in the number of features required
for various models to achieve optimal prediction results,
which intuitively re ﬂects the inherent differences in the
logic of feature information capture and processing
among different algorithms. When the number of features
is less than 4, the RR model has a high prediction error
due to insuf ﬁcient coverage of effective information; as
the number of features increases, the dimension of
effective information is supplemented, and the prediction
error decreases signi ﬁcantly. In addition, thanks to the
regularization mechanism of the RR model, which can
effectively suppress the interference of redundant fea-
tures, when the number of features further increases, the
prediction error only ﬂuctuates slightly; when the number

overﬁtting, and the model error reaches the minimum at
this time, the MAE is 56.39 °C, the RMSE is 69.21 °C, and
the NRMSE is 0.0865. The performance change trend of

ﬂuctuation range of the PR model under different num-
bers of features is relatively large. This is because the
change in the number of features directly affects the order
and quantity of polynomial terms. That is, if the non-
linear correlation between the newly added features and
the target variable is weak, the polynomial redundancy
will increase sharply, leading to an increase in error; while
a reduction in the number of features will cause a con-
traction in the polynomial dimension, resulting in
underﬁtting of the model and an increase in error as well.

Strain prediction performance
Figure 10 shows the prediction performance of 6
machine learning models on the strain signals of SAW
sensors. Since the sensitivity of the sensor to strain is
lower than that to temperature, the NRMSE of the strain
prediction results is generally higher than the deviation
level of temperature prediction. At the same time, the
performance of different models shows signi ﬁcant differ-
ences. Among them, the RR model and MLR model show
cross-task consistency in the strain prediction task, that is,
when the number of input features exceeds 4, the model
prediction error decreases rapidly, and when the number
of features increases to 14, the prediction error reaches
the global minimum. However, the prediction error of the
PR model still ﬂuctuates under the in ﬂuence of the
number of features, which is mainly because the number
of features will change the complexity of the polynomial.
The model achieves the optimal performance when the
number of features is 7, with a corresponding MAE of
33.84 µε, RMSE of 44.25 µ ε, and NRMSE of 0.0553. In
contrast, the XGBoost model still shows low dependence
on the number of features. When the number of features
is 9, its strain prediction error drops to the lowest among
the six models, with MAE of 8.42 µ ε, RMSE of 22.65 µ ε,
and NRMSE of 0.0283. In addition, the MAE of the RFR
model has dropped to 54.21 µ ε when the number of fea-
tures is 7, but as the number of features continues to
increase, redundant information causes over ﬁtting, lead-
ing to a gradual rise in the model ’s prediction error. The
DTR model maintains a high prediction error in the entire
feature range, indicating that its ﬁtting ability for such
strain signals is relatively weak.

Conclusions
In this work, we developed an LN-based single
resonator-type SAW sensor and broadened its operational
temperature to 600 °C by introducing an AlN protective
layer. Under environments with no temperature ﬂuctua-
tions or strain interference, the sensor exhibits excellent
strain/temperature sensing characteristics. To address the
cross-sensitivity issue between temperature and strain
signals in SAW sensors, we introduce machine learning
technology. By extracting the features of S11 spectra under
varying temperature and strain loading conditions,
machine learning algorithms are used to establish map-
ping relationships between input feature labels and output
target variables, thereby enabling temperature/strain
prediction. We investigate the performance of six
machine learning models in predicting temperature/strain
under different input feature conditions. Experimental
results demonstrate that the XGBoost model achieved the
best overall prediction performance. The R² values for
temperature and strain reached 99.97% and 99.23%
respectively, the NRMSEs were as low as 0.0283 and
0.0059 respectively, the RMSEs were 0.82 °C and 22.65 µ ε
respectively, and the MAEs were 0.35 °C and 8.42 µ ε
respectively. The research realizes high-precision tem-
perature-strain composite sensing under complex envir-
onmental conditions, and provides a new method for
multi-parameter information acquisition using monolithic
SAW sensors. In future work, this method can be
extended to directions such as multi-parameter decou-
pling, models adapted to lightweight edge computing,
dynamic condition robustness optimization, and spatial
multi-variable inversion based on sensor arrays, so as to
further improve the universality and applicability of the
SAW sensing system.
