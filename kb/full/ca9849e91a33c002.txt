ABSTRACT Macroinvertebrate‐based flow indices have potential application to guiding environmental flow allocations and monitoring their effectiveness, but their sensitivity to such flows has seldom been tested. We examined the response of the Australian Macroinvertebrate Flow Index (AMFI), calculated from family‐level data, to three stages of increasing environmental flow in a river whose flow regime has been heavily altered by impoundment and diversion. We hypothesised that increasing flow would cause AMFI values to rise in both riffles and pools, converging toward values in unregulated reference rivers.
