Freshwater scarcity and rising wastewater generation have intensified global reliance on desalination and reuse, with reverse osmosis (RO) providing 65–70% of installed desalination capacity and achieving energy reductions from 15 kWhm−3 in the 1970s to 1.8–2.5 kWhm−3 today. However, fouling caused by organics, inorganics, microorganisms, and colloids remains the major operational challenge, accounting for ≈25% of RO costs and over USD 15 billion annually. This review synthesises fouling mechanisms and mitigation strategies in renewable energy (RE)-driven RO wastewater-treatment systems, where intermittency exacerbates fouling through start–stop cycles and low-shear conditions.
