Access to safe water at pastoral watering points constrains livestock productivity across Kazakhstan’s extensive pasturelands, many of which remain underused because of inadequate water infrastructure. This review presents a decision framework for selecting off-grid groundwater treatment trains—membrane filtration, ultraviolet (UV) disinfection, and ozonation—according to source-water salinity, treatment capacity, operational constraints, and budget. Evidence from Kazakhstan, Central Asia, Australia, the Middle East, and sub-Saharan Africa indicates that photovoltaic (PV)-powered brackish water reverse osmosis (BWRO) combined with low-pressure UV disinfection is a strong baseline option when desalination and terminal microbial control are both required.
