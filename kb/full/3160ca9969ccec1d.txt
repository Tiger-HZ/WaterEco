Thallium (Tl) is a highly toxic metal with strong bioaccumulation potential, and industrial wastewater effluents after electrooxidation-electrocoagulation (EO-EC) treatment must consistently comply with the discharge limit of 5 μg L-1. However, direct online Tl monitoring remains costly and prone to sensor drift, limiting reliable compliance assurance. Here we develop a soft-sensing framework integrating optical surrogate parameters, data augmentation, and compliance-oriented decision rules to predict effluent Tl concentration. Ultraviolet-visible absorbance at 280, 380, 440, and 512 nm, together with electrical conductivity, pH, oxidation-reduction potential, turbidity, and current density, was used as model inputs. Interpretable spectral-derived features were further constructed to capture dissolved organic matter composition, chromophore evolution, and particle-induced optical interference. To improve robustness under extreme operating conditions, the training set was augmented using bootstrap resampling with jitter perturbation and tail-focused oversampling (reg-SMOTE tails), and a conservative safety threshold was applied to reduce false-compliance risk. Using an 80/20 random train-test split of EO-EC experimental data, the data-augmented Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) model achieved R2 = 0.963, RMSE = 0.247 μg L-1, and MAE = 0.188 μg L-1, outperforming the non-augmented model (R2 = 0.908) and a baseline model (R2 = 0.671). With a compliance threshold of 4.5 μg L-1, the model delivered zero false-compliance decisions, exceedance recall = 1, and shutdown-decision accuracy = 1. Overall, optical surrogates coupled with compliance-aware modeling provide a practical pathway for deployable Tl soft sensing.
