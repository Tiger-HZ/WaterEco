Background. Intermittent fasting (IF) has emerged as a prominent non-pharmacological strategy in lifestyle medicine and sports science, particularly in the context of cardiovascular disease (CVD) prevention and cardiometabolic optimization. Growing interest in IF reflects its potential to target cardiometabolic risk factors through time-dependent metabolic adaptations rather than continuous caloric restriction. Methods. This narrative review synthesizes current evidence derived from basic experimental research, clinical trials, and systematic reviews evaluating the cardiovascular, metabolic, and physical performance effects of intermittent fasting. The analysis focuses on three primary fasting regimens: time-restricted eating (TRE), alternate-day fasting (ADF), and modified fasting regimen
