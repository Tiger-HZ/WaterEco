Phosphorus (P) limitation constrains marine primary productivity, yet its impact can be modulated by phytoplankton physiological adaptation, notably through membrane lipid remodeling. However, the specific membrane lipid composition shifts under oceanic P limitation and their relationship to potential primary productivity loss remain poorly understood. Here, we examined phytoplankton membrane lipid compositions and their correlations with phosphate (PO 4 3− ) concentrations in the South China Sea (SCS) to evaluate the role of lipid substitution in mitigating low-P stress. Results showed that surface PO 4 3− concentrations in the SCS were near or below the P-limitation threshold (<0.1 μM). In the northern central basin, where surface PO 4 3− levels were lowest, phospholipids (phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylglycerol (PG) and phosphatidylcholine (PC)) decreased, while non-P lipids (sulfoquinovosyldiacylglycerol (SQDG) and betaine lipid (BL)) increased. Correspondingly, SQDG:PG and BL:PC ratios increased and showed significant negative correlations with PO 4 3− concentrations, indicating the occurrence of SQDG-for-PG and BL-for-PC substitution. The variation in SQDG:PG was more pronounced than that in BL:PC, likely because sulfolipid substitution is widespread across phytoplankton, including the dominant cyanobacteria in the SCS which lack BL. Notably, measured primary productivity (PP eu ) exceeded theoretical primary productivity (PP) predicted from P availability, and the (PPₑᵤ − PP)/PPₑᵤ was 2.25–79.3%. Moreover, the (PPₑᵤ − PP)/PPₑᵤ correlated positively with SQDG:PG and BL:PC ratios, suggesting that lipid substitution could mitigate part of the productivity loss under P-deficient conditions. However, its specific quantitative contribution requires further verification. These findings indicate that membrane lipid remodeling is a critical mechanism enabling phytoplankton to maintain primary production in oligotrophic oceans.
