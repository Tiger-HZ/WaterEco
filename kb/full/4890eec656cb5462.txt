In Morey et al ( 2026) the authors presented calculations of slack-
water sediment concentration inferred from mapped deposit thick-
ness and modeled settling time (Figure 8 in Morey et al., 2026).
They calculated grain settling velocity ws (Equation 2 in Morey
et al., 2026) using a constant drag coefficient CD. In fact, the drag
coefficients for grains of diameter d = 18 and d = 56 µm, the
representative sizes used in the calculations, are not constant but
vary as a function of particle Reynolds number Re because the
grains are small enough that their settling is resisted by viscous
drag caused by laminar flow around the grains (e.g., Ferguson and
Church, 2004). Within the range of particle Reynolds numbers
for these grain sizes, the drag coefficient is CD = 24/Re where
Re = wsd/𝜈 with 𝜐 = 1 × 10−6 m2/s being the kinematic viscosity
of water.
In this corrigendum the authors recalculate inferred sediment
concentrations using the correct expression for the drag coeffi-
cient ( Figure 1 ). The figure illustrates that suspended-sediment
concentrations between ca. 4-160 kg/m 3 can explain the mea-
sured thickness of event facies deposits observed in T1 terraces.
Inferred concentrations are much greater than total suspended-
solid measurements collected from the modern Siang River
(∼0.1 kg/m3; Das et al., 2014) and measurements from rivers expe-
riencing outburst floods (Marín et al., 2013; Bailey et al., 2025).
