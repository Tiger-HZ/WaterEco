Study region Songjiho lagoon, located on the east coast of Korea, is a coastal freshwater-seawater transition system in which seasonal water-level fluctuations shape hyporheic biogeochemical conditions. Study focus We investigated how seasonal changes in water level and salinity affect nitrate reduction and microbial community structure in the hyporheic zone of Songjiho Lagoon, South Korea. New hydrological insights for the region Managed sandbar breaching during the wet season maintained relatively saline conditions, whereas subsequent water-level decline increased groundwater influence and freshened the upper sediments. This hydrological shift altered porewater salinity profiles and coincided with elevated organic carbon and nitrogen concentrations, particularly at 20 cm depth, where potential denitrification was also highest. Potential denitrification rates were consistently greater under saline than freshwater conditions, reaching 85.1 ng N₂O-N g⁻¹ h⁻¹ . Putative nitrate-reducing taxa also varied with salinity, with genus Shewanella associated with saline conditions and genus Pseudomonas more abundant under fresher conditions. These results indicate that seasonal hydrological variability regulates nitrate-reduction potential by modifying salinity gradients, groundwater-surface water interactions, and associated microbial assemblages. Under drawdown-driven freshening, nitrate-reduction potential may decline, increasing the likelihood of nitrogen retention in lagoon sediments.
