Modern global climate change modeling frequently suffers from a reductionist monitoring bias, heavily prioritizing visible, point-source carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions from industrial manufacturing and transportation sectors while downplaying decentralized, non-point biological failures. This paper presents a comprehensive, multi-disciplinary framework uniting the principles of Physics, Chemistry, and Biology through the lens of Control Theory and Cybernetics. We trace the systemic collapse triggered by the mid-20th-century transition to intensive, synthetic Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium (NPK) fertilizer regimes—a core pillar of the Green Revolution. By analyzing agriculture as a closed-loop system, we map out how an open-loop chemical intervention creates a destructive, self-reinforcing positive feedback loop. We document how synthetic over-fertilization results in substantial industrial manufacturing footprints, disrupts soil cation-exchange capacities and mineral bioavailability , and decalibrates plant-microbiome carbon exchanges, prompting metabolic and cellular defense system failures . Finally, we track the hydrological and fluid transport mechanics that carry these unabsorbed nutrients into marine ecosystems, driving localized coastal ocean acidification and the expansion of oceanic "Dead Zones" (Eutrophication). The paper concludes by presenting a systems-aware path forward rooted in precision agroecology, polycultures, and regenerative biological restoration.
