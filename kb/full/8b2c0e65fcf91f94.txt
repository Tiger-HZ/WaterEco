The Sahel faces insecurity induced by diverse non-state actors. Strategies to secure the region led to the intervention of a broad range of internal and external partners. Conflict analysis shows that poverty, ineffective governance, and marginalization provided the fertile ground for recruitment of extremists by insurgent groups who exploit these grievances to garner local support. Existing ethnic and inter-communal tensions are manipulated by these extremists to divide Sahelian communities and weaken government legitimacy through violent extremism and terrorism against government institutions. The porous borders of the Sahel worsens the situation by facilitating the easy trafficking of rebels, weapons, and ideologies across the region, further spreading the insurgency in West Africa and the Sahel. This study therefore sets out to examine the nature of insurgency in the Sahel to identify the internal and external actors involved in the insurgency and the various counter insurgency operations deployed to restore peace and security to the region. Grounded on the theoretical postulations of social grievance theory, the research study is descriptive and historical in nature and relies on secondary sources of data. The data collected was analysed using content analysis a qualitative method of data analysis. The study revealed that military strategies alone are unlikely to achieve lasting peace in the Sahel. The convoluted interplay of internal and external support for insurgency and counterinsurgency in the Sahel therefore necessitates a multidimensional approach that addresses both immediate security concerns and long-term development needs, while ensuring respect for human rights and regional cooperation
