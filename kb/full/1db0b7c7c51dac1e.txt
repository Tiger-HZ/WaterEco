Abstract The availability of 20,000 line‐kilometers of airborne electromagnetic (AEM) data and the digitization of driller's logs across the Central Valley of California enables the transformation of AEM measurements of electrical resistivity into sediment type descriptions. A critical challenge is establishing the rock physics transform that relates resistivity to sediment type. Two approaches have been applied in the Central Valley; they differ in the model used to link resistivity to sediment type and in the level of confidence placed in the information in the driller's logs, with the latter determining the level of spatial heterogeneity allowed in the transform parameters.
