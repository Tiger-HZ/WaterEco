Abstract Marine phytoplankton blooms shape ecosystem health, food security, and climate by underpinning marine food webs and driving ocean carbon uptake. Yet how bloom phenology is changing across ocean biomes, what physical drivers underpin these changes, and whether Earth System models reproduce them remains poorly constrained. Using 27 years of global satellite chlorophyll observations, we identify widespread shifts in phytoplankton bloom timing, duration, magnitude, and seasonality across all major ocean biomes. Across polar regions, blooms are initiating, peaking, and terminating earlier; increasing bloom duration by 0.7-1.3 days year-1.
