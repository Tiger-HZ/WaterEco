Abstract Increase in temperature associated with climate change may alter community structure and disrupt developmental stability in ectothermic organisms. Using a natural geothermal gradient (20.6–40.3 °C) in Neotropical streams in central-western Brazil, we investigated how warming influences abundance, richness, body size, and fluctuating asymmetry (FA) in aquatic hemipterans. We sampled 18 streams and analyzed 285 individuals belonging to 12 species or morphotypes (Belostomatidae and Naucoridae). Species richness decreased significantly with increasing temperature, whereas total abundance showed no overall trend; however, species-level responses revealed marked reductions in Limnocoris burmeisteri De Carlo, 1967 in warmer streams.
