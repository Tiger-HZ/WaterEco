Comparative evaluation of soil and foliar 
micronutrient application for enhancing productivity, 
quality and nutrient use efficiency of groundnut 
(Arachis hypogaea L.) in sandy soils 
 
Wanjari Yash Shalikram , JS Bisen , Devendra Turkar and Sanjay 
Bhelawe 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/26174693.2026.v10.i9Sd.9850 
 
Abstract 
Micronutrient deficiencies , particularly zinc (Zn) , iron (Fe) , and boron (B) , are major constraints to 
groundnut ( Arachis hypogaea L.) production in sandy soils due to low nutrient retention and poor 
nutrient availability. A field experiment was conducted during the Rabi season of 2024 - 2025 to 
compare the effectiveness of soil and foliar application of micronutrients on growth , yield, quality, and 
nutrient use efficiency of groundnu t. Ten treatments comprising control , farmyard manure (FYM) , 
individual and combined soil applications of Zn , Fe, and B, and corresponding foliar applications were 
evaluated in a randomized block design with three replications. Growth , yield attributes , yield, and 
quality parameters were recorded and analyzed statistically. Application of FYM @ 10 t ha⁻¹ 
significantly improved plant growth, SPAD chlorophyll readings, nodulation, dry matter accumulation, 
and yield attributes , resulting in the highest pod yie ld (2506 kg ha⁻¹) , kernel yield (1864 kg ha⁻¹) , 
protein content (24.85%) , oil content (49.77%) , and oil yield (928.24 kg ha⁻¹). Among the inorganic 
micronutrient treatments, combined soil application of ZnSO₄, FeSO₄, and borax consistently produced 
superior growth , yield, and quality compared with their individual soil or foliar applications , while 
combined foliar application ranked next. The study demonstrated that integrated nutrient management 
involving FYM and balanced micronutrient application e ffectively enhanced productivity, quality, and 
nutrient use efficiency of groundnut in sandy soils and can be recommended for sustainable groundnut 
cultivation under micronutrient-deficient conditions. 
 
Keywords: Groundnut, zinc, iron, boron, soil application, foliar application, sandy soils, nutrient use 
efficiency, quality, yield 
 
Introduction 
Groundnut ( Arachis hypogaea L.) is one of the most important oilseed and food legume 
crops cultivated worldwide because of its high oil (45 -50%) and protein (20 -30%) content, 
making it an important source of edible oil , nutrition, and farm income. In India , groundnut 
is predominantly grown under rainfed and light -textured soils , where productivity is 
frequently constrained by low soil fertility , poor organic matter content , and widespread 
micronutrient deficiencies. Sandy soils are particularly susceptible to nutrient losses owing to 
their low cation exchange capacity and poor moisture -holding ability, resulting in reduced 
nutrient availability and lower crop productivity. Consequently , balanced nutrient 
management has become essential for sustaining groundnut production in these fragile 
agroecosystems (Vasuki & Sudhalakshmi , 2022; Abhigna et al., 2021; Rabari et al., 2024) 
[17, 1, 13]. Among the essential micronutrients, zinc (Zn) , iron (Fe) , and boron (B) play 
indispensable roles in plant growth and development. Zinc regulates enzyme activity, protein 
synthesis, chlorophyll formation , and auxin metabolism , whereas iron is essential for 
photosynthesis, respiration, and chlorophyll biosynthesis. Boron is involved in cell wall 
formation, pollen viability , fertilization, carbohydrate transport , and seed development. 
Deficiency of these micronutrients adversely affects nodulation , photosynthetic efficiency, 
reproductive development, pod formation, and kernel quality , ultimately reducing yield and 
nutrient use efficiency in groundnut (Haneena et al ., 2021; Sai Venkatesh et al ., 2024; 
Hadole et al., 2024) [6, 14, 5].

Micronutrients can be supplied through soil or foliar 
application, each having distinct advantages. Soil 
application provides a continuous nutrient supply 
throughout crop growth , whereas foliar application offers 
rapid correction of nutrient deficiencies d uring critical 
growth stages with higher nutrient use efficiency. Previous 
studies have demonstrated significant improvements in 
growth, yield, nutrient uptake, and quality following soil or 
foliar application of Zn , Fe, and B, either individually or in 
combination. However , comparative information on the 
effectiveness of these application methods under sandy soil 
conditions remains limited , particularly with respect to 
productivity, quality, and nutrient use efficiency (Dadhich & 
Yadav, 2021; Ghadiali et a l., 2023; Jagasia et al ., 2024; 
Patel et al ., 2024; Jegadeeswari et al ., 2025) [2, 3, 8, 11, 9] . 
Therefore, the present investigation was undertaken to 
compare the effectiveness of soil and foliar application of 
zinc, iron, and boron, individually and in combination, for 
improving growth , productivity, quality, and nutrient use 
efficiency of groundnut cultivated in sandy soils. 
 
Materials and Methods 
A field experiment was conducted during the Rabi season of 
2024-2025 at the Research Farm , School of Agriculture 
Science, Technology and Research, Sardar Patel University, 
Balaghat, Madhya Pradesh , India. The experimental site 
comprised sandy soil with low native fertility. The 
experiment was laid out in a Randomized Block Design 
(RBD) with ten treatments and three replications. 
Recommended agronomic practices were followed 
uniformly throughout the crop growth period. The 
treatments consisted of control (T₁) , farmyard manure 
(FYM) @ 10 t ha⁻¹ (T₂) , soil application of ZnSO₄ @ 16 kg 
ha⁻¹ (T₃), FeSO₄ @ 10 kg ha⁻¹ (T₄), borax @ 10 kg ha⁻¹ (T₅), 
combined soil application of ZnSO₄ + FeSO₄ + borax (T₆) , 
foliar application of ZnSO₄ @ 0.2% (T₇) , FeSO₄ @ 0.5% 
(T₈), borax @ 0.25% (T₉) , and combined foliar application 
of ZnSO₄ @ 0.2% + FeSO₄ @ 0.5% + borax @ 0.25% (T₁ ₀). 
Soil-applied micronutrients were applied as basal , whereas 
foliar sprays were imposed at 30 and 60 days after sowing 
(DAS). The treatment details are presented in Table 1. 
Growth observations , including plant height , number of 
branches plant⁻¹, SPAD chlorophyll reading, leaf area index, 
nodules plant⁻¹ , nodule dry weight , and dry matter 
production, were recorded at appropriate crop growth 
stages. Phenological observations comprised days to 50% 
flowering and days to maturity , while yield attributes 
included developed pods plant⁻¹ , total pods plant⁻¹ , kernels 
pod⁻¹, and 100-kernel weight. At harvest , pod yield, kernel 
yield, haulm yield , harvest index , shelling percentage , 
protein content , oil content , and oil yield were recorded 
using standard procedures. Nitrogen content was determined 
by the modified Kjeldahl method (Jackson , 1973) [7], 
phosphorus by the vanado -molybdate yellow colour method 
(Jackson, 1973) [7], potassium by flame photometry 
(Jackson, 1973) [7], zinc and iron by atomic absorption 
spectrophotometry (Lindsay & Norvell, 1978) [10], and boron 
by the curcumin method (Tandon, 2005) [16]. Nutrient uptake 
was computed by multiplying nutrient concentration with 
the corresponding dry matter or economic yield as described 
in the standard proced ures. The data were subjected to 
analysis of variance (ANOVA) appropriate for a 
Randomized Block Design as described by Gomez and 
Gomez (1984) [4]. Treatment means were compared using 
the critical difference (CD) test at the 5% probability level. 
All the analysis were performed using Rstudio (Posit team , 
2025) [12]. 
 
Results and Discussion 
Growth attributes of groundnut were significantly 
influenced by different micronutrient application methods 
(Table 2). Application of FYM @ 10 t ha⁻¹ (T₂) recorded the 
highest growth , followed by the combined soil application 
of ZnSO₄ + FeSO₄ + borax (T₆). These treatments 
significantly increased plant height , SPAD chlorophyll 
reading, nodulation, leaf area index , and dry matter 
production compared with the control , whereas individual 
foliar applications produced comparatively lower responses. 
Improved growth under FYM and combined soil 
micronutrient application could be attributed to enhanced 
nutrient availability , better root development , increased 
chlorophyll synthesis, and improved physiological activity. 
Similar improvements in vegetative growth following 
balanced micronutrient application have been reported by 
Abhigna et al. (2021) [1], Vasuki and Sudhalakshmi (2022) 
[17], Hadole et al. (2024) [5], and Rabari et al. (2024) [13]. 
Micronutrient application significantly influenced yield 
attributes of groundnut, whereas days to 50% flowering and 
days to maturity remained statistically unaffe cted (Table 3). 
Application of FYM @ 10 t ha⁻¹ (T₂) recorded the highest 
number of developed pods plant⁻¹ , total pods plant⁻¹ and 
100-kernel weight , followed by the combined soil 
application of ZnSO₄ + FeSO₄ + borax (T₆). In contrast , the 
control treatment produced the lowest values for all yield 
attributes. The improvement in pod development and kernel 
weight under integrated nutrient management indicates 
enhanced assimilate partitioning and reproductive efficiency 
resulting from balanced micronutrient nut rition. The 
superior performance of FYM and combined soil 
application may be attributed to improved nutrient 
availability, enhanced photosynthetic activity , efficient 
carbohydrate translocation and better pollen viability , which 
collectively promoted pod f ormation and seed filling. Zinc , 
iron and boron play complementary roles in enzyme 
activation, chlorophyll synthesis and reproductive 
development, thereby improving sink strength and kernel 
development. Similar enhancements in yield attributes 
following ba lanced micronutrient application have been 
reported by Vasuki and Sudhalakshmi (2022) [17], Hadole et 
al. (2024) [5], Sai Venkatesh et al. (2024) [14], and Shashank 
et al. (2024) [15]. 
Micronutrient application significantly improved pod yield , 
kernel yield, haulm yield , shelling percentage , protein 
content, oil content, and oil yield compared with the control 
(Tables 4 and 5). Among all treatments , FYM @ 10 t ha⁻¹ 
(T₂) recorded the highest pod yield (2506 kg ha⁻¹) , kernel 
yield (1864 kg ha⁻¹) , shelling percentage (74.48%), protein 
content (24.85%) , oil content (49.77%) , and oil yield 
(928.24 kg ha⁻¹), followed by the combined soil application 
of ZnSO₄ + FeSO₄ + borax (T₆). Although harvest index did 
not differ significantly among treatments , balanced 
micronutrient application markedly enhanced both yield and 
quality parameters over the control. The increased 
productivity under FYM and combined soil application can 
be attributed to improved nutrient availability , enhanced 
photosynthetic efficiency, greater assimilate translocation to 
developing pods, and better nutrient uptake throughout the 
crop growth period. Improved shelling percentage , protein

Conclusion 
The present study demonstrated that both soil and foliar 
application of micronutrients improved the growth , yield, 
and quality of groundnut cultivated in sandy soils; however , 
the magnitude of response varied with the method of 
application. Application of FYM @ 10 t ha⁻¹ (T₂) proved to 
be the most effective treatment , recording the highest 
growth, yield attributes , pod yield , kernel yield , protein 
content, oil content , and oil yield. Among the inorganic 
micronutrient treatments , the combined soil application of 
ZnSO₄ + FeSO₄ + borax (T₆) consistently outperformed 
individual soil and foliar applications , indicating the 
advantage of balanced micronutrient nutrition. Although 
combined foliar application also improved crop performance 
over the control , its response was comparatively lower than 
that of combined soil application. These findings suggest 
that integrated nutrient management through FYM or, where 
organic manure is unavailable , combined soil application of 
Zn, Fe, and B is an effective strategy for improving 
productivity and quality of groundnut under sandy soil 
conditions. The study highlights the importance of balanced 
micronutrient management for sustainable groundnut 
cultivation and provides a practical nutri ent management 
option for micronutrient-deficient sandy soils.
