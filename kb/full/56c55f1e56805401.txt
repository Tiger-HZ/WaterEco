Abstract Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are persistent contaminants of significant health concern. This study quantified the spatiotemporal occurrence of 40 PFAS in surface water and fluvial sediments across the Lavaca Watershed and Lavaca Bay, Texas, a rural Gulf Coast system with limited potential point sources. Monthly sampling was conducted at 12 sites from January to December 2025, and samples were analyzed following U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Method 1633A. Surface water exhibited low PFAS detection frequency, and detections were dominated by short-chain compounds.
