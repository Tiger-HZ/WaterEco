Abstract This study revisits Philo’s Therapeutrides, demonstrating that they belong in the genealogy of queer representation because they do not conform to culturally dominant gender expectations. Philo’s treatise describes a collective that rejected the pronatalist script of his day, queered the regular rhythms of Jewish life with their radical devotion to study and piety, and performed temporal drag in their communal gatherings. In justifying their way of life, Philo de-essentialises connections between femininity and childbearing, as well as between masculinity, philosophy, and priesthood, thereby queering the dominant gender expectations of his day and his own corpus.
