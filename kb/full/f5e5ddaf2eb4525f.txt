ABSTRACT Blue urban spaces foster mental health and well-being, yet they remain understudied compared to green spaces. Latin American cities are adopting green infrastructure, sponge cities, and nature-based solutions to improve ecological connectivity, multifunctionality, and climate adaptation. This study examines urban nature in Colombia's second-largest city, which is implementing a network of blue and green corridors. Using publicly available satellite, municipal, and Google Street View data, we assessed ecosystem services of nearly 300 streams, evidencing widespread degradation. Nearly 40% of reaches are buried; 70% of those remaining are inaccessible, 39% channelized, and many exhibit poor water quality.
