ABSTRACT Large wood (LW) affects worldwide rivers by adding complexity to their flow structure, their sediment transport dynamics and by contributing to the spatial and temporal occurrences of fluvial morphologies. As a result, LW also promotes biodiversity by increasing the variability and quantity of fluvial habitats. To fully address the role of LW on fluvial habitat and dynamics, it is a common practice to measure the LW volumes in a river corridor and to produce a LW budget at various spatial and temporal scales. The quantification of LW volume and LW budget remains, however, a difficult task for practical and theoretical reasons.
