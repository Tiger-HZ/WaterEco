Drift macroalgae are important indicators of nutrient dynamics in coastal embayments, yet long-term links between nutrient management, drift macroalgal production and benthic ecosystem structure remain poorly resolved. In Port Phillip Bay (Australia), historical nitrogen enrichment from wastewater supported extensive macroalgal production, but recent reductions in nutrient inputs have coincided with marked declines in biomass. Here, we repeated otter trawl surveys conducted in 2017–2018 to determine whether previously documented declines in drift macroalgal biomass persisted and combined these with spatially extensive remotely operated vehicle (ROV) surveys conducted between 2021 and 2023 to identify the environmental drivers of epibenthic community structure.
