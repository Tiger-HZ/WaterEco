The concentrations of Cu, Cd, Cr, Hg, and Pb in soils from automobile mechanic workshops in Onitsha, Anambra State, Nigeria, were determined to ascertain the extent of heavy metal contamination within the study area. Six soil samples were collected from six different automobile mechanic workshops. The samples were prepared, digested, and analyzed using an Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometer (AAS). The soil samples recorded mean concentrations of 2.403 ppm for Pb, 0.6385 ppm for Cd, 5.8223 ppm for Cu, and 0.000 ppm for Hg. The concentrations of Cu, Cr, Cd, and Pb exceeded the Minimum Concentration Level (MCL), indicating significant heavy metal contamination of soils within the automobile mechanic workshops. The physicochemical properties and heavy metal concentrations of borehole water located close to solid waste dumpsites in Inland Town, Onitsha, Anambra State, were also evaluated to assess the level of groundwater pollution during the rainy and dry seasons. A total of six boreholes were sampled, and seasonal variations were observed in the measured parameters. Sixteen physicochemical and heavy metal parameters were analyzed and evaluated in accordance with World Health Organization (WHO) standards for potable water. The borehole water exhibited acidic pH values ranging from 5.56 to 5.66, while elevated concentrations of lead (1.76 mg/L), cobalt (2.208 mg/L), silver (1.324 mg/L), and chromium (1.118 mg/L) exceeded permissible limits, indicating the need for treatment before the water is used for drinking or other domestic purposes. The water was also characterized by low dissolved solids throughout the study period. A positive relationship was observed between the distance from the solid waste dumpsites and the depth of the groundwater. Pollution index values greater than one indicated critical groundwater pollution and a corrosive nature of the water. Overall, the findings demonstrate significant heavy metal contamination of soils and deterioration of groundwater quality in the study area, highlighting the need for improved waste management practices, regular environmental monitoring, and appropriate groundwater treatment to safeguard public health.
