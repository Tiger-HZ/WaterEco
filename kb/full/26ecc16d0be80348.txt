Global geothermal exploitation has not yet adequately compensated for the shortage of fossil fuel resources, primarily owing to limited heat transfer efficiency and the adoption of inappropriate development strategies. This study develops a fluid–structure interaction model tailored to the geological characteristics of hot dry rock reservoirs to simulate the thermal exchange between reservoir rock and circulating heat transfer fluids. The heat-transfer mechanisms governing production-well performance are investigated from the complementary perspectives of molecular dynamics and fluid mechanics, focusing on the evolution of water properties and fluid–rock interactions during circulation.
