Background Wastewater surveillance has emerged as a cost-effective and powerful approach for monitoring infectious diseases at the community level, as demonstrated during the COVID-19 pandemic. However, translating wastewater viral signals into reliable epidemic forecasts remains challenging because measurements are noisy, environmental conditions vary over time, and disease transmission is inherently non-linear. Mechanistic models such as the Susceptible-Exposed-Infectious-Recovered-Virus (SEIR-V) framework can bridge wastewater signals with infection dynamics, but their performance depends heavily on accurate parameter estimation, and conventional least-squares optimization is prone to convergence to local optima.
