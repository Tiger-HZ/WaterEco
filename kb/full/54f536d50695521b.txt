Outdated approaches to the design and operation of wastewater treatment plants no longer meet today's stringent regulatory requirements. It requires a radical reevaluation of their role. The paper examines a fundamental paradigm shift in the water sector, which involves a transition from the traditional understanding of wastewater treatment plants as "cost-intensive pollution removal facilities" to the concept of "resource recovery and water reuse centers". Based on an analysis of professional publications from 2021-2025, indexed in scientific databases, the following key areas of sector development were analyzed and systematized: the problem of micropollutants and new generation pollutants; transition to the principles of the circular economy; achieving carbon neutrality and energy autonomy; implementation of nature-based solutions; microbiological process intensification; transformation of management based on digital twins, artificial intelligence, and the principles of the "Internet of Things". The new version of the EU Directive 2024/3019 on municipal wastewater treatment is outlined. The prospects for the development of the water sector towards the creation of fully autonomous, energy-positive wastewater treatment systems integrated into urban infrastructure are identified. The need for a systematic combination of technological innovations, economic instruments and institutional solutions at the international and national levels for the transition to closed water cycles is indicated. It is concluded that harmonizing Ukraine’s regulatory framework with European standards and the introduction of advanced technological solutions into wastewater treatment practice will contribute to the earliest possible transition of our country’s water management sector to the principles of a circular economy.
