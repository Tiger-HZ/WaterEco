Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is a cornerstone of global food security, yet its productivity is increasingly constrained by the interacting pressures of climate change, soil degradation, and imbalanced nutrient management. This review synthesizes current knowledge on how rising temperatures, erratic precipitation, and extreme weather events directly impair wheat growth and phenology while indirectly accelerating soil organic carbon depletion, structural degradation, and nutrient losses. These processes weaken soil functionality, disrupt nutrient cycling, and reduce nutrient use efficiency, ultimately limiting grain formation and yield potential.
