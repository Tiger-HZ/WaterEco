To explore how gradients in nutrient availability influence the stoichiometry of freshwater autotrophs, we sampled algal and submerged vascular plant tissues, as well as water for C : N : P ratios in 41 of north Florida's spring‐fed rivers, which are ideal natural laboratories because of unparalleled temporal stability in chemistry, temperature, and flow. Water chemistry across springs spanned a molar inorganic N : P gradient from 0.6 to 89.4, while repeated measurements within springs suggest low variation (coefficient of variation [CV] &lt; 20%) over interannual timescales. We observed significant interspecific differences in stoichiometry suggesting different nutrient requirements, but there was negligible evidence that variation in nutrient supply influenced taxa presence or absence.
