Abstract Background Urban exposure to volatile organic compounds (VOCs) is associated with several adverse health outcomes, including cancers and respiratory outcomes. We conducted a year-long VOC monitoring campaign in South Philadelphia to evaluate the impacts of traffic and redevelopment of a former oil refinery site on local air quality. Benzene was the community’s primary pollutant of concern, but we measured a broader suite of VOCs to enable source apportionment, as presented here.
