Victoria amazonica (Poepp.) Klotzsch, also known as the Amazon water lily, is a typical aquatic plant native to the tropical Amazon and Orinoco River basins in South America. The species is known to be one of the most remarkable plant adaptations in an aquatic ecosystem due to its extremely large floating leaves, which can reach up to 3 m in diameter, and its unique thermogenic flowers. It belongs to the Nymphaeaceae family and thrives in the shallow, slowly moving waters of floodplain lakes, oxbow lakes, and riverbanks. This chapter exhaustively describes V. amazonica , including its taxonomy, morphological features, ecological functions, and cultural importance, with a special focus on the structural adaptations that enable its survival in dynamic aquatic environments. Leaf morphology is central to its local ecosystem performance, as it provides hiding spots for fauna and regulates water temperature, while the evolution of its flowers favors nocturnal pollination by beetles. Although its distribution is wide, V. amazonica faces various threats such as habitat loss, water pollution, and climate change, all of which undermine its long-term survival. The chapter further discusses both the traditional and modern applications of the plant in food, medicine, and cosmetics, as well as the socioeconomic impact the plant has on rural populations in the area, highlighting the necessity of conservation efforts to help the species survive in the wild for future generations.
