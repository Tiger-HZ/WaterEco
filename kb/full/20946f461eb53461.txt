Abstract The increasing eutrophication of coastal seas is causing a shift in the most important phytoplankton groups from diatoms to dinoflagellates, but its feedback to marine carbon cycling remains unclear. Here, we investigated the potential of the key coastal phytoplankton, the diatom Skeletonema costatum , and the dinoflagellate Prorocentrum donghaiense , for refractory dissolved organic carbon (DOC) accumulation over dark degradation incubations of 70 d. Our multi‐method approach showed that dinoflagellate detritus, rather than diatom detritus, significantly contributes to refractory DOC.
