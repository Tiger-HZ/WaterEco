Abstract The worldwide shortage of freshwater has fueled interest in atmospheric water harvesting (AWH) as a promising option for decentralized water generation. In this study, a scalable method for the fabrication of superhydrophobic (SHP) metal surfaces by sandblasting and hot-water treatment (HWT) followed by fluorination is introduced to improve AWH performance. Aluminum alloy, copper, and zinc substrates were designed to have hierarchical micro-/nanostructures and then fluorinated to render them SHP. Of these, copper showed superior water collection efficiency owing to its high thermal conductivity and optimized surface texture.
