On the Rayleigh surface wave 
dynamics of porous piezo-
thermoelectric smart material 
with spatio-temporal nonlocality 
and fractional higher-order three-
phase-lag theory
Mohammed Aldandani1, Vipin Gupta2, Arun Kumar Yadav3, Abhik Sur4, Murat Yaylacı5 & 
Soumik Das6
This study examines Rayleigh-type surface wave propagation in a porous piezo-thermoelectric semi-
infinite medium incorporating spatio–temporal nonlocality together with fractional higher-order 
three-phase-lag heat conduction. The governing equations are formulated within the framework 
of porosity-dependent thermoelasticity and solved analytically using the wave-mode approach 
under traction-free boundary conditions. Numerical analysis is carried out to investigate the effects 
of nonlocal parameters, fractional order, thermal relaxation times, and porosity on phase velocity, 
attenuation, and particle motion. The results demonstrate that spatio–temporal nonlocal interactions 
and fractional thermal memory significantly influence wave dispersion and energy dissipation 
compared to classical thermoelastic models. Several existing theories are recovered as limiting cases, 
confirming the generality of the formulation. The study provides a useful framework for understanding 
wave behavior in porous piezo-thermoelectric materials relevant to sensing and surface acoustic wave 
device applications.
Keywords SDG7, Voids, Piezo-thermo-electric material, KG Nonlocal elasticity, Rayleigh type wave, 
Fractional order derivative, Three-phase lag model
Surface acoustic waves represent a fundamental mechanism for nondestructive testing, energy transport, and 
damage evaluation in both engineering and geophysical applications 1–6. Surface waves are generally classified 
into three main types: Rayleigh, Stoneley, and Love waves. Research on Rayleigh waves originates from the 
foundational study by Lord Rayleigh7, who, in 1885, identified their characteristic motion along the traction-free 
surface of an elastic medium.
Thermoelectric technologies8 have also evolved rapidly, with transient modeling of stepped-configuration 
generators, optimization of non-contact generator parameters 9–11. These efforts are complemented by recent 
demonstrations of stress-wave detection in semiconductor devices 12,13 and wearable thermoelectric devices 
inspired by natural systems for enhancing body-heat energy extraction 14. Piezo-thermo-electric materials 
integrate mechanical, electrical, and thermal field interactions, enabling efficient energy and signal transduction 
in smart sensors, actuators, surface acoustic wave devices, and MEMS 15. Recent studies have also utilized 
fractional thermoelastic formulations to investigate anomalous thermal transport and memory-dependent heat 
conduction phenomena16,17.

Nowacki18 established coupled thermal elastic diffusion equations, providing deeper insight into the 
interdependence of mechanical and thermal fields in piezoelectric materials. Cowin and Nunziato19 formulated 
linear and nonlinear theories for elastic materials containing voids, while Iovane and Nasedkin20, building upon 
the Cowin-Nunziato model, developed a linear formulation for piezoelectric media with voids, designed to 
describe the impact of porosity variation. Recent investigations have also explored advanced computational 
and thermo-mechanical frameworks involving phase-field modeling, nonlinear vibration control, solar-thermal 
energy storage systems, and flexible sensing technologies for analyzing complex multi-physics interactions in 
smart and functional materials21–24.
Edelen and Laws25 made notable contributions to the development of nonlocal interaction theory. However, 
the applicability of Eringen’s differential model of nonlocal elasticity has been critically examined by Romano 
et al.26, who presented counterexamples challenging its validity. To address such limitations, Anh and Vinh 27 
introduced a weakly nonlocal elasticity model in spatial domains and investigated surface wave propagation 
between two weakly nonlocal media. Later, Anh et al.28 extended this approach to explore Rayleigh type surface 
waves subject to impedance boundary.
In recent decades, fractional calculus29 has gained significant attention across several scientific and engineering 
disciplines, including control systems, electromagnetics, quantum mechanics, and nuclear physics. Guo and co-
workers30 presented a nonlinear electro-thermo-mechanical analysis of piezoelectric semiconductor junctions 
exposed to temperature gradients, demonstrating the coupling between thermal loading and electrical response. 
Saffari et al.31 examined the electromechanical energy conversion capability of layered piezoelectric microshell 
structures under combined moisture and thermal loading, highlighting the role of core geometry in enhancing 
harvesting efficiency. Several researchers32–36 have further explored plane wave propagation in the framework 
of distinct thermoelastic theories. Gupta et al. 37–39 analyzed energy ratios in piezo-thermo-electric interface 
models, considering phase lags and distinct fractional and memory-dependent theories.
Li and his collaborators40–43 have extensively investigated coupled thermo-electro-mechanical responses in 
piezoelectric composites, including stress-gradient thermoelasticity, size-dependent nanostructures, fractional 
heat conduction, and space–time nonlocal multi-physics modeling. Fekry44 analyzed thermo-mechanical stress 
development in fiber-reinforced porous viscoelastic materials within a nonlocal elasticity setting, emphasizing 
the interaction between porosity and time-dependent material behavior. Nandi and Pal Sarkar 45 proposed an 
advanced fractional thermoelastic framework to investigate heat transfer and stress evolution in porous continua, 
incorporating multi-phase thermal lag effects.
A comprehensive survey of the existing literature indicates that most investigations on piezo-thermo-electric 
media are primarily developed within the framework of classical or local elasticity theories, often neglecting the 
combined influence of spatio–temporal nonlocality, fractional heat conduction, and material porosity. Although 
some studies have addressed nonlocal elastic effects or fractional thermal responses individually, a coupled 
formulation incorporating these mechanisms simultaneously remains largely unexplored.
The present study addresses this gap by developing a unified model for Rayleigh-type surface wave propagation 
in a porous piezo-thermoelectric medium that integrates spatio–temporal nonlocal interactions with fractional 
higher-order three-phase-lag heat conduction. The formulation accounts for microstructural length-scale 
effects, thermal memory behavior, and porosity-dependent electromechanical coupling within a single analytical 
framework. Such interactions become particularly important in micro/nano-structured smart materials, porous 
piezoelectric composites, and thermally sensitive sensing platforms where classical thermoelastic descriptions 
are insufficient. Moreover, wave propagation analyses in porous piezo-thermoelectric materials remain relatively 
limited in the literature, especially under combined nonlocal and fractional thermoelastic environments. By 
incorporating these coupled mechanisms, the proposed framework provides an extended basis for examining 
dispersion, attenuation, and energy transport characteristics in advanced porous piezoelectric materials relevant 
to sensing technologies, surface acoustic wave devices, and nondestructive evaluation applications.

Model assumptions and modifications
The present theoretical formulation is developed under the following assumptions:
• The material medium is considered a porous piezo-thermoelectric semi-infinite half-space, bounded by a 
traction-free surface.
• The medium is assumed homogeneous in composition, while porosity effects are incorporated through void 
volume fraction parameters.
• Spatio–temporal (KG) nonlocality is incorporated to account for microstructural length-scale effects and 
time-dependent material interactions.
• The porosity behavior of the medium is modeled within the Cowin–Nunziato theoretical framework, ac -
counting for void distribution and equilibrated stress fields.
• Heat conduction is governed by a fractional higher-order three-phase-lag model to represent thermal relaxa-
tion and memory-dependent heat transport.
• Surface wave propagation is analyzed under traction-free mechanical boundary conditions, along with elec-
trically open and thermally specified surface constraints.
• Body forces, internal heat sources, and external electric excitations are neglected to focus on intrinsic wave 
characteristics.
• All field variables are assumed to vary harmonically along the surface direction and decay exponentially with 
depth into the medium.
Formulation of the problem
We consider the Rayleigh-type surface waves in a semi-infinite solid described by a KG nonlocal piezo-
thermo-electric half-space with voids. The material response is governed by a higher-order fractional TPL 
heat conduction law together with a Klein–Gordon type nonlocal elasticity kernel, which extends beyond the 
classical based formulations. The geometry of the model is formulated in a Cartesian coordinate framework. 
The boundary of the half-space corresponds to z =0 , while the x-axis is aligned with the intended path of 
wave motion. The positive z-direction extends vertically into the medium. Figure  1 presents a schematic view 
of the problem domain. For surface-wave motion restricted to the xz-plane, the relevant field quantities consist 
of the displacement vector ⃗u=( u (x, z, t) , 0,w (x, z, t)) , the electric potential φ = φ (x, z, t) , the void 
volume fraction ψ = ψ (x, z, t) , and the temperature field θ = θ (x, z, t) . These fields evolve according to the 
governing equations derived under the adopted nonlocal fractional piezo-thermo-poroelastic theory.

Solution algorithm
The earlier derivations describe the coupled influence of KG nonlocality, thermoelastic parameters, void, and 
piezoelectric coupling, forming the analytical basis for Rayleigh type wave analysis. The developed algorithm 
provides a systematic procedure to determine the phase velocity, attenuation coefficient, and surface particle 
trajectories in the considered medium.
• Beginning with the specified horizontal slowness q, the analysis involves solving a tenth-degree Eq. (39). The 
Durand–Kerner iterative technique is then used to compute all ten complex roots of s. From this set, five roots 
corresponding to the decaying phase ℑ(s) > 0, are selected for further analysis.
• To determine the amplitude ratios Pm, Qm, Rm, and Sm the equations presented in (41) solved for slowness 
vectors (q, s m).
• Assume a Rayleigh type surface wave propagating at an angle ϕ with the boundary of considered material. 
Define q = cos ϕ/ c, where c represent the undetermined wave velocity.
• A determinantal equation in c is obtained by employing Pm, Qm, Rm, Sm together with the slowness param-
eter q = cos φ/c in the boundary condition equations.
• Using Pm, Qm, Rm, Sm together with the slowness parameter q = cos ϕ/ c in the Eqs. (43) to (46) yields a 
determinantal equation in c.
• Subsequently, the Newton–Raphson iterative method is applied to compute the wave velocity c. The corre -
sponding attenuation and phase velocity of the Rayleigh-type surface wave are then evaluated using Eqs. (48) 
and (49).
• Based on Eq. (51), MATLAB is applied to visualize the temporal trajectories of surface particles correspond-
ing to displacement components. The motion is depicted through 2D and 3D graphical representations for 
enhanced clarity.
Numerical investigation and discussion
The computational scheme outlined earlier serves as a systematic tool for analyzing the characteristics of 
Rayleigh-type surface waves in the considered medium. Although the derived mathematical expressions are 
highly coupled due to the simultaneous incorporation of porosity, piezoelectricity, spatio-temporal nonlocality, 
and fractional higher-order thermal effects, the primary objective of the numerical study is to provide a clear 
qualitative interpretation of the underlying wave propagation behavior. In the numerical implementation, 
Cadmium Selenide is adopted as the representative material medium, and the corresponding material constants 
are taken from previously published studies49,58 and listed in Table 1.
The governing equations employed in the present work are formulated in dimensional form. The spatial 
nonlocal parameter ξ represents an effective characteristic interaction length associated with the porous 
microstructure and internal nonlocal interactions of the medium, whereas the temporal nonlocal parameter τ 
and the thermal phase-lag quantities characterize the corresponding thermal memory time scales. The adopted 
numerical values are selected primarily to examine the qualitative influence of spatio-temporal nonlocality, 
higher-order thermal memory, and fractional effects on the propagation characteristics of Rayleigh-type surface 
waves. Therefore, the selected parameter combinations should be interpreted as generalized phenomenological 
parameters commonly used in nonlocal Sthermoelastic wave propagation analyses rather than as a direct 
representation of a single experimental configuration.

Effect of spatial nonlocality on phase velocity and attenuation coefficient
In the following subsection, the influence of spatial nonlocality on the phase velocity and attenuation coefficient 
is examined under the influence of higher order TPL model. To present the results effectively, graphical 
illustrations are provided in Fig. 2a and b, which depict the characteristic variation of Rayleigh type wave under 
different values of the spatial nonlocality parameter ( ξ =0 , 0.001, 0.002, 0.003) in a piezo-thermo-electric 
medium containing voids. It is important to note that the case ξ =0 corresponds to the absence of spatial 
nonlocal interactions, whereas the fully local limiting formulation is recovered when both the spatial and 
temporal nonlocal parameters satisfy ξ =0 and τ =0 .
Figure 2a depict the profile of phase velocity of Rayleigh waves as a function of angular frequency  ω  for 
distinct values of the spatial nonlocal parameter ξ. For all parameter sets considered, the phase velocity shows 
a nearly linear dependence on the frequency, but the rate of increase becomes more pronounced as  ξ  rises. 
This indicates that nonlocal interactions, as characterized by the Klein-Gordon model, promote higher wave 
speeds for a given frequency. The nonlocal parameter  ξ captures the influence of microstructural interactions 
and spatial memory in the medium, enabling energy to be transmitted across larger distances and thereby 
accelerating wave propagation. When  ξ =0 , the medium behaves in a spatial local manner and the phase 
velocity remains lowest, showing only modest growth with frequency. As ξ  is increased to 0.001, 0.002, and 
then 0.003, each corresponding curve shifts upward, demonstrating that stronger nonlocal effects lead to a 
pronounced enhancement in Rayleigh wave velocity for the fractional-order three-phase lag model.
Figure 2b displays the corresponding attenuation profiles as frequency increases for the same values of  ξ. 
Attenuation remains close to zero at low frequencies, especially for the ξ =0 . However, as ξ grows, attenuation 
increases rapidly at higher frequencies, with the steepest rise seen for the largest  ξ. This behavior reflects the 
growing energy dissipation due to nonlocality and spatial memory effects at elevated frequencies. A higher 
nonlocal parameter means the material accommodates broader-range stress and heat interactions, leading to 
more complex internal damping mechanisms. Consequently, the wave loses energy more quickly as it travels, 
which becomes most evident at higher frequencies. T ogether, these results demonstrate that while enhanced 
nonlocality boosts phase velocity, it also intensifies energy loss through attenuation, highlighting the fundamental 
effect of the nonlocal parameter.

Validation and comparison with previously published results
To establish the reliability and physical consistency of the present formulation, a comparative validation has 
been carried out with previously published results available in the literature. The comparison is performed 
at two levels, namely dispersion characteristics and particle motion behavior associated with Rayleigh wave 
propagation.
Figure 8a and b presents the validation comparison of normalized phase velocity and normalized attenuation 
with angular frequency. The normalization has been performed by scaling the computed magnitudes with 
respect to their corresponding maximum values so that all variations remain confined within the 0–1 range. This 
approach enables a direct comparison of dispersion trends independent of absolute magnitude differences. It is 
observed from Fig.  8 that the dispersion trends of the two formulations do not coincide exactly. The variation 
patterns differ in slope, curvature, and attenuation evolution across the frequency spectrum. This deviation is 
physically justified due to fundamental differences in the constitutive modeling and material coupling. In the 
previously published work62, the medium consisted of a piezoelectric material coupled with a semiconductor 
phase, where thermoelastic interactions were governed through the Moore–Gibson–Thompson model and 
Eringen’s spatial nonlocal elasticity63.
In contrast, the present formulation considers a piezoelectric medium coupled with void inclusions instead 
of semiconductors, while retaining thermoelastic effects. The thermal behavior is described using a fractional 
three-phase-lag model incorporating higher-order thermal memory. Additionally, the nonlocal response follows 
the KG-type framework, which introduces both spatial and temporal nonlocality. Owing to these differences in 
thermal lagging, coupling mechanisms, and nonlocal interactions, quantitative deviations in phase velocity and 
attenuation are expected. Therefore, the non-coincident dispersion trends observed in Fig.  8 remain physically 
consistent and theoretically justified.
A more fundamental validation is obtained through the comparison of particle motion trajectories, illustrated 
in Fig. 9a and b. Rayleigh surface waves are well known to propagate with elliptic particle motion in the sagittal

www.nature.com/scientificreports
plane57,64, where horizontal (U) and vertical (W) displacement components remain phase shifted, producing 
closed elliptical paths. The particle trajectories plotted for both formulations exhibit clear elliptic motion. Despite 
differences in coupling phase (semiconductor versus void), thermal modeling, and nonlocal frameworks, the 
displacement paths align in their fundamental kinematic nature. The trajectories remain confined to the sagittal 
plane and follow the classical elliptic motion condition of Rayleigh waves without deviation toward circular or

Conclusions
This study presents an in-depth analysis of Rayleigh type wave propagation in a generalized thermal piezoelectric 
semi-infinite medium incorporating voids. The investigation employs a fractional-order Klein–Gordon-type 
nonlocal TPL framework, wherein the governing field equations are rigorously formulated and solved using 
an eigenmode analytical approach. The primary objective is to elucidate the coupled influences of spatial and 
temporal nonlocality, fractional-order effects, heat transfer models, and depth variations on surface wave. A 
detailed examination of the computational results, as illustrated in Figs. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9, leads to several 
significant findings and physical interpretations summarized below.
• The phase velocity of Rayleigh waves increases with the spatial nonlocal parameter, indicating that stronger 
microstructural coupling under the Klein–Gordon-type nonlocality enhances wave propagation.
• Increasing the spatial nonlocal parameter enhances the overall attenuation at higher frequencies, indicating 
that spatial nonlocality strengthens internal damping through extended stress–heat coupling and microstruc-
tural interactions within the medium.

• The temporal nonlocal parameter τ promotes higher phase velocities and sustained wave propagation, high-
lighting the role of temporal memory in energy transmission.
• With larger τ , attenuation first dips (transient reinforcement) and then rises sharply at high frequencies, re-
flecting the dual effects of energy storage and dissipation due to temporal memory.
• Higher fractional heat conduction orders ( n, p, l ) enhance wave dispersion and energy transport, with the 
model n =4 , p =3 , l =3 yielding the maximum phase velocity.
• At high frequencies, the higher-order model exhibits greater attenuation, indicating dominant thermal mem-
ory and damping effects.
• The fractional-order parameter α critically governs wave propagation. Lower values of α slightly reduce phase 
velocity and modify the attenuation behavior across frequencies, while the integer-order case α =1 ensures 
the fastest wave propagation.
• Among distinct heat conduction models, the DPL model provides the highest phase velocity, followed by TPL 
and LS; at high frequencies, the LS model exhibits a sharp attenuation rise, reflecting limited multi-relaxation 
capability.
• Rayleigh wave particle motion shows clear depth dependence, with larger horizontal and vertical displace -
ments at shallower depths. This near-surface amplification reflects localized energy concentration relevant to 
seismic and geotechnical applications.
The present findings provide useful insights for optimizing surface acoustic wave sensors, seismic characterization 
systems, and energy-harvesting devices. The model’ s ability to capture nonlocal and fractional thermal effects 
strengthens predictions of wave behavior in advanced piezoelectric and porous materials. These results also 
open up several future research opportunities, particularly in experimental validation, high-fidelity numerical 
simulations, and broader extensions of the theoretical framework.
