Understanding how species use habitats across environmental gradients is central to guiding fisheries management and habitat restoration. In coastal ecosystems, relationships between mobile fishes and crustaceans and seagrass habitats often vary because habitat structure, environmental context, and sampling methods are rarely considered together in predictive models. Here, we combine multi-gear field observations collected across the Gulf of America with a transfer-learning framework that first learns general habitat relationships from well-sampled functional groups and then transfers this ecological knowledge to data-limited, fishery-important species. We show that apparent inconsistencies in seagrass–nekton relationships were largely attributable to environmental context and differences in observation protocols. Integrating observations from different sampling gears improved overall predictive performance and enabled more consistent cross-site inference, while functional group pretraining strengthened prediction for sparsely observed species. Brown and pink shrimp exhibited contrasting responses to salinity, water clarity, and seagrass structure, whereas blue crab and spotted seatrout showed more consistent habitat associations across regions. These findings demonstrate that deep transfer learning can integrate heterogeneous monitoring data and support scalable, data-driven conservation and restoration across changing coastal ecosystems
