Monitoring environmental quality and landscape characteristics has taken on a central role in land-use planning, natural resources, protected areas, and habitat conservation in general. Interest in these topics is shared by several disciplines, such as ecology, nature conservation, environmental sciences, forestry, economics, spatial planning, landscape architecture, and sociology, among others. For this reason, since the 1970s, many authors have developed methodologies to estimate the quality and naturalness of places, districts, and 16 regions based on land-use and soil consumption data. Two main approaches estimating environmental quality can be envisaged, namely based on ecosystem functions for human society (i.e. environmental value) or on the intrinsic value of ecosystems (i.e. naturalness). Use of these dimensions as proxies for environmental quality (both in the field of nature conservation and land-use planning) is a widely accepted concept.
