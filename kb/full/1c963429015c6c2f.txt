Effect of integrated nutrient management on growth, 
yield attributes and yield of broccoli (Brassica oleracea 
L. var. italica) under bundelkhand conditions 
 
Shyam Kirar , Awanish Kumar , Satyavir Singh , Chetan Anand Dubey , 
Ashish Kumar Tiwari , Surya Pratap Patel , Sankalp Singh and Bikram 
Pal 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/26174693.2026.v10.i9Sn.9973 
 
Abstract 
A field experiment was conducted during [2024 -25] at the Chemical Research Farm, Department of 
Soil Science and Agricultural Chemistry, Institute of Agricultural Sciences, Bundelkhand University, 
Jhansi, to study the effect of integrated nutrient management (INM) on growth, yield attributes, and 
yield of broccoli (Brassica oleracea L. var. italica). Eight treatments were tested: T0 (Control/RDF), T1 
(RDF + PSB), T2 (10 t FYM + 100% RDF), T3 (10 t FYM + RDF + PSB), T4 (10 t VC + RDF), T5 (10 t 
VC + RDF + PSB), T 6 (15 t FYM + RDF), and T 7 (15 t VC + RDF). Results revealed significant 
differences among treatments. Maximum plant height (38.02 cm at 45 DAT and 51.9 cm at harvest), 
plant diameter (1.89 cm at 45 DAT and 3.98 cm at harvest), plant spread (56.87 cm N -S, 53.15 cm E -
W), and number of leaves (17.48) were recorded in T 7. Similarly, leaf length (47.02 cm), leaf width 
(17.90 cm), head length (18.69 cm), head diameter (16.08 cm), head weight (321.04 g), and y ield 
(154.75 q/ha) were also highest under T 7, followed by T 6. Phenological observations indicated early 
head initiation (49.02 days) and maturity (72.61 days) in T 7, whereas delayed maturity was observed in 
control. Vermicompost -based INM treatments outpe rformed FYM -based treatments. The study 
concludes that the integration of Vermicompost with RDF enhances growth, accelerates phenology, and 
maximizes yield, making it a sustainable approach for broccoli cultivation under Bundelkhand 
conditions. 
 
Keywords: Broccoli, integrated nutrient management, vermicompost, farmyard manure, growth 
parameters, and yield attributes 
 
1. Introduction 
Broccoli ( Brassica oleracea L. var. italica) is a highly nutritious Cole crop valued for its 
antioxidant compounds, vitamins, minerals, and anticarcinogenic properties. Its cultivation is 
expanding rapidly in India owing to increasing consumer demand and health awareness. 
However, achieving higher productivity in broccoli requires judicious nutrient management. 
Sole dependence on chemical fertilizers often leads to nutrient imbalance, declining soil 
health, and reduced sustainability (Singh et al., 2020) [16]. Integrated Nutrient Management 
(INM), which combines inorganic fertilizers with organic manures and bio fertilizers, is 
recognized as an eco -friendly approach to maintain soil fertility and enhance crop 
productivity (Meena et al. , 2021; Kumar et al. , 2022) [8, 10]. Vermicompost (VC) and 
Farmyard Manure (FYM) improve soil structure, microbial activity, and nutrient -use 
efficiency, while phosphate-solubilizing bacteria (PSB) mobilize phosphorus, contributing to 
sustainable crop growth and yield. 
Broccoli ( Brassica oleracea var. italica) is a nutrient -dense cool -season vegetable, highly 
valued for its abundant vitamins, minerals, and antioxida nt compounds making it 
increasingly popular among health-conscious consumers in India. However, as an exhaustive 
Cole crop, broccoli demands balanced nutrition. Over -reliance on chemical fertilizers often 
leads to declining soil health and unsustainable pr oduction systems, underscoring the need 
for more sustainable nutrient management approaches. Broccoli ( Brassica oleracea var. 
italica) is an important member of the Cole crop family, grown worldwide for its edible 
florets that are rich in vitamins, minerals, and bioactive compounds. It is considered a

“functional food” due to the presence of glucosinolates, 
flavonoids, carotenoids, and other phytochemicals that 
impart antioxidant, anticancer, and cardio -protective 
properties. In recent years, broccoli has gained popularity in 
India owing to the rising awareness of healthy diets, 
urbanization, and diversification of vegetable consumption 
patterns. Despite its nutritional and economic importance, 
broccoli production in India remains limited compared to 
other Cole crops like cabbage and cauliflower. One of the 
primary constraints is its high nutrient requirement. Broccoli 
is a heavy feeder that extracts large quantities of nitrogen, 
phosphorus, and potassium from the soil. Continuous 
reliance on chemical fertil izers, though beneficial in 
achieving higher yields, often leads to soil degradation, 
nutrient imbalance, and reduced fertilizer -use efficiency. 
This calls for sustainable nutrient management practices to 
maintain soil fertility while ensuring optimum crop 
productivity. 
Integrated Nutrient Management (INM), which combines 
organic manures, bio fertilizers, and inorganic fertilizers in a 
balanced manner, has emerged as a viable approach to 
enhance productivity and sustainability. Organic sources 
such as Farmyard Manure (FYM) and Vermicompost 
improve soil organic matter, microbial activity, and nutrient 
availability. Bio fertilizers like Phosphate-Solubilizing 
Bacteria (PSB) enhance phosphorus uptake, while judicious 
use of chemical fertilizers supplies readily available 
nutrients during peak crop demand. Together, these 
components synergistically improve growth, yield, and 
quality of vegetables including broccoli. 
Several studies have highlighted the effectiveness of INM in 
broccoli cultivation. Application of Vermicompost along 
with reduced doses of NPK has been reported to increase 
head size, weight, and yield compared to sole application of 
chemical fertilizers. Similarly, integrating FYM or compost 
with bio fertilizers improves plant growth, enhances soil 
health, and supports long -term sustainability. However, the 
performance of these nutrient combinations varies with 
agro-climatic conditions, soil fertility status, and 
management practices. 
In the Bundelkhand region of Central India, soils are often 
low in o rganic matter, deficient in available nitrogen and 
phosphorus, and subjected to moisture stress. These 
conditions pose challenges for successful cultivation of 
high-value crops like broccoli. Research evidence on the 
role of INM practices, especially combi nations of FYM, 
Vermicompost, and PSB in enhancing broccoli growth and 
yield under Bundelkhand conditions, is scarce. Generating 
such information is crucial for developing region -specific 
nutrient management strategies. 
2. Materials and Methods 
The experi ment was carried out during [2024 -25] at the 
Chemical Research Farm, Department of Soil Science and 
Agricultural Chemistry, Institute of Agricultural Sciences, 
Bundelkhand University, Jhansi (U.P.). 
The field experiment was conducted in a Randomized Block 
Design (RBD) with three replications to minimize 
experimental error and ensure reliability of results and there 
were eight treatments: T0: Control (100% RDF), T 1: RDF + 
PSB, T 2: 10 t/ha FYM + 100% RDF, T 3: 10 t/ha FYM + 
100% RDF + PSB, T 4: 10 t/ha VC + 100% RDF, T5: 10 t/ha 
VC + 100% RDF + PSB, T6: 15 t/ha FYM + 100% RDF, T7: 
15 t/ha VC + 100% RDF. The treatments were tes ted at 5% 
level of significance Different nutrient management 
treatments were assigned randomly within each block. 
Observations were reco rded on a set of growth and yield 
parameters including plant height, plant diameter, plant 
spread, number of leaves, leaf lengthand leaf width, days to 
head initiation, days to maturity, head length, head diameter, 
head weight, and yield. Data obtained fro m the experiment 
were subjected to statistical Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) 
as per the standard procedure for RBD, and treatment means 
were compared at a 5% level of significance (p ≤ 0.05) to 
determine the significance of differences among treatments. 
 
3. Results and Discussion 
3.1 Growth Parameters 
The results of growth parameter of broccoli presented below 
on table 3.1. The present investigation revealed that 
Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) exerted a significant 
influence on the vegetative growt h of broccoli. Among the 
different treatments, T 7 (15 t/ha Vermicompost + 100% 
RDF) recorded the maximum values for all the growth 
attributes. The highest plant height (38.02 cm at 45 DAT 
and 54.10 cm at harvest), plant diameter (1.89 cm at 45 
DAT and 3.98 cm at harvest) and plant spread (56.87 and 
53.15 cm), were observed in T 7, whereas the control (T 0) 
consistently exhibited the lowest values. The improvement 
in vegetative growth under T 7 may be attributed to the 
synergistic effect of Vermicompost and inorganic fertilizers, 
which ensured a steady supply of macro and micro-nutrients 
throughout the crop growth period. Vermicompost also 
enhances soil microbial activity, improves soil physical 
structure, and facilitates efficient nutrient uptake, thereby 
leading to robust plant growth and development (Kumar et 
al., 2019; Meena et al., 2021) [1, 9, 10]. These findings are in 
close agreement with the results reported by Sharma et al. 
(2020) [13], who also observed increased vegetative vigor of 
broccoli under combined organic and inorganic nutrient 
management practices.

3.2 Leaf Characteristics 
Leaf morphological traits such as leaf length and leaf width 
were also significantly influenced by INM trea tments and 
presented below in table 3.2. T 7 recorded the maximum leaf 
length (43.25 cm) and leaf width (22.63 cm), closely 
followed by T 6 (15 t/ha FYM + 100% RDF). The broader 
and longer leaves under these treatments resulted in a larger 
photosynthetic sur face area, which ultimately enhanced 
assimilate production and translocation towards developing 
heads. In contrast, control plots produced shorter and 
narrower leaves, limiting the photosynthetic efficiency of 
plants. Similar enhancement in leaf morphologi cal traits 
under integrated nutrient application has also been reported 
in cauliflower and cabbage by Singh et al. (2022) [15, 16] and 
Yadav et al. (2023) [18]. 
 
3.3 Phonological Development 
The influence of INM on the phonological parameters of 
broccoli was also pro nounced and presented below in table 
3.2. Plants receiving integrated nutrient treatments attained 
earlier head initiation and maturity compared to those under 
control. The minimum number of days required for head 
initiation (49.02 days) and physiological maturity (72.61 
days) were observed in T7, while the control plants exhibited 
delayed phonological development, with head initiation and 
maturity recorded at 65.23 and 83.11 days, respectively. 
This advancement in phenology under integrated nutrient 
supply may be attributed to the continuous and balanced 
availability of nutrients, which enhanced metabolic 
activities and improved physiological efficiency of the crop. 
Similar trends were reported by Choudharyet al. (2020) [7] in 
broccoli and by Pandey et al. (2021) [11] in cabbage.

3.4 Head Traits 
The improvement in vegetative and p hysiological traits 
under INM was presented below in table 3.3 which were 
positively reflected in head growth and development. T 7 
recorded the maximum values for head length (18.69 cm), 
head diameter (16.08 cm), and head weight (321.04 g), 
which were signi ficantly higher than those recorded in the 
control plots. This remarkable improvement in head traits 
under T 7 could be explained by the enhanced supply of 
photosynthetic to the sink organs, which promoted efficient 
head development and accumulation of dry matter. The 
balanced nutrient environment created by the combined 
application of Vermicompost and RDF also improved soil 
health and nutrient mineralization, thereby ensuring better 
head formation. Comparable findings have been reported by 
Meena et al. (2021) [10] in broccoli and Singh et al. (2022) [15, 
16] in cauliflower, wherein INM treatments improved head 
compactness and marketable yield attributes. 
 
3.5 Yield 
The positive impact of INM on growth, leaf traits, and head 
development ultimately translated into higher yields which 
was shown below in table 3.3. The maximum yield (154.75 
q/ha) was obtained in T 7, followed by T 6 (153.42 q/ha), 
while the control recorded the lowest yield (91.35 q/ha). The 
enhanced yield under vermicompost -based INM is a result 
of synergistic effects between organic and inorganic nutrient 
sources, which improve nutrient -use efficiency, plant 
vigour, and photosynthetic efficiency (Singh et al., 2021; 
AbouelMagd et al., 2019) [1, 21]. Biofertilizers such as PSB 
enhance phosphorus availability, supportin g curd formation 
and metabolic processes essential for high yield (Bhat et al., 
2022) [6]. Moreover, the combined application of organic and 
inorganic nutrients maintains a favourable microclimate in 
the root zone, stimulating microbial activity and nutrient 
cycling, which ultimately supports higher head weight and 
total yield (Adeleye et al., 2010; Asghar et al., 2016) [2, 3]. 
Overall, the results demonstrate that the integration of 
vermicompost, FYM, PSB, and RDF not only improves 
vegetative growth and leaf developme nt but also enhances 
head characteristics and productivity of broccoli. Among all 
treatments, T₇ (15 t ha⁻¹ vermicompost + 100% RDF) 
consistently outperformed other treatments, highlighting the 
importance of INM as a sustainable and effective strategy 
for achieving higher yield and superior quality in broccoli 
under semi -arid Indian conditions. These findings are in 
agreement with previous studies emphasizing the role of 
organic amendments, biofertilizers, and balanced chemical 
fertilization in improving bo th yield and nutrient -use 
efficiency in cole crops (Anonymous, 2018; Bhardwaj et al., 
2018; Bahadur et al., 2017) [4, 5].

Conclusion 
The study clearly demonstrated that integrated nutrient 
management significantly improved the growth, 
phonological stages, yield attributes, and yield of broccoli. 
The treatment 15 t/ha Vermicompost + 100% RDF (T 7) 
proved most effective, followed by 15 t/ha FYM + RDF 
(T6). Vermicompost -based INM not only enhanced 
productivity but also reduced crop duration, indicating its 
suitability for sustainable broccoli production under 
Bundelkhand agro-climatic conditions.
