Abstract Tidal Freshwater Zones (TFZs), typically situated in the upper reaches of estuaries, are highly dynamic ecosystems where hydrodynamic, biogeochemical, and anthropogenic processes strongly interact. This high complexity, together with increasing climate extremes, poses substantial challenges for diagnosing algal bloom dynamics in these regions. Using the TFZs of the Patuxent River (USA) as a case study, we examined a series of rare and intense algal blooms triggered by a severe drought in summer 2007, utilizing extensive in situ observations and an interpretable bloom risk index.
