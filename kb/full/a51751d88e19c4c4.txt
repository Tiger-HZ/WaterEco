Abstract Food, water, and energy security are conventionally governed as three separate problems. They are, in fact, a single tightly coupled system — the food–energy–water nexus — in which each resource is an indispensable input to the others: water is needed to produce energy and grow food, energy is needed to move and manufacture water and to run modern agriculture, and both underpin the food supply. This report argues that the nexus is now under a compound stress that siloed policy is structurally unable to meet, and that the strategic task of the century to 2050 is to build food, water, and energy systems that are integrated by design and resilient to shocks that arrive together.
