Surface water quality is a key determinant of human and ecosystem health, particularly in urban areas 
of sub-Saharan Africa, where wastewater discharges and agricultural runoff are often poorly regulated. 
This study aimed to assess the impact of human activities on the water quality of the Tighèn River in 
the urban municipality of Lola, Guinea. Fifteen water samples were collected during each season, 
yielding a total of 30 samples. In situ measurements of temperature, pH, electrical conductivity, 
dissolved oxygen, and turbidity were carried out using a multiparameter probe. Laboratory analyses of 
nitrate, nitrite, phosphate, and suspended solids were performed using a DR 890 spectrophotometer. 
The results revealed significant spatial and temporal variations in water quality. During the dry season , 
the river water was acidic, with pH values below 6.5, whereas turbidity and suspended solids reached 
their highest levels during the rainy season, with suspended solids increasing to 741 mg L ⁻¹. The mean 
concentrations of nitrate and phosphate were 126.67 mg L ⁻¹ and 1.43 mg L ⁻¹, respectively, exceeding 
the REEE-ESC Guide recommendations for surface water. Application of the Water Quality Index (WQI) 
classified the river water as being of poor quality. The elevated concentrations of nutrients and 
suspended solids were primarily attributed to agricultural activities and pig farming along the 
riverbanks. 
 
Key words: Water quality, Tighèn River, human activity, Guinea. 
 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
Surface waters play a fundamental role in sustaining life, 
supporting ecosystems, and promoting the development 
of human societies (Naylor et al., 2021). Despite their 
importance, these water resources are highly vulnerable 
to chemical pollution, particularly in urban areas. Human 
activities have significantly disrupted aquatic ecosystems 
by affecting both the quantity and quality of water 
resources. Water quantity is reduced through intensive 
exploitation, while water quality is degraded by changes 
in physicochemical and biological characteristics resulting

from the excessive input of nutrients and potentially toxic 
chemical substances ( Hayzoun, 2014; Ado et al., 2000). 
In many West African cities, rivers are commonly used as 
dumping sites for solid waste and untreated domestic 
effluents, accelerating the degradation of water quality. 
Similar observations have been reported in the Sebou 
watershed in Morocco (Hayzoun, 201 4), the Alibori River 
in Benin ( Chitou et al., 2019 ), and the Tilé watershed in 
the urban municipality of N'Zérékoré, Guinea ( Lamah et 
al., 2022). This problem is not limited to Africa but is also 
evident in several Asian countries. In South Korea, the 
water quality of the Han River has deteriorated 
significantly in sections receiving discharges from 
polluted tributaries (Chang, 2005). Likewise, in China, the 
major rivers receiving wastewater from the city of Xi'an 
are experiencing similar levels of pollution ( He et al., 
2008). Guinea, often referred to as the "Water Tower of 
West Africa ," possesses abundant surface water 
resources. Nevertheless, despite the existence of water 
resource management policies, many of its rivers are 
subjected to increasing anthropogenic pressures. These 
pressures have contributed to the progressive 
degradation of water quality and, in some cases, the 
disappearance of smaller watercourses. The town of 
Lola, one of the largest urban centers in Forested 
Guinea, attracts a growing population from surrounding 
areas. However, the Tighèn River and its tributaries 
constitute the town's primary surface water resources. 
Local communities depend on the river for a wide range 
of domestic and economic activities, including bathing, 
laundry, irrigation, and livestock watering. At the same 
time, the river serves as the main recipient of domestic 
waste, agricultural runoff, and other effluents generated 
by urban activities. Although several studies have 
evaluated river water quality in Guinea and neighboring 
West African countries, no comprehensive assessment 
has been conducted on the Tighèn River despite 
increasing urbanization and the local population's heavy 
reliance on this river for domestic and agricultural 
purposes. Therefore, this study aims to assess the 
seasonal variation in the physicochemical quality of the 
Tighèn River and to identify the main human activities 
contributing to its pollution. 
 
 
Study area 
 
The urban commune of Lola located between 07°42’ and 
07°47’ north latitude, -008°46’ and -008 °51’ west 
longitude. The population is estimated at 50,049 
inhabitants including 25,264 women primarily 
 
 
 
 
agropastoral according to the latest general census of 
population and housing (Regional General Public 
Hospital [RGPH], 2014; Food and Agriculture 
Organisation [FAO], 2007). This region was known for its 
humid climate and abundant rainfall almost all year round 
(8 months out of 12 ). According to the weather, the 
rainfall is about 3046 mm over the year, with a minimum 
of 16 mm in January and a maximum of 591 mm in july. 
The river Tighèn has its source at the east of the urban 
commune of Lola in the rural commune of Gbèkè. This 
river crosses the urban commune of Lola before flowing 
into the Mano River with several tributaries: Yokon, 
Wèya, and Coaly (Figure 1). 
 
 
Equipment and method 
 
This descriptive study was based on a mixed approach (qualitative 
and quantitative) because there is a need to link the environment of 
the river to its mode of exploitation by residents and other local 
actors, and to assess the state of pollution of the river. 
 
 
Investigation 
 
A preliminary field survey was conducted to identify potential 
locations for collecting water samples from the Tighèn River. The 
surveys also allowed us to identify the various activities taking place 
along the river. 
 
 
Physico-chemical study of river water 
 
Choice of sampling sites 
 
The sampling sites were selected based on human activities taking 
place around and along the river, as well as on water accessibility 
throughout the year and water depth. Five sites (Homeakoly 1 
upstream, Gotèkoly, Tighèn -mou 1, and Thièta in the city, and 
Foulayapo downstream) distributed along the Tighèn River were 
selected. 
 
 
Sampling and analysis methodology 
 
Sampling was conducted in November 2024, during the rainy 
season, and in March 2025, during the dry season when the river is 
at its lowest level in order to assess the seasonal variation of the 
physico-chemical quality of the water in the Tighèn River. As part of 
this study, a total of thirty (30) water samples were collected from 
the Tighèn River during the two (2) sampling campaigns, that is, 
fifteen per campaign, or three samples per site. The water samples 
were collected approximately 5 cm from the water’s surface in 1.5 L 
plastic bottles that had been cleaned beforehand and rinsed with 
water from the site prior to filling. The samples were kept cool in a 
portable cooler bag and then transported to the analytical chemistry 
laboratory at the University of N’Zérékoré for chemical analysis.

Regarding in situ analysis, we determined the physical parameters,

rinsed with distilled water and then with water from the site. The 
probes are then immersed in the water, and the various parameters 
are read and recorded in a table. In the laboratory, suspended 
solids were determined using the volumetric method with MN 615

Nitrates, nitrites and phosphates were determined using the 
photometric method with a 7500 photometer; iron (Fe), potassium 
(K), and manganese (mn) were determined using an AL 450 
photometer. This method involves placing the sample to be 
analyzed into a clean cuvette, followed by the specific reagent, in 
accordance with the photometric protocol. The cuvette containing 
the mixture is placed in the instrument, which is set to the specific 
wavelength for the parameter, after first performing a blank test with 
the sample without reagent. The results are read directly and 
recorded in an Excel spreadsheet. Three measurements were 
performed to verify the accuracy of the results. 
 
 
Data analysis and processing 
 
The dynamics of each interview were analyzed separately and 
summarized. The physicochemical analysis data were analyzed 
spatially (from upstream to downstream ) and temporally (from 
October 2024 to March 2025) using Excel. A statistical analysis was 
carried out with the XLSTAT to determine the average and type 
bounds. The XLSTAT is also used to determine the correlation 
between different parameters in order to determine the influences 
of different parameters and seasons on the water quality of the 
river. The results of the analysis were compared to the Surface 
Water Quality Standard (Ifremer, 200 9). The phenomenon of 
diagnosing the ecological state and its evolution in relation to 
human activities are determined on the basis of ecological status 
watercourses-general physico -chemical parameters of (Ifremer, 
2009).

DISCUSSION 
 
The development of riparian areas of the Tighèn River, 
under the combined effect of population growth and 
increased human activities, is accompanied by a 
significant increase in solid, liquid and gaseous 
discharges into this watercourse. These inputs, often 
uncontrolled, contribute to the degradation of water 
quality, with significant repercussions on aquatic 
ecosystems and on the health of local populations 
dependent on this resource for their domestic uses. This 
situation is part of a broader context observed in Africa, 
where surface water pollution results from socioeconomic 
constraints, a lack of sanitation infrastructure and poor 
compliance with environmental regulations. The results 
obtained show that water temperature varies slightly 
between seasons, with averages of 23.293°C in the rainy 
season and 23.993°C in the dry season. These values 
are lower than those reported by Onivogui (2013) in the 
Konkouré River (30.8 to 31°C in the dry season), where 
hydromorphological conditions favor more marked 
warming of the waters. The relative thermal stability 
observed in the Tighèn River could be explained by its 
location in a forest area, characterized by a dense plant 
cover limiting direct heating. 
Nevertheless, these temperatures, slightly below the 
optimal interval (24 to 35°C) required for the development 
of many aquatic species (Dèdjiho, 2011 ), could 
negatively influence their growth. The pH, a fundamental 
parameter reflecting the acid -base balance of the 
medium, has average values of 6.85 in rainy season and 
5.85 in dry season. If the values recorded during the rainy 
season remain compatible with the recommended 
standards for surface water (6.5 to 9) according to

Nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) nutrients show 
marked seasonal variations. High nitrate (126.67 mg.L −1) 
and phosphate (1.431 mg.L −1) concentrations during the 
rainy season indicate a significant contribution related to 
runoff and leaching from agricultural soils. Conversely, 
nitrites show higher concentrations in the dry season 
(0.13 mg•L −1), which is generally associated with low 
oxygenation conditions. These results corroborate the 
observations of Chitou (2022), who points out that the 
predominance of nitrites in surface waters is often linked 
to a lack of dissolved oxygen. Indeed, according to Diaz 
(2001), the oxidation of nitrites into nitrates requires 
sufficient oxygen availability, thus conditioning the 
dynamics of nitrogen forms. The high levels of nitrates, 
nitrites and phosphates observed in the Tighèn river 
reflect a significant influence of agricultural activities, 
notably the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides. This 
finding is in line with the results of Lamah et al. (2022), 
which state that agricultural practices strongly affect the

metallic elements, the average concentrations of 
potassium, manganese and iron, respectively higher in 
the rainy season than in the dry season, reflect a 
combined influence of natural processes (weathering of 
rocks, leaching of soils) and anthropogenic inputs. The 
increase in rainy season concentrations suggests an 
increased transport of these elements by runoff, 
reinforcing the hypothesis of a significant contribution 
from human activities. Finally, the seasonal variations 
observed for all the analyzed parameters highlight the 
decisive influence of hydrological conditions on the 
chemical quality of the waters of the Tighèn River. These 
results confirm the major role of diffuse anthropogenic 
inputs in the degradation of this aquatic system, calling 
for the implementation of integrated and sustainable 
water resources management strategies. 
 
 
Conclusion 
 
The results of this study indicate that the Tighèn River is 
subjected to significant anthropogenic pressure, primarily 
in the form of chemical pollution characterized by 
elevated concentrations of nitrogen and phosphorus 
compounds. The physicochemical analyses revealed that 
several of the measured parameters exceeded the 
recommended guideline values for the protection of 
aquatic ecosystems and domestic water use. In addition, 
metallic elements such as Fe, Mn, and K were detected 
in the river water. Although the concentrations of iron and 
potassium observed in this study do not appear to pose 
an immediate threat to the aquatic environment, their 
regular monitoring remains essential for the sustainable 
management of the river. However, the absence of a 
comprehensive assessment of potentially toxic heavy 
metals in relation to the identified anthropogenic activities 
represents a limitation of the present study. The elevated 
concentrations of nutrients indicate an increased risk of 
eutrophication, most likely resulting from urban runoff and 
agricultural activities within the watershed. Therefore, the 
implementation of effective watershed management 
strategies, including improved wastewater treatment, 
restoration of riparian buffer zones, and continuous 
monitoring of water quality, is strongly recommended to 
reduce pollution and protect the ecological integrity of the 
Tighèn River.
