Digital technology promotes intangible cultural heritage from “static protection” to “living innovation,” but colleges and universities still face triple obstacles—superficial resources, curriculum fragmentation, and practice homogeneity—when using digital intangible cultural heritage to collaboratively improve aesthetic education and humanistic quality. Based on 1,187 data from 12 universities, this study reveals the three-dimensional collaboration mechanism of “content-technology-value” through structural equation model and potential profile analysis, and finds that “multiple collaboration” (0.38) and “content precision” (0.35) are the core driving forces.
