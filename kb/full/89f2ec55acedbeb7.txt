The advent of wearable devices and digital biomarkers has fundamentally changed how we manage chronic diseases by giving us the means to monitor patients' physiological parameters, how they behave, and how their condition is progressing in the real world continuously and non-invasively. Digital health technology advances have also enhanced the ability to provide an objective clinical database that can be used for early diagnosis, developing a personalized risk profile, and implementing appropriate treatment options in a timely manner. Wearable sensors are able to track vital signs such as heart rate, physical activity, sleep patterns, blood pressure, glucose levels, cardiac rhythms and many other health indicators, allowing clinicians to establish a thorough and accurate database of their patients for use in creating a more tailored plan for managing their illness. The combination of Digital Biomarkers with Artificial Intelligence, Mobile Health Platforms and Electronic Health Records allows for the use of predictive analytics, Remote Patient Monitoring and Precision Medicine strategies. However, despite the increased clinical benefits of digital biomarkers and wearables, significant limitations still exist: variability in measurement accuracy, a lack of standardisation, data security/privacy issues, regulatory hurdles, level of patient compliance, and limited external clinical validation across different populations (e.g., ethnic minorities). As such, further advances in technology and rigorous clinical testing will need to occur in order to increase the reliability of wearable devices and digital biomarker data and expand their clinical use. https://doi.org/10.52783/jchr.v16.i4.15495
