Abstract Flood frequency analysis (FFA) forms the basis of many hydrological assessments and practical design calculations. Traditionally, FFA is based on the assumptions of stationary distributions and homogeneous samples. These assumptions are questionable in light of climate change and the wide variety of hydrological processes that lead to flooding. Furthermore, these assumptions can obscure important insights, including process‐specific changes. In this study, we develop a process‐aware, non‐stationary mixture model that combines type‐specific distributions with time‐varying parameters. An application to more than 600 catchments across Europe shows that the various flood types change differently over time.
