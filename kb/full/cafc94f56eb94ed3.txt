Abstract Coastal eutrophication and habitat degradation can have long-term impacts on coastal ecosystems and communities. In the Mexican Caribbean, the Puerto Morelos reef lagoon was highly oligotrophic in 1995, but by 2020 it had changed to mesotrophic-eutrophic because of a gradual increase in nutrient and organic matter inputs related with an explosive growth in population and coastal development since the late 1990s, and massive inundations of holopelagic Sargassum since 2014. Here, we aimed to compare the seagrass-associated decapod assemblage in the reef lagoon after 25 years of environmental change.
