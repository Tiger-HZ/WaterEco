The present chapter investigates the relationship between urban expansion, accessibility, and land-use change, highlighting the contribution of spatial analysis to the study of forestation/deforestation dynamics associated with exurban development. The theoretical framework is delineated, referring to specific economic models and the forest transition theory, developing and discussing some criticisms to the Kuznets environmental curve in specific contexts. The methodology also proposes a practical exercise dealing with deforestation pattern in a metropolitan region, adopting a radial grid divided 30 into concentric bands and sectors to assess the variation in forest cover over a sufficiently long time period, namely from 1938 to 2006. The spatial analysis highlights the decisive impact of road axes and the lack of market instruments in preserving forest heritage. The results underscore the need for high-resolution spatial planning and public regulation interventions to promote the reversal of deforestation dynamics.
