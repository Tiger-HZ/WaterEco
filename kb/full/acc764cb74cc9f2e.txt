Sustainable horticultural production requires nutrient-management strategies that reduce dependence on inorganic fertilizers while maintaining crop performance. This study evaluated fermented goat urine liquid organic fertilizer as a recycled nutrient source for red lettuce (Lactuca sativa var. crispa) under reduced nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium fertilization. Treatments comprised a reference fertilizer rate of 3 g plant−1 per application and fermented goat urine at 50, 100, 150, 200, or 250 mL L−1 combined with 1.5 g plant−1 per application. Plant height, leaf number, root length, and shoot fresh weight did not differ significantly among treatments. Root fresh weight (F5,15 = 13.85, p < .001) and root-to-shoot fresh mass ratio (F5,15 = 4.70, p = .009) were significantly affected. The 250 mL L−1 treatment produced the highest root fresh weight (37.06 g plant−1) and root-to-shoot ratio (0.436), representing increases of 105.4% and 88.5%, respectively, relative to the reference treatment. These findings indicate that fermented goat urine can partially replace inorganic fertilizer while maintaining aboveground growth and promoting greater root fresh biomass. Further studies should characterize fertilizer composition, growing-medium properties, and plant nutrient uptake.
