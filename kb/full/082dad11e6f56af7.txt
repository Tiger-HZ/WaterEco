Adsorption processes for environmental sustainability

This special issue of Environmental Science and Pollution 
Research (ESPR) presents selected contributions from the 
15th Brazilian Meeting on Adsorption (EBA 15), held in 
Maceió, Alagoas, Brazil, from November 20 to 22, 2024. 
Researchers used the meeting to present recent advances, 
debate current challenges, and sketch where adsorption sci-
ence and related fields may go next. Professor Lucas Meili 
of the Federal University of Alagoas, Brazil, organized EBA 
15.
Since its first edition, the Brazilian Meeting on Adsorp-
tion (EBA) has grown into one of Brazil's main scientific 
forums for adsorption science. It is held biennially, in a dif-
ferent region of the country each time. Researchers, students, 
professionals, and industry representatives come to exchange 
ideas, discuss emerging challenges, and share advances in 
fundamental and applied adsorption. The meeting has also 
done something harder to measure: it strengthened the Bra-
zilian adsorption community, brought research groups into 
contact with one another, and widened the community's 
links with colleagues abroad.
EBA has always mixed research, education, and scientific 
networking. The clearest example is the Adsorption School, 
where students and early-career researchers work through 
fundamental concepts, experimental approaches, and 
emerging applications in lectures given by experienced 
researchers. The meeting communicates new results and 
trains the people who will produce the next ones.
The 15th edition reflected this broad scope. EBA 15 gath-
ered 198 participants and 193 scientific contributions, presented 
in oral and poster sessions. The program included 16 invited 
speakers and researchers from institutions in Brazil, Portugal, 
Argentina, Colombia, the United States, and the United King-
dom, reflecting the meeting's growing international reach. The 
Adsorption School comprised five specialized lectures and 
remained a core part of the scientific and educational program.
The contributions presented at EBA 15 show how diverse 
the field has become. Topics ran from the development and 
characterization of new adsorbent materials to environmen-
tal remediation, CO₂ capture and storage, biotechnology, 
catalysis, energy applications, and the modeling and simu-
lation of adsorption processes. The meeting also covered 
advanced porous materials, hybrid adsorbents, and work 
aimed at moving adsorption technologies out of the labora-
tory and into practical and industrial use.
Selected contributions from the meeting were invited for 
submission to this special issue of Environmental Science and 
Pollution Research. Seven research articles were accepted 
after the journal's regular peer-review process, reflecting the 
quality of the work and the conference's thematic range.
The accepted articles cover a broad range of adsorption 
topics, with emphasis on environmental applications and 
advanced adsorbent materials. They address modified clays for 
dye removal, porous carbons derived from waste materials for 
CO₂ capture, magnetic and conducting polymer-based com-
posites for adsorbing textile dyes, and nanostructured materials 
for treating oilfield-produced water. Other studies explore Ca-
modified biochar for phosphate removal, zeolite-geopolymer 
composites for adsorbing toxic metals, and biochars produced 
from agro-industrial residues for removing methylene blue.
Together, these studies point to the directions now 
shaping adsorption science: valorization of waste and bio-
mass-derived materials, the design of multifunctional and 
nanostructured adsorbents, the removal of emerging and con-
ventional contaminants from aqueous systems, and adsorp-
