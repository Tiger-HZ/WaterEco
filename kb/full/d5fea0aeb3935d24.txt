Intensive agricultural production generates large volumes of lignocellulosic residues and contributes to heavy metal pollution, particularly copper (Cu). The valorization of these by-products as low-cost adsorbents offers a promising strategy for circular economy implementation and environmental remediation. In this study, lignin–silica nanocomposites were synthesized via a sol–gel process from two abundantly available agricultural wastes in Cuba: rice husk and coffee parchment. The nanocomposites were characterized by FT-IR, TEM and TGA methods. The nanocomposites showed a spherical shape and a diameter between 50 and 60 nm and their silica composition depended on the composition of the starting material.
