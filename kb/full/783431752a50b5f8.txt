Abstract Evaluating the performance of long-term water quality monitoring networks against international reference systems requires analytical tools capable of comparing entire empirical distributions — not merely summary statistics — yet distributional benchmarking methods have rarely been applied at the cross-continental scale. This study develops and applies a four-method analytical framework — combining Wasserstein-1 distributional distance (earth mover’s distance), Shannon-entropy-weighted TOPSIS, linear programming (LP) convergence planning, and principal component analysis with hierarchical cluster analysis (PCA/HCA) — to compare ten São Paulo river basins (Brazil) against 23 European countries monitored under the Water Framework Directive (WFD), using a harmonised dataset of 646,35
