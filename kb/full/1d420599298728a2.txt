While marine policies increasingly demand functional ecosystem metrics like the Trophic Transfer Index (TTI) to assess eutrophication, basin-wide biological monitoring remains logistically prohibitive. This study applies artificial neural networks (ANNs) to deduce continuous TTI and Good Environmental Status (GES) for the Baltic Sea directly from standard environmental variables. Using 70 years of data, we compared feedforward (MLP) and recurrent (LSTM) architectures. The LSTM excelled at spatial extrapolation (accuracy: 0.89) by acting as a non-linear spatial regulariser. Conversely, the MLP proved superior for temporal forecasting (accuracy: 0.8), adapting faster to recent ecological recoveries where the LSTM struggled with historical inertia.
