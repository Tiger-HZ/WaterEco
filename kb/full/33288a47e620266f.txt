Abstract As sessile organisms, plants rely on intricate molecular regulatory networks to navigate complex, fluctuating environments and diverse stress conditions. Nitrogen Limitation Adaptation (NLA), a RING-type ubiquitin ligase harboring an SPX domain, has emerged as a central hub integrating plant responses to nutrient availability, pathogen attack, and cold stress. This review provides a systematic overview of the structural features of NLA, with a particular focus on the regulatory role of inositol pyrophosphates (PP-InsPs) in plant phosphorus signaling. We highlight the core functions of NLA in maintaining nitrogen–phosphorus homeostasis and mediating the crosstalk between nutrient status and immune defense.
