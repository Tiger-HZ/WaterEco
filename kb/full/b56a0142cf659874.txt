Natural porous environments, such as soils and aquifers, are often highly heterogeneous, exposing microorganisms to variable fluid flow and nutrient landscapes. Chemotactic bacteria can locate nutrient hotspots and thereby accelerate subsurface reactions, yet how chemotaxis is affected by pore-scale flow remains poorly understood. Here, we show that pore-scale heterogeneity enhances the foraging benefits afforded to bacteria by motility and chemotaxis, compared to nonmotile cells advected by the flow.
