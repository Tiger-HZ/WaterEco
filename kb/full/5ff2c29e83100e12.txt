Maintaining agricultural production while reducing groundwater contamination is a challenge communities across the Midwest are striving to address. The Wisconsin Central Sands (WCS) exemplifies these challenges, as the sandy soil, high water table, and production of high nitrogen (N)-demanding crops have left the region vulnerable to nitrate (NO3) leaching. Because of the documented high levels of NO3 in groundwater across the region, N management practices have been proposed to reduce NO3 loss to the environment while maintaining crop yields. Using the Agro-IBIS agroecosystem model, we examined 42 N management scenarios across the WCS spanning the years 1979 to 2021. We evaluated the impact of N management strategies on corn (Zea mays L.) yield and NO3 leaching, as well as their effectiveness under a changing climate. We found that by implementing either fertigation or split application management practices, NO3 leaching could be reduced by more than 20% with little reduction in corn yield. However, NO3 leaching reductions approaching 50% were only achieved when N application rates were reduced by 100 kg N ha−1 and N was applied during multiple events. Furthermore, increased precipitation, which is likely to be more common as climate change continues, hindered the effectiveness of these management strategies. Despite altering the rate, timing, and source of fertilizer application, the NO3 concentration of water draining beneath the soil profile never fell below 10 mg L−1, the standard for safe drinking water. Our findings underscore the limited ability of N management strategies alone to reduce NO3 leaching to meet safe drinking water standards in the WCS.
