Area-based conservation beyond traditional Protected Area (PA) networks is recognized as essential for addressing biodiversity loss, particularly in highly anthropized regions such as the Mediterranean. In this context, Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (OECMs) have emerged as complementary tools to achieve the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework targets. This review synthesizes evidence from 300 peer-reviewed studies to assess the status, potential, and limitations of OECMs across Mediterranean countries, including several countries containing IUCN Green List protected areas used as reference examples of conservation governance effectiveness. A structured comparative synthesis and evidence-mapping analysis reveals that OECM implementation remains limited, with only a small number of formally reported sites concentrated in a few countries, while many potential OECMs remain unrecognized. Governance patterns are dominated by state-led systems, despite the ecological importance of community-managed and culturally embedded landscapes. The study was designed as a structured narrative review complemented by evidence-mapping and comparative synthesis approaches. Although it was not designed as a formal systematic or scoping review, methodological transparency was informed by key reporting principles outlined in PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-ScR guidance. The analysis highlights key Mediterranean-specific opportunities, including multifunctional systems and landscape mosaics that support biodiversity and connectivity beyond protected areas. However, OECM effectiveness is constrained by land-use pressures, climate change, governance fragmentation, and limited data availability. The review identifies critical variables conditioning OECM performance, including governance quality, land tenure, connectivity, land-use intensity, and monitoring capacity. It concludes that OECMs represent a strategic pathway for expanding conservation in Mediterranean landscapes, but their effectiveness depends on improved recognition, governance integration, and regional coordination.
