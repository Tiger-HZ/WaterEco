Discrepancies in the definitions of flowing waters, stagnant waters, and land covered by these waters, as provided in the Water Law Act, the Water Framework Directive (WFD), and the legal provisions governing the land and building register, are not limited to purely theoretical legal considerations. They also have significant practical implications, particularly with regard to ownership relations. Particular controversies concern the classification of lakes, which under the Water Law Act may be regarded either as flowing or stagnant waters, whereas the Water Framework Directive consistently classifies lakes as stagnant waters. As indicated in this paper, however, the implementation of the WFD with regard to the definitions of flowing and stagnant waters does not require legislative intervention and remains consistent with the objectives of the Directive. Fundamental doubts arise, however, with respect to the classification of land covered by such waters. The Water Law Act limits this category to natural water bodies, while the land and building register extends it to canals and artificial water reservoirs. This lack of coherence gives rise to numerous interpretative inconsistencies and contributes to the consolidation of flawed practices among both administrative authorities and courts. In this context, it appears necessary to clarify the existing regulations and introduce separate land-use categories, such as “Wp-k” for land covered by inland flowing waters in canals and “Wp-u” for land covered by inland flowing waters within hydraulic structures. Such amendments would help restore systemic coherence and eliminate the current interpretative ambiguities.
