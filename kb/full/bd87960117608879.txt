Abstract Decadal variability of the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation (AMOC-DV) is a major source of climate variability in the Atlantic and exerts broad influence on the climate system. However, the factors controlling its amplitude across climate models and background climate states remain insufficiently understood. Here, we show that AMOC-DV amplitude is closely constrained by the mean surface density flux over the subpolar North Atlantic across CMIP6 models and climate states, including preindustrial, historical, and future warming climates. This emergent relationship is further supported by the CESM2 Large Ensemble.
