Legacy pesticides remain a persistent environmental challenge despite regulatory bans and reductions in agricultural pesticide use. Continued detections in surface water and groundwater indicate that historical residues stored in soils and sediments contribute to long-term contamination. This review evaluates the mechanistic processes governing legacy pesticide persistence, including adsorption–desorption dynamics, sediment storage, preferential flow pathways, and groundwater recharge behavior. A structured literature synthesis of peer-reviewed studies published between 2000 and 2025 was conducted to assess monitoring trends, physicochemical determinants of persistence, and remediation technologies applicable at the catchment scale.
