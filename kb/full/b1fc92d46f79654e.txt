Abstract The Stump Bay wetland is part of the South Taupo wetland, situated on the southern coast of Lake Taupo in the central North Island, New Zealand. The water level of Lake Taupo has been artificially controlled since 1941. The present study found that surface and subsurface water levels in the wetland are closely related to each other. These water levels are influenced mainly by rainfall and evapotranspiration (which is seasonal) in areas that are greater than c. 100 m landward from the lake edge, but by the lake level for areas in close proximity to the lake. The wetland is a discharge and/or through‐flow area, and the general flow direction is towards the lake.
