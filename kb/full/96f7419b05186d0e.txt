Cyanate, a by‐product of urea decomposition, is a potential nitrogen (N) source in marine environments, but to date it has received scant attention. Cyanobacteria presumably acquire this compound via a substrate‐specific ABC‐type transporter ( cynABD ), and they convert it to ammonium and carbon dioxide by cyanase ( cynS ) activity. Participation of cyanate utilization genes in N‐stress responses in cyanobacteria has been implied previously, but its ecological context has not been studied. We employed polymerase chain reaction protocols for cynA amplification to examine the potential for cyanate recruitment in the Gulf of Aqaba, northern Red Sea.
