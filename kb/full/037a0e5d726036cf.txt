Abstract: This study assessed the effects of heavy metals on plant community species diversity in 9 
the restoration and reconstruction area of Xinjian Dyke in Caizi Lake National Wetland Park. Using 10 
a positive matrix factorization (PMF) model, principal component analysis (PCA), regression, and 11 
correlation analysis, our study determined that the main sources of soil heavy metals in the study 12 
area were organic fertilizer, transportation and agricultural activities, and natural sources. Hg, Cd, 13 
Zn, As, Cr, and Cu were the main heavy metals in the study site. The Shannon-Wiener, Margalef, 14 
and Simpson indices were used to evaluate plant diversity. Our findings indicated that Hg was not 15 
significantly correlated with any of the three indices, Cr and As were positively correlated with the 16 
indices, and Cd and Zn were negatively correlated with the indices. Furthermore, Cu was positively 17 
correlated with the Shannon-Wiener and Simpson indices, and negative ly correlated with the 18 
Margalef index. These findings indicated that the uniformity of t he individual distribution among 19 
species was an important factor of community diversity, and the differences in the community 20 
diversity indices were related to the heavy metals in the soil. To improve the difference and 21 
functional complementarity between coexisting individuals, single superior species must be avoided 22 
and niche differentiation must be maintained as much as possible. Soil pH value, SOM, and other 23 
conditions should be improved to inhibit the migration and diffusion of heavy metals, improve their 24 
bioavailability, and give priority to local pioneer plants instead of functional redundant species. 25

1. Introduction 27 
The Caizi Lake wetland is an important freshwater ecological func tion area located in the 28 
middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River, which breeds abundant biological resources. It is an 29 
important wintering habitat for the bean goose (Anser fabalis) and cygnets along the migration route 30 
from East Asia to Australia, as well as for endangered species such as the white-headed crane and 31 
Oriental white stork in East Asia (An et al., 2021). Therefore , strengthening the protection of the 32 
Caizi Lake wetland is critical for ecosystem and species diversity conservation. The Yangtze Huaihe 33 
River Diversion Project was launched in December 2016, and Caizi Lake is an important node lake. 34 
The project carries water northward through Caizi Lake. After the completion of the project, the 35 
water level rose and the wetland area decreased, which impacted the diversity of wetland plants and 36 
the survival of migratory birds in the tidal plain (Li et al., 2019). Plant community species diversity 37 
is not only the basis for maintaining wetland ecosystem functions, but al so an effective indicator 38 
that directly reflects community structure and functional characte ristics (Zheng et al., 2019). By 39 
improving the species diversity of plant communities, the number of ecological niches and possible 40 
habitats can also be increased (Garbisu et al., 2020). As an important measure to expand the habitat 41 
area of migratory birds and restore the ecological function of wetlands, the conversion of farmlands 42 
into wet vegetation areas has recently garnered increasing attention. 43 
However, Caizi Lake was polluted by pesticides and heavy metals in the past. Previous studies 44 
have found that the soil heavy metal content in some areas of th e Caizi Lake wetland exceed the 45 
Chinese GB15618-2018 standard (2018). Therefore, additional efforts are required not only to 46 
identify the sources of heavy metals, but also to study the adaptation and enrichment characteristics 47 
of native plants to heavy metals to understand the dynamic changes of plant communities in the 48 
4 
 
process of ecological restoration. Several studies have assessed the bioavailability of heavy metals 49 
in different types of soils, and some plants have been found to supera ccumulate specific heavy 50 
metals in a species-dependent manner (Juhasz et al., 2007; Teng et al., 2013; Gallego et al., 2012; 51 
Yang et al., 2021; Cyriac et al., 2021). However, soils are typically polluted with a mixture of many 52 
different heavy metals, and therefore the synergistic effect of multiple plants is required to remove 53 
these metals from the soil, meaning that community species diversity is a key component of wetland 54 
ecosystem resilience (Du et al., 2020). Several studies have assessed the toxicity and ecological risk 55 
of heavy metals on individual plants (Tóth et al., 2016; Ekengele et al., 2017; Matayoshi et al., 2021; 56 
Zuran et al., 2019). However, the effects of heavy metals on plant community species diversity are 57 
seldom reported. Most studies on plant population effects have focused on the community diversity 58 
of plants and microbes in aquatic environments and abandoned mines (Jia et al., 2019; Yang et al., 59 
2020; Deng et al., 2021). Therefore, the present study assessed the e ffects of soil heavy metals on 60 
the community diversity of native hygrophytes in the Xinjian Dyke res toration and reconstruction 61 
area of Caizi Lake National Wetland Park, after which an optimal plant community species structure 62 
was proposed. Collectively, our findings provide a reference for the development of wetland 63 
restoration and management strategies in the Yangtze and Huai rivers. 64

subtropical monsoon climate region, with abundant rainfall and a pleasant climate. The wetland is 68 
located in the north bank of the Yangtze River, at the junction of Yixiu District, Zongyang and 69

2.2 Plant diversity 82 
The survey was conducted from October 11 to 14, 2021. A tidal flat survey method was adopted 83 
to estimate community type and species richness based on the geograp hical and topographical 84 
characteristics of the study area. Based on the design of the soil sampling sites in the four transect 85 
zones, 1 m × 1 m quadrats were set according to the distribution characteristics of the plants in the 86 
transect zone, and 10 m ×10 m quadrats were set for plots with shrubs to conduct statistics on the 87 
species composition, abundance, coverage, and dominant species of herbac eous and shrub 88 
communities (Xie et al., 2014). Scattered and non-native plants were not evaluated in this study. 89 
Twelve representative wild plant communities were investiga ted. The transect of each type of 90 
community was shown in Figure 2, among which the shrub community was dominated by 91 
Broussonetia papyrifera and Pyrus betulifolia Bunge communities. 92

2.3 Soil sampling and determination 93 
Four representative 20 m × 5 m transect zones (YD1, YD2, YD3 and YD4) were selected in 94 
alternating land and water zones (Fig. 1). Soil samples with a 20 cm depth were collected in the 95 
center of each transect zone using a columnar soil sampler. Eac h sample site was mixed with 3 96 
subsamples, and 2 kg of original fresh sample was collected via the quartering method. After natural 97 
air drying, 100 g of sample was ground through a 200 mesh a nylon sieve via the shrinkage method 98 
for heavy metal element analysis. The total concentrations of heavy metals (Hg, As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, 99 
and Zn) were measured via inductively coupled plasma - optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES; 100 
iCAP™ 7400, Thermo Fisher, USA) after pretreatment via the microwave-assisted leaching method 101 
(Timoney, 2008). The pH of soil was measured with a ratio of soil to deionized water of 1:2.5 and 102 
a minimum pH value display unit of 0.01 (Seven Compact™, Mettler-Toledo, Switzerland). 103

The PMF 5.0 model was used for several calculations, the sources of heavy metals in the soil 181 
of Xinjian Dyke were largely attributed to five factors, and the results are shown in Figure 4. Studies 182 
have shown that external sources of Cd include industrial sources, fossil fuel combustion, pesticides, 183 
and fertilizers ( Yuan et al., 2019; Men et al., 2018 ; Chen et al., 2019 ; Zhang et al., 2018), some 184 
studies suggest that Cd is a common elemental marker of agricultural production in the absence of 185 
industrial activity (Kartal et al., 2006; Cloquet et al., 2006). Pb and its compounds are importan t 186 
industrial raw materials. He et al. (2013) studied heavy metals in the Yangtze River Delta and found 187 
that 84% of Pb inputs came from the atmospheric deposition of vehicle exhaust, and the generation 188 
of Pb and Cd was also related to traffic emissions (Chaturvedi et al., 2018). Cu is the main product 189 
of coal mining, smelting, and fossil fuel combustion. Moreover, organic fertilizer typically contains 190 
high concentrations of Cu. The main sources of Zn are zinc mining, smelt ing and processing, 191 
machinery manufacturing and emissions from galvanizing, instrumentation, organic synthesis, and 192 
paper making. The accumulation of Zn in soil is also related to the excessive use of livestock manure 193 
in agriculture (Loska and Wiechu ła, 2003). After preliminary analyses, our study excluded 194 
emissions from industrial sources such as mining and fossil fuel com bustion, non-ferrous metal 195 
smelting, machinery manufacturing and electroplating, instruments and meters, organic synthesis, 196 
and manufacturing. Furthermore, there were no co al-fired heating sources and mulching film has 197 
not been used in the area in recent years. However, transport roads were identified in the study area. 198

and Zn, indicating that F1 constitutes a traffic source. F2 was highly correlated with Cu, but weakly 200 
correlated with Cr and Pb, indicating that F2 was an agricultural non -point source of organic 201 
fertilizer emissions. Anthropogenic sources of As in soil are mainly related to the burning of fossil 202

3.2 Principal component analysis of heavy metals 227 
Heavy metals constitute important abiotic environmental factors in the soil of Xinjian Dyke 228 
wetland, and therefore their influence on plant species diversi ty cannot be ignored. In this study, 229 
heavy metal content and diversity indices were taken as primary va riables, and multiple variables 230 
were used to concentrate and extract heavy metal information through dimensionality reduction to 231 
identify the primary heavy metals in the soil (Talukder et al., 2021; Rachdawong and Christensen , 232 
1997). The KMO (Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin) and Bartlett's tests were performed on the data. The KMO 233 
test coefficient was 0.725 (P<0.05) and the correlation between pr imary variables was significant, 234 
which was suitable for principal component analysis. Principal components are extracted according 235 
to the principle of eigenvalue >1, and the total variance decomposition of each heavy metal content 236 
is summarized in Table 2. The characteristic values of the f irst and second principal components 237 
were >1, and the cumulative contribution rate reached 94.896%. In contrast, the characteristic values 238 
of principal components 3–7 are < 1, meaning that they only made a small contribution to the total 239 
variance. Therefore, the first two factors were selected as the load values of the main components, 240 
and the factor with the largest load value was considered the main driving factor. The first principal 241 
component carried the most information and contributed 74.432% of the total va riance on the 242 
interpretation of the original variables. The indicators with the highest load value were Hg, Cd, Zn, 243 
As and Cr (based on an absolute value threshold > 0.8), indicating that these elements were the main 244 
drivers of heavy metal pollution in the Xin Jian Dyke wetland. The c ontribution of the second 245 
principal component was 20.464%, where the load of Cu was 0.974 and that of the remaining metals 246 
13 
 
was below 0.7. Our findings thus indicated that Pb is not the main i nfluencing factor of soil heavy 247 
metals in the Xinjian Dyke wetland. 248

 The Shannon-Wiener index includes richness and evenness, and can t herefore comprehensively 335 
reflect the diversity and evenness of the community (Wilsey and Stirling, 2007). A larger Shannon-336 
Wiener index means that there are more plant species in the community, and therefore there is a 337 
higher species richness. At the same time, the uniformity of the individual distribution among 338 
species increases the community diversity. The Margalef i ndex reflects richness, whereas the 339 
Simpson index is more closely related to frequency and species unifor mity. Zhang et al. (2015) 340 
reported that species richness takes the number of species as an indicator, implying that all species 341 
contribute equally to the community. However, plants do not have equivalent f unctions in a 342 
community (Zhang et al., 2015; Adrián et al., 2021), and therefore species richness alone cannot 343 
explain the functional aspects of plant communities. Our findings sugg ested that the developing 344 
wetland community has a more uniform succession trend, and therefor e the uniformity of the 345 
individual distribution among species is an important determinant of com munity diversity. 346 
Therefore, the Simpson index and Shannon-Wiener indices of the plant community in the study area 347 
remained consistent. 348 
A comparison of communities within the same zone indicated that the H values and DS values 349 
of the three communities in the YD1 and YD4 transects had the same change trend, whereas the H 350 
and D M values of the three communities in the YD2 and YD3 transects al so exhibited the same 351 
change trend. These variations in the trends of the three indices were likely because most diversity 352 
indices currently rely on three untrue assumptions, meaning that all current biodiversity indices have 353 
their own unique limitations . Therefore, the differences in the diversity indices are not only related 354 
to abundance, richness, and internal factors of dominant species, but also to abiotic environmental 355 
factors (Fu et al., 2020). 356

3.5. Effect of heavy metal stress on plant diversity 357 
The differences in community diversity were influenced by both biotic and abiotic factors. The 358 
high degree of anthropogenic disturbance was evidenced by the high degree of community similarity 359 
and small patch aggregation and distribution in the study area. Water level, water quality, soil quality, 360 
grazing, mowing, and the river diversion project were all abiotic fact ors that affected the diversity 361 
of hygrophytes in this area. Our findings indicated that water level, water quality, grazing, mowing, 362 
and construction activities had little effect on hygrophyte diversity, meaning that the soil 363 
composition has a greater impact. Soil quality parameters includ e soil texture, structure, nutrients, 364 
pH, and salinity, as well as anthropogenic factors such as polluta nts. The pH values of the soil in 365 
four transect zones ranged from 5.37 to 6.63, soil organic matter (SOM) ranged from 1.09% to 366 
1.88%, and the coefficient of variation was 0.112 (Table S2), indicating these factors have little 367 
effect on plant diversity. Therefore, the effects of pesticides and heavy metals on plant community 368 
diversity should not be ignored (Zhang et al., 2016). 369 
Table 4 summarizes the results of the correlation analysis of heavy metal content and plant 370 
diversity indices. There was no significant correlation between Hg and the three diversity indices. 371 
Cr was positively correlated with the Shannon-Wiener and Simpson indices ( P < 0.01 ), with 372 
correlation coefficients of 0.741 and 0.762, respectively. As was posit ively correlated with the 373 
Margalef index (P < 0.05, r = 0.652). These findings indicated that as the content of Cr and As in 374 
the transect increased, the corresponding diversity index also increased. The positive effect of these 375 
metals on plant diversity could potentially be attributed to a combinat ion of factors including pH 376 
value, SOM, and other soil physical and chemical parameters, as well as biological characteristics 377 
of plant species, microorganisms, and other environmental factors (Ma et al., 2022). Alternatively, 378 
19 
 
the presence of heavy metals may reduce the advantages of a si ngle plant, increase competition 379 
among plants, and promote plant coexistence (Li et al., 2019). The positive effect of heavy metals 380 
on species diversity might be related to their complementary effec t or selection effect on plant 381 
species. The complexity and specificity of regional plant diversity in creased with increasing soil 382 
heavy metal content, which may be due to the use of supplementary resources by some species to 383 
promote inter-species diversity. It may also be that increasing species numbers increases the 384 
probability of random introduction of high-yielding species (Zhang et al ., 2015). Additionally, the 385 
existence of some species with heavy metal enrichment ability a lleviates the toxic stress of heavy 386 
metals on other species, thereby improving community species diversity. 387 
In contrast, Cd and Zn were negatively correlated with diversity i ndex. The correlation 388 
coefficient between Cd and the Shannon-Wiener index was -0.666 (P < 0.05), and that between Cd, 389 
Zn, and the Simpson index was -0.636 and -0.594 (P < 0.05), respectively, indicating that as Cd and 390 
Zn content increased, the corresponding diversity index decreased. The content of Cd and Zn in this 391 
study exceeded the GB 15618-2018 standard screening value and exhibited differe nt degrees of 392 
toxicity and ecological risk to plant individuals and populations. Therefore, high concentrations of 393 
these heavy metals had negative effects on community diversity indices. The content of Hg exceeded 394 
the GB 15618-2018 standard screening value but was not correlated with the community species 395 
diversity index. The toxicity of Hg on individuals and populations may be inhi bited by other 396 
environmental factors, thus indirectly reducing the negative effect of Hg on species diversity. This 397 
hypothesis is mentioned in Connell's theory of intermediate interference (Connell, 1978). Fantaw et 398 
al. demonstrated that the distribution characteristics of vege tation were greatly influenced by soil, 399 
as well as other environmental factors in addition to their mutual influence (Fantaw et al., 2006). 400

Cu was significantly positively correlated with the Shannon-Wiener and Simpson indices (P < 401 
0.01), and the correlation coefficients were 0.983 and 0.970, respectively . However, Cu was 402 
significantly negatively correlated with the Margalef index (P < 0.05, R = -0.628). The background 403 
value of Cu content in soil was 66.40±0.615 mg/kg in the study area, the content of Cu in the YD1 404 
and YD2 transects was lower than the background value. Therefor e, native plants with strong Cu 405 
resistance or hyperaccumulation occupied an important ecological nic he in the study area. The 406 
increase of Cu content promoted the increase of the Shannon-Wi ener and Simpson indices, which 407 
translated to a positive effect on the species diversity of the herbaceous community in Xinjian Dyke. 408 
Deng et al. (2021) found that certain Cu concentrations were significantly positively correlated with 409 
plant height, stem thickness, crown diameter, and plot coverage (Deng et al., 2021), and therefore 410 
this metal may promote community species diversity. Séleck et al. (2013) suggested that Cu may be 411 
a key factor in regulating plant community species diversity. However , studies have shown that 412 
wetlands have a high exchangeable Cu content and this heavy metal is hi ghly toxic. Once the Cu 413 
content exceeds the nutrient tolerance limit of plants, plant growth an d distribution become 414 
restricted (Stuckey et al., 2021). The analysis of single-factor regression confirmed the results of 415 
correlation analysis. As shown in Table 5, there was a good fitting degree between soil heavy metal 416 
content and the diversity indices. 417 
Conclusion 418 
The values of H, D S, and D M were significantly different in the communities of the same 419 
established species. The values of H, DM, and DS showed significant differences, and the variation 420 
trend of the diversity indices was not consistent for communities in the same transect zone. Coverage 421 
was not the influencing factor of species diversity in newly-built wetland communities. Instead, the 422 
21 
 
species composition and abundance were the internal factors that dete rmined the species diversity 423 
indices of the communities in the four transect zones. Differences in community diversity were not 424 
only related to species abundance, richness, and dominant species, but also to abiotic factors. 425 
The distribution of heavy metals in the soil of Xinjian Dyke wetland w as uniform and the 426 
regional differences were small. Hg was not significantly correlated with species diversity indices, 427 
Cu was positively correlated with the Shannon −Wiener and Simpson indices, Cr was positively 428 
correlated with the Shannon-Wiener and Simpson indices, and As was positively correlated with the 429 
Margalef index. There was a significant negative correlation between Cd and the Shannon-Wiener 430 
and Simpson indices, Zn and the Simpson index, and Cu and the Margalef in dex. These findings 431 
indicated that plant diversity increased as the content of Cu, Cr, and As increased in the transect. In 432 
contrast, diversity decreased when Cd and Zn content increased. Additiona lly, the Margalef index 433 
decreased with increasing Cu content. In this study, improving soil pH value, SOM, and other 434 
conditions can inhibit the migration and diffusion of heavy metals and improve their bioavailability. 435 
To improve the differences and functional complementarity between coexisting individuals, single 436 
superior species must be avoided and niche differentiation must be maintained as much as possible. 437 
Native pioneer plants should be given priority in artificial planting, whe reas functional redundant 438 
species should be avoided. Additionally, more studies are needed to confir m the changes of soil 439 
heavy metal distribution characteristics and accumulation proce ss before, during, and after 440 
ecological restoration. 441
