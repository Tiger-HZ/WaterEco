Lobelia dortmanna L. is an endangered isoetid species and a key bioindicator of oligotrophic soft-water lakes. This study investigates the morphological variability, population structure, and genetic diversity of L. dortmanna in two Lithuanian lakes: Lake Ešerinis (sandy, oligotrophic) and Lake Salinis (sludgedy, mesotrophic). Results demonstrate significant morphological plasticity; individuals in the nutrient-rich, sludged sediments of Lake Salinis exhibited higher dry biomass, longer leaves, and more extensive root systems. However, despite this "gigantism," the Lake Salinis population showed a critical lack of recruitment, with almost no seedlings observed.
