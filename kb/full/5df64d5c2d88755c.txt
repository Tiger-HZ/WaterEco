In March 2023, Freie Universität Berlin held a funeral for human remains belonging to 54 individuals which had been uncovered on the previous site of the notorious Kaiser Wilhelm Institute of Anthropology, Human Heredity, and Eugenics. These bones were found independently from many hundreds of skulls housed in the Museum für Völkerkunde (Ethnological Museum), in the Königliche Museen zu Berlin (Royal Museums of Berlin) and in the Rasseschädelsammlung (racial type skull collection). Suspecting that the fragments were study items collected from victims of genocides by the German colonial empire in South West Africa, East Africa and the Holocaust by Nazi Germany, the University decided not to investigate their origins, citing the necessity of avoiding what Nazi scientists did in separating people according to a fiction of race (‘ Bone Fragments ,’). Now meshed and indistinct, the bones carry a story back to the provenance of extermination techniques and the longer-running thread in the sinister history of scientific racism in Europe.
