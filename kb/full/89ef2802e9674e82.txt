Due to inadequately treated water, the majority of people on Earth, particularly in underdeveloped nations, are afflicted with water-borne illnesses such skin abscesses, cellulitis, infections, and diarrhea. A significant fraction of the world's population still lacks access to safe drinking water, which is an international concern. This work focused on the design and evaluation of charcoal, activated carbon, Moringa seed and sand water filtration systems. The objective was to evaluate the performance of the filters using flow rate and water quality parameters and also compare the cost and the performance of the filters with an existing commercial imported filter. For this work, the filtration approach was used. A variety of components, including sand, charcoal, activated carbon, and ground moringa seeds, were used to create four distinct filters due to the high expense and elimination of several microorganisms, including bacteria and pathogens. A number of tests were performed on the water quality, including those measuring the potential hydrogen (pH), electrical conductivity (EC), turbidity, dissolved oxygen (DO), total dissolved solid (TDS), and flow rates. According to the results, the best flow rate is 4.08 mL/sec for sand filters, followed by 2.45 mL/sec for charcoal filters, 2.05 mL/sec for activated carbon filters, and 0.75 mL/sec for moringa filters. In terms of turbidity, moringa filter has the best value at 5.39 NTU, while sand filter has the lowest at 22.48 NTU. In terms of EC, moringa filter remains the best, while sand filter has the highest values at 196 µS/cm and 261 µS/cm, respectively.
