Abstract Anthropogenic climate change is exacerbating water scarcity in Mediterranean climate regions, such as Central Chile, which already has a low annual precipitation rate, increasing the likelihood of a potentially permanent “mega-drought”. While carbon-neutrality policies often combine energy- and land-sector measures, their specific implications for water-energy-land interactions in Chile remain insufficiently explored. This study presents the first national-level application of GCAM-Chile to assess how combined energy- and land-sector carbon-neutrality pathways may reshape sectoral water-demand patterns in an already water-stressed context.
