Influence of various nutrient management practices on 
growth, yield, quality and economics of Chandrasur 
(Lepidium sativum L.) 
 
Shivangi Lalwani, Yeman Kumar Dewangan, Madhusudan Dewangan and 
Savita Dewangan 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9h.6561 
 
Abstract 
The present investigation was conducted to evaluate the “Influence of various nutrient management practices 
on growth, yield, quality and economics of Chandrasur (Lepidium sativum L.)” under the agro -climatic 
conditions of Chhattisgarh. The field experimen t was conducted during the rabi season of 2025 -26 at the 
University Farm, Indira Gandhi Krishi Vishwavidyalaya, Raipur (C.G.). The experiment comprised eight 
nutrient management treatments with three replications and was laid out in a randomized block desi gn 
(RBD). The nutrient management treatments included different combinations of recommended dose of 
fertilizers (RDF), farmyard manure (FYM), biofertilizers and organic liquid inputs. Among the treatments, 
application of 75% RDF @ 60:45:30 kg ha⁻¹ N:P₂O₅:K ₂O + 25% FYM @ 3 t ha⁻¹ (T₅) recorded superior 
performance for major growth attributes. The treatment recorded the highest plant height at 30, 60, 90 DAS 
and at harvest), as well as the highest number of primary branches plant⁻¹). It also recorded the maxi mum 
number of leaves plant⁻¹ and dry matter accumulation at the different growth stages. The control treatment 
(T₁) recorded the lowest values for the major growth attributes. The superior performance of 75% RDF @ 
60:45:30 kg ha⁻¹ N:P₂O₅:K₂O + 25% FYM @ 3 t ha⁻¹ may be attributed to the complementary effects of 
inorganic fertilizers and FYM, which ensured a readily available as well as sustained supply of nutrients and 
improved the soil environment for better nutrient uptake and plant growth. The study indicates that integrated 
application of 75% RDF @ 60:45:30 kg ha⁻¹ N:P₂O₅:K₂O + 25% FYM @ 3 t ha⁻¹ (has considerable potential 
for improving the growth and productivity of Chandrasur under the conditions of Chhattisgarh. 
 
Keywords: Chandrasur, Lepidium sativum L., nutrient management, integrated nutrient management, RDF, 
FYM, growth, yield, quality, economics 
 
Introduction 
Chandrasur ( Lepidium sativum L. ), commonly known as garden cress, is an underutilized but 
highly nutritious seed spice and medicina l herb belonging to the family Brassicaceae. The crop 
has been used in Ayurveda and folk medicine for centuries due to its health -promoting effects, 
particularly in enhancing haemoglobin levels, supporting bone health, increase the milk 
production in lactating women and milch animal, improving digestive functions and also used to 
treatment of menstrual disorder. (Doke and Guha 2014) [3]. Chandrasur is believed to have 
originated in the Mediterranean and Middle Eastern regions, from where it spread to India and 
other Asian countries (Ghosh, 2005) [4]. In India, it is cultivated in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya 
Pradesh, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and also in chhattisgarh mainly cultivated for research purpose 
under varied agro-climatic conditions. In Chhattisgarh, rice harvesting takes place during October-
November month and due to delayed harvesting, the available window for sowing the subsequent 
crop be comes very limited. Under such conditions, farmers prefer short -duration crops like 
Chandrasur, which can be successfully grown with residual soil moisture and minimal inputs. The 
crop mature within 110-120 days. Adoption of integrated nutrient management practices, which 
combine the judicious use of organic manures with inorganic fertilizers, is critical to sustaining 
soil fertility and enhancing yield potential of chandrasur, thereby ensuring its economic and 
medicinal importance across diverse agro-climatic zones.

Organic farming improves soil organic carbon, accessible nutrient 
content, microbial population and enzymatic activity, making it 
more sustainable for organic crop production (D ahiphale) 11, 
Adding different organic origin nutrients in the soil such as 
organic manure, bio -fertilizer and FYM will be effective in 
further improving productivity sustainably. Ghanjeevamrit and

Materials and Methods 
A field experiment was conducted during rabi season 2025-26 at 
the University Farm, Indira Gandhi Krishi Vishwavidyalaya , 
Raipur (C.G.), India. The soil of the experimental field was clayey 
loam in texture and neutral to slightly alkaline in reaction, with 
low available nitrogen, high available phosphorus and high 
available potassium. The experiment consisted of eight diffe rent 
nutrient management treatments involving inorganic fertilizers, 
farmyard manure (FYM), biofertilizers and natural farming-based 
nutrient sources, with three replications in Randomized Block 
Design (RBD). The treatments were T₁: Control; T₂: 100% RDF 
@ 80:60:40 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹; T₃: 50% RDF @ 40:30:20 kg 
N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹ + 50% FYM @ 6 t ha⁻¹; T₄: 100% FYM @ 12 t 
ha⁻¹ + Azotobacter @ 1.5 kg ha⁻¹ + Phosphobacter @ 2 kg ha⁻¹; 
T₅: 75% RDF @ 60:45:30 kg N:P₂O₅:K₂O ha⁻¹ + 25% FYM @ 3 
t ha⁻¹, T₆: Ghanjeevamrit @ 1000 kg ha⁻¹ + Jeevamrit @ 500 litres 
ha⁻¹ at 15 days intervals; T₇: Ghanjeevamrit @ 1000 kg ha⁻¹ + 
Jeevamrit @ 500 litres ha⁻¹ at 15 days intervals + 20% cow urine 
foliar spray at 20 days intervals; and T₈: FYM @ 12 t ha⁻¹ + 
Jeevamrit @ 500 litres ha⁻¹. 
Chandrasur (Lepidium sativum L.) variety GA-2 was used as the 
test crop. The crop was sown on 18th November 2025 at a spacing 
of 30 × 10 cm. The crop was harvested on 10th March 2026. 
The growth observations viz., plant height (cm), number of 
primary branches plant⁻¹ and number of leaves plant⁻¹ were 
recorded at different stages of crop growth at 30, 60 and 90 days 
after sowing (DAS) and at harvest. For recording observations, 
five representative plants were randomly selected and tagged in 
each plot, and the mean values were used for statistical analysis. 
The data obtained for different growth attributes were subjected 
to analysis of variance (ANOVA) appropriate for Randomized 
Block Design. The significance of treatment differences was 
tested at 5% prob ability level and critical difference (CD) was 
calculated wherever the treatment effect was significant. 
 
Results and Discussion 
Growth attributes : The plant height, number of primary 
branches plant⁻¹ and dry matter accumulation increased with the 
progress in crop age, whereas the number of leaves increased up 
to 90 DAS and declined at harvest due to leaf senescence. These 
growth attributes significantly varied due to different nutrient 
management practices. Among the different nutrient management 
practices, application of 75% RDF @ 60:45:30 kg ha⁻¹ 
N:P₂O₅:K₂O + 25% FYM @ 3 t ha⁻¹ (T₅) recorded the highest 
plant height, number of primary branches plant⁻¹, number of 
leaves plant⁻¹ and dry matter accumulation. Plant height under T₅ 
remained at par with 100% RDF @ 80:60:40 kg ha⁻¹ N:P₂O₅:K₂O 
(T₂) at 30 DAS and with T₂ and 50% RDF @ 40:30:20 kg ha⁻¹ 
N:P₂O₅:K₂O + 50% FYM @ 6 t ha⁻¹ (T₃) at 60 and 90 DAS, while 
at harvest it remained at par with T₂, T₃ and 100% FYM @ 12 t 
ha⁻¹ + Azotobacter @ 1.5 kg ha⁻¹ + Phosphobacter @ 2 kg ha⁻¹ 
(T₄). The number of primary branches under T₅ was at par with 
T₂, T₃ and T₄ at 30 DAS, with T₂ at 90 DAS and with T₂, T₃ and 
T₄ at harvest, while T₅ was significantly superior to all treatments 
at 60 DAS. The highest number of leaves und er T₅ remained at 
par with T₂ at all stages, while dry matter accumulation under T₅ 
remained at par with T₂, T₃ and T₄ throughout the crop growth 
period. On the other hand, the lowest values of these growth 
attributes were recorded under control (T₁). The better growth 
under T₅ may be attributed to the balanced and sustained 
availability of nutrients through the combined application of 
inorganic fertilizers and FYM, which enhanced nutrient uptake, 
vegetative growth, leaf development and dry matter productio n. 
Similar improvement in growth and dry matter accumulation of 
Chandrasur due to integrated nutrient management was reported 
by Chouhan et al. (2014) [2] and Hemalatha et al. (2007) [6]. 
 
Yield and economics 
Seed yield significantly varied due to different nutrient 
management practices. Application of 75% RDF @ 60:45:30 kg 
ha⁻¹ N:P₂O₅:K₂O + 25% FYM @ 3 t ha⁻¹ (T₅) recorded the highest 
seed yield of 17.27 q ha⁻¹, which was at par with 100% RDF @ 
80:60:40 kg ha⁻¹ N:P₂O₅:K₂O (T₂) recording 16.05 q ha⁻¹, while it 
was significantly superior to 50% RDF @ 40:30:20 kg ha⁻¹ 
N:P₂O₅:K₂O + 50% FYM @ 6 t ha⁻¹ (T₃) and 100% FYM @ 12 t 
ha⁻¹ + Azotobacter @ 1.5 kg ha⁻¹ + Phosphobacter @ 2 kg ha⁻¹ 
(T₄). The lowest seed yield was recorded under control (T₁) with 
7.19 q ha⁻¹. The higher seed yield under T₅ may be attributed to 
the complementary effect of inorganic fertilizers and FYM, which 
provided readily available as well as sustained nutrients 
throughout crop growth and thereby improved yield formation. 
The B:C rat io was also highest under T₅ (3.40), followed by T₂ 
(3.30), whereas the lowest B:C ratio was recorded under control 
(T₁) (1.82), indicating better economic efficiency of integrated 
nutrient management with 75% RDF + 25% FYM. Similar 
enhancement in seed yie ld and economic returns of Chandrasur 
under integrated nutrient management was reported by Chouhan 
et al. (2014) [2] and Rajnish Kumar and Sarvesh Kumar (2019) [8].

Conclusion 
Based on my research trail, the treatment combination 75% RDF 
@ 60:45:30 kg ha⁻¹ N:P₂O₅:K₂O + 25% FYM @ 3 t ha⁻¹ (T₅) was 
found to be more productive. Although the finding are based on 
one season further research is needed to confirm the findings and 
their recommendation.
