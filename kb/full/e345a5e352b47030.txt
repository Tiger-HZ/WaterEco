Flow-induced noise from circular cylinders involves both surface-associated dipole and wake-associated quadrupole radiation, but their coupling and relative contributions remain difficult to resolve experimentally. Here, synchronized measurements of wake velocity, surface pressure, and far-field sound are combined through coherence analysis to elucidate these source mechanisms for baseline and porous-coated cylinders. The baseline noise is predominantly dipole-related, whereas the porous coating produces a mixed dipole--quadrupole contribution, revealing how modified wake dynamics and source coupling lead to substantial noise reduction.
