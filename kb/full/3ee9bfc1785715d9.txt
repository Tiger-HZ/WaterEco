Rice, the staple food for over half of the global population, faces a critical challenge in nitrogen use efficiency (NUE). Traditional broadcasting methods of nitrogenous fertilizers result in substantial nutrient losses, often exceeding 60–70% of applied nitrogen, through ammonia volatilization, surface runoff, denitrification and deep leaching. These losses escalate cultivation costs and inflict severe ecological damage, including eutrophication of water bodies and greenhouse gas emissions. Fertilizer deep placement (FDP) has emerged as a pivotal nutrient management technology, wherein compacted urea or NPK briquettes are placed 7–10 cm beneath the soil surface, directly adjacent to the active root zone. This study details the plan view, development and field evaluation of a mechanized deep placement fertilizer applicator specifically engineered for machine-transplanted paddy systems. The applicator leverages a groundwheel-driven metering mechanism to synchronize briquette placement with the precise row spacing of the transplanter. The machine is constructed from robust, corrosionresistant materials to withstand highly puddled, anaerobic field conditions. Field evaluations demonstrated that the developed machine effectively restricts nutrient losses, delivering a continuous, controlled nutrient release profile. Consequently, the deep placement technology exhibited a marked improvement in rice grain yield, registering an increase of approximately 20–30%. Concurrently, it achieved a 20–25% reduction in total fertilizer consumption compared to conventional broadcasting, with significant reductions in labor drudgery. This study explores the mechanical design, operational principles, agronomic benefits and economic viability of the developed applicator, proposing it as a sustainable solution for precision rice cultivation.
