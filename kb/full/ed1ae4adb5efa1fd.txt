Abstract The North Atlantic phytoplankton bloom (NAB) forms the base of the food web upon which marine ecosystem services throughout the northern North Atlantic rely. The NAB's nutrient supply is predominantly driven by deep winter mixing, which is most pronounced in the subpolar gyre where it has been projected to abruptly decline within the next few decades. Here we use idealized experiments with an Earth system model to investigate how mixed layer depth, surface nutrients and chlorophyll concentrations over the gyre may respond to positive carbon emissions followed by both zero and negative emissions mitigation efforts initiated at various Global Warming Levels (GWLs).
