Accelerated urbanization and land reclamation have caused widespread loss of coastal wetlands, reduced habitat availability and increased migration costs for shorebirds. However, the interactive effects of climate change and land-use transitions on habitat networks remain insufficiently understood, especially in rapidly developing bay regions. We developed a scenario-based framework to disentangle climate and land-use impacts on shorebird habitat networks. Integrating species distribution models, landscape connectivity assessments and circuit theory, we mapped potential habitat networks in the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area and projected dynamics under three scenarios: Current Trend Scenario (CTS), Sustainable Development Scenario (SDS), and Ecological Protection Scenario (EPS) for 2030-2070. Each scenario included two sub-scenarios to compare climate change and land-use effects. Results show that EPS initially expands habitat network over a short period but declines thereafter; SDS shows a steady decline throughout the projection period; and CTS generates the most severe habitat loss. Comparison of sub-scenarios revealed that land-use change influences climate impacts based on development pathways. Under EPS and SDS, incorporating land-use change enhanced habitat connectivity in early decades, whereas under CTS, land-use change consistently accelerated habitat loss. Our findings reveal that climate and land-use change jointly drive irreversible degradation of shorebird habitat networks, characterized by patch loss, corridor simplification, and network collapse. While ecologically oriented land-use can slow this trajectory and sustain connectivity in the short term, it cannot offset long-term climate pressures. This demonstrates the need for more ambitious conservation measures that integrate climate mitigation and land-use adaptation to sustain habitat networks under global change.
