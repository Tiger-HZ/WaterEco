Nutrient availability differs among habitats and can promote evolutionary divergence in metabolic traits. For example, freshwater habitats are typically deficient in polyunsaturated fatty acids, such as docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), and freshwater fishes often exhibit a higher capacity for DHA biosynthesis from precursor fatty acids than marine fishes. However, it remains unknown whether freshwater and marine fishes differ in the patterns of lipid deposition or in the utilization of fatty acids such as DHA. In this study, we tested whether marine and stream ecotypes of the three-spined stickleback (Gasterosteus aculeatus) have diverged in lipid deposition strategies and DHA utilization.
