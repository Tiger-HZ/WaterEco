Microcystis aeruginosa is a dominant species of blooming cyanobacteria that causes vegetation damage in eutrophic waters, and M. aeruginosa exudate (MaE) impacts root development of plants. To investigate the underlying mechanisms, we used MaE to treat Arabidopsis thaliana auxin transport/signal transduction proteins GUS marker lines and mutation lines, and compared their root development with that of HGZ-treated plants. The results revealed that MaE increased lateral root development while inhibiting primary root elongation, but this effect diminished when the MaE concentration decreased to less than 5 × 10⁵ cells/ml. An analysis of the stages of lateral root development revealed that MaE significantly accelerated stage II development, which indicated that auxin transport and signal transduction of auxin in vascular bundles were sensitive to MaE and that PIN1, PIN7, and ARF7 played critical roles in these processes. The auxin efflux carriers PIN1, PIN2, PIN3, and PIN7 and the auxin signal transcription factors ARF7 and ARF19 mediated MaE-induced lateral root development. In the reporter lines pPIN1:GUS , pARF7:GUS , and pLBD16:GUS , the accumulation of PIN1 and ARF7 in lateral root primordia and the content of ARF7 in root tips was increased following exposure to MaE. The qRT-PCR results not only confirmed the above findings but also revealed a significant increase in PIN5 expression induced by MaE. In conclusion, MaE promoted lateral root development by modulating long-distance rootward auxin transport, local spatial redistribution, and downstream signal transduction pathways. This study provides new insights into the impact of cyanobacteria on plant root growth and provides a foundation for understanding the ecological risks of cyanobacterial blooms to agricultural production and crop health.
