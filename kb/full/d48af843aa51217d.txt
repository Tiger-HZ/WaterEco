The dual challenge of escalating carbon emissions and increasing aquatic pollution demands integrated biotechnological solutions capable of addressing both environmental and resource recovery goals. Microalgae have emerged as promising candidates for sustainable carbon sequestration and wastewater treatment. This chapter explores the role of microalgae in bioremediating aquatic pollutants, including heavy metals, pesticides, and industrial effluents, while simultaneously contributing to carbon fixation and biomass production. The integration of microalgal cultivation with wastewater valorization is discussed in the context of circular bioeconomy principles, highlighting opportunities for resource recovery in the form of biofertilizers, biofuels, and bioplastics. Furthermore, the chapter assesses the environmental safety and sustainability of using algal biomass derived from polluted waters. This work provides a holistic view of microalgae-driven environmental biotechnology as a key pathway toward water sustainability and carbon neutrality.
