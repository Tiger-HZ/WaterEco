Abstract East Asian cold surges occasionally propagate deep into the tropics and develop into cross-equatorial northerly surges (CENS) over the western Maritime Continent. Previous studies showed close association between the cross-equatorial propagation of cold surge and cold sea surface temperatures (SSTs) in the South China Sea (SCS). However, it remains unclear whether these cold SSTs are merely a response to cold surges or actively influence their propagation and evolution into CENS. This study addresses this question using numerical experiments on several cold surges under cooler and warmer SST conditions.
