Super nano urea and sulphur nutrition enhance nutrient 
use efficiency and profitability of greengram for 
sustainable production

Abstract
Improving nutrient-use efficiency while maintaining crop productivity and profitability is essential for sustain -
able greengram. Super Nano Urea (SNU), combined with balanced sulphur nutrition, improve nutrient acqui -
sition and fertilizer-use efficiency while reducing dependence on conventional nitrogen fertilizers. Therefore, 
a two-year field experiment was conducted during 2024 and 2025 to evaluate different nitrogen, sulphur and 
foliar SNU management strategies for improving growth, productivity, nutrient uptake, fertilizer-use efficiency 
and profitability of irrigated greengram. The treatment receiving 100% of the recommended Nitrogen, Phos -
phorus and Potassium together with 100% recommended sulphur produced the highest seed yield (1305 kg 
ha− 1) and protein yield (314 kg ha− 1), exceeding the treatment receiving 75% of the nitrogen with sulphur @ 
15 kg ha − 1 and two foliar sprays of SNU @ 8 mL L − 1 by 2.2% and 2.6%, respectively. In contrast, reduc -
ing basal nitrogen by 25% and supplementing it with sulphur and foliar SNU increased nitrogen and sulphur 
agronomic efficiency by 23.3% and 31.1%, respectively, and nitrogen and sulphur partial factor productivity 
by 23.9% and 32.1%, respectively, compared with 100% recommended NPK + sulphur treatment. Economi -
cally, 100% recommended NPK without additional sulphur recorded the highest net returns and benefit ratio, 
whereas the reduced-nitrogen SNU treatment generated economically competitive returns with substantially 
greater fertilizer-use efficiency. These findings demonstrate that full recommended fertilization with sulphur is 
advantageous when the primary objective is maximizing yield, whereas partial substitution of basal nitrogen 
with foliar SNU can improve nutrient-use efficiency while maintaining comparable productivity. From a farmer 
perspective, the choice of nutrient management should therefore consider the balance among yield, input costs, 
profitability and fertilizer-use efficiency. Integrating SNU with balanced sulphur nutrition provides a promising 
strategy for improving nitrogen-use efficiency and reducing dependence on conventional nitrogen fertilizer in 
sustainable greengram production.

Introduction
Greengram (Vigna radiata L.) is an important short-duration pulse crop in India, contributing to dietary protein 
security, cropping-system diversification and soil fertility. Despite its importance, productivity remains relatively 
low at the national and state levels1. Greengram occupied approximately 1.59 million ha in India, with a produc-
tion of 3.74 million tonnes and average productivity of only 570 kg ha-1; Karnataka, one of the major greengram-
producing states, recorded an average productivity of 623 kg ha -12. This substantial productivity gap highlights 
the need for improved crop and nutrient management practices. Nutrient management in pulse-based systems 
is frequently constrained by inadequate or imbalanced fertilizer use and limited consideration of soil nutrient 
variability, which can reduce nutrient recovery and fertilizer-use efficiency3. Recent field evidence further dem-
onstrates that balanced sulphur fertilization with recommended NPK can substantially improve mungbean yield 
and nutrient uptake, emphasizing the importance of synchronizing N and S supply4.
Nitrogen is one of the most important nutrients regulating crop growth, photosynthesis and yield formation in 
pulse crops. However, conventional nitrogen fertilizers often exhibit low recovery efficiency because of substan-
tial losses through volatilization, leaching and denitrification5. Excessive and inefficient use of nitrogen fertilizers 
not only increases production costs but also contributes to environmental degradation and greenhouse gas emis-
sions. Improving nitrogen-use efficiency has therefore become a major priority in sustainable crop production 
systems6. Recently, nano-fertilizers have emerged as innovative nutrient delivery systems capable of enhancing 
nutrient availability and uptake efficiency while minimizing nutrient losses7. Unlike traditional fertilizers, which 
often release nutrients rapidly irrespective of crop demand, nano fertilizers are designed to synchronize nutrient 
availability with plant physiological requirements, thereby improving nutrient uptake and overall fertilizer-use 
efficiency8–10. Nano urea formulations contain nanoscale nitrogen particles that facilitate rapid foliar absorption, 
controlled nutrient release and efficient translocation within plant tissues, while reducing losses associated with 
soil-applied nitrogen fertilizers 6. Several recent studies have demonstrated that foliar application of nano urea 
improves chlorophyll synthesis, photosynthetic efficiency, nutrient uptake and yield performance in cereals and 
pulse crops11–13. Nevertheless, crop response to nano-fertilizers varies depending on crop species, environmental 
conditions and integrated nutrient management practices14,15; Nandhakumar et al., 2024).
Alongside nitrogen, sulphur has emerged as a critical nutrient limiting pulse productivity in many agricultural 
soils due to continuous nutrient mining, declining use of sulphur-containing fertilizers, and intensive cropping 
practices3. Sulphur is another essential nutrient involved in chlorophyll synthesis, amino acid formation, protein 
metabolism and enzymatic activity. In legumes, sulphur plays a crucial role in nodulation and biological nitrogen 
fixation16. Sulphur deficiency has become increasingly widespread in intensively cultivated soils due to con -
tinuous use of high-analysis fertilizers lacking sulphur. Balanced application of sulphur with nitrogen has been 
reported to enhance nutrient assimilation, protein synthesis and productivity in pulse crops 1,17–19. Consequently, 
sulphur nutrition regulates both the quantity and quality of seed protein, while simultaneously improving photo-
synthetic efficiency, chlorophyll stability, and antioxidant defence mechanisms20,21.
The interaction between nitrogen and sulphur nutrition has attracted increasing scientific attention because 
both nutrients are metabolically interconnected. Balanced nitrogen and sulphur nutrition improves chlorophyll 
formation, photosynthetic efficiency, enzyme activation, biological nitrogen fixation, and the synthesis of proteins 
and secondary metabolites16,22. Consequently, integrated nitrogen-sulphur management has been recognized as 
an effective approach for enhancing nutrient-use efficiency, crop productivity, and quality in legumes23,24. Recent 
investigations have highlighted that balanced N-S nutrition not only increases yield but also improves nutrient 
recovery efficiency and sustainability of pulse-based production systems17,25,26.
Although previous studies have evaluated the individual effects of nano urea or sulphur nutrition in pulse crops, 
limited information is available regarding the combined application of Super Nano Urea (SNU) and sulphur 
under reduced nitrogen fertilization. In particular, the recently developed Super Nano Urea (20% N w/v), which 
possesses enhanced nutrient concentration and improved delivery characteristics compared with earlier nano urea

effects, multivariate analytical approaches to elucidate the mechanisms through which nano-enabled nitrogen 
delivery enhances crop performance. The study specifically examined whether foliar-applied Super Nano Urea 
could compensate reduced basal nitrogen application while sustaining productivity, nutrient uptake, seed qual -
ity and profitability. The findings provide important insights into sustainable nutrient management strategies for 
improving fertilizer-use efficiency and reducing fertilizer dependency in pulse-based production systems.
Materials and methods
Experimental site
A field experiment was conducted during the summer seasons of 2024 and 2025 at the Integrated Farming System 
Demonstration Unit, Zonal Agricultural Research Station, University of Agricultural Sciences, Bengaluru, India 
(13°08′ N latitude and 77°58′ E longitude; 924 m above mean sea level). The initial physico-chemical properties 
of the experimental soil were provided in Supplementary Table S1. Weather parameters recorded during the crop 
growth period are presented in Supplementary Figure S1.
Experimental design and treatments
The field experiment was conducted using a randomized complete block design (RCBD) comprising seventeen 
treatments, each replicated three times. The detailed description of the individual treatments is presented in 
Table 1.
Nutrient management
After land preparation, FYM was uniformly applied to all plots at 7.5 t ha − 1 before sowing to enhance the soil 
organic matter content and improve the nutrient availability to the crop. Phosphorus and potassium were applied 
as basal through single super phosphate (SSP) and muriate of potash (MOP), respectively, while nitrogen and 
sulphur (S) were applied as per the treatment schedule through urea and bentonite sulphur at sowing. Sulphur 
supplied through SSP was accounted for while calculating the sulphur dose. Foliar applications of super nano 
urea and water-soluble bentonite sulphur (90% S) were imposed as per treatments at flowering and pod formation 
stages.
Super nano urea
Super nano urea liquid (20% N) is a second-generation nano nitrogen fertilizer developed from IFFCO Ltd, to 
overcome the limitations of first-generation Nano Urea (4% N). It is designed for enhanced nutrient delivery 
through foliar application.

Crop management
Growth observations, including plant height, number of branches plant-1, above-ground biomass and chlorophyll 
status, were recorded at the appropriate crop growth stage using representative plants selected from the net plot 
area, excluding border plants. Plant height was measured from ground level to the growing tip and expressed in 
cm. Chlorophyll content was measured using a Soil Plant Analysis Development meter and expressed as SPAD 
units. Dry matter production was determined by oven-drying representative plant samples to constant weight and 
expressed as g plant-1. Yield attributes including number of pods plant-1, pod length, number of seeds pod -1 and 
100-seed weight were recorded from the tagged plants at harvest. Seed and haulm yields were recorded from the 
net plot area and expressed as kg ha -1. Harvest index (%) was calculated as the ratio of seed yield to biological 
yield (seed+haulm yield). The details of the crop variety were presented in Table 1

root nodules plant-1 were not significantly affected (P > 0.05). Among the treatments, T17 (100% RDF + 100% sul-
phur) consistently recorded the highest during 2024, 2025 and pooled values for plant height, branches per plant, 
above-ground biomass, root nodules and SPAD value. These values remained statistically at par with T16 (100%

RDF) and T12 (75% RDN + sulphur + two foliar sprays of SNU @ 8 mL L -1) for all significant parameters. Con-
versely, the lowest vegetative growth and physiological performance were observed under T 1 (25% RDN + sul-
phur) (Tables 2 and 3). Relative to T 1, T17 increased plant height, branches per plant, above-ground biomass, 
root nodules and SPAD value by 34.0, 37.5, 49.5, 9.9 and 29.2%, respectively, while the corresponding increases

32.6%, above-ground biomass by 47.5%, root nodules by 6.8% and SPAD value by 27.0% compared with T 1. 
Despite these numerical improvements, differences in root nodulation remained statistically non-significant.
The response to foliar SNU was dose dependent within each basal nitrogen level. Increasing the SNU concen-
tration from 2 to 8 ml L -1 progressively improved plant height, branch production, biomass accumulation and 
SPAD value. At 75% RDN, T 12 (SNU @ 8 mL L -1) produced 8.2% greater plant height, 9.9% more branches, 
16.9% higher above-ground biomass, 1.5% more root nodules and 7.3% higher SPAD value than T 15, which 
received foliar application of conventional urea (10 g L-1) along with water-soluble sulphur. Similarly, though 
less pronounced, responses were also evident under 25% and 50% RDN. Year significantly affected plant height 
and branches plant⁻¹, with higher values in 2025 than 2024, whereas above-ground biomass, root nodules and 
SPAD value were unaffected. The treatment × year interaction was non-significant for all parameters.

The pooled analysis further confirmed the consistency of treatment responses across years (Table 5; Fig. 1).

Discussion
Growth, physiological responses and yield formation
The current study showed that greengram growth, physiological efficiency, nutrient uptake, and yield forma -
tion were all enhanced by the combined application of basal N, S, and foliar SNU. The reduced basal nitrogen 
treatment supplemented with S and foliar SNU remained statistically equivalent, despite the fact that the full 
recommended nutrient treatment with sulphur yielded the best overall performance. This suggests that foliar nano 
nitrogen can partially offset decreased soil-applied N without impairing crop performance when combined with 
sulphur nutrition. Further evidence that the response was linked to nano-enabled nutrient transport rather than 
foliar nitrogen addition alone comes from the SNU-based therapy superiority over the matching traditional foliar 
urea treatment.
The superior performance of T 12 can be attributed to the complementary physiological roles of N and S. 
Nitrogen is the principal constituent of chlorophyll, amino acids and photosynthetic enzymes, whereas S is indis-
pensable for the synthesis of cysteine, methionine, glutathione and iron sulphur proteins that regulate enzyme

observed under T12 and T17 reflect improved photosynthetic capacity and more efficient conversion of absorbed 
nutrients into structural dry matter, consistent with previous reports in pulse crops receiving integrated nano-
nitrogen and sulphur fertilization16; Sharma et al.,2024.
The advantage of T12 over T15, even though the basal N and S levels were the same for both treatments, was a 
significant discovery. This suggests that the addition of foliar nitrogen alone did not cause the response, but rather 
the nano-formulation34. Because of their larger surface area and improved leaf adhesion, nano-urea particles may 
penetrate cuticular and stomatal routes more effectively and gradually deliver N during times when crop need 
is at its highest 35,36. As a consequence, compared to plants receiving traditional foliar urea, those given SNU 
retained higher levels of chlorophyll and photosynthetic activity, which led to increased biomass production and 
reproductive potential.
Improved vegetative growth was subsequently translated into enhanced reproductive development through 
more efficient assimilate partitioning. Greater biomass increased the photosynthetically active source, while sus-
tained N availability during flowering and pod development supported efficient translocation of assimilates to 
developing reproductive sinks9. As a result, compared to lower N treatments, T12 and T17 recorded considerably

these two superior treatments. Delayed leaf senescence, prolonged photosynthetic activity, and enhanced source-
sink connections during grain filling have all been linked to comparable increases in reproductive efficiency after 
foliar nano-fertilizer administration37.
The significance of balanced nitrogen-sulphur feeding is further highlighted by the same productivity of T 12 
and T17. Instead of just enhancing nutritional intake, S improves N assimilation by promoting the synthesis of 
proteins and amino acids, which improves the metabolic use of ingested nitrogen38. According to Bonnot et al.,20 
and Dawar et al.,16, this synergistic connection allows crops to produce more biomass and seed yield even when 
N intake is reduced. The number of root nodules did not differ significantly between treatments, but as sulphur-
containing proteins are essential for biological nitrogen fixation, the minor increase shown under T12 and T17 may 
indicate improved nodule functionality rather than more nodule formation.
Nutrient uptake, fertilizer-use efficiency and protein accumulation
The increased delivery efficiency of nano-formulated nitrogen is responsible for the increased N uptake shown 
under T12. Under field conditions, conventional urea has a relatively low fertilizer recovery owing to significant N 
losses from ammonia volatilization, nitrate leaching, and denitrification. In contrast, foliar-applied SNU provides 
a gradual and synchronized N supply through efficient absorption across the leaf surface, maintaining nutrient 
availability during periods of high physiological demand while minimizing environmental losses 35,36; Mustafa 
et al., 2022.
Recent isotope-tracing studies have shown that nano-enabled fertilization delays nitrogen release, reduces 
ammonia volatilization by around 53%, reduces nitrate leaching by about 39%, and improves N uptake by better 
synchronizing nutrient release and crop demand. These findings give mechanistic support for the present studies 
the better agronomic efficiency6,39. As a result, T12 maintained N intake comparable to T17 despite reduced base-
line N application, suggesting that improved fertilizer recovery compensated for lower N input. Similar gains in 
nutrient recovery following nano-urea application have been documented in pulses, cereals, and oilseed crops, 
where improved N synchronization increased fertilizer-use efficiency while maintaining productivity28,40.
Balanced sulphur diet bolstered this reaction by increasing the metabolic utilization of ingested nitrogen. Sul-
phur is required for the synthesis of S-containing amino acids, glutathione, and many iron-sulphur proteins that 
participate in nitrate reduction, electron transport, and enzyme activation41. As a result, appropriate S availability 
improves N absorption and promotes the conversion of ingested N into functional proteins, rather than allowing it 
to accumulate in non-protein forms20,21. The equivalent S intake found under T12 and T17 suggests that combined 
N and S nutrition improved nutrient metabolism rather than simply boosting fertilizer uptake. Similar synergistic

The superiority of T12 was further confirmed by its higher agronomic efficiency and partial factor productivity. 
The higher values under T12 show that fertilizer recovery and crop response were significantly enhanced by nano-
enabled fertilization, allowing productivity comparable to T17 but requiring significantly less N fertilizer. Accord-
ing to recent syntheses, nano urea should be considered a supplementary foliar technology that can maintain 
productivity when combined with modest reductions (roughly 20–25%) in basal N application rather than a total 
replacement for soil-applied N 43. This new evidence is closely consistent with the current findings. Contrarily, 
although T17 produced the highest absolute yield and nutrient uptake, its efficiency indices were comparatively 
lower because nutrient-use efficiency generally declines as fertilizer application exceeds crop demand44; Mustafa 
et al., 2022. Internal utilization efficiency differed only marginally among the superior treatments, suggesting that 
the principal advantage of T12 was improved nutrient capture and synchronization rather than greater physiologi-
cal utilization after nutrient absorption.
The increased nutrient absorption under T 12 was directly reflected in better protein output, emphasizing the 
importance of balanced N and S nutrition in influencing seed quality. Protein accumulation is dependent not only 
on N absorption, but also on the effective incorporation of N into amino acids and storage proteins, both of which 
require an appropriate supply of sulphur. As a result, T12 produced protein yields statistically equal to T17 despite 
lower basal N, demonstrating that SNU significantly improved N absorption and protein synthesis. The correla -
tion study revealed strong positive relationships between N absorption, S uptake, protein yield, and seed yield, 
indicating that effective nutrient acquisition was a major physiological driver of production.
Multivariate analysis
The multivariate analysis provided strong evidence that the superior performance of integrated SNU and S nutri-
tion resulted from coordinated improvements in physiological processes rather than isolated effects on individual 
traits. While the analysis of variance confirmed significant treatment differences, correlation analysis, principal 
component analysis (PCA), multiple regression, Random Forest and structural equation modelling (SEM) consis-
tently identified above-ground biomass, N uptake, S uptake, seed weight plant-1 and protein yield as the principal 
determinants of seed yield.
The strong positive correlations among SPAD value, biomass, nutrient uptake, protein yield and seed yield 
indicate that enhanced uptake of these nutrients increases photosynthetic efficiency, biomass accumulation and 
assimilate production, ultimately supporting greater pod development and seed filling. Raliya et al.,10, Sharma,28. 
The clustering of T12, T16 and T17 within the positive quadrant of the PCA biplot indicates that these treatments 
shared a common physiological response characterized by improved nutrient acquisition, greater biomass pro -
duction and enhanced reproductive performance. In contrast, treatments with reduced nitrogen inputs or standard 
foliar urea were separated from this group due to lower physiological efficiency and productivity. Similar PCA 
uses in agronomic research have demonstrated that multivariate ordination effectively separates nutrient man -
agement techniques by combining multiple physiological and agronomic characteristics into a single analytical 
framework Olivoto and Lucio45,46.
Multiple regression revealed that AGBM and seed weight plant -1 were the strongest positive predictors of 
seed output, lending credence to the classical source-sink model, in which vegetative tissues serve as the primary 
source of assimilates and growing seeds serve as the key sink. The negative coefficient for pods plant -1 after 
accounting for other predictors should not be interpreted as a biological disadvantage. It reflects statistical adjust-
ment among highly correlated explanatory variables, which is a common outcome in multivariate regression 
involving closely related yield components. These data confirm that biomass production and assimilate partition-
ing, rather than a single yield feature, determine final seed productivity (Mustafa et al. 2022).
Random Forest studies confirmed that protein yield, pod length, pods plant⁻¹, N uptake, S uptake, seed weight 
plant⁻¹, and biomass are the most significant factors influencing seed production. Random Forest, unlike standard

regression, allows for complex, nonlinear interactions among predictors, resulting in a more realistic portrayal

efficiency under diverse management systems47–49.
The SEM indicated that biomass accumulation exerted a strong positive influence on seed yield directly and 
indirectly through seed weight plant -1, confirming that enhanced photosynthetic capacity increased the avail -
ability of assimilates required for seed filling. These pathways demonstrate that the beneficial effects of SNU 
extended beyond simple nutrient supplementation and involved coordinated improvements in physiological pro-
cesses governing carbon assimilation, nutrient metabolism and assimilate partitioning50,51.
Economics
The economic response of greengram closely followed the productivity pattern of the nutrient management treat-
ments. The treatment with 100% RDF + sulphur @ 20 kg ha-1 (T17) gave the highest gross returns due to the maxi-
mum seed yield. However, the maximum biological production under T 17 did not always equate to the highest 
economic efficiency, as the added cost of sulphur and full fertilizer input raised the total cost of agriculture. T16, 
which consisted of 100% RDF alone, had the highest B: C since it delivered a high yield at a lower input cost 
than T17. This suggests that maximum yield and maximum profitability may not necessarily be achieved under 
the same conditions. While T17 provided a higher gross yield, T16 was more economically efficient due to lower 
spending on additional S fertilizer.
The treatment T12, which reduced basal N input by 25% while maintaining seed and protein yields comparable 
to T17 and T16, had great economic potential despite the extra costs associated with Super Nano Urea and foliar 
spray operations. As a result, T12 is a resource-efficient nutrient management solution that can lessen reliance on 
traditional N fertilizer without significantly lowering production. When compared to T 15, which included 75% 
RDN + foliar application of conventional urea @ 10 g L -1+ water-soluble sulphur @ 5 g L -1 during the flower-
ing and pod formation stages, the economic benefit of T12 becomes evident. Both T12 and T15 received the same 
basal N level, but T 12 produced higher yield and better returns, indicating that nano-enabled nitrogen delivery 
was more effective than conventional foliar urea. This improvement may be attributed to better foliar absorption, 
synchronized nitrogen availability, improved nutrient uptake and enhanced assimilate partitioning during repro-
ductive stages10,40,52.
Conclusion
The present study demonstrated that integrating Super Nano Urea (SNU) with balanced N and S nutrition signifi-
cantly improved growth, physiological performance, fertilizer-use efficiency, protein yield and seed productivity 
of greengram. Although the recommended fertilizer practice100% RDF + sulphur (T 17) produced the highest 
numerical values, the application of 75% RDN + sulphur @ 15 kg ha-1 supplemented with two foliar sprays of 
Super Nano Urea @ 8 mL L-1 (T12) achieved statistically comparable performance while reducing basal N appli-
cation by 25%. This treatment also recorded superior agronomic efficiency and partial factor productivity, indi -
cating that improved fertilizer-use efficiency rather than increased fertilizer input was responsible for enhanced 
crop performance. Multivariate analyses consistently identified biomass accumulation, N uptake, S uptake and 
seed weight plant-1 as the principal determinants of seed yield, confirming that balanced nutrient acquisition and 
efficient assimilate partitioning governed productivity and integrating foliar Super Nano Urea with balanced S 
nutrition provides an effective strategy for maintaining high productivity while reducing dependence on conven-
tional N fertilizers. Therefore, the application of 75% RDN + sulphur @ 15 kg ha⁻¹ with two foliar sprays of Super
