Abstract The construction industry faces mounting pressure to address complex interdependencies between water, energy, and food systems, yet construction management education has been slow to prepare graduates for the AI-enhanced, systems-level decision-making these challenges demand. This study investigates the pedagogical foundations and curricular requirements for preparing construction managers to leverage artificial intelligence in Water-Energy-Food (WEF) Nexus decision-making contexts. Through a systematic review of recent literature on AI applications in construction education, WEF Nexus decision-support tools, and sustainability-oriented curriculum frameworks, the research identifies significant gaps between emerging industry expectations and current educational practices.
