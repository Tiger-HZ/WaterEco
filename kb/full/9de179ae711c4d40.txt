Abstract Invasive plant species can alter belowground biogeochemical processes, but the specific dynamics and mechanisms associated with these shifts remain unclear. We compared the effects of aqueous root extracts from the invasive Impatiens glandulifera and the native Urtica dioica on the carbon processing and functional stability of non-invaded riparian soil. In a 21-day laboratory incubation, we amended soil with two concentration levels of root extracts to simulate root lysis and exudation. We assessed microbial responses by measuring respiration rates (MicroResp™) and enzyme activities, while the stoichiometric properties of the root extracts were characterized by dissolved carbon (DC), nitrogen (DN), and total phenolics (TPC).
