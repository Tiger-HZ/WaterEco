Indoor air quality (IAQ) has become a critical environmental health concern, particularly in rapidly urbanizing cities where outdoor pollution infiltrates residential environments. This study assessed indoor air quality in selected residential buildings in the Gyaneshwor area of Kathmandu Valley using a low-cost PM2.5 sensor combined with an occupant questionnaire survey. Real-time measurements of PM2.5 concentration, temperature, and relative humidity were conducted in kitchens and living rooms of four residential houses over seven consecutive days during winter. A structured questionnaire survey involving 15 occupants were conducted to examine awareness of air quality, ventilation practices, and health symptoms related to indoor exposure.
