This chapter investigates the application of the Quality 4.0 concept for urban systems by analyzing a selection of smart city case studies as well as identifying the systemic mechanisms that tend to limit their performance. It applies a socio-technical perspective considering the city as a complex multi-actor system, quality arising through interplays of data, digital technologies and governance mechanism. Using examples from pioneering cities like Singapore, Barcelona, Dubai, and selected continuous improvement mechanisms in Central and Eastern Europe Katowice, Tallin, Brno, and the Global South (Medellín, Kigali, Nairobi, and Chennai) this chapter illustrates how Quality 4.0 principles are translated into practice through data-driven governance, digital platform integration, and continuous quasi automatic monitoring of targets and corrective action mechanisms. In parallel, the analysis highlights the systemic failure mechanisms constantly repeating themselves such as fragmentation of data, low interoperability between information systems, limited integration of process, ethical risks posed by algorithmic decisions, and digital inequalities lowering access to public services. The results show that Quality 4.0 implementation in urban systems is highly contextual and influenced by technological maturity, institutional capacity, and the effectiveness of governance arrangements.
