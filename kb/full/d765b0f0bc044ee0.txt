The sustainability of urban development is increasingly contingent on the evolution of water infrastructure systems from linear, resource-intensive models to integrated, circular frameworks. This chapter analyzes the infrastructural components essential for embedding circular economy principles into urban water systems, focusing on technological innovations, institutional reforms, and environmental integration. Emphasis is placed on the systemic transition required to ensure resilient, efficient, and sustainable urban water management in alignment with global environmental and development agendas. Urban water management systems can create valuable synergies with other sectors, fostering broader sustainability and resource optimization. Decentralized systems offer an innovative approach to urban water management, focusing on treating wastewater closer to its source. The sustainable management of urban water resources necessitates a paradigm shift from linear extraction–use–discharge models toward integrated circular frameworks. Nature-Based Treatment Systems, particularly constructed wetlands, have emerged as cornerstone technologies for integrating circular economy principles into wastewater management.
