Title of the manuscript: Stage-specific prediction of body weight in Hanwoo steers using 
body measurements and nutrient intake variables 
 
ABSTRACT 
Objective: Accurate body weight ( BW) prediction is important for growth management and nutrient 
utilization in Hanwoo steers. However, prediction performance may vary across growth stages and input 
variables. Therefore, this study evaluated stage -specific machine learning models for BW prediction 
using body measurements and nutrient intake variables.This study investigated stage-dependent factors 
associated with body weight (B W) and average daily gain (ADG) and evaluated the predictive 
performance of machine learning (ML) models using body size traits and nutrient intake variables in 
Hanwoo steers. 
Methods: Data from 136 Hanwoo steers were collected during the growing (6–12 months), early 
fattening (13–21 months), and late fattening (22–31 months) periods. Body size traits and nutrient intake 
variables were used to develop prediction models using Random Forest (RF), Linear Regression (LR), 
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). To minimize repeated -
measurement bias, datasets were divided into training and testing sets based on individual animal 
identification. Prediction performance was evaluated using coefficient of determination (R²), root mean 
square error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE). 
Results: BW showed strong associations with skeletal -related body traits during the growing period, 
whereas nutrient intake variables became more strongly assoc iated with average daily gain ( ADG) 
during the fattening periods. Among the evaluated ML models, RF generally demonstrated robust 
predictive performance across growth stages. For BW prediction, RF achieved the highest prediction 
accuracy during the growing period (R² = 0.952), whereas LR achieved the highest prediction accuracy 
during the early-fattening period (R² = 0.947). Prediction performance for ADG was generally lower 
than that for BW. Variable importance analysis indicated that skeletal -related body traits contributed 
most to BW prediction, whereas nutrient intake variables contributed more to ADG prediction during 
fattening. 
Conclusion: Factors associated with BW and ADG in Hanwoo steers differed according to production 
stage. Skeletal-related body traits were more strongly associated with BW during the growing period, 
whereas nutrient intake variables became increasingly important during the fattening stages. The present 
findings suggest that integrating body measurements and nutritional information may contribute to 
stage-specific growth monitoring and provide preliminary information for developing nutritional 
management strategies in Hanwoo production systems.

INTRODUCTION 
Accurate prediction of growth performance is important for improving productivity, feed efficiency, 
and economic profitability in beef cattle production systems. In Hanwoo production, body weight (BW) 
and average daily gain (ADG) are major indicators used for nutritional management and production 
efficiency because Hanwoo cattle are characterized by relatively long fattening periods and high 
production costs [1] . Therefore, precise growth monitoring is essential for efficient management and 
sustainable beef production. 
Conventional evaluation of cattle growth generally relies on direct BW measurements using livestock 
scales. However, repeated weighing procedures are labor -intensive, time-consuming, and may cause 
stress to animals during handling. In addition , conventional approaches may not adequately reflect 
dynamic growth changes occurring throughout different fattening stages. Consequently, interest has 
increased in precision livestock management approaches capable of predicting growth performance 
using easily measurable phenotypic and nutritional variables. 
Recent studies have reported that machine learning (ML) approaches can be effectively applied for 
predicting cattle growth traits using body measurements and feeding information [2, 3]. In Hanwoo 
cattle, body size traits have shown significant relationships with growth and carcass characteristics [4, 
5]. In addition, nutrient intake and feed efficiency are closely associated with growth performance 
during the fattening period [6, 7] . However, most previo us studies have primarily focused on overall 
growth prediction without considering biological differences among growth stages [8]. 
Growth mechanisms in beef cattle vary according to production stage. Skeletal and muscular 
development are dominant during th e growing period, whereas nutrient utilization efficiency and fat 
deposition become increasingly important during the fattening period. Therefore, factors associated 
with BW and ADG may differ according to growth stage, and stage-specific prediction approaches may 
improve growth prediction accuracy and biological interpretation. 
Despite increasing interest in ML-based growth prediction in beef cattle, most previous studies have 
primarily focused on overall prediction performance using pooled datasets withou t considering stage-
dependent physiological changes throughout the production cycle. In addition, limited studies have

MATERIALS AND METHODS 
Animals and experimental design 
A total of 136 Hanwoo steers were used in this study. Animals were managed under commercial feeding 
conditions at the Hanwoo Research Institute, National Institute of Animal Science, Republic of Korea. 
Repeated measurements of BW, ADG, body size traits, an d nutrient intake variables were collected 
throughout the experimental period. 
To evaluate stage-dependent growth characteristics, animals were classified into three production stages 
according to age: growing period (6–12 months), early fattening period (13–21 months), and late 
fattening period (22–31 months). Growth performance, bo dy measurements, and nutrient intake 
variables were evaluated to identify stage-specific factors associated with growth and to develop ML-
based prediction models for BW and ADG. 
 
Measurement of body weight and body size traits 
Body weight was measured mon thly before morning feeding using an electronic livestock scale 
throughout the experimental period. Body size traits were measured according to standard livestock 
measurement procedures at 2 -month intervals throughout the experimental period. Measured trai ts 
included withers height (WH), hip height (HH), body length (BL), chest depth (CD), chest width (CW), 
rump width (RW), pelvic width (PW), hip width (HW), rump length (RL), and chest girth (CG), as 
illustrated in Figure 1. All measurements were obtained b y trained personnel using standardized

measuring tools to minimize measurement variability. Because body size traits were measured at 2 -
month intervals rather than at every BW recording, the number of observations for body measurements 
was lower than that for BW records. 
 
Nutrient intake analysis 
Feed intake and nutrient composition were used to calculate dry matter intake (DMI), organic matter 
intake (OMI), crude protein intake (CPI), ether extract intake (EEI), neutral detergent fiber intake 
(NDFI), acid detergent fiber intake (ADFI), ash intake (AshI), total digestible nutrient intake (TDNI), 
and non-fiber carbohydrate intake (NFCI). Chemical analyses were conducted according to AOAC [9], 
and fiber fractions were analyzed using the method of Van Soest et al. [10]. Feed conversion ratio (FCR) 
was calculated as the ratio of DMI to ADG and was used only for correlation analysis and biological 
interpretation. FCR was excluded from ADG prediction models to avoid target leakage because FCR is 
mathematically associated with ADG. ADG was calculated based on BW changes between consecutive 
measurement intervals. Therefore, the number of ADG observations was lower than that of BW records 
because the initial BW measurements could not be used for ADG calculation. Nutrient intake variables 
used for ADG prediction were calculated over the same measurement interval used to determine ADG, 
ensuring temporal consistency between predictor and response variables. 
 
Machine learning models 
Four ML algorithms, including Random Forest (RF), Linear Regression (LR), K -Nearest Neighbors 
(KNN), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), were applied to predict BW and ADG in Hanwoo steers. 
Input variables included body size traits (WH, HH, BL, CD, CW, RW, PW, HW, RL, and CG) and 
nutrient intake variables (DMI, OMI, CPI, NDFI, ADFI, EEI, AshI, TDNI, and NFCI). Prior to model 
development, observations containing missing values were excluded. The final numbers of unique 
animals and observations included in each production stage are summarized in Supplementary Tables

To minimize information leakage associated with repeated measureme nts, datasets were divided into 
training and testing sets based on individual animal identification. Repeated observations from the same 
animal were not simultaneously included in both datasets. The dataset was randomly divided into 
training (70%) and testing (30%) sets at the animal level using a fixed random seed of 42. This approach 
was selected to ensure complete independence between the training and testing datasets while 
preserving sufficient training data for model development under the relatively li mited sample size 
available at each production stage. 
Hyperparameter optimization was conducted only within the training dataset to avoid information 
leakage during model development. For RF models, hyperparameters including the number of trees 
(n_estimators = 100–500), maximum tree depth (max_depth = 5 –20), and minimum samples required 
for node splitting (min_samples_split = 2 –10) were optimized using grid search procedures based on 
minimum mean squared error (MSE). 
For KNN models, the optimal number of ne ighbors (k = 2 –20) was selected according to minimum 
MSE within the training dataset using Euclidean distance metrics. 
ANN models were developed using multilayer perceptron structures consisting of fully connected 
hidden layers. The ANN architecture included one hidden layer with 100 neurons, rectified linear unit 
activation functions, Adam optimizer, learning rate of 0.001, batch size of 32, and a maximum of 500 
training epochs. Early stopping procedures were applied to reduce overfitting during model trai ning. 
Unlike RF and KNN, the ANN architecture and training parameters were predefined and were not 
subjected to additional hyperparameter optimization. 
Model development, preprocessing, hyperparameter optimization, and prediction analyses were 
conducted using Python software (version 3.11; Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE, USA) 
with pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, matplotlib, and seaborn libraries. 
Although repeated longitudinal observations within animals were retained to preserve biological growth

Model evaluation 
Prediction performance was evaluated using coefficient of determination (R² ), root mean square error 
(RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE). The best-performing model was selected based on the highest 
R² together with the lowest RMSE and MAE values. 
R² was calculated as: 
R² = 1 − (Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)² / Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)²) 
where yᵢ represents observed values, ŷᵢ represents predicted values, and ȳ represents the mean observed 
value. 
Lower RMSE and MAE values indicated improved prediction accuracy and reduced prediction error. 
 
Statistical analysis 
Descriptive statistics, correlation analyses, and ML analyses were conducted using Python software 
(version 3.11; Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE, USA). 
Pearson correlation analysis was performed as a descriptive assessment of pairwise linear associations 
among variables. Because repeated long itudinal observations from the same animals were retained, 
these correlations reflect both between -animal differences and within -animal changes over time and 
therefore should not be interpreted as evidence of independent biological relationships or causal effects. 
Data preprocessing, model development, hyperparameter optimization, and visualization were 
performed using pandas, NumPy, scikit -learn, matplotlib, and seaborn libraries. Prior to correlation 
analyses, variable distributions and outliers were visually inspected using histograms and boxplots.

Random Forest variable importance analysis 
RF variable importance analysis revealed clear stage -dependent differences in factors associated with 
BW and ADG prediction (Figure 5). 
For BW prediction during the growing period, skeletal-related body measurements showed the greatest 
importance, particularly CG, BL, and HH . Similar patterns were observed during the early and late 
fattening periods, where CG consistently remained the most influential variable for BW prediction. 
In contrast, nutrient intake variables showed relatively greater importance for ADG prediction. During 
the growing period, EEI, NDFI, and TDNI contributed substantially to ADG prediction. During the 
early and late fattening periods, TDNI, CPI, ADFI, and NDFI showed relatively high importance. 
These findings suggest that BW prediction is primarily associated with skeletal and body frame 
development, whereas ADG prediction is more strongly influenced by nutrient utiliza tion and dietary 
composition, particularly during the fattening stages. 
 
DISCUSSION 
The present study suggests that stage -dependent physiological and nutritional changes should be 
considered when developing ML-based growth prediction models for Hanwoo steers. 
Unlike previous studies that primarily focused on overall prediction accuracy using pooled datasets, the

present study evaluated stage -dependent shifts in factors associated with growth by integrating body 
measurements and nutrient intake variables under repeated-measurement conditions. 
Prediction performance for BW was generally high during the growing and early fattening periods, 
particularly in the RF, LR, and KNN models. These findings suggest that BW can be estimated relatively 
reliably using body size traits and nutrient intake variables in Hanwoo steers. The strong prediction 
performance observed during the growing stage may be associated with the relatively stable relationship 
between skeletal development and BW during early growth. During t his period, body growth is 
primarily characterized by increases in frame size, muscle development, and structural tissue accretion 
rather than excessive fat deposition. Consequently, body measurements such as chest girth, body length, 
and hip height tend to show strong linear or nonlinear relationships with BW. 
Previous studies have similarly reported significant associations between body measurements and 
growth traits in Hanwoo cattle and other beef breeds. Park and Park [4] and Choy et al. [5] reported that 
chest girth and body length were strongly associated with BW and growth performance in Hanwo o 
cattle, while Firdaus et al. [1 2] demonstrated that body measurements could effectively estimate BW 
across multiple beef cattle breeds. Recent advances in precision livestock farming have also increased 
interest in ML -based BW prediction systems as practical tools for real -time animal monitoring and 
feeding management [1, 13]. The relatively high prediction accuracy observed in the present study 
therefore supports the applicability of ML approaches for practical growth monitoring in commercial 
Hanwoo production systems. 
In contrast to BW prediction, prediction performance for ADG was substantially lower across all 
evaluated models and growth stages. This result s uggests that short -term growth performance is 
influenced by more complex and variable biological processes than BW alone. Unlike BW, which 
reflects cumulative growth over time, ADG is highly sensitive to short -term fluctuations in nutrient 
intake, rumen fe rmentation characteristics, metabolic efficiency, environmental stress, animal health 
status, and behavioral variation. Consequently, predicting ADG under practical production conditions 
is inherently more challenging. 
The relatively lower prediction performance during the late fattening period may also reflect increased

 13 
biological variability associated with adipose tissue deposition and nutrient partitioning during finishing 
growth. As cattle approach market weight, growth efficiency generally declines be cause a greater 
proportion of dietary energy is directed toward fat deposition rather than lean tissue accretion. In 
Hanwoo production systems, late fattening is particularly associated with intensive marbling 
development, which involves substantial metabo lic variation among individuals. These physiological 
changes may reduce the consistency of growth responses and increase prediction error for ADG. Cho 
et al. [7] similarly reported that feed efficiency traits in Hanwoo steers showed variable repeatability 
across growing and fattening periods, indicating that physiological responses to nutrient intake differ 
dynamically throughout the production cycle. In addition, nutrient digestibility and rumen fermentation 
characteristics are known to substantially influence growth responses during different production stages 
[14]. 
Among the evaluated ML models, RF generally demonstrated robust and consistent predictive 
performance across production stages. However, LR achieved the highest BW prediction accuracy 
during th e early - and late -fattening stages, suggesting that linear relationships between body 
measurements, nutrient intake variables, and BW remained strong during these production periods. RF 
models are capable of effectively capturing nonlinear relationships and interactions among explanatory 
variables while remaining relatively robust to multicollinearity, outliers, and noisy field data [11]. 
Because growth performance in beef cattle is i nfluenced by multiple interacting biological and 
nutritional factors, the ability of RF models to accommodate complex relationships may explain their 
superior performance in the present study. 
Previous livestock studies have similarly reported that RF mode ls provide relatively robust prediction 
accuracy for animal growth and production traits under commercial production conditions. Ruchay et 
al. [2] reported that RF-based approaches effectively predicted livestock body traits under variable field 
environments, while Duwalage et al. [3] demonstrated that RF models outperformed several 
conventional approaches for cattle weight prediction. In the present study, the consistent performance 
of RF across production stages suggests that ensemble-based ML approaches may be particularly useful 
for practical precision livestock farming applications in Hanwoo systems.

In contrast, ANN showed substantially lower predictive performance than the other evaluated models, 
with negative R² values, indicating performance worse t han a baseline model that predicts the mean 
value of the test dataset. This finding should be interpreted cautiously because ANN performance is 
influenced not only by dataset size and biological variability but also by network architecture, 
hyperparameter configuration, and optimization strategy. In the present study, the ANN architecture 
remained fixed, whereas RF and KNN underwent hyperparameter optimization during model 
development. Therefore, the relatively lower performance observed for ANN may reflect the specific 
modeling framework adopted in this study in addition to the characteristics of the available dataset, 
rather than an inherent limitation of ANN for predicting BW and ADG. Previous livestock studies have 
similarly reported that ANN performance is highly dependent on dataset structure, sample size, and 
model configuration. Future studies incorporating alternative network architectures and more extensive 
hyperparameter optimization may further improve the predictive performance of ANN models for 
growth prediction in Hanwoo steers. The RF variable importance analysis further supported the 
biological differences observed among production stages. During the growing period, skeletal -related 
body measurements, particularly chest girth, body length, and hip height, were the most important 
variables associated with BW prediction. These findings indicate that structural body development is 
closely associated with BW during early growth. Skeletal growth and frame expansion are generally 
prioritized during t he growing stage, and body measurements therefore provide reliable indicators of 
overall growth status. 
In contrast, nutrient intake variables showed relatively greater importance for ADG prediction during 
the fattening periods. Variables associated with dietary energy and nutrient intake, including TDNI, CPI, 
ADFI, and NDFI, contributed substantially to ADG prediction during the fattening stages. These 
findings suggest that nutrient utilization efficiency becomes increasingly important as cattle progress 
into the finishing phase. 
Previous studies have similarly demonstrated that dietary energy concentration and nutrient utilization 
strongly influence growth performance and carcass characteristics in Hanwoo steers [6]. In addition, 
nutrient composition and d ietary energy level influence nutrient utilization efficiency and growth

performance in beef cattle [15]. Recent studies have further shown that total mixed ration composition 
and dietary energy level can substantially affect feed utilization efficiency an d growth performance 
during the fattening period [14, 16, 17]. Therefore, the increased importance of nutrient intake variables 
observed during the fattening stages likely reflects metabolic shifts toward adipose tissue deposition 
and changes in nutrient partitioning associated with finishing growth. 
Collectively, the present findings suggest that factors associated with BW and ADG differ according to 
production stage. Skeletal-related body traits showed stronger associations with BW during the growing 
period, whereas nutrient intake variables contributed relatively more to ADG prediction during the 
fattening periods. Although Pearson correlation analysis showed generally weak pairwise associations 
between nutrient intake variables and ADG across production stages, RF variable importance identified 
several nutrient intake variables, particularly TDNI and NFCI, as important predictors of ADG during 
the fattening periods. Unlike Pearson correlation, which evaluates pairwise linear relationships, RF 
variable imp ortance reflects the relative contribution of predictors within a multivariable nonlinear 
prediction model. Consequently, predictors with relatively weak individual correlations may still 
substantially improve prediction accuracy when considered jointly wi th other variables. These results 
highlight the importance of applying stage -specific approaches when developing growth prediction 
systems and nutritional management strategies for beef cattle. 
From a practical perspective, integrating body measurements and nutritional information may improve 
growth monitoring and feeding management in commercial Hanwoo systems. Stage-specific prediction 
models could support more precise nutritional management by allowing producers to adjust feeding 
strategies according to physiological growth priorities during each production stage. Such approaches 
may contribute to improved feed efficiency, optimized growth performance, and more sustainable beef 
production systems. 
Nevertheless, several limitations should be considered when interpreting the present findings. First, the 
study was conducted using data collected from a single research environment under relatively uniform 
management and feeding conditions, which may limit the generalizability of the developed models 
under diverse commercial production systems. Therefore, the present models should be interpreted as

exploratory stage-specific prediction frameworks rather than universally applicable prediction systems. 
Second, although repeated longitudinal observations were retained to preserve biological growth 
information and datasets were separated at the animal level to minimize information leakage, model 
performance was evaluated using a single animal -level train –test split. Therefore, prediction 
performance may have been influenced by the specific data partition. Because of the limited number of 
animals available in each production stage, particularly during the late fattening period, grouped cross-
validation or repeated animal-level splitting was not applied in the present study. Future studies using 
larger datasets should evaluate model stability and variability through grouped cross -validation or 
repeated animal-level resampling approaches. Furthermore, because several body size traits and nutrient 
intake variables were correlated with one another, the RF variable importance rankings should be 
interpreted as relative measures of predictor contribution within the prediction model rather than 
independent measures of biological importance. 
Third, the predictive performance for ADG remained relatively limited across growth stages, 
particularly during the late fattening period. This finding likely reflects the complex biological 
variability associated with short -term gro wth responses, including nutrient utilization efficiency, 
metabolic variation, environmental stress, feeding behavior, and health -related factors that were not 
included in the present models. 
Finally, although the present study incorporated body measuremen ts and nutrient intake variables, 
additional physiological and environmental information, including rumen fermentation characteristics, 
blood metabolites, climate-related variables, behavioral traits, and genomic information, may further 
improve prediction accuracy and biological interpretation in future precision livestock farming 
applications. 
Overall, the present study demonstrated that ML-based prediction performance and variable importance 
differed according to production stage in Hanwoo steers. The fi ndings suggest that integrating stage -
specific body measurements and nutritional information may provide preliminary information for 
developing nutritional management and growth management systems in beef cattle production.

CONCLUSION 
The present study demonstrated that ML-based prediction performance for BW and ADG in Hanwoo 
steers differed according to production stage. Skeletal-related body measurements were more strongly 
associated with BW prediction during the growing period, whereas nutrient intake v ariables became 
increasingly important during the fattening stages, reflecting dynamic physiological and nutritional 
changes throughout the production cycle. 
Although LR achieved the highest BW prediction accuracy during the early- and late-fattening stages, 
RF generally demonstrated robust predictive performance across production stages and across both BW 
and ADG prediction tasks. In contrast, ADG prediction accuracy remained limited across models, 
indicating the greater biological complexity and variability associated with short-term growth responses 
under practical production conditions. The relatively lower performance of ANN should be interpreted 
in the context of the modeling framework adopted in the present study, in which the ANN architecture 
remained fixed whereas RF and KNN underwent hyperparameter optimization. Therefore, the observed 
ANN performance should not be interpreted as an inherent limitation of deep learning approaches, and 
further optimization of network architectures and hyperparameters may improve prediction accuracy in 
future studies. 
These findings suggest that integrating stage -specific body measurements and nutritional information 
may facilitate stage -dependent growth evaluation and support future precision feeding and growth 
management studies. However, additional validation under diverse production environments and 
incorporation of physiological, environme ntal, and behavioral variables will be necessary to improve 
model generalizability and prediction accuracy.
