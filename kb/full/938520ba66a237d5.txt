Abstract Sulfate radical-based advanced oxidation processes (SR-AOPs) have emerged as promising and efficient technologies for the removal of antibiotics (ATBs) from aquatic environments due to their ability to generate highly reactive oxygen species and achieve effective contaminant degradation. The increasing persistence of ATBs in water bodies and their associated ecological and human health risks have stimulated extensive research on SR-AOP-based treatment strategies, including sulfate radical-driven oxidation, photocatalysis, electrochemical oxidation, sono-chemical processes, ozonation, and integrated hybrid systems.
