ABSTRACT Crystal quality is essential for high‐performance and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Surface and grain‐boundary defects can induce non‐radiative recombination, while water molecules and high temperatures can degrade perovskite and shorten device lifetime. Dual strategies of defect passivation and interfacial hydrophobicity are crucial for fundamentally boosting device performance. Herein, 12‐Pentafluorophenoxydodecylphosphonic acid (PFPA) is employed as a surface passivator for perovskite films. Its multiple active sites bind under‐coordinated Pb 2+ and fill iodide vacancies, suppressing phase transition, non‐radiative recombination, and extending operational stability.
