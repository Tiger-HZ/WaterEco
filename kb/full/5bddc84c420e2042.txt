Background: Neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPSs) are among the most clinically disruptive manifestations of major neurocognitive disorder (MND) and major contributors to caregiver distress. However, the differential contribution of specific neuropsychiatric domains to caregiver burden and sleep quality has remained insufficiently characterized. Methods: This single-center retrospective observational study included 164 patient–caregiver dyads. Patients were diagnosed with MND according to DSM-5 criteria. NPS were assessed using the Neuropsychiatric Inventory (NPI-Q). Caregiver burden and sleep quality were evaluated using the 12-item Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI-12) and the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), respectively.
