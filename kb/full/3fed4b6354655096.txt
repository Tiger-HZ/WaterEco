The escalating accumulation of microplastics (MPs) in global water systems poses a severe threat to aquatic life and human health, necessitating rapid, high-throughput, and decentralized monitoring solutions. Conventional analytical methods, such as FTIR and Raman spectroscopy, though accurate, are time-consuming, expensive, and require bulky laboratory infrastructure. To bridge this technological gap, this paper presents the design and development of an integrated, portable microfluidic device capable of continuous, label-free separation and real-time quantification of microplastics from water samples.
