Abstract Laccase with substrate non‐specificity is particularly effective in pollutant degradation. However, the practical application of the laccase is limited due to its poor stability and reusability. In this study, a simple in situ encapsulation method was used to immobilize laccase into amorphous zeolitic imidazolate frameworks‐8 (aZIF‐8), and the synthesized Lac@aZIF‐8 biocomposites were used to remove Reactive Blue 19 (RB 19). Results indicated that the presence of mesoporous endowed the immobilized laccase with higher substrate affinity. Compared with free laccase, the Lac@aZIF‐8 showed higher thermo‐tolerant performance, wider pH range, better storage stability, and reusability.
