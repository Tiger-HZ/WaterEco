Field surveys on Chlorophytum in known and new localities were conducted across 70 districts within the four FTEA floral regions of Uganda between June 2019 and January 2020. The main aim was to map the distribution of taxa that existed in the country and document their habitats through a focused field survey. This was being done for the first time since the publication of the FTEA in 1997. Location coordinates and habitat characteristics were recorded, and voucher specimens collected for encountered plants. The study recorded 14 taxa consisting of 11 species. The U1 region recorded the highest species richness (11). Four species were recorded in U2, three in U4, and none in U3. Chlorophytum tordense, C. elgonense and C. blepharophyllum were the most frequent species while C. longifolium, C. gallabatense and C. miserum were the least frequent. Chlorophytum longifolium is reported here as a new record for Uganda. Chlorophytum species frequently occurred on sandy loams in undisturbed sites particularly in open woodland and wooded grassland habitats. Some eleven species previously recorded in the country were not encountered during this study, probably highlighting species extirpation.
