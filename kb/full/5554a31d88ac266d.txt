Grasping the complex relationship between land use and carbon emissions is crucial for fostering sustainable, low-carbon growth. This study explored the interconnectedness of these elements in the Yellow River Basin Urban Agglomerations from 2000 to 2020. Additionally, the Multi-scale Geographically Weighted Regression model is employed to analyze the driving forces behind this relationship in 2020. The main conclusions include: (1) The land use mix index remained stable, peaking at 0.9 downstream. (2) Carbon emissions decreased by 3.6%, with higher emissions in the east. (3) Coupling coordination remained stable; 53% were ‘barely out of tune’ or ‘basic coordinated’ at the raster scale, while 47% were ‘basic coordinated’ at the county and city scales, showing a ‘V’-shaped trend. (4) Topography was a key factor across scales, while GDP, population density and industrial structure had scale dependent impacts. This study underscores the value of multiscale analyses for dual carbon goals.
