This Final Project Report summarizes the research activities, accomplishments, and outcomes of the U.S. Department of Energy Industrial Technologies Office (ITO) cooperative agreement DE- EE0009494, titled "Targeted Extraction of Valuable Intermediate Products and Clean Water from Municipal Wastewater Using Electroactive Anaerobic Membrane Bioreactors." The project was conducted from October 1, 2021, through July 31, 2025, by a collaborative team at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) and Princeton University, under the direction of Principal Investigator David Jassby. The central objective of this project was to develop and demonstrate an Anaerobic Electric Membrane Bioreactor (AnEMBR) system capable of efficiently treating municipal wastewater while simultaneously producing fit-for-purpose water and extracting commercially valuable chemicals including volatile fatty acids (VFAs), ammonia (NH3), and phosphate. The system integrates three core platform technologies: (1) electrofermentation (EF) for controlled VFA production, (2) electroactive gas stripping membranes for targeted extraction of VFAs and ammonia, and (3) electroactive water treatment membranes including phosphate-selective mixed- matrix membranes. Techno-economic analysis (TEA) and life cycle assessment (LCA) were applied throughout the project to guide research priorities and assess economic and environmental performance. Over the course of the project, the team achieved all major milestones, including the development and optimization of each platform technology at the bench scale, the integration of these platforms at laboratory scale, and a field-scale demonstration at the Hyperion Water Reclamation Facility in El Segundo, California. Seven peer-reviewed journal publications were produced, covering electroactive membranes for ammonia and VFA recovery, phosphate-selective membranes, temperature effects on electro-fermentation, machine-learning-guided pervaporation membrane design, and techno-economic analysis. The project remained on track and within budget throughout its duration. Key technical achievements include: demonstration of a simple, low-cost electrically conducting gas stripping membrane achieving ammonia fluxes of 141.3 g(NH3-N)·m−2·day−1; development of a novel multilayer electroactive pervaporation membrane achieving an acetic acid separation factor of 51.55 with energy consumption of only 3.37 kWh/kg; synthesis of a phosphate-selective mixed- matrix membrane with 8.5-fold enhanced phosphate flux; characterization of temperature effects on electro-fermentation product profiles; application of machine learning to identify promising polymer candidates for pervaporation-based organic recovery; and successful pilot-scale operation at the Hyperion wastewater treatment plant demonstrating the viability of the integrated AnEMBR concept.
