Abstract The ecosystem response to anthropogenic nitrogen (N) loading in estuarine systems is determined by hydrodynamics, biogeochemical transformation rates, and other system-specific characteristics. Historically, San Francisco (SF) Bay has been an outlier from other estuaries with an unusual resistance to eutrophication, despite having extremely high rates of nitrogen loading. Recent increases in phytoplankton biomass and an unprecedented harmful algal bloom, however, have increased the urgency to understand rates and drivers of nitrogen removal in the system.
