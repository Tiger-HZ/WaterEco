Access to safe drinking water remains a major public health challenge in many rural communities in Nepal due to the premature failure of community-based water supply schemes. This study explores the sustainability of community-based water supply systems in Madi Rural Municipality, Kaski District using both field observation methods and interviews with key informants. Out of 168 identified water sources, 45 schemes were within their design period, and among these 45, only 11 sources were selected for detailed assessment. The results present that technical, institutional, and social factors play a vital role in the influence of the water supply system’s sustainability.
