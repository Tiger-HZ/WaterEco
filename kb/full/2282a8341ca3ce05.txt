Heavy metal contamination and nutrient antagonism in cocoa ecosystems have become a critical environmental and food safety concern in tropical agricultural systems because of the increasing anthropogenic activities, prolonged agrochemical use, and declining soil quality maintenance. This study evaluated heavy metal toxicity, nutrient interactions, soil–plant transfer, and associated food safety risks in cocoa (Theobroma cacao L.) plantations across Abia State and Oyo State respectively. Soil and cocoa plant parts (roots, barks, leaves, beans, and pod husks) were analyzed for selected macro/micronutrients and heavy metals using standard analytical procedures.
