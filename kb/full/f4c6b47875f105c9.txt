Abstract Groundwater conditions within mining environments evolve continuously throughout mine development, operation, rehabilitation and closure. Despite this, many closure groundwater models use static hydraulic-property assumptions that may not adequately represent changing hydrogeological conditions. This study presents a practical framework for implementing time-varying hydraulic conductivity and storage-property schedules within a regional groundwater model to simulate mine development, landform rehabilitation, tailings consolidation and groundwater recovery.
