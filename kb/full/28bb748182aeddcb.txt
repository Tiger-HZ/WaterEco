The use of constructed wetlands (CWs) has gained relevance due to their high efficiency in wastewater treatment, low operational costs, and minimal maintenance, which facilitates compliance with Mexican regulations. However, the effectiveness of CWs depends on the adaptability of plant species to different climatic conditions and wetland configurations. This study evaluated the performance of Gymnocoronis sessilis S.F. Blake after six months of establishment in free-flow constructed wetlands (FFCW) and subsurface-flow constructed wetlands (SFCW). Twelve CW units (2.5 × 1.2 × 1.0 m; water depth 0.5 m) were operated with a flow rate of 200 L/day, using hydraulic retention times (HRTs) of 4.5 days for SFCW and 7.5 days for FFCW. Treatments consisted of three FFCW with plants (FFCW-GYM), three FFCW without plants (FFCW-W), three SFCW with plants (SFCW-GYM), and three SFCW without plants (SFCW-W). Removal efficiencies of color, turbidity, and chemical oxygen demand (COD) from domestic wastewater were determined. SFCW achieved the highest removal rates, with color removals of 63.5% (SFCW-W) and 63.2% (SFCW-GYM); turbidity removals of 61.5% (SFCW-W) and 66.2% (SFCW-GYM); and COD removals of 81.3% (SFCW-W) and 87.5% (SFCW-GYM). In contrast, FFCW exhibited negative color and turbidity removals and lower COD performance (45.5% in FFCW-W and 74.7% in FFCW-GYM). These findings indicate that G. sessilis enhances pollutant removal in SFCW, supporting its potential use as a suitable species for CW applications in Tabasco, Mexico.
