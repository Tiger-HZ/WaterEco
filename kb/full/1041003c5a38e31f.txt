Harmful algal blooms can make drinking water and recreational water unsafe for people and wildlife. Satellites can monitor large areas, but their images may miss small blooms near shore. This project asks whether a low-cost autonomous Crazyflie drone equipped with an AI Deck can detect early algal blooms as accurately as satellites. The drone was built as a proof of concept for autonomous, close-range image collection and processing. To create a satellite benchmark, California bloom records and satellite products were combined with field measurements from the Water Quality Portal. The field data included 5,160 chlorophyll-a and 2,864 phycocyanin measurements which indicate algae growth, and 1,630 microcystin results, which is a dangerous toxin produced by some blooms from California lakes
