An organic geochemical analysis of sediments in three cores from the mesohaline Chesapeake Bay was carried out to reconstruct the progression of eutrophication and anoxia/hypoxia over the past five centuries. Evidence of eutrophication was found in the stable isotopic and lipid biomarker signatures of organic matter in sediments of these cores beginning in the late 18th and early 19th centuries and continuing to the present. Enrichments in the carbon and nitrogen isotopic signature of these sediments likely result from enhanced primary productivity and nitrogen recycling, respectively, and occur, coincidentally, with increased fluxes of total organic carbon (TOC) and episodic enrichments (relative to TOC) of algal and bacterially derived lipid biomarker compounds.
