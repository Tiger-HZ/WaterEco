Abstract Dragonflies and damselflies provide crucial insights into environmental health as bioindicators of aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems, making it essential to study eutrophication effects on their populations. These insects exhibit diverse reproductive and territorial behaviours, which may be the first response to environmental disturbances. We hypothesized that eutrophication disrupts physiology and alters energy investment, affecting nutritional status and time allocation to territorial and reproductive behaviours. We estimated flight capacity and body condition-related traits in two sympatric dragonfly species at 12 sites along an eutrophication gradient, and recorded their territorial and reproductive behaviours.
