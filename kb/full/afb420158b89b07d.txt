Negative experiences with water services can shape perceptions that municipal drinking water is unsafe and foster dissatisfaction with providers. Though prior research links such experiences to lower trust in municipal governments, less is known about how trust influences households’ willingness to pay (WTP) for service improvements. This relationship is critical, as municipal utilities rely heavily on ratepayer revenue and household support for operational sustainability and infrastructure investments. This paper examines the determinants of households’ WTP for improved drinking water quality and infrastructure across the United States using three years of data from the national Value of Water survey.
