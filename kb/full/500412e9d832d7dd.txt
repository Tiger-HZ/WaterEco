The non-breeding ecology of the Wilson's Plover subspecies (Anarhynchus wilsonia beldingi) is poorly understood. This subspecies is listed as threatened in Mexico, and we used survey data from the Migratory Shorebird Project (MSP) to analyze Wilson's Plover winter abundance trend for northwest Mexico. In addition, we used count data from the MSP and the Christmas Bird Count to evaluate Wilson's Plover winter abundance trend for Ensenada de La Paz. We also used MSP data to quantify the impact of disturbance on the plover abundance, as well as the association of abundance with environmental variables during the non-breeding season. Wilson's Plover abundance decreased with lower mean temperature, possible due to effects on prey availability, but it was also associated with higher minimum temperatures, indicating that the species benefits from a warmer, tropical winter climate. Wilson's Plover populations during winter were stable in Ensenada de La Paz and northwest Mexico. However, wintering habitat with human disturbance had 32% less Wilson's Plovers as compared to an equivalent sampling unit with no human disturbance. Our study highlights the importance of maintaining alternative habitats that enable small shorebirds to move from disturbed to less disturbed areas.
