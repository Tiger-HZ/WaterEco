High Resolution Image Download MS PowerPoint Slide The reuse of treated effluents is a promising strategy for agricultural irrigation, especially in contexts of water scarcity. This study evaluated the agronomic performance of ornamental sunflower ( Helianthus annuus L. cv. “Anão de Jardim”) irrigated with different dilutions of synthetic graywater previously treated by a constructed wetland (CW). The experiment was conducted in a completely randomized design with six treatments and four replicates, including combinations of treated effluent and well water, as well as a treatment with substrate modified by coarse sand. At 60 days after sowing, the treatment with 100% treated effluent combined with coarse sand (T5) provided the best results for morphometric and biomass variables, with increases in plant height and stem diameter by 58.14 and 35.48%, respectively, compared to the control. Furthermore, only the treatments irrigated with effluent reached reproductive stages. Higher water use efficiency and potential for complete replacement of well water were observed, without phytotoxic effects on the plants. The presence of nutrients in the effluent, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus, combined with greater substrate porosity, enhanced plant development. These results indicate that treated graywater by the CW technology can enhance sunflower growth under the studied conditions. However, further studies under long-term application are required to confirm the broader applicability of this approach.
