Granular cold-water-swelling oat starch was prepared under both low-temperature (90–130 °C) and high-temperature (150–190 °C) conditions using subcritical ethanol–water treatment, and the resulting structural and property changes were systematically investigated. The low-temperature treatment largely retained the native orthorhombic crystal structure and produced granules with finer morphology, whereas the high-temperature treatment induced crystallization of a new orthorhombic amylose phase and generated a swollen granular state with superior cold-water swelling capacity (11.60 g/g vs. 1.99 g/g for native starch).
