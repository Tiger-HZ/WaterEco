Abstract
Aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) and poor aqueous solubility severely restrict
electrochemiluminescence (ECL) applications of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Herein, a
water-induced supramolecular self-assembly strategy is proposed to address these drawbacks. Via a
simple reprecipitation route, representative PAH meso-tetrakis(4-carboxyphenyl)porphyrin (TCPP) forms
ordered supramolecular aggregates driven by noncovalent interactions, which suppress π–π stacking
and ACQ and achieve a low triggering potential of − 1.0 V. Iron-based MOF supported molybdenum
disulﬁde (MIL-101@MoS2) is adopted as a multifunctional ampliﬁer: reversible valence interconversion
of Fe2+/Fe3+ and Mo4+/Mo6+ sustains continuous redox cycling to improve redox activity, while abundant
oxygen vacancies accelerate electron transfer and coreactant activation for further ECL enhancement.
After preparing MIL-101(Fe)@MoS2@TCPP nanoparticles (MMST), an ECL aptasensor is fabricated for
4-ﬂuoromethamphetamine (4-FMA) determination relying on target-triggered cyanine dye displacement.
This sensor exhibits an ultralow limit of detection of (1.87 × 10− 15 g/L) (S/N = 3) and a broad linear range
from (1.0 × 10− 14 – 1.0 × 10− 6 g/L) g/L. It also shows favorable analytical results for real e-cigarette
samples, with recoveries of 95.0–104.0% and relative standard deviations (RSD) less than 5.0%. This
work offers a green route for high-performance PAH-based ECL luminophores and a reliable tool for
rapid ﬁeld screening of emerging psychoactive contaminants in complicated food-related matrices.
INTRODUCTION
New psychoactive substances (NPS) are synthetic drug analogs artiﬁcially fabricated by modifying the
chemical structures of controlled substances to evade legal supervision, and they can induce equivalent
or even stronger stimulant, hallucinogenic, and anesthetic effects than traditional illicit drugs.[1, 2] As the
third-generation emerging drugs following traditional and synthetic drugs, NPS have posed severe
challenges to global public security governance and forensic toxicology detection systems. 4-
Fluoromethamphetamine (4-FMA) is a typical amphetamine-type NPS, which exhibits stronger
neurostimulatory and hallucinogenic activities and higher abuse risks compared with amphetamine and
methamphetamine.[3, 4] Nevertheless, the toxicological characteristics and supporting precise detection
systems of 4-FMA remain poorly established due to its late emergence and regulatory implementation.
Currently, high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC-
MS/MS) serves as the mainstream detection method for 4-FMA.[5] Despite its high sensitivity and
accuracy, HPLC-MS/MS suffers from cumbersome sample pretreatment procedures, high detection
costs, and heavy reliance on large-scale precision instruments, which severely restricts its scalable
applications in grassroots drug control monitoring and on-site law enforcement, and fails to meet the
core demand for rapid on-site screening. Therefore, the development of eﬃcient, rapid, facile, and ﬁeld-
deployable detection technologies for 4-FMA is urgently required in forensic toxicology and public
security research.
Page 3/22
Electrochemiluminescence (ECL) integrates the dual advantages of electrochemistry and
photochemistry and has emerged as a high-speciﬁcity and ultra-sensitive analytical technique.[6–8]
Featuring low background signal, excellent sensitivity, and tunable reaction controllability,[9, 10] ECL has
become a promising alternative technique for rapid 4-FMA detection and has been widely applied in
biomarker analysis, food safety assessment, immunoassay, and water quality monitoring.[11–13] In
recent years, the concept of aggregation-induced electrochemiluminescence (AIECL) has attracted
extensive research attention and promoted the innovation of ECL sensing technology.[14, 15] AIECL
provides a feasible strategy to address the inherent aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) effect and
poor water solubility of conventional organic luminophores, which have long been the core bottlenecks
limiting the practical applications of high-performance ECL sensors.[16, 17] Polycyclic aromatic
hydrocarbons (PAHs) are a class of high-performance ECL luminophores, and mitigating their ACQ effect
is critical for the construction of advanced ECL sensing platforms. Our previous work successfully
inhibited the ACQ effect and improved the luminescence stability of PAHs by immobilizing PAH
molecules within metal–organic framework (MOF) matrices via coordination anchoring.[18] However,
conventional MOF-based composite luminophores are predominantly fabricated via solvothermal
methods, which involve long reaction durations, complicated synthetic procedures, expensive organic
solvents, and harsh high-temperature and high-pressure conditions. These limitations hinder the low-
cost and large-scale preparation of MOF composites and severely restrict their practical deployment in
on-site detection.
A reprecipitation strategy addresses the intrinsic drawbacks of poor water solubility and severe
aggregation-caused quenching of PAHs, [19, 20] and proposes a facile, green, and eﬃcient route to
alleviate the ACQ effect of meso-tetrakis(4-carboxyphenyl)porphyrin (TCPP), a representative PAH
luminophore. Driven by noncovalent intermolecular interactions, TCPP molecules spontaneously self-
assemble into highly ordered and structurally stable supramolecular architectures in aqueous media.[21,
22] Such well-deﬁned supramolecular structures effectively optimize interfacial electron transfer and
energy migration, fundamentally mitigating the ACQ behavior and substantially boosting ECL
performance. However, the stability and signal ampliﬁcation capability of these self-assembled nano-
architectures alone still face challenges in meeting the demands of ultra-trace detection. In order to
solve this problem, it is imperative to introduce strategies that can further stabilize the luminophore and
amplify the ECL response.
Recent studies have demonstrated that various strategies—including antenna effect-coupled Schottky
heterojunctions,[23] co-reactant enrichment,[24] core–shell structures,[25] and band gap modulation[26]
—can effectively stabilize luminophores and amplify ECL responses. In contrast to these approaches,
MOF materials exhibit unique carrier advantages by virtue of their three-dimensional porous structures
and the synergistic effect of multivalent metal centers.[27, 28] They can effectively disperse luminescent
molecules, inhibit aggregation-induced deactivation, and remarkably improve the intensity and stability
of ECL signals.[29, 30] More importantly, MOFs containing multivalent metal elements can continuously
drive the redox cycling reactions in ECL systems through reversible valence-state conversion of metal
nodes and ligands.[31–34] This multivalent cycling can signiﬁcantly accelerate coreactant activation
Page 4/22
while also lowering the ECL trigger voltage to − 1.0 V by facilitating electron transfer and reducing kinetic
barriers. Such valence-variable behaviors not only enhance the redox activity of luminophores but also
precisely regulate the ECL reaction kinetics, thereby achieving further ampliﬁcation and stabilization of
sensing signals.[35, 36]
In this work, iron-based MOF supported molybdenum disulﬁde (MIL-101@MoS₂) was adopted as a
multifunctional signal ampliﬁcation carrier. Combined with the water-induced supramolecular self-
assembly of TCPP, a novel ECL sensing platform with low trigger voltage, high stability and excellent
sensitivity was successfully constructed. To achieve the ultra-sensitive and speciﬁc detection of 4-
ﬂuoromethamphetamine (4-FMA) in complex matrices, a label-free DNA-cyanine dye (DNA-Cy7) ECL
aptasensor was further fabricated based on the speciﬁc recognition capability of nucleic acid aptamers.
[37–39] In this sensing system, the target 4-FMA competitively displaces Cy7 dye molecules from the
aptamer strand, thereby generating a speciﬁc ECL response. This detection procedure eliminates
complicated separation and labeling operations, which greatly simpliﬁes the analytical process. This
ﬁnding ﬁlls the technical gap in the low-voltage and low-energy-consumption detection of 4-FMA and
provides a desirable luminescent medium for the development of portable ECL detection devices. The
constructed anti-interference and label-free sensing system enables reliable detection of 4-FMA in e-
liquid samples and can be further extended to the rapid screening of adulterants in complex food
matrices, including functional beverages, condiments and dietary supplements. The green and facile
porphyrin supramolecular assembly strategy proposed in this study effectively addresses the inherent
ACQ defect of conventional porphyrin luminophores, realizing the ultra-sensitive and convenient
detection of 4-FMA in complex real samples. Compared with traditional large-scale mass spectrometry,
this method exhibits prominent advantages of high eﬃciency, portability and low cost, holding great
application potential in drug control, public security monitoring, forensic toxicology analysis and food
safety detection. This work establishes a green and facile porphyrin supramolecular assembly strategy
for high-performance ECL emitters and provides a universal analytical methodology for rapid screening
of emerging contaminants in complex matrices.
EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Reagents and materials, as well as the apparatus and characterization. The reagents and materials, as
well as the apparatus and characterization, were described in the Supporting Information.
Preparation of TCPP NPs. TCPP NPs were prepared through a simple precipitation method using
tetrahydrofuran (THF) and water as good and poor solvents. First, 0.001 g of TCPP was dispersed in 10
mL THF and dissolved under vigorous stirring; then, the mixture was poured into 20 mL of water rapidly
under high-power sonication. Subsequently, continuous ultrasound experiment was carried out on the
mixture until the solution became homogeneous. Finally, the mixture was transferred into a vacuum
freeze dryer for 48 h and TCPP NPs were collected.
Page 5/22
Preparation of MIL-101(Fe)@MoS 2 @TCPP NPs (MMST). The detailed preparation of MIL-
101(Fe)@MoS2 was provided in the Supplement Information. Different from the preparation of
nanoparticles above, the MMST was prepared by changing the water into MMS aqueous solution with
different concentrations. In short, a certain amount of MMS was added into 20 mL of ultrapure water and
sonicated for 30 min. Subsequently, 1 mg/mL of TCPP solution was quickly poured into 20 mL of MMS
aqueous solution. Finally, the MMST was collected by vacuum freeze-drying.
ECL Measurement. Scheme 1 illustrates the preparation process for ECL sensors. After careful polishing
with 0.3 and 0.05 µm of alumina powder on ﬁne grinding paper, the glassy carbon electrode (GCE, φ = 3
mm) undergoes ultrasonic cleaning with water and ethanol prior to use. Then, MMST (4 µL, 0.5 mg/mL)
were dripped onto the electrode surface and dried naturally in turn. Then, Cy7-aptamer (4 µL, 3 µM) was
dripped on the electrode surface and incubated overnight at 4°C. The constructed biosensor was tested
in 50 mM K2S2O8/phosphate buffered saline (PBS) (pH = 7.4) through ECL with − 1.0 to 0 V and the
scanning rate of 100 mV/s. The photomultiplier tube (PMT) was 800 V.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Characterisation of materials. The morphologies of the as-prepared materials were characterized using
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and ﬁeld-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM). Self-
assembled TCPP nanoparticles ( TCPP NPs ) were prepared via a straightforward rapid solution
dispersion method as reported in a previous study.[20] To investigate the structural properties of water-
induced porphyrin nanoparticles, a series of characterizations were conducted. The morphology of TCPP
NPs was obtained by SEM, as shown in Fig. 1B. The water-induced effect transformed the originally
irregular bulk structure (Figure S1) of porphyrin into a nanosheet-like morphology with lengths ranging
from approximately 1 µm to 50 µm. As illustrated in Fig. 1A, MMS was synthesized using a facile
hydrothermal method. The FE-SEM image of MMS (Fig. 1C) reveals a ﬂower-like structure composed of
an array of rough nanosheets. This unique ﬂower-like ring structure consists of MIL-101(Fe) and MoS₂
arrays. Furthermore, the morphology of the MMST composite is displayed in Fig. 1D, where MMS and
TCPP supramolecules are closely interconnected. The small-sized MMS particles are uniformly
anchored onto the sheet-like structure of the TCPP supramolecules, accompanied by an increase in the
diameter of TCPP NPs, which is attributed to the high dispersibility of MMS.
To identify the functional groups and chemical bonds of the as-prepared samples, Fourier transform
infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy was conducted. The water-induced porphyrin supramolecules generally
exhibit an FT-IR spectrum consistent with that of TCPP, as shown in Fig. 2A. FT-IR analysis of TCPP NPs,
MMS, and MMST (Figure S2) revealed distinct structural changes: the appearance of Mo-O and C-O-C
stretching vibrations in MMST, accompanied by a reduction in surface hydroxyl (-OH) groups, veriﬁed the
successful chemical coupling between MMS and TCPP NPs. The UV-Vis absorption spectra show
(Figure S3) that MMST retains both the characteristic absorption of MMS in the UV region and the typical
Page 6/22
Soret band of TCPP NPs at ~ 420 nm, conﬁrming the successful integration of TCPP into the composite.
Furthermore, to explore the crystal structure of the obtained materials, X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis
was performed. XRD analysis of TCPP and TCPP NPs (Figure S4) revealed that pure TCPP has well-
deﬁned crystalline peaks, whereas TCPP NPs exhibit broad and weak diffraction signals, conﬁrming that
the self-assembly process leads to the destruction of long-range order and the formation of low-
crystallinity nanoparticles.As illustrated in Fig. 2B, the XRD intensity of TCPP NPs is signiﬁcantly lower
than that of porphyrin, which is likely attributable to the nanosizing process. In the case of MMST, the
characteristic peaks include both the typical diffraction peaks of MoS2 and those of MIL-101(Fe).
Speciﬁcally, four weak diffraction peaks at 2θ = 14.4°, 32.7°, 39.5°, and 58.3° correspond to the (002),
(100), (103), and (110) crystal planes of MoS₂ (PDF#37-1492), respectively. Additionally, the diffraction
peaks of MIL-101(Fe) (CCDC No. 605510) are also present.
Finally, the chemical composition and elemental valence states of MMST were analyzed using X-ray
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). Figure 2C reveals
the presence of ﬁve elements—C, O, S, Mo, and Fe—in MMST. EDS analysis (Figure S5) further
demonstrates the uniform presence and favorable distribution of these elements, consistent with the
XPS results shown in Fig. 2C, thereby conﬁrming the successful fabrication of the MMST composite. To
further investigate the chemical states of the catalyst, the Fe 2p, S 2p, and Mo 3d spectra were
examined. Figure 2D displays the high-resolution Fe 2p spectrum, where peaks at 710.57 and 724.17 eV
correspond to Fe²⁺ in the 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 orbitals, respectively, while peaks at 712.96 and 726.85 eV are
attributed to Fe³⁺ in the 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 orbitals, indicating the coexistence of Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ in MMST.[40]
Additionally, satellite peaks are located at 720.05 and 733.01 eV. In the S 2p spectrum (Fig. 2E), the
binding energies of S 2p3/2 (161.59 eV) and S 2p1/2 (162.70 eV) are assigned to the S²⁻ orbital,[41]
suggesting the presence of sulfur species and Fe–S bonds on the MMST surface. The peaks at 168.83
and 163.36 eV correspond to sulfate species (–SO⁻) and unsaturated edge S, respectively.[42] The high-
resolution Mo 3d spectrum of MMST is deconvoluted into four peaks at 226.06, 229.00, 232.47, and
236.00 eV. The predominant peaks at 229.00 and 232.47 eV are assigned to Mo 3d5/2 and Mo 3d3/2,
respectively, indicating the presence of Mo4⁺ (Fig. 2F). The small peak at 225.71 eV, arising from Mo–S
bonding, is attributed to S 2s. Another peak at 336 eV is ascribed to Mo6⁺ oxide species formed during
the in situ synthesis of MMST.[43] The high-resolution XPS spectra of O 1s and C 1s are shown in Figure
S6 and Figure S7. Based on the above results, multiple active sites, including Fe3⁺/Fe2⁺, Mo6⁺/Mo4⁺, and
various sulfur species, coexist in MMST.
The ECL enhancement mechanism of MMST. To investigate the origin of the enhanced ECL
performance of TCPP NPs, Raman spectroscopy was ﬁrst employed. By focusing on the characteristic
peaks at 1366 cm⁻¹ (designated as peak I) and 1560 cm⁻¹ (designated as peak II), which are sensitive to
structural changes in the porphyrin ring, the ratio of the relative intensities of these two vibrational peaks
was calculated to evaluate the degree of π–π stacking.[7] The results show that TCPP NPs exhibit a
relative peak intensity ratio of 0.59, which is approximately 0.09 lower than that of TCPP (0.67),
indicating a relatively weaker π–π stacking in TCPP NPs. This ﬁnding reasonably explains that although
Page 7/22
porphyrin, as a planar polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon, tends to form aggregated states via
intermolecular π–π stacking, the reduced π–π stacking in TCPP NPs may attenuate the ACQ effect,
thereby enhancing the luminescence eﬃciency of the luminophores.[44]
To further explore the inﬂuence of π–π stacking on the optical properties, ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis)
absorption spectroscopy was conducted. Compared with the corresponding spectrum of the porphyrin
solution, the UV-Vis spectrum of the porphyrin nanoparticles exhibits pronounced differences. The
absorption spectrum of TCPP displays a classical Soret band and four Q bands, which are characteristic
features of porphyrin. In comparison with TCPP, TCPP NPs show a blue shift of these bands and a
decrease in absorbance, which are hallmark signatures of J-type (edge-to-edge) aggregation of TCPP
molecules, likely induced by supramolecular interactions. This effectively restricts the aggregation of
porphyrin molecules. Furthermore, the band gaps of TCPP and TCPP NPs were derived from Tauc plots.
TCPP NPs exhibit a narrower band gap than TCPP, which is favorable for ECL emission. Collectively,
these analyses clearly demonstrate that the optical properties of porphyrin can be effectively modulated
by tuning its molecular structure. The water-induced effect not only alters the morphological structure of
porphyrin but also inﬂuences its π–π stacking degree and optical characteristics, thereby opening new
possibilities for the application of porphyrin in ﬁelds such as ECL.
Moreover, the high-resolution O 1s XPS spectrum shows a dominant peak at 531.57 eV assigned to
defective Mo–O species, implying the formation of oxygen vacancies in the MMST composite.
Combined with the EPR signal at g ≈ 2.000 originating from unpaired electrons trapped in oxygen
vacancies, XPS and EPR (Figure S8) mutually conﬁrm the rich oxygen vacancy defects of MMST. These
oxygen vacancies serve as active centers to accelerate charge transfer and promote the activation of
S2O82− coreactant, thereby greatly improving the ECL emission eﬃciency.
To further verify the suppression of the ACQ effect in TCPP NPs, time-resolved ﬂuorescence decay
measurements were carried out. As depicted in Fig. 3D and 3E, TCPP NPs exhibit a distinctly prolonged
ﬂuorescence lifetime (τ = 11.60 ns) in comparison with monomeric TCPP (τ = 10.60 ns). Typically, the
severe π–π stacking formed in aggregated polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) aggregates
dominates the ACQ effect, which accelerates non-radiative decay and thus shortens the excited-state
lifetime of luminophores. The obvious extension in the ﬂuorescence lifetime of self-assembled TCPP
NPs demonstrates that water-induced supramolecular assembly effectively restricts the unfavorable
intermolecular π–π stacking and inhibits non-radiative energy dissipation. Beneﬁting from the prolonged
excited-state lifetime, a higher population of excited-state TCPP luminophores can be well maintained,
which is favorable for eﬃcient luminescence and ECL emission.
To verify that the cyanine dye can be displaced from the DNA-cyanine dye aptamer in the presence of 4-
FMA, UV-Vis absorption spectroscopy was performed on APT/Cy7 and 4-FMA/APT/Cy7 solutions, with
particular attention to the absorption peaks at 670 nm and 780 nm. As shown in Fig. 3F, the absorption
spectra of the two solutions nearly completely overlap around 550 nm and 880 nm, indicating that the
total dye concentration and optical path conditions are consistent between samples, thereby ruling out
Page 8/22
dilution or injection errors. In contrast, within the range of 660–780 nm, the absorbance of APT/Cy7 is
markedly higher than that of 4-FMA/APT/Cy7. This selective decrease in absorbance upon addition of 4-
FMA suggests a reduced contribution of the cyanine dye in the characteristic absorption region of the
bound state, consistent with the spectral signature of dye displacement from the aptamer by 4-FMA.
Therefore, this method enables label-free detection by directly monitoring the 4-FMA-induced
displacement reaction via UV-Vis spectroscopy, without the need for laborious separation steps.
In this work, K2S2O8 was employed as the coreactant, TCPP NPs served as the luminophore, and the
MOF-based material MMS acted as the luminophore carrier, which effectively enhanced the ECL
performance of the luminophore. The as-prepared composite MMST was subsequently utilized as the
ﬁnal cathodic ECL emitter in the sensing system. The proposed ECL mechanism of the MMST/K2S2O8
system can be expressed as follows: In route 1, a small number of S2O82⁻ gained electrons to produce
SO4•⁻ (Eq. (1)), which reacted with MMST to produce a moderate ECL signal. In route 2, Fe3⁺ and Mo6⁺
captured electrons for conversion to lower valence states (Fe2⁺ and Mo4⁺) (Eq. (2)), which accelerated
the catalytic decomposition of S2O82⁻ and consistently produced a large amount of SO4•⁻ (Eq. (3)),
enhancing the ECL signals and realizing a reversible transition of the valence state of the metal ions. In
route 3, the rich oxygen vacancies in the composite could adsorb and activate dissolved O2, which was
reduced to H2O2 (Eq. (4)), and OH• was formed by reacting variable valence metal ions with H2O2
(Eq. (5)), which further reacted with S2O82⁻ to generate more SO4•⁻ (Eq. (6)), achieving superimposed
ampliﬁcation of ECL signals. In the ECL emission route, the ground state MMST ﬁrst gained electrons to
become MMST•⁻ (Eq. (7)), which subsequently reacted with SO4•⁻ to produce the excited state (MMST*)
(Eq. (8)). Finally, MMST* produced ECL emission and returned to MMST (Eq. (9)), and the corresponding
mechanism equations were as follows:

Electrochemical Characterization of the Modiﬁed Electrode. The stepwise construction of the sensor
was characterized by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). As shown in Fig. 4A, the bare
glassy carbon electrode (GCE) exhibits the smallest impedance (curve a). After modiﬁcation with MMST,
a signiﬁcant increase in impedance is observed (curve b), which is attributed to the poor conductivity of
MMS, thereby hindering electron transfer at the electrode surface. Upon further modiﬁcation of the
MMST/GCE with the aptamer, the impedance increases further (curve c) due to steric hindrance effects.
Subsequently, after incubating the resulting DNA-Cy7/MMST/GCE with a solution containing the target
analyte 4-FMA, the speciﬁc binding between DNA-Cy7 and the analyte occurs, leading to the
displacement of Cy7. Consequently, no substantial change in impedance is observed (curve d). These
results collectively conﬁrm the successful fabrication of the ECL aptasensor.
In parallel, cyclic voltammetry (CV) was employed to monitor the interfacial current variations during
each modiﬁcation step of the sensor fabrication process. As depicted in Fig. 4B, upon modiﬁcation of
GCE with MMST, the redox current of the composite decreases markedly (curve b), which is consistent
with the diminished electron transport capability of MMST. Following the immobilization of the aptamer
DNA-Cy7 onto the electrode surface, the increased surface resistance leads to a reduced electron
transfer rate, thereby further decreasing the current signal (curve c). Subsequently, after incubation with
the target analyte 4-FMA, the peak current remains almost unchanged (curve d), which is attributable to
the dye displacement effect. The EIS and CV results exhibit consistent trends in electron transfer
behavior, collectively validating the successful assembly of the sensor.
To evaluate the feasibility of the proposed sensor, the electrochemiluminescence (ECL) behaviors of
different modiﬁed electrodes were examined. As shown in Fig. 4C, with K₂S₂O₈ serving as the coreactant
in the ECL reaction system, a weak ECL signal is observed at the bare GCE (curve a). Upon modiﬁcation
with the luminophore TCPP, the ECL signal increases slightly to approximately 1200 a.u. (curve b). In
contrast, when TCPP NPs are modiﬁed onto the GCE, the ECL signal of the sensor exhibits an
approximately six-fold enhancement compared to that of monomeric TCPP (curve c). This signal
ampliﬁcation is attributed to the water-induced porphyrin supramolecules effectively alleviating the
aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) effect, thereby generating stronger ECL emission. Furthermore, the
ECL intensity of MMS alone is approximately 3000 a.u. (curve d), whereas the MMST composite
achieves an ECL intensity close to 18000 a.u. (curve e). This enhancement arises from the presence of
multivalent Fe and Mo ions in the MOF-based carrier MMS, whose valence state changes can actively
participate in the ECL redox process, thereby boosting the ECL performance. Subsequently, upon further
incubation of the aptamer DNA-Cy7 on the electrode, the ECL signal decreases (curve f) due to the steric
Page 10/22
hindrance effect of the biomacromolecules, which impedes electron transfer. Finally, when the fabricated
aptasensor is used for detection, the speciﬁc recognition and immobilization of the target analyte on the
electrode surface lead to a further reduction in the ECL signal (curve g). These results collectively
conﬁrm the successful construction of the aptasensor.
Sensing Performance. To evaluate the sensitivity of the proposed ECL aptasensor, the ECL intensities in
response to varying concentrations of 4-FMA were measured under optimal experimental conditions
(Figure S9). As shown in Fig. 5A, the ECL intensity gradually increases with the concentration of 4-FMA
ranging from 1.0 × 10⁻14 g/L to 1.0 × 10⁻6 g/L. A good linear relationship is observed between the
enhanced ECL intensity (ΔIECL) and the logarithm of the 4-FMA concentration. The corresponding
calibration curve is presented in Fig. 5B, yielding a linear regression equation of ΔIECL = 15433.83 + 
856.41 lg C₄‑FMA, with a correlation coeﬃcient (R²) of 0.985. The detection limit is determined to be 1.87
× 10⁻15 g/L (S/N = 3). These results demonstrate that the constructed aptasensing platform exhibits
satisfactory sensitivity. Moreover, in comparison with many previously reported detection methods, the
proposed ECL biosensor offers a lower detection limit and a wider linear range (Table S1).
The speciﬁcity, ECL signal stability, and reproducibility of the sensor are also of critical importance.
Speciﬁcity serves as a key parameter for evaluating the detection capability of the designed sensor. The
selectivity of the aptasensor was assessed using furanyl fentanyl, ﬂu alprazolam, AB-PINACA, MDPV,
RCS-4, and 4-CEC as interfering substances (chemical structures are shown in Figure S10). Brieﬂy, the
experimental procedure was identical to that used for 4-FMA detection, with the concentration of these
interfering substances (1.0 × 10⁻4 g/L) being 10,000-fold higher than that of 4-FMA. As shown in Fig. 6A,
the enhancement of the ECL signal induced by 4-FMA is markedly higher than that of the other NPS,
indicating the favorable selectivity of the proposed sensor toward 4-FMA.
As illustrated in Fig. 6B, ten consecutive cyclic voltammetry scans were performed on different modiﬁed
electrodes in the detection solution. The results demonstrate that each modiﬁed electrode exhibits
excellent ECL signal stability, with relative standard deviation (RSD) values all below 5.0%. This ﬁnding
strongly conﬁrms the superior stability of the constructed sensor in terms of ECL signal output, thereby
providing a reliable foundation for its practical application. Storage stability testing was conducted by
placing the ECL aptasensor-modiﬁed glassy carbon electrode in a 4°C refrigerated environment. The
experimental data (Fig. 6C) reveal that the ECL signal intensity of the sensor exhibits only a slight
decreasing trend over storage durations of 0, 3, 9, 12, and 15 days. This result indicates the excellent
storage stability of the constructed sensing platform, further validating the reliable stability of the ECL
aptasensor system.
Under identical experimental conditions, ﬁve batches of independently prepared aptasensors were used
to detect the same concentration of 4-FMA. The corresponding ECL response values were measured and
recorded. The experimental data show that the RSD of the ECL intensities across the ﬁve sensor batches
is below 5.0% (Fig. 6D), fully demonstrating the excellent detection reproducibility of the developed
aptasensor system.

Detection of 4-FMA in real samples. To validate the feasibility of the proposed ECL method, a standard

CONCLUSION
In this study, a simple reprecipitation method was employed to alleviate the ACQ effect of porphyrin
molecules, yielding water-induced supramolecular assemblies with prolonged excited-state lifetimes and
enhanced luminescence eﬃciency. Meanwhile, a multivalent metal–organic framework was utilized as
the luminophore carrier, which not only effectively immobilized the luminophore but also actively
participated in the ECL redox reactions through reversible valence state transitions of the multivalent
ions. Speciﬁcally, the rapid cyclic interconversion of the Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ and Mo⁴⁺/Mo⁶⁺ redox pairs
Page 12/22
substantially enhanced the ECL response and lowered the trigger voltage to − 1.0 V. The resulting MMST
composite was successfully integrated into an ECL sensing platform for the ultrasensitive detection of 4-
FMA in complex e-liquid matrices, achieving an exceptionally low detection limit of 1.87 × 10⁻15 g/L and
a wide linear range spanning ten orders of magnitude (1.0 × 10⁻14 – 1.0× 10⁻6 g/L). Upon introduction of
the target analyte, it speciﬁcally bound to the DNA-Cy7 aptamer, leading to the displacement of the
cyanine dye Cy7 and signiﬁcantly improving the speciﬁcity of the sensor. The satisfactory recoveries
(95.0–104.0%) and low relative standard deviations (< 5%) obtained in viscous, multi-component e-liquid
samples containing propylene glycol, glycerol, nicotine, and ﬂavoring agents demonstrated the robust
matrix tolerance and operational reliability of the proposed platform. These analytical attributes
establish a versatile analytical strategy for the detection of emerging contaminants in complex matrices,
providing a methodological foundation for rapid on-site screening applications.
