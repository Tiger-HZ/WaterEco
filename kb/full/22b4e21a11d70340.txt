Abstract This study evaluated the performance of bench and pilot-scale facultative aerated lagoons (FAL) treating Kraft pulp effluents subjected to organic load shocks, with bioaugmentation of Bacillus thuringiensis NR114581.1 immobilized on rice husks. The effluents from the treatments were characterized in terms of chemical oxygen demand (COD), biochemical oxygen demand (BOD 5 ), specific lignin compounds, color, and turbidity. Batch tests were performed to optimize the bioproduct dosage and evaluate its removal performance. During bioaugmentation in the bench-scale FAL, the average removal reached 72% for BOD 5 and 36% for COD, while the removal of specific compounds was low or zero.
