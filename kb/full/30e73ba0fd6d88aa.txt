Approximately 70–80% of the feed supplied in aquaculture systems is typically excreted as nitrogenous waste. The accumulation of such nitrogen compounds can deteriorate water quality and suppress fish appetite, ultimately leading to suboptimal growth performance. One potential strategy to mitigate this issue is phytoremediation using aquatic macrophytes, such as Amazon frogbit (Limnobium laevigatum (Humb. &amp; Bonpl. ex Willd.) Heine). This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of Amazon frogbit as a phytoremediator for improving water quality in Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) nursery systems.
