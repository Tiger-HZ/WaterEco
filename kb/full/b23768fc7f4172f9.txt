Abstract Treeholes are important microhabitats in forest ecosystems and support various invertebrates. The nesting activities of cavity-nesting birds continuously supply animal-derived resources, such as prey remains, feces, and carcasses, thereby substantially altering the internal resource environment of treeholes. However, the relative importance of animal-derived resource inputs and the environmental conditions associated with treeholes for scarab beetle abundance remains poorly understood.
