Abstract Rapid urbanization in tropical wetland cities has increasingly disrupted natural groundwater regimes through land filling, soil sealing, and conventional drainage practices, resulting in the gradual deterioration of wooden pile foundations that depend on permanently saturated conditions for long-term durability. Existing studies have primarily examined wooden foundations from structural and geotechnical perspectives, while the ecological relationship between foundation performance and wetland hydrology remains insufficiently explored.
