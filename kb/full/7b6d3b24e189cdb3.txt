 Response of integrated nutrient management on growth, yield and soil health of durum wheat under Bundelkhand region
 
 
 
 Author(s): 
 
 Sankalp Singh, Awanish Kumar, Satyavir Singh, Chetan Anand Dubey and Ashish Kumar Tiwari 
 
 
 Abstract: 
 
 Wheat ( Triticum durum L. ) is a major cereal crop with significant nutritional and economic importance, yet its productivity in the Bundelkhand region of Uttar Pradesh remains below the national average due to soil fertility decline, moisture stress, and imbalanced nutrient management. A field experiment was conducted during the Rabi season of 2024-25 at the Students&rsquo; Research Farm, Department of Soil Science and Agricultural Chemistry, Bundelkhand University, Jhansi, to evaluate the effect of integrated nutrient management (INM) on growth, yield, and soil health of durum wheat. The experiment was laid out in a Randomized Block Design (RBD) with three replications, comprising eight treatments: T₀ - RDF only (control), T₁ - 100% RDF + 10 t ha⁻&sup1; FYM, T₂ - 100% RDF + 15 t ha⁻&sup1; FYM, T₃ - 100% RDF + 10 t ha⁻&sup1; vermicompost (VC), T₄ - 100% RDF + 15 t ha⁻&sup1; VC, T₅ - 100% RDF + 5 t ha⁻&sup1; FYM + 10 t ha⁻&sup1; VC, T₆ - 100% RDF + 10 t ha⁻&sup1; FYM + 5 t ha⁻&sup1; VC, and T₇ - 100% RDF + 10 t ha⁻&sup1; FYM + 10 t ha⁻&sup1; VC. Results indicated that INM significantly improved growth parameters, including plant height and tiller number, at all growth stages compared to the control. Yield attributes such as number of effective tillers, ear length, spikelets per ear, grains per ear, and grain weight were markedly enhanced under integrated treatments. Among all treatments, T₇ produced the highest grain yield (44.35 kg plot⁻&sup1;), followed by T₆, demonstrating the superior effect of combined organic and inorganic nutrient application. Soil health parameters, including water holding capacity, soil organic carbon, and available N, P, and K, were significantly improved under INM, indicating enhanced soil fertility and sustainability. The study concluded that integrated application of 100% RDF with FYM and vermicompost (T₇) is the most effective and sustainable nutrient management practice for improving growth, yield, and soil health of durum wheat under Bundelkhand conditions. The findings provide a strong basis for adopting INM in cereal-based cropping systems to achieve higher productivity and soil sustainability.
