Abstract Traditional three-dimensional planing in deep water is studied in detail using potential-flow theory together with linearized free-surface conditions. The planing surface is modeled as a traveling pressure distribution. Thus the analysis solves the inverse problem of finding the pressure that generates the required hull shape. Specifically, the pressure is discretized as a two-dimensional array of overlapping tent-function pressure elements acting on the free surface. The current work includes a study of the convergence properties of the method with respect to the numerical integration required to compute the response functions of the pressure elements.
