Localized energy gradients and velocity shelters created by boulders, bars, and channel banks are often essential components of aquatic habitat. Two-dimensional hydraulic models have the potential to predict the amounts and locations of such spatially varying flow patterns. However, little effort has been devoted to reproducing these flow features and developing spatial habitat metrics to describe and differentiate between various types of flow patterns. Two-dimensional numerical simulations, based on actual channel geometry, are used here to model a variety of flow patterns encountered in natural streams.
