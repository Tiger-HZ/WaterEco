Abstract
Although ultraviolet (UV) and persulfate (PS) have been widely used in water disinfection process, their
incompleteness of disinfection, such as inducing the production of viable but non-culturable cells (VBNC),
has attracted extensive attention. In this study, the disinfection effect of combined UV and PS was
evaluated from the perspective of inducing Pseudomonas aeruginosa into VBNC, and the roles of SO4•−
and HO• radicals in UV/PS disinfection were also analyzed. UV/PS more effectively inactivated cells and
reduced the number of culturable cells, but induced more VBNC cells. On the other hand, the test of
bacterial dark activation suggested that UV/PS disinfection effectively prolongs the recovery time of
VBNC cells. The mechanisms of UV/PS disinfection were the increase of membrane permeability and
oxidative stress, where SO4•− radicals played more role than HO• radicals. Furthermore, UV/PS
disinfection more signicantly perturbed the metabolism of Pseudomonas aeruginosa (p < 0.05), mainly
involving glyoxylate and dicarboxylic acid metabolism, aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, Alanine, aspartate
and glutamate metabolism, citric acid cycle (TCA cycle). This study, from the production and recovery of
VBNC, provides a new idea for the evaluation of UV, persulfate and their combined disinfection effect, and
provides guidance for improving disinfection process.
1. Introduction
Disinfection is critical for inactivating pathogens and preventing waterborne diseases, but the production
of viable but non-culturable cells (VBNC) during the disinfection process cause potential ecological risks
and health hazards(Achouri et al. 2019), such as ultraviolet (UV) radiation and chlorine disinfection (Chen
et al. 2018, Zhang et al. 2015). In recent years, persulfate (PS) has been widely used in water restoration
and has achieved promising results for water treatment (Bianco et al. 2017, Marjanovic et al. 2018).
Compared with chlorine disinfection, persulfate disinfection can be activated to generate free radicals
with high oxidation potential. Sulfate radicals have strong oxidizing power and are more selective
oxidants than HO• (Liu et al. 2022, Lutze et al. 2015). Methods of persulfate excitation to produce free
radicals comprise such as radiation, homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysis, heat, or ultrasound
(Zhang et al. 2022). Compared to Fe (II), the activation of PS by UV radiation is a more eco-friendly
method for the generation of radicals (Wang et al. 2019), in which SO4•− and HO• were the main radicals
(Kaur et al. 2020), and have been demonstrated to play an important role in degrading pollutants and
inactivating resistant bacteria (De Luca et al. 2017, Hong et al. 2017, Moreno-Andres et al. 2019).
UV disinfection primarily targets intracellular nucleic acids and thus generally preserves cellular integrity
(Bui et al. 2022, Zheng et al. 2021b). Compared with hydroxyl radical (HO•), activated persulfate can
generate more selective and stable sulfate radical (SO4•−) with longer half-life (Moreno-Andres et al. 2019,
Zhuang et al. 2015). Therefore, the role of SO4•− and HO• in UV/PS disinfection is studied in this research.

Heterotrophic plate count (HPC) is a frequently used method for determining whether P. aeruginosa has
been inactivated after disinfection (Pousty et al. 2021, Tran et al. 2022). However, HPC is unable to detect
pathogens in VBNC state that are still metabolically active. These VBNC cells can be "revived" under
environmentally stimulating or permissive growth conditions, restoring their ability to grow (Campo et al.
2021, Fu et al. 2021). When 5-cyano-2,3-ditolyl tetrazolium chloride (CTC) staining is combined with ow
cytometry (FCM), metabolically active cells can be rapidly and accurately detected (Lin et al. 2017,
Wilkinson 2018). Using heavy water (D2O) stable isotope tracing combined with Raman microscopy can
identify and sort active microorganisms at the single-cell level of complex samples (Berry et al. 2015).
Cellular metabolic activity can be assessed by detecting the formation of C-D bonds during fatty acid and
protein synthesis (Guo et al. 2019). Metabolomics enables the quantitative analysis of all metabolites in
an organism, providing information about overall changes in biochemical pathways(Lisec et al. 2006).
Therefore, this research evaluated the disinfection effect of P. aeruginosa by UV/PS through CTC-FCM,
HPC, GC-MS/MS, and D2O-labeled single-cell Raman spectroscopy methods from the perspective of
VBNC.
So far, the disinfection mechanism of the advanced oxidation of PS has not yet been claried. Most
research results indicate that it is related to the integrity of the bacterial cell structure and the leakage of
intracellular functional substances (Wang et al. 2020). Free radicals (SO4•− and HO•) can damage the
integrity of the membrane and cause the leakage of intracellular substances. Membrane lipid
peroxidation changes the physical function of the lipid bilayer, affecting the uidity and permeability of
the membrane (Gaschler &Stockwell 2017). Reactive oxygen species (ROS) can affect metabolism,
oxidative stress, and signal transduction. Superoxide Dismutase (SOD) is the rst line of defense against
active oxygen (Apel &Hirt 2004). The degree of oxidative stress in the cell depends on the amount of
superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl free radicals (Sies et al. 2017). Therefore, this article
evaluates the disinfection mechanism of UV/PS by investigating cell membrane permeability,
Malondialdehyde (MDA), Catalase (CAT), and Superoxide Dismutase (SOD).
The purpose of this study is to understand the effect and mechanism of UV/PS disinfection from the
perspective of VBNC bacteria. P. aeruginosa is famous for its wide distribution and conditional
pathogenicity in the environment, so it was selected as the target microorganism of this study (Martegani
et al. 2020). The cultivability, metabolic activity, and metabolic pathways of P. aeruginosa after
disinfection were evaluated. In addition, the disinfection mechanism of UV/PS was investigated from the
aspects of membrane permeability and oxidative stress. Potential environmental risks that may be
associated with UV/PS disinfection are also assessed.
2 Materials And Methods
2.1 Bacterial strain and Chemicals
The gram-negative bacterium P. aeruginosa [CMCC10104] freeze-dried powder was purchased from
Nanjing Clinic Biotechnology Company. The Reactive oxygen species Assay Kit was purchased from Med
Page 4/21
Chem Express USA. Malondialdehyde (MDA), Superoxide Dismutase (SOD), Catalase (CAT) and Inhibition
and produce superoxide anion assay kit were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of
Bioengineering. Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. provided the sodium persulfate and other
substances used in this study.
2.2 Cell culture
The lyophilized powder of P. aeruginosa was dissolved in 100 mL of sterile LB medium. Then, the
bacteria (OD600 = 0.2 ~ 0.8) were inoculated into 1000 mL of sterile medium at an inoculum of 1% (v/v),
and for shaking culture at 37°C overnight. After that, the bacteria were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10 min
at room temperature and washed twice with 0.9% saline to remove the culture medium for subsequent
experiments.
2.3 Experimental Procedure
The bacterial solution (OD600 = 0.17 ~ 0.18) was placed in a quartz container, and then a certain volume
of sodium persulfate solution was added to a concentration of 1 mM, followed by UV light irradiation (15
W, 254 nm). Bacterial characteristics after 60 min of treatment were analyzed.
2.4 GC-MS/MS to test the effect of UV/PS on cell metabolic
pathways
The pretreatment of GC-MS/MS is an improvement on the method of Lisec et al. (2006). Specically, after
the bacteria were exposed and crushed (SCIENTZ, Ningbo), 1.4 mL of 100% methanol was added to the
bacterial suspension, mixed and centrifuged (11,000 × g, 10 min) followed by 750 µL of chloroform and
centrifuged again for 10 min (220 × g). After freezing dried, 40 µL of methoxyamine hydrochloride and 70
µL of N-methyl-N-(trimethylsilyl) triuoroacetamide were sequentially added, and derivatized at 37°C for 2
h and 30 min in the dark, respectively. Then, 410 µL of ethyl acetate was added for analysis.
2.5 Detection of cell membrane integrity
2.5.1 Measurement of membrane permeability
According to the method of Helander and Mattila-Sandholm (2020), 200 µL of the bacterial suspension
after exposure was added to a 96-well plate and mixed with 1.5 µL of 100 mM NPN. When the cell
membrane is damaged, NPN will enter the phospholipid layer, which will promote uorescence. The
uorescence value was immediately measured with a microplate reader ((TECAN SPARK, USA) at
excitation and emission wavelengths of 350 and 420 nm, respectively. Denote the untreated group as "A"

2.6 Determination of metabolic activity in single cells
D2O was added after mixing the LB broth and post-exposure bacterial solution to obtain the nal 50%

times with sterile water and centrifuged at 8000 × g for 5 min. Finally, 10 µL of the prepared solution was
dropped on the microscope slide and air-dried for measurement before Raman analysis (Thermo DXR2,
USA).

ROS, SOD and CAT were measured using ROS, SOD and CAT kit. After exposure, the bacteria were

2.8 Resuscitation experiment
Bacterial resuscitation ability was measured in 96-well microplates, using OD600 as the standard to
determine the growth of bacteria (Wang et al. 2021a, Zhang et al. 2015). Simply, the bacteria treated in
different ways were diluted according to the previous culture results to ensure that the bacteria are all in
the VBNC state. Fill each well with 100 µL of diluted bacterial solution and 100 µL of LB broth, and place
it in the dark at 37°C for shaking culture. The absorbance at 600 nm was measured by a microplate
reader on days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 9.
2.9 Statistical analysis
All experimental treatments were carried out in triplicate, and the results were expressed as mean
standard deviation. The data were analyzed by one-way ANOVA. Use lowercase letters to indicate
signicant differences (P < 0.05), those with the same letters indicate no signicant differences between
groups, and those without the same letters indicate signicant differences between groups.
3 Results And Discussions
3.1 Disinfection effect of Ultraviolet stimulated persulfate
Figure 1 depicts the number of cultivable P. aeruginosa cells after 60 minutes of UV, PS, UV/PS, and
UV/H2O2 disinfection. UV/PS disinfection inactivates 2.9-log10 cultivable bacteria within 60 min, while the
Page 6/21
cultivable bacteria decrease by 0.9-log10 after 60 min of UV disinfection. Activated bacteria are counted
using FCM combined with CTC. Experimental data demonstrated that UV/PS disinfection also exhibited
better results in inactivating cells than either UV or PS alone, suggesting the important role of HO• and
SO4•− (Tsitonaki et al. 2010). SO4•− is an electrophilic reagent, SO4•− is more prone to electron transfer
reaction than HO•, while HO• is more readily undergoes hydrogen abstraction or addition (Liu et al. 2021).
The different reaction mechanisms of the two highly active substances can act synergistically to
enhance the disinfection effect.
Methanol (MeOH) and tert-butanol (t-BuOH) as radical scavengers were used to further analyze the effect
of radicals (Fig. 1). MeOH can react with HO• (9.7×108mol−1s− 1) and SO4•− (3.2×106 mol− 1s− 1), In
addition, t-BuOH can react with HO• (6.0×108 mol− 1s− 1), which has a much higher reaction rate than
SO4•− (Zhou et al. 2020). Therefore, MeOH is mainly used to quench SO4•− and HO•, t-BuOH is mainly
used to quench HO•. For culturable cells, either adding MeOH or t-BuOH increased the number of
culturable cells, indicating that both SO4•− and HO• played a very important role. Compared with UV/PS,
the addition of MeOH increased the number of cultivable bacteria by 0.2-log10, while the addition of t-
BuOH had no signicant effect on the number of cultivable bacteria, only an increase of 0.01-log10. This
result also indicated that SO4•− played a more important role in effectively reducing culturable cells in
UV/PS system.
3.2 Survival of VBNC Cells in UV, PS, and UV/PS
disinfection
The viable but non-culturable cells (VBNC) are dened by combining HPC and FCM assay results in this
study (Fig. 1). Figure 1 shows that all disinfection treatments induce bacteria to enter the VBNC state in
varying degrees. Among them, UV can make bacteria enter the VBNC state, which is the same as the
previous conclusions (Zhang et al. 2015), while UV/PS and UV/H2O2 disinfection resulted in a signicant
increase in the proportion of VBNC was unexpected. This difference in disinfection may be caused by
different free radicals. The cell viability of bacteria was examined after UV/PS and UV/H2O2 disinfection
at the same oxidant concentration of 1 mM to further investigate the functions of HO• and SO4•− in
UV/PS disinfection. P. aeruginosa cultivable bacteria after UV/PS and UV/H2O2 disinfection were 1.91-
log10 and 1.87-log10, respectively, while VBNC cells were 2.41-log10 (UV/H2O2) and 2.31-log10 (UV/PS).
The degradation results of Nitrobenzene (NB) showed that the HO• concentration measured in the UV/PS
process is lower than that in the UV/H2O2 process at the same oxidant concentration of 1 mM (Fig. S2).
All these results suggest that HO• may play an important role in the induction of VBNC cells compared
with SO4•−. Unfortunately, although UV/PS has advantages in both bactericidal ability and reduced
cultural capacity, UV/PS increases the relative abundance of VBNC, which is more likely to lead to
potential risks.
3.3 Assessing the resurrection risk of VBNC
Page 7/21
Figure 2 depicts P. aeruginosa reactivation after 60 minutes of UV, PS, and UV/PS disinfection. The
bacteria in the control group quickly reach logarithmic phase (OD600 = 0.8 ~ 0.9) on the rst day (Fig. 2a).
However, UV group and UV/PS group show the opposite situation that OD600 of bacteria only reach 0.4 
~ 0.5 on the rst day, which shows that both UV group and UV/PS disinfection have good bactericidal
effects (inducing bacteria to enter VBNC or killing bacteria). The difference is that persulfate shows
sustained antibacterial effect not bactericidal effect (Fig. 2c). In the process of recovery, the VBNC
bacteria induced by UV and UV/PS groups begin to recover from the rst day, but the recovery of VBNC
bacteria induced by UV/PS group is slower (Fig. 2d), and the total number of bacteria recovered is one
order of magnitude less than that treated by UV. Therefore, UV/PS has better bactericidal effect than
single UV disinfection in inhibiting resuscitation and bacterial death.
To further determine the metabolic activity of the bacteria within 1–2 d, single-cell Raman assays were
performed on the bacteria after re-culturing for 36 h. Figure S3a depicts the C-D/(C-D + C-H) ratios, which
represent P. aeruginosa single-cell metabolic activity after 60 min of UV, PS, and UV/PS disinfection. The
C-D/(C-D + C-H) ratio of bacteria remains relatively low (35%) after 36 h of recultivation, and increases to
36.2% after UV/PS disinfection, which indicates that cells still keep the ability to continue to absorb
nutrients after UV/PS disinfection. Figure S3b shows that C-H + C-D in the blank group is second only to
the UV/PS group, and the decrease in the proportion is the uptake of C-D by the cells. Metabolic activity
based on substrate uptake involves complex active pathways, including transcription, translation, energy
dependence, and enzyme activity (Ye et al. 2019, Ye et al. 2017).
The results of cell recovery experiments and Raman data showed that UV/PS cells had higher cell
viability after 36 h of re-cultivation, but the total bacterial count was lower than that of the UV group.
Sublethal injury cells can be quickly recovered when they are re-cultured in an appropriate medium and
temperature, and regain their normal physiological and biochemical properties (Nasi ł owska et al. 2019).
However, the main difference between sublethal injury cells and VBNC cells is that the cell membrane of
sublethal injured cells is damaged and more sensitive to external stimuli(Li et al. 2017). Through
resuscitation experiments, Raman data, and the analysis of membrane permeability and lipid
peroxidation degree in 3.3, it can be speculated that UV/PS disinfection is more likely to cause sublethal
damage to cells.
3.4 Membrane integrity of cells after UV, PS, UV/PS
disinfection
The permeability of the cell membrane reects the integrity of the bacterial cell membrane to a certain
extent (Zheng et al. 2021a). Different disinfections damage the integrity of cell membranes (Fig. 3a). The
dye permeability of bacteria in the UV/PS disinfection group signicantly increased by 167.75%, which
means that the cell membrane has been severely damaged. The dye permeability of bacteria in the UV
disinfection group and PS disinfection group increased by 132.46% and 66.08%, respectively. The cell
membrane, as the rst barrier, could maintain a stable metabolic intracellular environment. Once the cell
membrane is disrupted, the entire bacterial metabolic system falls apart (Liu et al. 2019). In order to
Page 8/21
further conrm the role of the two main free radicals in UV/PS system, UV/H2O2 and UV/PS groups with
MeOH and t-BuOH were set up, respectively. The low cell permeability of UV/H2O2 indicates that HO• has
no signicant effect on cell membranes. After stopping the action of HO• at the same time, it was found
that the permeability did not signicantly decrease, and after stopping the action of SO4•− and HO• at the
same time, the permeability of the cell membrane decreased by 60%. In short, SO4•− has a greater effect
on the permeability of cell membranes than HO•.
To further clarify the mechanism of cell membranes, we tested the degree of lipid peroxidation and
protein content (Fig, 3b and Fig. S4). As the main component of cellular membranes, lipids have an
indispensable role in maintaining the structural integrity of cells. Like the results of cell permeability, the
degree of lipid peroxidation of the cell membrane also increases. Specically, in the experimental group,
lipid damage by UV and UV/PS increased by 28.91% and 43.10%, respectively. Meanwhile, among
bacterial metabolites (Table 1), linoleic acid was only detected in the control group, and the other groups
are all below the detection line. These results indicate that different disinfection methods can cause
different degrees of oxidative damage to lipids. As shown in Figure S3, the protein content of UV/PS
disinfection decreased signicantly. The damage of both structural protein and functional protein will
cause the loss of normal physiological function of cells. The dual destruction of lipids and proteins by
UV/PS is the main cause of membrane perforation, which lays the foundation for the continuous action
of subsequent free radicals.

3.5 Response of P. aeruginosa to oxidative stress
ROS is very important for cell signaling, but excessive ROS can cause genome instability and damage
lipids, proteins, and deoxyribonucleic acid (Wang et al. 2017). As shown in Fig. 4a, compared with the
control group, the level of ROS increased by 22.8% and 41.4% after UV and UV/PS disinfection,
respectively. In the oxidative stress system, a UV/H2O2 experimental group was established to compare
Page 9/21
and analyze the effects of SO4•− and HO• on ROS. As shown in Fig. 4a, compared with the control group,
ROS levels increased by 23.2% after UV/H2O2 disinfection, which was lower than that of UV/PS
disinfection. This indicates that the presence of SO4•−in UV/PS system promotes stronger oxidative
stress in cells.
Organisms have formed a complete antioxidant system in the long evolutionary process, which is divided
into enzymatic antioxidant systems (such as CAT, SOD) and non-enzymatic antioxidant systems (Ahmad
et al. 2020). The enzymatic antioxidant systems indicators are shown in Fig. 4, and the non-enzymatic
antioxidant system indicators are shown in Table 1. As shown in Fig. 4c, SOD levels by 7.2%, 7.8%, 7.8%,
and 6.0%, respectively, while CAT levels increased by 25.8%, 34.4%, 13.1% and 15.9%. SOD acts as a
central regulator of ROS levels, capable of catalyzing the decomposition of superoxide into hydrogen
peroxide and water (Landis &Tower 2005). The level of intracellular superoxide is consistent with the
change trend of SOD (Fig. 4b). Specically, compared with control group, UV, UV/PS, PS disinfection
increased by 2.7%, 19.4%, 17.5%, and UV/H2O2, H2O2 decreased by 4.6% and 9.7%. These data suggest
that SO4•− produced by PS can induce the production of more ·O2− and accelerate cell death. By
decomposing hydrogen peroxide, catalase (CAT) is involved in cellular antioxidant defense (Ho et al.
2004), and shows its importance when peroxidase is depleted (Liochev 2013). The signicant increase in
CAT levels after UV/PS disinfection indicated that the cells suffered severe oxidative damage.
At the same time, Table 1 shows that the non-enzymatic antioxidant system activity of UV/PS is
signicantly lower than that of the UV and PS groups. Glycine, L-Glutamate, L-Cysteine, 5-Oxoproline are
part of Glutathione metabolism. The decrease of glycine, L-Glutamate, 5-Oxoproline levels ultimately
affects the biosynthesis of glutathione (GSH). Glutathione is a signicant non-enzymatic cellular
antioxidant that works with selenium-dependent glutathione peroxidase to remove free radicals such
hydrogen peroxide, hydroxyl radicals, and peroxynitrite (Zilmer et al. 2005). GSH depletion is an early sign
of the cell death process (Zhang et al. 2021). The decreased activity of the non-enzymatic antioxidant
system also means that the intracellular redox is unbalanced, and the sharp increase of ROS causes the
enzyme to be inhibited or even inactivated (Wang et al. 2021b).
3.6 Global visualization of metabolic pathway changes in
response to UV, UV/PS, PS
The main metabolites and corresponding metabolic pathways of P. aeruginosa treated with UV, UV/PS
and PS are shown in Fig. 5. 59 metabolites with signicant differences were screened, mainly involving
carbohydrate metabolism, amino acid metabolism, genetic information processing (such as aminoacyl-
tRNA biosynthesis) and energy metabolism (such as sulfur metabolism) (Fig. 5b). Specically, as shown
in Fig. 6a-b, glyoxylate and dicarboxylic acid metabolism, sulfur metabolism and Citrate cycle (TCA cycle)
were down-regulated, but alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism was up-regulated after UV and
UV/PS disinfection. The results suggest that both UV and UV/PS can defend against external stimuli by
down-regulating carbohydrate metabolism and up-regulating protein metabolism.

To further discover the reason for the increased VBNC cells ratio caused by UV/PS, the metabolite levels
after UV and UV/PS disinfection were analyzed (Fig. 6c). KEGG analysis found that the metabolic
pathways signicantly disturbed by UV/PS disinfection mainly included Citrate cycle (TCA cycle),
Glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, Alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, Aminoacyl-
tRNA biosynthesis (P < 0.05). Compared to UV disinfection, it was found that D-Glucose, D-Alanine, and L-
Cysteine all increase signicantly after UV/PS disinfection. D-Glucose mainly affects the glycolysis
pathway, which is the energy source of life and central metabolite in many biological pathways (Korch et
al. 2013). Liao et al. (2020) found that most energy-dependent physiological activities of bacteria in the
VBNC state are restricted. Therefore, regulation and redistribution of energy is one of the main factors
that keeps bacteria into VBNC state. Moreover, glucose promotes the development of P. aeruginosa
biolm, which can provide support and protection mechanisms for cell metabolism and growth (She et al.
2019). The manufacture of bacterial peptidoglycan, which protects bacteria against osmotic lysis,
requires D-alanine (D-Ala) metabolism. (Jiang et al. 2021). The existence of L-Cysteine can maintain
protein stability. The up-regulation of these three substances may be one of the material bases for UV/PS
induced VBNC bacteria to maintain certain activity.
4 Conclusions
This study investigated the eciency and mechanism of UV/PS disinfection on inactivation of P.
aeruginosa from the perspective of VBNC. Compared with UV, UV/PS disinfection shows a stronger
bactericidal effect by reducing the number of culturable bacteria, where SO4•− radicals in UV/PS system
played a more important role. From the perspective of VBNC, although UV/PS disinfection induces more
bacteria to enter the VBNC state with certain potential risk, it prolongs the recovery time of VBNC bacteria
compared with UV disinfection. The production of VBNC is more due to HO• than SO4•−, while the lasting
antibacterial effect is owing to SO4•−. Although UV/PS disinfection leads to the death of bacteria through
the increase of cell membrane permeability and oxidative stress, metabolic regulation of typical
carbohydrates and typical amino acids causes some bacteria to enter the VBNC state.
