The present work analyses the relationship between climate and Italian forest ecosystems using the International Co-operative Programme on Assessment and Monitoring of Air Pollution Effects on Forests (ICP Forests) monitoring network − active since 1997. Using a historical series of meteorological data collected homogeneously in Italy, selected climatic variables of interest to forestry (such as temperature, precipitation, humidity, and wind) and their seasonal and inter-annual variability have been extensively examined in this contribution. The empirical analysis highlights relevant trends, including recurring patterns and climatic anomalies, with particular attention to 102 extreme events and their potential impact on ecosystems. Another section of our study is dedicated to the quantitative (continuous) assessment of water balance and soil water content at selected sites corresponding with gauging field stations. In brief, the results confirm the importance of continuous monitoring to understand the response of forests to climate change, and to support long-term ecological and physiological analysis of natural ecosystems. The work finally highlights the fundamental contribution of ICP Forests’ network to the detailed characterization of Italian forest (weather and climate) conditions and to the assessment of the future impacts of climate variability on natural habitats.
