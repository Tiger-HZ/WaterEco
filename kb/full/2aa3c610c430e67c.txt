The black soldier fly (BSF, Hermetia illucens L.) has gained interest for its ability to convert organic waste into high-quality protein. The eggs of BSF are typically sold in fixed quantities, which often does not match the fluctuating waste input of processing facilities; post-hatch starvation offers a practical strategy to buffer this supply-demand mismatch. However, how post-hatch starvation affects larval development and nutrient accumulation remains unclear. After being reared to approximately 5 mg, neonate larvae were subjected to starvation periods of 0, 7, 14, and 21 days in wheat bran.
