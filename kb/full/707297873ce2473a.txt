Effect of organic manure and zinc levels on growth, yield
and nutrient uptake by wheat (Triticum aestivum L.)
Umesh Kumar1, Hansraj Shivran2, Anil Jakhar3, Sidra Qidwai4, Vaishali Chaturvedevi5
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i9m.6624
A BSTRACT
A field experiment entitled “Effect of Organic Manure and Zinc Levels on Growth, Yield and Nutrient
Uptake by Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.)” was conducted on loamy sand soil at Research Farm, School
of Agriculture, Suresh Gyan Vihar University, Jaipur during Rabi 2024-25 seasons. The treatments in
wheat during rabi seasons comprising 20 combinations having four organic manures FYM @ 10 t/ha,
FYM @ 20 t/ha, Vermicompost @ 3 t/ha and Vermicompost @ 6 t/ha and five zinc levels (Control,
Foliar spray of ZnSO4 @ 0.5%, Soil application of ZnSO4 @ 6 kg/ha, Soil application of ZnSO4 @
12 kg/ha and Soil application of ZnSO4 @ 6 kg/ha + Foliar spray of ZnSO4 @ 0.5%) treatments was
laid out in a spilt plot with three replications. Wheat was sown at 22.5 cm row spacing using seed rate
of 100 kg ha−1.
The application of vermicompost @ 6.0 t/ha significantly increased the seed, stover and biological yield
of wheat which was significantly higher than FYM @ 10 t/ha and vermicompost @ 3 t/ha, but remained
statistically comparable with FYM @ 20 t/ha. Zinc application significantly improved the growth, yield,
nutrient content and economic performance of wheat. Among the treatments, soil application of ZnSO4
@ 6 kg/ha + foliar spray of ZnSO4 @ 0.5% significantly increased the grain, straw and biological
yields. The highest net return ( ₹ 92,737/ha) was obtained with vermicompost @ 6.0 t/ha, followed by
vermicompost @ 3 t/ha ( ₹ 91,606/ha) and FYM @ 20 t/ha ( ₹ 86,000/ha). The maximum net return
( ₹ 94,725/ha) was recorded with soil application of ZnSO4 @ 6 kg/ha + foliar spray of ZnSO4 @ 0.5%,
followed by soil application of ZnSO4 @ 12 kg/ha ( ₹ 91,293/ha), soil application of ZnSO4 @ 6 kg/ha
( ₹ 87,661/ha) and foliar spray of ZnSO4 @ 0.5% ( ₹ 83,444/ha).
Keywords: Effective tiller, Grain, Straw and yield
Introduction
Wheat (Triticum aestivum L) is the most important food grain crop among cereals and stands next only to
rice in our country.
India is self-sufficient in catering to the needs of major food staples like rice and wheat. On an average,
wheat contributes to around 35% of total food grain production and 21% of cultivated area in the country
(Singh et al., 2020) [1]. In India, wheat (Triticum aestivam L.) is the second most important cereal crop after
rice and pre-dominant winter season crop of north-western plains zone. Wheat is grown on 33.60 million
hectares (m ha) lands with total production of 120.65 million tons (m t) contributes 34 per cent of the total
food grain production in the country with the productivity of 3591 kg ha−1 (Ministry of Agriculture, GOI
2025-26). Wheat Productivity has to be raised to meet ever increasing food demand in the country. In
Rajasthan area under wheat crop is 3.59 m ha with production of 15.05 m t and productivity of 4197 kg ha−1
(Ministry of Agriculture, GOR, 2025-26). The application of FYM and vermicompost in the soil helps in in-
creasing the fertility of the soil as physical condition including its water holding capacity. Organic manures,
which were perhaps the main sources of plant nutrients in traditional agriculture, receive less emphasis with
the advent of high analysis chemical fertilizers. The decision on the optimum use of fertilizer required
knowledge of crop response to applied fertilizer, inherent nutrients by soil and its short or long-term fate ef-
fects (Kohli et al., 2025) [2]. Zinc is essential micronutrient and has a number of crucial functions in plant
system. Its deficiency in plants results in significant decreases in both productivity and nutrient quality. But
30 to 40% of the total area of the world is reported zinc deficient. Zn fertilizers are used in the bio fortifica-
tion and to fulfill the Zn deficiency of cereal crop. The concentration of zinc in wheat grain increased by
18.8% and the dry weight of seedling roots and fruits were significantly improved by applying zinc fertilizer
(Ali et al. 2021).

Results and discussion
Effect of organic manure
The results revealed that grain, straw and biological yields of wheat
were significantly influenced by the application of organic manures.
Vermicompost @ 6.0 t/ha recorded the highest grain yield
(4978 kg/ha), straw yield (6199 kg/ha) and biological yield
(11177 kg/ha), representing increases of 22.58, 5.62 and 9.28 per cent
in grain yield and 25.56, 6.51 and 7.66 per cent in straw yield over
FYM @ 10 t/ha, FYM @ 20 t/ha and vermicompost @ 3 t/ha, respec-
tively. The greater productivity under higher vermicompost applica-
tion may be attributed to improved nutrient availability, enhanced mi-
crobial activity, better root growth and efficient utilization of nutri-
ents, which promoted greater biomass production and translocation of
assimilates towards grain formation (Kaur et al., 2026) [3]) and
Debebe et al. (2026) [4]. The highest net return ( ₹ 92,737/ha) was ob-
tained with vermicompost @ 6.0 t/ha, which was 22.46, 7.83 and 1.23
per cent higher than FYM @ 10 t/ha, FYM @ 20 t/ha and vermicom-
post @ 3 t/ha, respectively. The higher net return under vermicompost
application may be attributed to better nutrient availability, improved
soil physical and biological properties and consequently greater crop
productivity. Vermicompost supplies nutrients in readily available
forms and also enhances microbial activity, which can improve nutri-
ent-use efficiency and crop growth. Similar findings were reported by
Meena et al. (2024) [5] and Niranjan et al. (2024) [6]. Thus, vermi-
compost @ 3 t/ha appeared to be more economical in terms of return
generated per rupee invested. Similar economic benefits from vermi-
compost-based nutrient management in wheat have been reported by
Singh et al. (2018) [7].
Effect of zinc levels
The highest grain, straw and biological yield was recorded with sow-
ing on 20th November, which was significantly superior to 10th
December and remained statistically at par with 30th November. The
per cent increased the seed yield by 20th November and 30th
November in tune of 9.32 and 4.59 per cent over to 10th December,
respectively. The higher yield under timely sowing may be attributed
to favourable temperature during vegetative and reproductive phases,
longer grain-filling duration and better synchronization of crop
growth with available moisture and solar radiation. The present find-
ings are supported by Yadav and Khan (2025).
The highest net return ( ₹ 91,006 ha ⁻ ¹) was recorded under the weed-
free treatment, which was significantly superior to the weedy check
( ₹ 54,979 ha ⁻ ¹), one hand weeding @ 30 DAS ( ₹ 83,139 ha ⁻ ¹), metsul-
furon methyl @ 4.0 g ha ⁻ ¹ at 30 DAS ( ₹ 86,997 ha ⁻ ¹) and carfentra-
zone ethyl @ 20 g ha ⁻ ¹ at 30 DAS ( ₹ 81,458 ha ⁻ ¹). The higher prof-
itability under weed-free conditions was mainly due to greater grain
and straw yields resulting from effective suppression of weed compe-
tition. Among herbicides, metsulfuron methyl recorded comparatively
higher net returns and B:C ratio because of its effective weed control
and relatively low application cost (Bhakar et al., 2026) [8].
