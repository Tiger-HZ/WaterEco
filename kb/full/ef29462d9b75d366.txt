Abstract Understanding the latent risk of hydrocarbon exposures at the cellular level is a challenge due to environmental mixtures complexity and the diversity of biological targets. Oil spills occur frequently in highly producing zones, such as Vaca Muerta in Neuquén-Argentina. While most spills remain confined to surrounding soil, some can reach waterways, posing potential risks to aquatic organisms, livestock and humans. In this study, we first determined the cytotoxicity of a water-accommodated oil fraction (WAF) from Neuquén, in human breast cancer and non-tumorigenic cells. These effects were compared to those induced by anthracene, a representative polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH), considered the most toxic fraction in oil.
