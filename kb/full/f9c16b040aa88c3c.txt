 A new and unusual water mite species of the subgenus Truncaturus Thor, 1901 from Tasmania, Australia, and notes on other Arrenurus species from Australia (Acari: Hydrachnidia: Arrenuridae)

 Abstract 
 Species of the subgenus Truncaturus are usually small, unmodified and with a yellowish or brownish colour. Here a new species of the subgenus is described from Tasmania, which is not only relatively large, but also coloured dark red, a combination not found before within the subgenus. Furthermore, some remarks are made on several other Arrenurus species from Australia. 
 Introduction 
 The genus Arrenurus Dugès, 1834 is the most species-rich genus of the water mites with more than 1000 species known (Smit 2020a). The genus has a cosmopolitan distribution, and occurs on every continent except Antarctica. From Australia 63 species are known (Cook 1986; Halík 1940, 1941; Harvey 1998; Lundblad 1941, 1947; Smit 1992, 1997, 1999, 2002, 2003a,b, 2007, 2010, 2021; Smith & Harvey 1989; K. Viets 1955; K.O. Viets 1975, 1981, 1984). Within Australia, Arrenurus species are known from almost all states and territories, only from the Australian Capital Territory no species are known. Most species are known from tropical Australia (in brackets the number of species): Queensland (27), Western Australia (25), Northern Territory (17), New South Wales (14), Tasmania (11), Victoria (9), South Australia (4) and Australian Capital Territory (0). 
 Currently, 11 subgenera are known within the genus (Smit 2020b). The following subgenera occur in Australia: Arrenurus s.s., Brevicaudaturus Smit, 1997, Dividuracarus Smit, 1997, Megaluracarus K. Viets, 1911, Micruracarus K. Viets, 1991, Rhinophoracarus K. Viets, 1916 and Truncaturus Thor, 1901. The subgenus Dividuracarus is only known from Australia. However, the subgeneric classification is artificial. Cook (1974) stated that ''the subgenera are poorly defined, undoubtedly polyphyletic and are almost meaningless in indicating true relationships for many of the species groups''. Many authors still use the subgeneric subdivision to subdivide this large genus in smaller units. 
 Most species of the subgenus Truncaturus are small and with a yellowish or brownish idiosoma colour, not only in Australia but also in other bioregions of the world. Here an unusual new species is described, which is not only relatively large, but also dark red, a combination not found in any other Truncaturus species. 
 Material and methods 
 The results presented in this paper are based on a collecting trip to New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania and South Australia in the period November 2025-March 2026. Numbers are given as males/females/deutonymphs. Abbreviations used: asl = above sea level; Cx-I = first coxae; NP = National Park, TMAG = Tasmanian Museum and Art Gallery, Hobart. 
 Measurements of segments of palp and legs are of dorsal margins. All measurements are in µm. Ventral idiosoma length is measurand from tip of Cx-I to posterior idiosoma margin. 
 All material is collected by the author. Non-type material is lodged in Naturalis Biodiversity Center, Leiden. The data of common and widespread Arrenurus -species not treated in this paper will be uploaded to Gbif, and can be examined on the website of Atlas of Living Australia ( https://www.ala.org.au ) later on.

 Material examined 
 Holotype male, Little Pine Lagoon, Little Pine Lagoon Conservation Area, Tasmania, Australia, 41°59.883′ S 146°36.685′ E, 1009 m asl, 12 Jan. 2026 (TMAG). Paratype: one female, same data as holotype (TMAG). 
 Diagnosis 
 Male: Male and female relatively large, colour of the idiosoma dark red; dorsal shield of male incomplete, dorsal shield of female complete; male idiosoma with a straight posterior margin. 
 Description 
 Male holotype &mdash; Idiosoma colour dark red, 778 long and 672 wide, ventrally 794 long. Idiosoma posteriorly straight, anteriorly convex (fig. 1a). Dorsal shield incomplete. All glandularia striated; associated setae of Dgl-4 distanced from glandularia. Cx-I extending slightly beyond anterior idiosoma margin. Medial margin of Cx-IV longer than medial margin of Cx-III. Genital plates longer than wide, straight (fig. 1b), genital field 405 long; gonopore70 long. Excretory pore near posterior idiosoma margin. Length of P1-5: 32, 80, 48, 78, 64. P2 medially with four stout setae (fig. 1c), dorsally with two stout setae; P1 dorsally with one stout seta, P3 dorsally with one seta, P4 dorsally with two fine setae. Length of I-leg-4-6: 154, 164, 150. Length of IV-leg-4-6: 186, 162, 178; IV-leg-4 without spur. Legs with many swimming setae. 
 Female paratype &mdash; Idiosoma colour dark red, dorsally 1077 long and 859 wide, ventrally 1021 long. Due to its very dark colour it's not visible if the glandularia are striated as in the male. Dorsal shield complete, 883 long and 705 wide. Cx-I not extending beyond anterior idiosoma margin. Medial margin of Cx-IV longer than idiosoma margin of Cx-III. Gonopore 138 long, with small sclerotized areas anteriorly and posteriorly. Genital field 462 long, genital plates straight, directed obliquely towards lateral idiosoma margin, longer than broad (fig. 1d). Length of P1-5: 34, 80, 68, 84, 44; palp as in male but P2 medially with five setae. Length of I-leg-4-6: 166, 178, 170. Length of IV-leg-4-6: 235, 211, 203. Legs with many swimming setae. 
 Etymology 
 Named for its unusual shape and colour for a Truncaturus species (Latin: insolitus = unusual, strange, uncommon, odd). 
 Remarks 
 The new species is the sixth Truncaturus species from Australia. Species of the subgenus Truncaturus are usually small and coloured yellowish or brownish. The new species is an exception, it is relatively large and the colour is dark red. Three other Australian Truncaturus are also large, i.e. A. haswelli Cook, 1986, A. indentatus Smit, 2010 and A. novaecambricus Smit, 2010. Arrenurus haswelli and A. indentatus differ from the new species in having the posterior idiosoma margin with a median cleft. The male of Arrenurus novaecambricus is larger (850-907), the posterior idiosoma margin is concave, the genital plates are shorter and slightly bowed and the idiosoma colour is yellowish brown.
