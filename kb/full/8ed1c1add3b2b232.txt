Abstract
The mussel Mytilus coruscus is an important and very popular seafood in China and widespread along the eastern coast of China. In this study, we
investigated the molecular response of mussel gonads to cadmium hyperaccumulation at two concentrations (80 and 200 µg/L) for 30 days using
ionomics and proteomics techniques. The shrinkage of the cells and moderate hemocytic inltration were observed in the Cd-treated groups. The
strontium, selenium (Se), and zinc contents were signicantly altered, and the relationships between iron, copper, Se, manganese, calcium, sodium,
and magnesium were also signicantly altered. Label-free quantitative proteomics analysis revealed a total of 227 differentially expressed proteins.
These proteins were associated with multiple biological processes, including the tricarboxylic acid cycle, structural reorganization of cells,
biosynthesis of amino acids, inammatory response of cells and tumorigenesis. Nonetheless, our ionomics and proteomics analysis revealed that
mussels could partly alleviate the adverse effects of Cd by altering the metal contents and correlations between minerals, thereby enhancing the
biosynthesis of some amino acids and activity of antioxidant enzymes. Overall, this study provides insight into the mechanism underlying Cd toxicity
in mussel gonads from a metal and protein perspective.
1. Introduction
The East China Sea faces enormous environmental challenges due to the release of industrial euent and rapid development of coastal aquaculture.
Metal pollution is one of the most common and predominant types of anthropogenic pollution. According to previous studies, the cadmium (Cd)
concentration in seawater has reached to 16 µg/L along the coast in northern China (Ma et al., 1995; Zhang et al., 2000). Additionally, the Cd
concentration has reached 50 µg/L in some heavily polluted estuaries, harbors and ports (Chester 1990). The mussel Mytilus coruscus is one of the
major mariculture species in Zhejiang, China. Mussels can enrich metals in the aquatic environment through the food chain by an enrichment factor
of 105~106 (Wallace et al., 2003). Based on previous studies by our team, the average contents of Cd in mussels from the East China Sea were 1.8
mg/kg (including visceral tissues) and 0.56 mg/kg (muscle tissues) respectively (Gu et al., 2020, 2014).
Some studies have attempted to address why mussels survive despite their substantially high body burdens of metals. Shellsh generally undergo
detoxication of trace metals at the subcellular level via two pathways. It has been found that the ratio of copper (Cu) in cell debris and zinc (Zn) in
metallothionein-like protein increased simultaneously with increasing metal concentrations, suggesting that oysters are detoxied by chelation
between metals (Yu et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2011). A metabolomics study revealed changes in metabolites in three life stages in mussels, suggesting
that osmotic and anaerobiosis regulation occurred under Cd stress (Wu et al., 2017). Xu et al (2016) reported that D-shape larval mussels could
signicantly regulate triosephosphate isomerase and metallothionein to resist the damage of Cd. Cd may affect reproductive function in humans and
animals by interfering with the endocrine system (Henson and Chedrese, 2004; Thompson and Bannigan, 2008). Cd exposure is associated with
defective steroidogenesis (Piasek et al., 2002), and it inhibits oocyte maturation (Leoni, et al., 2002) in mammals.
Proteomics is widely employed in biological research to obtain high-throughput protein data. Some studies have analyzed the expression patterns of
proteins under metal stress using two-dimensional-gel-electrophoresis-proteomics (2D-SDS-PAGE) (Donagh et al., 2005; Helmholz et al., 2015;
Company et al., 2019). However, a nite number of proteins can be identied using 2D-SDS-PAGE. Recently label-free quantitative proteomics and
isobaric tags for relative and absolute quantitation based proteomics have become important technologies, they are able to not only identify a larger
number of proteins but also quantify the relative abundances of protein in various samples. Previous studies have used these technologies to
determine the proteomic responses of bivalves to niclosamide, ocean acidication and environmental variation (Sanson et al., 2020; Quan et al.,
2015; Venkataraman et al., 2019). Ionomics has been gradually applied to the discussion of the relationship between elements in organisms. Gagné
et al (2016) used a metallomic approach to study the elemental congurations of freshwater mussels exposed to with silver (Ag) nanoparticles, and
found that exposure to Ag nanoparticles produced a characteristic change in the elemental composition of gills, digestive gland and gonad tissues in
freshwater mussels. The contents of strontium (Sr) and barium (Ba) were readily decreased by Ag and appeared to respond more sensitively to nano
Ag than to ionic Ag.
Nevertheless, few studies have employed both ionomics and proteomics to investigate bivalve shellsh under metal stress. In this study, we examined
changes in mussel gonads after exposure to Cd from two aspects: metal and protein. Our study aimed to provide a more mechanistic understanding
of the systemic ionomics and proteomics responses of gonad mussels with high body burdens of Cd.
2. Materials And Methods
2.1. Mussel growth conditions
Adult Mytilus coruscus were collected from a mussel farm in Zhoushan, China in June 2019. A total of 135 mussels were randomly and equally
divided into three groups (control and two Cd-treatment groups), and they were kept in seawater in a plastic culture box for 7 days to adapt to the
laboratory environment. During the experiment, two groups of mussels were exposed to two different concentrations of Cd2+: (80 and 200 µg/L).
Exposure concentrations used are higher than that is typically found in an environmental pollution mainly to consider: the accumulation of Cd in
mussels under short laboratory exposure time (30 d) simulating a year or even two years of exposure in a real environment. Each experimental group
included three replicate boxes with each containing 15 individuals. The seawater in the boxes was replaced with 100% fresh seawater containing
Page 3/15
Cd2+ every 48 h, and the mussels were fed chlorella (1 mL of chlorella/20 L of seawater) every 7 days. The laboratory exposure test was conducted
for 30 days. Next, 10 mussels were dissected promptly and their gonads were collected. After grinding and mixing, the gonads were frozen in liquid
nitrogen and stored in a refrigerator at − 80°C until analysis.
2.2 Tissue sectioning and H.E. staining
After exposure for 30 days, the gonad samples were xed in 4% neutral formaldehyde for 24 h. Then, they were embedded in paran. Serial sections
with a thickness of 5mm were routinely dewaxed and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), which included rehydration, hematoxylin staining,
differentiation, eosin staining, dehydration, and neutral resin xation on the slide prior to observation.
2.3 Sample preparation and determination of enzyme activities
The activity of the enzymes catalase (CAT), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), and glutathione s-transferase (GST) were determined in triplicate
samples for all treatments. For sample preparation, 0.5 g of mussel gonads (from 10 individuals) was ground in 0.86% sodium chloride (g/L). The
extracts were centrifuged at 4000 g at 4°C for 20 min, the supernatant was collected and the protein concentration was determined using a
commercial kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Biological Reagent Co., Ltd.) for the analysis of enzymatic activity. All procedures were conducted following the
kit instructions.
2.4 Metal content
The metal contents of gonad samples were determined using an Aglient 7900 inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) spectrometer.
Briey, 0.2 g of gonad samples (from 10 individuals) was digested in 5 mL of HNO3 and 2 mL of H2O2 in a microwave. Then, the samples were
diluted in Milli-Q water. Multielement calibration standard solutions (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) were used after dilution. Scandium,
germanium, rhodium, and terbium were used as the internal standards, and an external calibration was performed using the multielement standard at
concentrations between 1 and 1000 µg/L. Certied reference materials (GBW10050 Prawns Powder and GBW 10024 Scallop Powder) were used to
verify the accuracy of the analysis.
2.5 Extraction and analysis of protein
Briey, each of the pooled frozen gonad samples (from 10 individuals) was suspended in lysis solution (50 mM NH4HCO3, 75 mM NaCl, and 2%
sodium deoxycholate) containing a protease inhibitor (Halt Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail, Thermo Scientic, USA), homogenized,
sonicated (pulse on 2 s; pulse off 4 s; 15%W) and centrifuged (at 10,000 g, for 10 min, at 4°C). Then, the supernatants which contained soluble
proteins were removed. The proteins were quantied using the Bradford method (Bradford 1976). A 100 µg protein solution was dissolved in the
dissolution buffer (50 mM NH4HCO3,75 mM NaCl, and 2% sodium deoxycholate, and 1X protease inhibitor). Added 1.0 µg/µL of Trypsin to each
sample (1.0 µg of enzyme for every 100 µg of protein substrate) and bathed in water at 37°C for 4h, then supplement with 1.0 µg of Trypsin and
bathed in water at 37°C for 12h. The peptides were separated using an Easy-nanoLC 1200 system (Thermo Fisher Scientic, USA). The LC fractions
were analyzed by MS/MS using an Orbitrap Fusion Lumos mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientic). The conditions used for the peptide
separation through the Easy-nanoLC 1200 system and the subsequent mass spectrometer were listed in Tables 1 and 2.

One-way and two-way ANOVA in SPSS 22.0 was applied to test the signicance difference of metals among multiple groups. The relationships
between metals was assessed by the Pearson correlation in GraphPad 8.0.
3. Results And Discussions
3.1 Morphological changes in mussel gonads under Cd treatment
When we observed the histological changes of mussel gonads using H&E staining (Fig. 1) to evaluate the effect of Cd exposure on morphology we
found that the gonads in the control group were dense, and their cell morphology was round and full. However, in the Cd-treated groups, severe
shrinkage of cells was noted, and the number of cells was reduced. Therefore, the tissue appeared sparse. Additionally, moderate hemocytic
inltration was observed in the Cd-treated groups, suggesting cell inammation (Fig. 1B and C). Previous studies reported that oyster gonads treated
with mercury (Hg) exhibited severe follicular atrophy and a signicant decrease in sperm cell abundance, making the entire tissue appear sparse
(Zhang et al., 2013). Therefore, it can be presumed that the accumulation of heavy metals, such as Cd and Hg, in the body disrupts the gonadal
function of shellsh.
 
3.2 Cd-induced changes enzyme activity in the gonad mussels
The activities of GSH-Px and GST in the high exposure group were signicantly higher than those in the control group (p < 0.05), indicating that
oxidative damage to the gonads of mussels was severe, and the activities of enzymes were induced to resist oxidative damage. Additionally, the
antioxidant stress system was fully activated. In the low exposure group, there was no signicant effect on the activity of GSH-Px. In contrast, the
activities of GST and CAT were induced in the Cd-treated groups (p < 0.01), and they played a joint role in protection against oxidative stress in the
high exposure group, whereas in the low exposure group, only GST played a role. This suggests that oxidative damage occurred in the low-exposure
group. However, the mechanism by which the organism responded was different to that in the high exposure group. Therefore, we hypothesize that
exposure concentrations of 80 and 200 µg/L may be in two different critical ranges of the mussels’ antioxidant system, leading to the activation of
different mechanisms of response to antioxidant stress. Overall, the responses of these antioxidants could change depending on the exposed organ,
metal concentration, and individual physiology of animal species (Zhou et al., 2021).
 
3.3 Ionomics Analysis
It is known that Cd causes oxidative-mediated injury and affects ion homeostasis in organisms. The exposure of mussels to Cd2+ resulted in a
signicant accumulation of Cd in the gonads and showed a dose-dependent relationship. The Cd concentration in the gonads of mussels in the high
exposure group was signicantly higher than that in the control group (p < 0.01). However, there was no signicant increase in the Cd concentration in
the mussel gonads in the low Cd exposure group (Fig. 3).
Two-way analysis of variance analyses revealed that exposure time had a signicant effect on the gonadal levels of Cd, potassium (K), sodium (Na),
magnesium (Mg), Cu, iron (Fe), Zn, lead (Pb), manganese (Mn), arsenic (As), and Sr. Additionally, the exposure concentration had a signicant effect
on the contents of Cd, K, Zn, and Mn in the gonads (Table 3, Fig. 4). When we analyzed the Pearson correlation coecient of these 14 elements in the
2 Cd-treated groups (Table 4), and we found that Cd-treatment changed the correlation between some metal elements such as Fe, Cu, Se, Mn, Ca, Na,
and Mg. The elements with the highest correlations (|r| ≥ 0.7) with each other were considered to be antioxidants, such as Sr, Se, and Zn, which were
usually correlated with other elements, including Mg, Na, Ca, Fe, and Mn. Compared with other elements such as Cr, Mn, Fe, Mo, Cd, and As, these
highly correlated elements had signicantly lower electronegativities and reduction potentials (p < 0.01) (Emsley, 1989). These lower reduction
potentials indicate stronger reducing properties. Additionally, these elements have a lower ability to retain acquired electrons. Therefore, they can exert
antioxidant activity. The elements with the strongest correlations had low electronegativity and electron anity, and showed a strong reducing
capacity (antioxidant). The Sr content was signicantly decreased in the low-exposure group, and Sr was strongly correlated with Na (r = 0.843) and
Mg (r = 0.904) contents, which suggested similar effects in the gonad. However, Sr did not seem to show this ability in the high Cd-exposure group. A
previous study reported increased protection against reactive oxygen species in rats treated with strontium ranelate compared with control rats (Yalin
et al., 2012). This nding is similar to our nding of the reduction of Sr in gonads with evidence of oxidative damage. If this is the case, measuring
the Sr content in gonads might be a subtle marker for low concentration Cd-treatment (< 80 µg/L), because it is not physiologically regulated like Na
Page 5/15
and Mg. Sr is nonessential element, and it has no physiological function, such as serving as an enzyme co-factor or in active transport. However, Zn
is an essential element that also acts as an antioxidant metal at low concentrations in cells. The contents of Zn in mussel gonads decreased
signicantly with increasing exposure concentrations, and Zn was strongly negatively correlated with Na (r = − 0.908) and Mg (r = − 0.953) levels,
suggesting a contrary interaction in mussel gonads. The correlation between Zn and Cu was inuenced by the concentration of Cd exposure: the
higher the concentration of Cd, the lower the correlation. The correlation between Zn and Cu was regulated by MTs (Santon et al., 2008). Se is an
essential trace element with a protective effect against the toxicity of Cd. It forms complexes with Cd, preventing its accumulation in tissues (Preedy
et al., 2021). The contents of Se increased signicantly (p < 0.05) in gonad mussels exposed to high concentrations of Cd. At the same time, GSH-Px
activity was increased signicantly (Fig. 2), indicating an increase in the Se level. A study reported that Se ameliorates cadmium-induced mouse
leydig TM3 cell apoptosis (Ren et al., 2020). On the other hand, Se alone did not protect the body from oxidative stress, but it can reduce the toxicity
of metals by chelating with them (sequestration as with sulfur?) (Gagné et al., 2016). Therefore, it can be speculated that in the high Cd-exposure
group, Se and Cd binding prevented the accumulation of Cd in mussel gonads. The changes in contents and correlations of elements were not
identical in the two Cd-treatment groups, leading to speculation that the two Cd-treatment levels induced different mechanisms of antioxidant stress
or detoxication in the gonads of mussels.

3.4 Proteomic analysis
3.4.1 Global proteome alterations in response to Cd exposure
The use of trypsin hydrolysis with label-free LC-MS/MS quantication identied 1067 proteins after data ltering, the removal of missing values and
normalization. The number of DEPs could reect the proteomic response to Cd treatment to some extent. Among these, 207 and 229 proteins were
signicantly different between the low exposure group/control group (L/C) and high exposure group/control group (H/C), respectively. There were 79
and 87 upregulated proteins (Volcano analysis, p < 0.05), and 129 and 142 downregulated proteins, respectively, in L/C and H/C (Fig. 5A). As shown
in Fig. 5B, 91 proteins showed a common response to Cd treatment in L/C and H/C (Fig. 5B). Additionally, 80 proteins showed the same adjusted
patterns in both the low- and high-exposure group, with 33 upregulated and 47 downregulated proteins. However, 11 proteins showed contrasting
regulation patterns.
3.4.2 TCA cycle
In organisms, the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle has catabolic and anabolic duality and it is also a common pathway for gluconeogenesis, lipid
metabolism and protein metabolism. It produces co-enzymes such as NADH and FADH2, for the electron transport chain. In our study, there were ve
proteins related to the TCA cycle (Table 5, Fig. 6), ATP-citrate synthase (ACLY), succinate-CoA ligase (LSC1), dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase (DLD),
isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH1), glutamate dehydrogenase (GLUD1), that were inhibited by Cd treatment. ACLY, LSC1, and DLD were downregulated
in both Cd-treated groups, and SDHB and IDH1 were downregulated in the high Cd-treated group. ACLY catalyzes the rst critical step in the TCA cycle,
which is the condensation of acetyl CoA and oxaloacetate to citryl CoA (Schmidtmann et al., 2014). The function of LSC1 is to couple the hydrolysis
of succinyl-CoA with the synthesis of either ATP or GTP, which is the only step of phosphorylation at the substrate level in the TCA cycle. In this study,
Cd exposures caused signicant (p < 0.05) downregulations of ACLY and LSC1 in mussel gonad, which might subsequently reduce the production of
ATP and energy. The enzyme DLD is considered a key constituent of multiple mitochondrial dehydrogenase complexes, including branched-chain
amino acid dehydrogenase complex, α -ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex, and pyruvate dehydrogenase complex. It provides acetyl-CoA and
succinyl-CoA to the TCA cycle. The downregulation of DLD implied that Cd exposure reduced the inuxes of succinyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA into the TCA
cycle. The enzyme IDH1 decarboxylates isocitrate by oxidation to form α -ketoglutarate, it primarily exists in the cytoplasm, and its activity is regulated
by NADP. Additionally, it acts as a regulatory enzyme in the TCA cycle, and its activity is activated by ADP metathesis. The enzyme has a mutual
synergistic effect with the binding of isocitrate, Mg2+, NAD, and ADP. The accumulation of citrate is channeled toward fatty acid synthesis (Popova et
al., 1998; Hathaway et al., 1965). IDH1 was slightly downregulated in the high Cd-treated group, but it was signicantly up-regulated in the low Cd-
treated group. This may be due to the different levels of demand for α -ketoglutarate and ATP in the two exposure groups. Perhaps the high
concentration of Cd inhibited vital gonad activities and the demand for ATP was reduced. However, the low concentration of Cd had a lower effect on
the vital activities of the gonad, and all physiological metabolisms were active and the demand for ATP was higher. Simultaneously, GLUD1 was
signicantly downregulated in the high Cd concentration group. The function of GLUD1 is to catalyze the reversible conversion of glutamate to α -
ketoglutarate. Therefore, the downregulated GLUD1 implied the reduction of α -ketoglutarate provided to the TCA cycle. The malate dehydrogenase
level was downregulated under Hg-treatment in oyster gonads, resulting in hampering the TCA cycle in gonad cells (Zhang et al., 2013). In agreement
with our results, the TCA cycle was also signicantly inuenced by Cd-treated in a study on clams (Ji et al., 2019). Other ndings that are consistent
with our ndings suggest that exposure to heavy metals could harm the gonadal function of shellsh.
3.4.3 Structural reorganization of cell
The integrity of the cytoskeleton is essential for the healthy functioning of cells including maintaining the cell frame and mobility, but high metal
concentrations can lead to stress on the cell structure (Mateer et al., 2003). A previous study reported that the cytoskeleton component was one of the
targets of severe metal pollution in oysters (Li et al., 2020), and Li used proteomics to reveal that cytoskeleton protein levels were downregulated
under Cu and Zn contamination in oysters (Li et al., 2021). In our study, actin-related protein 2/3 complex subunit (0.10-fold, 0.38-fold, and 0.39-fold)
and tubulin alpha chain (0.47-fold) were downregulated in the high Cd-exposure group (Table 5). However, the same changes were not observed in the
low-exposure group. Actin and tubulin are involved in the structure and function of the cytoskeleton. Additionally, actin is associated with protein
synthesis (Dalle-Donne et al., 2001; Bektas et al., 2004). Oxidative stress effects lead to a decrease in tubulin levels (Miura et al., 2005). Actin
downregulation has been reported in the clam Ruditapes decussatus following exposure to Cd, (Chora et al., 2009) and in Sydney rock oysters
(following exposure to Cu and Zn) (Li et al., 2021). In general, structural reorganization could destroy cell homeostasis, inhibit signal transduction and
Page 8/15
cell proliferation. Therefore, the rearrangement of the cytoskeleton could be used as a new marker to determine whether mussels are severely
contaminated by Cd.
3.4.4 Inammatory response of cells and tumorigenesis
Succinate dehydrogenase iron-sulfur subunit (SDHB) plays a role in the electron transport chain of mitochondria, and it is responsible for transferring
electrons from succinate to coenzyme Q. Succinate concentrations are regulated by succinate dehydrogenase. Additionally, it is an inammatory
signal, and inammation and cancer have an important similarity. The inammatory process may have a tumorigenic effect with increasing
succinate (Tannahill et al., 2013). On the other hand, deletion mutations in the enzymes SDHA, SDHB, SDHC and SDHD are closely associated with
susceptibility to cellular tumors and renal cell carcinoma (Bardella et al., 2011; Baysal et al., 2000; Feichtinger et al., 2010; Janeway et al., 2011).
Tumorigenesis caused by SDH deciency is said to be associated with the deleterious effects of supraphysiological levels of succinate (Selak et al.,
2005; Xiao et al., 2012). SDHB was signicantly downregulated in the high Cd concentration group, causing the accumulation of succinate, which
indicates in vivo inammation, while the absence of SDHB also implies the possibility of tumorigenesis.
3.4.5 Amino acid biosynthesis
Cadmium exposure affects the production of different amino acids. The glycine cleavage system P protein (0.37-fold) catalyzes the degradation of
glycine, and it was signicantly downregulated in the high concentration group. Therefore, glycine accumulated in the mussel gonads. Glycine is
required for glutathione synthesis as a response to cadmium stress (Cobbett, 2000). On the other hand, phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase (4.76-fold),
which contributes to serine production, was upregulated. This enzyme is involved in the production of serine, which is required for the synthesis of
cysteine and glycine and supports the synthesis of glutathione (Zhou et al., 2017). Notably, cysteine, glycine, and serine in the microalga Chlorella
sorokiniana were upregulated under exposure to 250 µM Cd2+ for 40 h (Léon-Vaz et al., 2021). Both GSH and Cys can reduce Cd toxicity in the
organism.
3.4.6 Other phenomena
Cd-induced oxidative stress decreased the activities of creatine phosphokinase (Almeida et al., 2002). We observed the decreased expression of
serine/threonine-protein kinase in the high Cd-treatment group, suggesting that Cd-treatment-induced oxidative stress may also reduce kinase activity
in the gonads, which may cause severe damage to M. coruscus by interfering with reproduction.
Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (AARSs) are enzymes that covalently link amino acids to their cognate tRNA in the rst step of protein translation.
AARSs are responsible for the production of raw materials for translation, as well as for ensuring accurate translation from nucleic acids to amino
acids (Rajendran et al., 2018). Direct inhibition of AARS affects protein metabolism, and this interference with the translation machinery leads to
decreased protein synthesis (Zhao et al., 2014). Alterations in the process of aminoacyl tRNA biosynthesis were observed in both exposure groups. In
the high Cd-exposure group, the expressions of glutamyl-tRNA synthetase (0.05- fold) and alanine-tRNA ligase (0.45-fold) were signicantly
downregulated, and whereas that of phenylalanyl-tRNA synthetase beta subunit (5.58-fold) was upregulated. In the low-exposure group, tyrosyl-, and
histidyl-tRNA synthetase were downregulated, and phenylalanyl-tRNA synthetase was upregulated. Thus, protein synthesis was altered in both
groups.

4. Conclusions
The present study investigated responses at the metal and protein-level to exposure to different Cd concentrations in the gonads of M. coruscus. The
mussel gonads in the two Cd-treated groups were signicantly altered in terms of metal contents (e.g., K, Zn, and Mn), correlation of metals (e.g., Fe,
Cu, Se, Mn, Ca, Na, and Mg), and protein expression compared with the control group. Due to the hyperaccumulated of Cd, there was a characteristic
change in the elemental composition of gonad tissues in mussels. Simultaneously, high oxidative stress resulted in damage to the TCA cycle, some
protein components of the cytoskeleton, part of the amino acid biosynthesis machinery, and protein translation. However, neither the specic changes
in metal contents and correlations nor the response in protein levels were similar in the two exposure groups, indicating the dose-dependent effects of
Cd on stress resistance. This study corroborated that even for the same tissue, the specic Cd detoxication mechanisms initiated in the mussels
Page 10/15
were not identical for different levels of Cd exposure. Therefore, the study contributes to forming a better understanding of the toxicological
mechanisms of Cd.
