Monitoring the quality of lentic inland waters is essential for assessing ecosystem health, supporting sustainable water-resource management, and informing environmental policy. Although not originally designed for aquatic applications, the Landsat programme has become a cornerstone of inland water-quality remote sensing owing to its five-decade observational legacy, moderate spatial resolution, global coverage, and open-access archive. This systematic review synthesises 229 studies published between 1983 and 2026 to examine how the technological legacy of Landsat has shaped the evolution of lentic inland water-quality remote sensing.
