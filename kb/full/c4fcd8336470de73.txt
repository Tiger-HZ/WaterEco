Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) are carcinogenic contaminants formed endogenously during high-temperature processing of protein-rich foods. This investigation evaluated the occurrence and dietary risk associated with four priority PAHs — benzo[a]pyrene (BaP), chrysene (Chr), benz[a]anthracene (BaA) and benzo[b]fluoranthene (BbF), collectively termed PAH4 — in traditionally processed fish and meat foods sampled from markets across six geopolitical zones of Nigeria. A total of 60 samples comprising smoked catfish, smoked tilapia, smoked mackerel, suya, kilishi and balangu were collected and analysed by high-performance liquid chromatography with fluorescence detection (HPLC-FLD) following solid-phase extraction (SPE) clean-up.
