The Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) mission represents a major advance in satellite hydrology by extending conventional nadir altimetry to two-dimensional wide-swath observations of the inland-water surface elevation, extent, width, and slope. This review combines a bibliometric analysis of 556 publications indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection from January 2019 to April 2026 with a thematic synthesis of representative post-launch studies. In contrast to earlier reviews centered largely on mission preparation or individual application domains, we examine the evolution of SWOT research together with study-level evidence on data products, water-surface-elevation retrieval, river-discharge estimation, hydrological applications, uncertainty, and multi-mission integration.
