Before Cyclone Senyar made landfall on Sumatra in November 2025, it drew moisture from across the South China Sea, the Gulf of Thailand, and the Indian Ocean. The substantial damage its heavy rainfall caused on the NE and W coasts of Sumatra urges us to rethink the relationships between climate, forests, hydrology, land use, human presence, and vulnerability. Cyclones will recur, but flood damage does not have to be repeated. The simple narrative that “deforestation causes floods” is not adequate for guiding a “building back better” strategy.
