ABSTRACT Riparian zones are vital for biodiversity and are increasingly threatened by natural and human‐induced factors that disrupt their structure and function. Arthropods, as dominant terrestrial organisms in these habitats, are essential for ecosystem services, influencing connectivity between terrestrial and aquatic webs, and are sensitive indicators of environmental changes. This study aimed to systematically evaluate global trends in the responses of riparian arthropods to flooding, alien plant invasion and river engineering, and to identify implications for conservation and management.
