Abstract The arid and semi-arid lands of Kenya cover about 88 percent of the country’s landmass and are home to approximately 75 percent of the national livestock herd, and over 15 million people. Pastoralism is the primary economic activity in these regions, where access to water and pasture is crucial for sustaining livelihoods and is also a leading cause of conflict. The study examined the potential of water as a source of peace in Bisan Hargeisa, located in Tana River County, Kenya. Data were collected through structured interviews and direct observation methods. Respondents were stratified into host and non-host communities from which fifty pastoralists were interviewed.
