Abstract Background: Having a usual source of care (USC) supports continuity and access to health services. Recent national trends among U.S. children have not been comprehensively characterized. We examined changes in USC prevalence, subgroup heterogeneity, and reported reasons for not having a USC using the 2015-2024 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey. Methods: We analyzed the 2015-2024 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey Household Full-Year Consolidated Data. Annual prevalence rates were estimated for children aged 0 to 17 years overall and by sex, age group, race and ethnicity, and insurance status.
