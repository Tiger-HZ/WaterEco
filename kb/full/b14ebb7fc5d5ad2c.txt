Lake Maninjau, a volcanic caldera lake in Indonesia, frequently experiences sulphide upwelling events that trigger periodic fish kills. Although these events are commonly attributed to eutrophication and organic matter enrichment, the potential contribution of residual volcanic activity remains unclear. This study re-evaluates the hydrogeochemical characteristics of Lake Maninjau to distinguish volcanic signatures from biotic sulphide generation. Field observations were conducted at four sites representing contrasting environments: a dome-proximal area with presumed volcanic influence (DM3), a eutrophic bay affected by intensive aquaculture (DM1), and two additional stations (DM2 and DM4) representing transitional and deep-water reference sites.
