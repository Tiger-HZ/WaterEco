Ensuring access to safe drinking water remains a public health priority, especially in rural Nigeria where boreholes are the major source of water. This study investigated the spatiotemporal distribution of heavy metals in borehole water in Bali Town, Taraba State, focusing on lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd), chromium (Cr), arsenic (As), and copper (Cu). Ten boreholes were sampled during both dry (March–May) and rainy (June–August) seasons. Physicochemical parameters were measured in situ, while heavy metal concentrations were determined using Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (AAS). The results showed that Pb (0.01–0.04 mg/L) and Cd (0.001–0.006 mg/L) exceeded World Health Organization (WHO, 2022) limits in several boreholes, with higher values recorded during the rainy season.
