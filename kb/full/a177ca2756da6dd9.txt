Bi+ individuals face unique challenges with intimacy due to bi+-specific discrimination, yet little is known about the quality of their relationships in general and regarding different relationship models. This study examined relationship quality and its correlates among 725 German-speaking bi+ adults in monogamous and consensually non-monogamous (CNM) relationships. Participants reported high relationship quality (M = 5.82/7), high sociosexuality (M = 50.81/81), moderate-to-high sexual satisfaction (M = 6.60/10), moderate conflict (M = 4.63/10), and low levels of outness (M = 3.91/10) and internalized binegativity (M = 3.94/18). Across the overall sample, sexual satisfaction was positively associated with relationship quality, whereas conflict and internalized binegativity were negatively linked to it. Relationship quality did not differ between participants in monogamous and CNM relationships. Participants in CNM relationships reported significantly higher sexual satisfaction, sociosexuality, and outness and lower internalized binegativity. LGB+ partner sexual orientation emerged as another key factor associated with more positive relational outcomes, especially within the monogamous subgroup. Overall, the findings indicate that, although bi+ individuals in this population experienced generally satisfying relationships across relationship models, being in a CNM relationship and sharing a sexual minority status with a partner are aspects connected to both more positive relational outcomes and lower minority stress.
