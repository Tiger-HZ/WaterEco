Microplastics exert toxic effects on aquatic plants, thereby impacting ecosystem functions and services. Currently, research on the effects of nanoplastics on aquatic plants has mainly focused on algae, while floating macrophytes have received less attention. To investigate the physiological responses and uptake and accumulation effects of floating macrophytes to polystyrene nanoplastics (PS-NPs), we exposed Limnobium laevigatum, Pistia stratiotes and Eichhornia crassipes to 0.1, 1 and 10 mg·L−1 of PS-NPs (20/100 nm) for 45 days.
