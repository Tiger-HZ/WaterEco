ABSTRACT Why did long‐horizon private investment in Brazilian water and sanitation expand after 2016 despite fragmented authority and persistent political contestation? This article places the Brazilian Development Bank (BNDES) inside, rather than outside, the credible‐commitment framework. It argues that the bank operated as a credibility intermediary: it assembled service territories at investible scale, financed and coordinated project preparation, translated statutory goals into comparable contracts, and linked concessions to long‐term finance. These functions complemented courts, legislatures, the national standard‐setting role of the National Water and Basic Sanitation Agency (ANA), and post‐award supervision by infranational regulators.
