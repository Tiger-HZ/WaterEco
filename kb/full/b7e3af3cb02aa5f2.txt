Open-sea Atlantic salmon aquaculture releases large quantities of effluents, significantly impacting nearby benthic ecosystems. This study investigates the in situ effects of a newly established fish farm on a vulnerable Isidella lofotensis bamboo coral garden located nearby. Over a three-year period, video surveys were conducted along a spatial gradient from the farm, documenting the health status of coral colonies during different production phases. Additionally, molecular indicators of energy balance and oxidative stress were analyzed in coral tissues sampled at varying distances from the farm during peak production. These biological responses were then correlated with sedimentation rates of particulate organic matter, estimated using farm production data and dispersion models. Findings reveal both lethal and sublethal impacts of aquaculture effluents on I. lofotensis . Corals exposed to elevated sedimentation showed excessive mucus production, while those directly beneath or adjacent to the farm exhibited severe tissue degradation and, in extreme cases, complete mortality, resulting in coral graveyards. This study provides the first in situ sensitivity thresholds for cold-water corals, offering critical insights for conservation and management in aquaculture-affected regions. Given the potential slow growth and long lifespan of I. lofotensis , proactive protection strategies are urgently needed to mitigate long-term ecological damage.
