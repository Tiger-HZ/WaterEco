Abstract Plant-derived adjuncts for periodontal care require antimicrobial activity together with acceptable host-cell compatibility. This study compared aqueous Calendula officinalis extracts from three commercial sources (Egyptian origin, Tierra Mil Colores, and Mi Granero) using cytocompatibility and bacterial screening assays. Human dental pulp stem cells (hDPSC) were the primary mammalian-cell model, and breast adenocarcinoma (MCF-7) cells were included only as a secondary transformed-cell metabolic comparator. Antibacterial effects against Porphyromonas gingivalis , Streptococcus mutans , Staphylococcus aureus , and Escherichia coli were evaluated by agar diffusion and broth microdilution followed by MTT-based metabolic assessment.
