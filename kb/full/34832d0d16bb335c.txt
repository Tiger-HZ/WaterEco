ABSTRACT Groundwater continues to be managed predominantly as an abiotic resource for human use, a paradigm that overlooks the biological communities responsible for many indispensable ecosystem services and ultimately the integrity of the global water cycle. A shift towards a holistic ecological perspective is therefore essential but requires innovative techniques to monitor a challenging environment. Environmental DNA (eDNA) approaches are being rapidly applied to groundwater ecosystems, but comprehensive community assessment is hampered by taxonomic challenges leading to the exclusion of informative, high‐value biological information.
