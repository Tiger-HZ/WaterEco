Introduction Increasing water scarcity and climate variability threaten rural livelihoods in semi-arid regions. Sand dams are widely used as Nature-based Solutions (NbS) to retain water within the sandy matrix of ephemeral riverbeds, yet their catchment-scale hydrological effects remain insufficiently quantified. Methods We applied the Soil and Water Assessment Tool (SWAT+) in a 22.35 km 2 ungauged ephemeral catchment in Makueni County, Kenya, containing 22 sand dams. The model combined open Earth observation and geospatial datasets to characterize baseline hydrology for 1990–2024. Future hydrological responses were explored under an RCP4.5 climate scenario through 2099, while a third scenario tested the feasibility of representing sand dams as in-channel SWAT+ reservoir objects.
