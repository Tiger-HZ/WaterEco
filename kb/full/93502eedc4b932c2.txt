Estimating the trophic position of predators in food webs using stable isotopes requires establishing a “baseline”, typically served by bivalves or snails. However, the frequent absence of such organisms in lakes leaves researchers in a difficult situation. Here we test the hypothesis that trophic position (TP) of largemouth bass (LB; Micropterus salmoides) in lakes can be estimated from δ 15 N of benthic organic material (BOM) in littoral surficial sediment. δ 15 N LB as a function of δ 15 N BOM was significant across 51 lakes with an R 2 of 0.84, supporting our hypothesis. In a subset of six lakes, the mean TP LB did not differ significantly when calculated using baselines of δ 15 N in BOM, unionid mussels (UN), gastropods (SN), or from a stable isotope mixing model.
