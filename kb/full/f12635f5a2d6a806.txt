Abstract Frequent slash-and-burns and shortening fallow periods reduce soil fertility and agricultural productivity and threaten the sustainability of shifting cultivation agroecosystems. Improved fallows established with fast-growing N 2 -fixing tree legumes have been proposed as a strategy to restore declining soil fertility. Here, we evaluate the legacy effects of improved Mimosa caesalpiniifolia Benth. fallows on soil surface macrofauna and topsoil mesofauna communities during the subsequent upland rice cultivation cycle, and we assess the additional effects of nutrient management. The study was conducted in Pirapemas county, eastern Amazonia.
