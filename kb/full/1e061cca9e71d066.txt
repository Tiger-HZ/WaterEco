Abstract Coastal anthropization poses a severe threat to Mediterranean littoral ecosystems. This study provides a historical baseline for assessing long-term environmental changes in Bou Ismaïl Bay (central Algerian coast) across three sectors exhibiting varying anthropogenic pressures: Tipaza (Corne d’Or, reference), Bou Ismaïl (highly industrialized), and Sidi Fredj (urban/touristic), where comprehensive multi-compartment datasets remain scarce. Surface seawater, Posidonia oceanica sheaths/rhizomes, superficial matte sediments, and the sea urchin Paracentrotus lividus were concurrently sampled to assess physicochemical parameters, dissolved nutrients, fecal indicator bacteria, and trace element (Al, Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Zn) bioaccumulation.
