ABSTRACT: 19 
This research identifies factors associated with the contamination by ciguatoxins 20 
(CTXs) in a population of fish and proposes a predict ive score of the presence of CTX -21 
like toxicity in amberjack samples from the official control program of ciguatera in the 22 
Canary Islands of the Directorate-General (DG) Fisheries (Canary Government). Out of 23 
the 970 samples of fish studied, 177 (18.2%) samples showed CTX-like toxicity. The fish 24 
were classified according to the species, amberjack (Seriola dumerili and S. rivoliana) (n 25 
= 793), dusky grouper (Epinephelus marginatus) (n = 145) and wahoo (Acanthocybium 26 
solandri) (n = 32). The data were separated by species category and statistically 27 
examined, resulting in 137 (17.3%) amberjack and 39 (26.9%) grouper samples 28 
showing CTX -like toxicity; regarding wahoo species, only 1 toxic sample (3.1%) was 29 
found. According to fishi ng location the contamination rates suggested grouping the 30 
islands in four clusters; namely: { El Hierro: HI; La Gomera: LG; La Palma: LP}, { Gran 31 
Canaria: GC; Tenerife: TF}, {Fuerteventura: FU} and { Lanzarote: LZ}. For the amberjack 32 
species, the multivariat e logistic regression showed the factors that maintained 33 
independent association with the outcome , which were the warm season (OR = 3.617; 34 
95%CI = 1.249 – 10.474), the weight (per Kg, 1.102; 95%CI = 1.069 – 1.136) and the 35 
island of fish catching . A prediction score was obtained for the probability of 36 
contamination by CTX in amberjack fish samples. The area under de curve ( AUC) 37 
obtained using the validation data was 0.747 (95% CI = 0.662 – 0.833). Regarding 38 
grouper species, the island of fishing was the only factor that showed significant 39 
differences associated with the presence of CTX -like toxicity. We provide herein data 40 
for a better management and prediction of ciguatera in the Canary Islands, suggesting 41 
a review of the minimum limits of fish we ight established by the Canary Government 42 
for the control program. 43

1. INTRODUCTION 48 
 49 
Ciguatera Fish Poisoning (CFP) is one of the most relevant seafood -borne illnesses 50 
worldwide and the most commonly reported human food poisoning related to natural 51 
marine toxins (Friedman et al., 2008; Suzuki et al., 2017). It consists of a debilitating 52 
human neuro -intoxication caused by consumption of varieties of fish species from 53 
tropical and subtropical waters, contaminated with bioaccumulat ed ciguatoxins (CTXs) 54 
(Meyer et al., 2016). CFP is characterized by causing gastrointestinal, neurological, and 55 
cardiovascular symptoms (Friedman et al., 2017). A range of 10 to 50 thousand people 56 
suffering from CFP annually worldwide has been estimated (EFSA, 2010). However, 57 
epidemiological data remain unreliable, given that it has been estimated that only ∼ 58 
10-20% of cases are properly diagnosed and reported ( Azziz-Baumgartner et al., 2012; 59 
Laurent et al., 2005). 60 
 61 
CFP is found endemically in tropical and subtropical water s such as the Caribbean Sea, 62 
the Indian and the P acific Oceans (Lewis, 2006 ). In the 40 years th at followed the 63 
discovery of CTX (Yasumoto et al., 1977), more than 400 fish species have been 64 
implicated in poisoning incidents (Tester et al., 2010), most of which are high -order 65 
carnivores (Lehane and Lewis, 2000; Lewis, 2006). In Europe, CFP and CTXs h ave been 66 
gaining interest in recent years due to several reported cases in European countries 67 
(e.g., France, Spain, the Netherlands, Germany, and Italy), mostly related to 68 
consumption of imported ciguateric fish or people who visited e ndemic areas of CFP 69 
(Caillaud et al., 2010). However, none of the current methods of analysis to determine 70 
CTX-group toxins in fish have been formally validated (EFSA, 2010). 71 
 72 
Regarding the East Atlantic Ocean, in the Canary Archipelago CFP had not been 73 
described until 2004, when 5 people became poisoned (Pérez-Arellano et al., 2005). In 74 
2008 two more outbreaks were reported in the Canary Islands (Boada et al., 2010) and 75 
11 people were also affected by CFP in Madeira Archipelago (Otero et al., 2010). 76 
Therefore, f or some species collected from authorized first sale points , considered a 77 
risk factor in the Canary Islands, an action protocol with the objective of making the 78 
detection of CTX prior to sale and human consumption, has been implemented since 79 
2011 by the DG Fish eries of the Canary Government (DG of Fisheries of the Canary 80 
Government, 2018). In the last decade, several outbreaks of CFP affecting 113 people 81 
(Canary Government, 2017b ) have been confirmed in the Canary Archipelago, 82 
following the consumptio n of subsistence and recreational harvested fish and not 83 
related to controlled fish . Additionally, s ince 2015, CFP has been designated a 84 
notifiable disease in the Canary Islands. 85 
 86 
Regarding the marine biotoxins, p recursors of CTXs (Gamberitoxins) are produced by 87 
benthic dinoflagellates of the genus Gambierdiscus (Rodríguez et al., 2017). These 88 
precursors are transferred and metabolized through the food web, as Gambierdiscus 89 
cells are ingested by herbivorous fish, which are then taken by piscivorous fish, both of 90 
which are finally consumed by humans. It is believed that CTXs are bioaccumulated 91 
through the trophic webs, thus, fish higher in the food web tend to contain the highest 92 
CTX concentrations (Banner et al., 1966; Dickey and Plakas, 2010). In addition, CTXs are 93 
tasteless, colourless and odourless, which increase s the risk of poisoning (Friedman et 94 
l., 2008). To date, more than 29 CTX congeners have been identified and grouped 95 
according to geographic distribution : Indian CTXs (I -CTX), Caribbean CTXs (C -CTX) and 96 
most investigated, Pacific CTXs (P -CTX) according to the presence in the waters where 97 
CFP is endemic (Hamilton et al., 2002; EFSA, 2010). 98 
 99 
Different species of Gambierdiscus have been isolated from water samples collected in 100 
the Canary Islands during a spatial study (Aligizaki et al., 2008; Rodríguez et al., 2017). 101 
These authors highlighted that socioeconomic impact of ciguatera on fisheries activity 102 
and public health in the Canary Islands requires further efforts to implement a faster 103 
analytical response to detect CTXs in fish samples, and multidisciplinary research to 104 
know life cycle, distribution and toxicity of Gambierdiscus spp. 105 
 106 
The major goal of the present research was to study several factors which may be 107 
associated with the probability to find CTX-like toxicity in fish obtained from first sale 108 
points in the Cana ry Islands in order to describe the statistical significance of these 109 
associations or achieve a predictive score, if possible. 110

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS 112 
 113 
2.1. Study area 114 
 115 
The Canary Archipelago is located in the North eastern Atlantic Ocean near Europe 116 
(2000 Km SW from the Iberian Peninsula) and North Africa (100 Km W from the 117 
Moroccan coast), FAO Major Fishing Area 34 in the subdivision 1.2 (FAO 2004). 118 
 119 
Two co -occurring amberjack species present in this area were analyzed (Seriola 120 
dumerili and S. rivoliana). These predators can reach large sizes living up to 15 years 121 
(Murie and Parkyn, 2008 ); when the fish are mature, they migrate to coastal areas to 122 
spawn. Amberjack species are important fish stocks in the Canary Islands being the 123 
fourth and sixth most caught species in La Gomera (Seriola dumerili ) and El Hierro 124 
(Seriola rivoliana ), respectively (Canary Government, 2017a ). Dusky g rouper 125 
(Epinephelus marginatus) is a benthonic species that lives on the coast within 5 to 45 126 
meters depth; it is considered a solitary fish, very territorial and sedentary (Göthel, 127 
1992) which predates other fish, crustaceans and cephalopods (Smale, 1986); although 128 
it is a fish of relevance to human consumption in the Canary Archipelago, dusky 129 
grouper is not among the 10 most caught species in the islands (mainly represented by 130 
fish shoals) (Canary Government, 2017a), possibly due to its solitary behaviour . Wahoo 131 
is a pelagic animal and a seasonal migratory spec ies, relatively new in the Canary 132 
Islands waters where it h as settled . Normally it feeds on pelagic prey (Espino et al., 133 
2006). 134 
 135 
2.2. Fish sample collection 136 
 137 
The official control protocol for CFP implemented by the Canary Government ( DG of 138 
Fisheries of the Canary Government, 2018 ) establishes a list of certain species and 139 
weights of fish considered a risk factor. This list, based on the local experience, shows 140 
the limit weights for fish to be sampled at the authorized first sale points and 141 
investigated in the Institute of Animal Health and Food Safety (IUSA) laboratory for CTX 142 
detection (Table 1). This list has been updated in 2018. 143 
 144 
The f ish samples used in the present study were obtained between April 2016 and 145 
December 2017 from the official monitoring of CFP ( DG Fisheries of the Canary 146 
Islands). Through t he mentioned programme , fish samples from professional 147 
fishermen associations were sent to the IUSA laboratory of the University of Las 148 
Palmas de Gran Canaria (ULPGC) in order to determine the CTX pres ence in fish flesh. 149 
The laboratory received 1538 samples in the study period. However, this research only 150 
included 970 fish samples selected from the total, based on the availability of relevant 151 
information, such as fish species, weight of the specimen and the island of fishing. Fish 152 
species and weights were consistent to those established by the offici al control 153 
protocol, particul arly amberjack (Seriola spp.) (n=793), dusky grouper ( Epinephelus 154 
marginatus) (n=145) and wahoo ( Acantocybium solandri ) (n=32). Thus, the samples 155 
were classified according to the specie s and different data such as the year of fish 156 
catching (2016/2017), period (cold/warm), weight and fishing island. 157 
 158 
2.3. Sample preparation and extraction of CTX 159 
 160 
Toxin extraction was performed following the protocol proposed by Lewis in 2003 and 161 
carried out by Hossen et al. in 2015 with slight modifications according to the 162 
laboratory needs. Fish samples were first homogenized and then a portion of 10 g of 163 
flesh wa s cooked at 70 oC during 10 minutes. When samples reached room 164 
temperature, 30 ml of acetone was added, mixed with ultraturrax and centrifuged at 165 
3000 xg during 5 minutes at 4 oC; this last step was repeated twice and both 166 
supernatants were pooled. The resulting acetone was filtered through a 0.45 μm of 167 
PTFE filter and evaporated with a rotary evaporator at 55 oC. The dried extract was re -168 
suspended in methanol:water (9:1) and N -Hexane for phase separation. The uppe r 169 
phase of N-Hexane was discarded and the methanol phase was dried under N 2 current 170 
at 40 oC for a subsequent partition with ethanol:water (1:3) and Diethyl-Ether (DE). The 171 
DE was reserved and evaporated under N 2 current to dryness. The final residue was 172 
resuspended in 4 ml of methanol and kept at -20 oC to be used in the cellular bioassay 173 
analysis (CBA). 174 
 175 
2.4. Neuroblastoma (neuro-2a) cell-based assay (CBA) 176 
 177 
Neuro-2a cells (Cell line: CCL131, from ATCC, LGC Standards S.L.U., Barcelona, Spain) 178 
were maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) -1640 medium 179 
supplemented with 5 -10% of foetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37 °C in a 5% CO 2 180 
atmosphere. The P -CTX-1 standard ( STD) (R.J. Lewis, The Queensland University, 181 
Australia) was used as a reference toxin for the assessment of CTX -like toxicity, as C -182 
CTX reference material is not commercially available (Soliño et al., 2015). 183 
 184 
For the cytotoxicity assay, cells were seeded i n a 96 -well flat bottom microplate (200 185 
µl/well) following the procedure of Caillaud et al in 2012 using RPMI medium 186 
supplemented with 5% of FBS at a density of 70,000 cells per well. Cells were 187 
incubated 24 h in the same mentioned conditions. Ouabain (0.1 mM) and veratridine 188 
(0.01 mM) were added to half of the seeded wells to favour cell mortality in case of the 189 
presence of CTX. In parallel to fish sample extracts testing by N euro-2a assay, a dose -190 
response curve with P -CTX-1 STD was always performed as an internal control, for cell 191 
response evaluation and limit of detection and quantification (LOD/LOQ) 192 
establishment. Thus, fish extracts and the STD were evaporated and re suspended in 193 
RPMI medium supplied with 5% FBS. Then, cells were exposed to flesh extract and the 194 
P-CTX-1 STD at decreasing concentrations in order to ensure the cells sensibility to 195 
CTX. Every sample extract and the STD were assayed in triplicate. After a 24 h-period of 196 
exposure to fish extracts and to P -CTX-1 standard solution, cell viability was evaluated 197 
with MTT [3 -(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium] and DMSO solutions. 198 
The corresponding absorbances were read at 570 nm by a multi -well 199 
spectrophotometer scanner and plotted into the Microsoft Office Excel 2016 and 200 
GraphPad Prism 7 softwares (GraphPad, San Diego, California, USA). For the 201 
interpretation of the dose-response curves, cell viabilities were related to the cell 202 
viability of the control colum n (cells with and without pre -treatment with 203 
ouabain/veratridine, O/V). 204 
 205 
The assessment of fish matrix effect on the N euro-2a assay was performed using 206 
different concentrations of several fish extracts to expose the cells with or without O/V 207 
(50 - 200 mg T issue Equivalents (TE)/ml). Several muscle samples from grouper and 208 
amberjack showed interference with the assay with concentrations higher than 100 209 
mg TE/ml ; on the contrary extracts from muscle tissue of Wahoo species displayed 210 
interference above 80 mg T E/ml. For that reason and to unify methodology, 80 mg 211 
TE/ml was set as the maximum concentration for testing with the Neuro-2a assay. 212 
 213 
Due to the large amount of samples used in this surveillance study, the 50% inhibition 214 
concentration (IC50, with O/V) was only assessed with the P-CTX-1 STD in ppb units (pg 215 
P-CTX-1/ml) in order to evaluate the cell response and limits. Samples extracts we re 216 
tested using 1 to 4 columns (serial dilutions) of the 96 -well microplate, depending on 217 
the number of samples received every week; thus, semi -quantitative estimation of the 218 
content in P -CTX-1 equivalents in fish extracts was not always possible to determ ine. 219 
Therefore, in the present study, a response producing less than 20% cell mortality was 220 
considered as a non -toxic effect, as other authors suggested (Caillaud et al., 2012), 221 
being the concentration of P -CTX-1 STD causing 20% inhibition of cell viabilit y (IC20) 222 
set as the LOD and LOQ according to this concentration of fish extract used for testing. 223 
Thus, a “positive sample” was considered when the corresponding extract showed an 224 
inhibition of cell viability over this LOD value. According to t he mean valu e of IC20 225 
(1.359 pg P-CTX-1/ml) obtained from all dose-response curves performed with the STD 226 
in the study period and the maximum concentration of extracts set to avoid matrix 227 
effect (80 mg TE/ml), the LOD/LOQ was 0.017 ppb. 228

2.5. Statistical analysis 230 
 231 
The statistical analysis was performed using the R package, version 3.3.1 (R 232 
Development Core Team, 2016) and IBM SPSS Statistics 23.0 software. 233 
 234 
Univariate analysis : Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and 235 
percentages and continuous as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR = 25th – 75th 236 
percentile). The percentages were compared, as appropriate, using the Chi -square (χ2) 237 
test or the exact Fisher test, t he means by the t-test and the medians by the Wilcoxon 238 
test for independent data. As usual, the statistical signiﬁcance was set at p-value < 0.05 239 
and t he rates of contamination by the CTX were estimated by means of confidence 240 
intervals (CI) at 95% using a bootstrap method. 241 
 242 
Multivariate analysis: In order to identify those factors that maintain independent 243 
association with the outcome, a multivariate logistic regression analysis were 244 
performed. All variables of the study were entered into the analysis and a selection 245 
based on complete enumeration algorithm (Morgan and Tatar, 1972) and Bayes 246 
information criterion (BIC) was carried out. For each one of these regressions, we 247 
evaluate the lack of fit according the BIC criteria (Schwarz, 1978). The models were 248 
summarized as coefficients (SE), p-values (likelihood ratio test) BIC values (for the 249 
residual models) and odds-ratio, which were estimated by 95% CI. 250 
 251 
Receiver operating characteristics: The discriminant power of the score deduced from 252 
the logistic model was evaluated from a receiver -operating characteristic analysis 253 
(ROC). The area under the ROC curve was estimated by means of the 95% CI. Statistical 254 
significance was set at p < 0.05. 255

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 257 
 258 
To identify the factors associated with the contamination by CTX in the population of 259 
fish under study, data were analyzed according to the following variables: 260 
 261 
3.1. Influence of fish species in the presence of CTX-like toxicity 262 
 263 
Out of the 970 fish samples included in this research, 793 (81.8%) belonged to both 264 
amberjack specie s, 145 (14.9%) to the grouper and 32 (3.3%) to the wahoo. Overall, 265 
228 ( 18.2%) of all samples exhibited measurable CTX -like toxicity.C omparing species 266 
categories, Pearson’s C hi-squared (χ2) test revealed a significant difference in CTX 267 
prevalence between species of the samples tested (p = 0.002). Although most of the 268 
samples correspond to amberjack species , the grouper displayed the highest 269 
percentage of positive samples (26.9%), see Table 2. This observation may indicate 270 
that the chance for catching a positive grouper is two -fold higher than fishing a 271 
positive amberjack in the Canary Islands waters possibly explained by the sedentary 272 
behaviour of grouper species (Göthel 1992; Espino et al., 2006) which may continually 273 
feed in areas where Gambierdiscus are more abundant, allowing a continuous 274 
accumulation of the toxin . However, it must be highlighted that the fish analyzed in 275 
this study correspond to certain weights considered as risk factors for human health 276 
(Table 1) and therefore, this limitation must be taken into account before raising any 277 
conclusion. In addition , the high percentage of toxic grouper samples obtained may 278 
support the review of the lower limit of weights previously suggest ed for this species 279 
(see Table 1 and Figure 1). Additionally, 32 samples of wahoo species were analyzed 280 
corresponding to the 3.3% of all flesh fish studied and note that only 1 of them showed 281 
a CTX-like toxicity. Thus, this species was not considered in this analysis. 282 
 283 
3.2. Influence of the fish weight 284 
 285 
Bioaccumulation of CTXs in amberjack has been reported to be highly dependent on 286 
the weight of the specimen (Bravo et a l., 2015). Thus, an analysis of the presence of 287 
CTX in fish against this variable is essential to fully understand the accumulation of this 288 
biotoxins through the lifetime of these animals. Accordingly, in comparison with the 289 
last mentioned reference, the present study has tripled the number of samples under 290 
investigation. 291 
 292 
Due to the biolog ical difference between species (Reid et al., 2016; Šegvić -Bubić et al., 293 
2016), each species weight data were analyzed separately using Mann-Whitney (M -294 
W)/Wilcoxon non-parametric tests (Table 2), demonstrating that the median value of 295 
weight for fish which showed CTX-like toxicity was significantly higher than the median 296 
weight of negative fish, both in amberjack (27 Kg vs. 20 Kg; p < 0.001) and grouper 297 
species (22.5 Kg vs. 21.1 Kg; p = 0.013, respectively). In both species, CTX toxicity was 298 
more frequently observed in larger specimens. The descriptive statistics of the weight 299 
of the CTX positive and negative samples according to fish species ( interquartile range-300 
IQR, median and minimum-maximum range) are summarized in Table 2. The 301 
distribution of weight data between species and CTX results is represented in th e box 302 
plot diagrams below (Figure 1). Regarding wahoo samples, only one positive result was 303 
obtained, what makes the statistical analysis impossible to be performed. 304 
 305 
It is remarkable to note the presence of positive fish with a weight close to the 306 
minimum limit established by the government of the Canary Islands (see Table 2 and 307 
Figure 1). The smallest positives amberjack and grouper species weighed 14.5 Kg and 308 
17.4 Kg respectively, see Figure 1. This observation may justify an extension of the 309 
minimum weight proposed for analysis in these two species, mainly in grouper fish, as 310 
mentioned before. Additionally, a CFP outbreak occurring in 2016 also support s this 311 
suggestion, when two people in Tenerife Island were poisoned by the consumption of 312 
7 Kg grouper, and the presence of CTX-like toxicity confirmed in our laboratory (Canary 313 
Government, 2017b). 314 
 315 
3.3. Influence of the fishing island 316 
 317 
The information of the island where the fish were caught is important to analy ze due 318 
to the nature of the studied species and the possible risk of poisoning related to the 319 
location. From the selected samples , significant difference in CTX result was found 320 
between islands of fishing (p < 0.001). Lanzarote showed the highest contamination 321 
rate (52.9%) which was more than two fold greater than the value obtained in samples 322 
from Fuerteventura (21. 0%) and Gran Canaria (17.8%) and more than three times 323 
higher than the CTX positivity showed from El Hierro (15%) and Tenerife (13.5%). The 324 
number of positive samples obtained from these Canary Islands far exceeded those 325 
resulted in the islands of La Palma (5.1%) and La Gomera (2.4%) where the lowest 326 
number of positive fish was observed (Figure 2). However, it must be considered that, 327 
under the official control program of c iguatera during the studied period, more than 328 
300 samples received from Lanzarote were not accompanied by the necessary 329 
information, and thus, wer e not included in this research, which could limit accurate 330 
result from this island. These results must be considered with caution. 331 
 332 
An in depth analysis by species, both amberjack and grouper also showed a statistical 333 
difference in CTX results between islands of capture (p < 0.001). For amberjack species, 334 
the number of positive samples seems to decrease from the Eastern islands to the 335 
Western islands (see Table 3 and Figure 2), thus, these rates suggested grouping the 336 
island category in four cluster; namely: {HI; LG; LP}, {GC; TF}, {F U} and {LZ}. Table 3 337 
displays the contamination rates corresponding to each cluster. 338 
 339 
On the contrary, the profile mentioned above was not found regarding grou per 340 
species, which showed positive results more likely linked to certain islands in 341 
particular, possibly due to its sedentary behaviour (Göthel, 1992; Espino et al., 2006). 342 
Thus, the highest number of ciguatoxic grouper was obtained in El Hierro, with a 343 
remarkable percentage of toxic samples (10 samples, 90.9%) and Lanzarote (19 344 
samples, 41.3%). Although Fuerteventura provided the highest number of samples, 345 
only 3 were found positive to CTX (5.6%). Additionally, the low number of results from 346 
Gran Canaria and La Palma precluded any conclusion. 347 
 348 
Despite of these findings, other confounding variables should be taken into 349 
consideration before any conclusion can be drawn (see section 3.4.). 350 
 351 
In addition, it is important to emphasise that results from each island presented one or 352 
more positive individuals weighted close to the minimum control limit except for those 353 
fish caught in La Gomera. 354 
 355 
Furthermore, t he only positive wahoo obtained in this study was fished in El Hierro, 356 
representing the 4.5% of all samples analyzed from this island. 357 
 358 
3.4. Influence of the period of fishing 359 
 360 
Fish samples studied in the present research were received in the period from April 361 
2016 to December 2017 and analyzed for the presence of CTX-like toxicity. 362 
 363 
Considering the results obtained by Rodr íguez et al. (2017), peaks of Gambierdiscus 364 
spp. cells densities were observed in the Canary Islands associated to temperatures 365 
higher than 20 oC. For this reason, time frame was divided in “cold period” (January to 366 
April) and “warm period” (May to December) in accordance with the surface seawater 367 
temperature registered in both years ( NOAA, 2017), with a difference of 3oC between 368 
both period s. Samples available for this research only allowed comparison of data in 369 
the warm period between both years of study. Thus, considering species separately, a 370 
significant decrease (p < 0.001) was observed in the percentage of positive samples of 371 
amberjack species caught within the warm period between 2016 and 2017 (31.4% and 372 
12.1%, respectively). This finding could be explained due to the modification in the 373 
official protocol of the lower weight limit in amberjack species from 15 Kg in 2016 to 374 
14 Kg in 2017 (Table 1) and the increasing demand for analysis with the consequent 375 
rise of samples received in the laboratory in 2017 over 2016 (573 and 220 samples, 376 
respectively, see Table 3). In contrast , grouper species maintained a similar rate of 377 
toxicity in the warm periods of both evaluated years. 378 
 379 
Considering both years in conjunction, the a mberjack specie s showed a rate of CTX 380 
toxicity of 12.5% (95% CI = 5.6 – 20.8) and 17.8% (95% CI = 15.0 – 20.5) in the cold and 381 
warm period, respectively, what seems to be an increase in the number of positive 382 
samples from the cold to the warm season, but no statistically significant difference (p 383 
= 0.261) was found ( Table 3). In this regard, it is important to stress that in the warm 384 
period the laboratory received a considerably greater amount of samples (see table 3) 385 
what could therefore partially explain the difference of CTX toxicity rates found 386 
between seasons. Regarding grouper fish, the number of CTX-positive sam ples were 387 
quite similar, being 26.3% (95% CI = 10.5 - 47.4) in the cold period and 27.0% (95% CI = 388 
19.8 - 34.9) in the warm period. 389

3.5. Risk gradient assessment: Predictive score of the presence of CTX-toxicity in a 391 
population of amberjack fish. 392 
 393 
For the statistical analysis of results of samples from t he amberjack species, using the 394 
training data, the multivariate logistic regression showed that the factors that 395 
maintained independent association with the outcome (contamination by CTX) were 396 
the warm seas on (OR = 3.617; 95%CI = 1. 249 - 10.474), the weight (per K g, 1.102; 397 
95%CI = 1.069 - 1.136) and the island of fishing (grouping according gradient, see Table 398 
3). It should be noted that the season did not show statistical significance in the 399 
univariate anal ysis (see section 3.4) but did so in the multivariate testing. This is 400 
attributable to the confounding effect of the weight, since in the warm season the 401 
weight of the fish was significantly lower than in the cold period (p < 0.001) , as it is 402 
shown in Figure 3. 403 
 404 
For the a mberjack species, a predictive score of contamination by CTX was obtained. 405 
For this purpose, the data were randomly divided into a training data set (n = 510) and 406 
a validation data set (n = 283). The predictive score was obtained by mea ns of the 407 
multivariate logistic analysis using the training dataset. Its discriminant power was 408 
evaluated by means of the ROC analysis using the validation dataset and was 409 
summarized as the estimated area under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC, Figure 4). 410 
 411 
The next prediction score was then obtained from this logistic analysis: 412 
Score = 1.286 × Warm + 0.097 × Weight + 1.962 × D1 + 2.555 × D2 + 4.191 × D3 413 
 414 
Here, the season is a binary variable (1 = Warm; 0 = Cold) and D1, D2 and D 3 are the 415 
dummies variables associated with the island of fish catching (clusters) according to 416 
the design shown in table 4 . Four clusters were considered: El Hierro -La Gomera -La 417 
Palma; Gran Canaria-Tenerife; Fuerteventura and Lanzarote. 418 
 419 
The AUC obtained using the validation data was 0.747 (95% CI = 0.662 – 0.833) (Figure 420 
4). Table 5 displayed the increasing probabilities of the contamination by the CTX 421 
according to a gradient from the cluster of western islands , in the cold season and fish 422 
with low weight to the eastern islands, in warm season and fish with high weight. 423 
Therefore, the probability of finding a positive result in a sample from amberjack is less 424 
than 1% if the sample comes from a small specimen (less than 22 Kg), from the 425 
western cluster islands {HI; LG; LP} and fished in the cold season. Additionally, as can 426 
be seen in Table 5, the change to the warm season leads to a strong increase in the 427 
probability of contamination, being over 50% when fish weighing more than 35 Kg are 428 
caught in Fuerteventura. And, in contrast, this probability reaches higher values (more 429 
than 90%) in fish caught in Lanzarote, weighting more than 41 Kg. 430 
 431 
The decreasing gradient of presence of CTX in amberjack species observed from t he 432 
eastern to the western islands is strongly consistent with the results obta ined by 433 
Rodríguez et al. (2017). These authors found that the higher density of Gambierdiscus 434 
spp. was found in Lanzarote and Fuerteventura islands , and they also reported that 435 
G. exentricus, one of the most toxic Gambierdiscus found in the Canary Islands , was 436 
more abundant in the eastern islands compared to the western ones. This finding 437 
could be explained by the conjunction of Northwestern African upwelling and the Cold 438 
Canary Current (Sang il et al., 2012) at the bottom of these islands, along with the 439 
shallow waters of these eastern islands which provides abundance of nutrients for the 440 
growth of plankton and seaweeds (Rodríguez et al., 2017). 441 
 442 
Regarding results obtained from g rouper species, the only factor that showed 443 
significant differences associated with the presence of CTX was the island of fishing (p 444 
< 0.001) , mentioned before in section 3.3. These results are in accordance with 445 
Dierking and Campora who analyzed samples of Cephalopholis argus from the 446 
Hawaiian Islands in 2009 and found no geographic patterns in toxicity between or 447 
within islands. 448 
 449 
Ciguateric groupers showed a different profile of distribution, as exposed above, which 450 
may be explained for a s edentary and very territorial behaviour of these fish (Reid et 451 
al., 2016). Furthermore, i t must be considered the presence of an algae bloom of 452 
G. caribaeus occurred in October 2016 in El Hierro (Soler-Onís et al., 2016) and the rate 453 
of CTX bioaccumulation in fish tissue (Lehane and Lewis, 2000; Banner et al., 1966) , 454 
what could explain that this certain island represents the highest percentage of CTX 455 
positive groupers in the Canary Archipelago (90%) in this period of study (Table 3). 456 
 457 
Even so, the aim of the present study was not to calculate the real prevalence of CTX in 458 
fish from the Canary Islands, but to propose a predictive value of finding a positive 459 
sample according to different associated factors, such as fish species, weight, season 460 
or fishing island. Although results showed different probabilities of contamination by 461 
CTX-like toxicity between islands, none of them is free of ciguateric fish. Therefore, 462 
official monitoring should continue throughout the archipelago to ensure the food 463 
safety. 464 
 465 
4. Conclusions 466 
 467 
This study confirms the Canary Islands as an area of expansion of CFP endemicity and 468 
contains the first reported predictive score for the presence of CTX -like toxicity in 469 
amberjack fish samples from this area. 470 
 471 
This work identifies the several factors associat ed with the probability of 472 
contamination by CTX-like toxicity of fish caught in the Canary Archipelago. 473 
 474 
A risk gradient was obtained for amberjack, considering weight of fish, season and 475 
island of fishing, this latter being the only factor significantly associated with grouper 476 
species. The risk of contamination by CTX could not be adequately assessed for wahoo 477 
due to small sample size of this species. 478 
 479 
Presence of CTX in amberjack from some areas seems to be highly related to the 480 
season of the year which may be related to the abundance of the most toxic 481 
Gambierdiscus found in the Canary Islands. 482 
 483 
The minimum weight limits establis hed by the official control of c iguatera in the 484 
Canary Islands for amberjack and dusky grouper need to be reviewed to safeguard 485 
consumer health. 486
