Sea cucumbers are an important echinoderm lineage for studying the evolution trajectory and environmental adaptability. However, the limited availability of high-quality chromosomes for sea cucumbers from deep sea ecosystems has hindered a thorough understanding of mechanisms underlying their adaptive evolution. In this study, we reported the first chromosome-level genome assembly in the order Persiculida, Hansenothuria sp. M1226 from a deep seamount in the Western Pacific Ocean, which was sequenced on the PacBio Sequel II platform using the CLR (continuous long reads) mode. The Hansenothuria sp. M1226 genome assembly has a total length of 3.52 Gb with a scaffold N50 length of 133.1 Mb, encompassing 23 chromosomes, highlighting high genome continuity. Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO) assessment yielded a completeness estimate of 85.0% for the genome assembly and 90.6% for its gene set. We predicted a total of 32,054 protein-coding genes (PCGs), with 27,273 functionally annotated. The availability of this high-quality chromosome-level genome of Hansenothuria sp. M1226 contributes to our understanding of the origin and evolution of sea cucumbers.
