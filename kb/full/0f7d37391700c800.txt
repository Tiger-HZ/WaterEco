Association Between Breathlessness Severity and 
Quality of Life Among Autorickshaw Drivers in 
Jalgaon: A Cross-Sectional Study 
 
 
Kimaya Ahire1; Dr. Sachin Tendulkar2; Dr. Riya Agrawal3 
 
1Intern, Dr. Ulhas Patil College of Physiotherapy, Jalgaon, Maharashtra, India 
2Assistant Professor, Department of Cardiorespiratory Physiotherapy, Dr. Ulhas Patil

3Assistant professor, Department of Musculoskeletal Physiotherapy, Dr. Ulhas Patil

Publication Date: 2026/10/02 
 
 
Abstract: 
 
 Background: 
Chronic respiratory symptoms, particularly breathlessness, can have broader effects on an individual’s daily 
functioning and well -being, extending beyond physical limita tions to psychological, social, and environmental aspects of 
quality of life. Autorickshaw drivers may be at increased risk of respiratory symptoms due to prolonged exposure to 
traffic-related pollutants and other occupational conditions, which may subsequ ently be associated with poorer quality of 
life. 
 
 Objective: 
To determine the association between breathlessness severity and quality of life among autorickshaw drivers in 
Jalgaon. 
 
 Methods: 
A cross-sectional study involving 104 autorickshaw drivers from Jalgaon was carried out. The WHOQOL-BREF was 
used to assess quality of life, while the ATS dyspnea grading was used to measure the degree of dyspnea. The quality-of-life 
scores were summarized using descriptive statistics, and the association between the four WHOQOL-BREF domains and 
the ATS breathlessness grade was determined using Spearman's correlation. The level of significance for statistical 
significance was p < 0.05. 
 
 Results: 
The social interactions domain had the highest mean score (54.50 ± 8.98) while the environmental domain had the 
lowest mean score (41.81 ± 8.14). ATS breathlessness grade showed significant negative correlations with the physical 
health domain (ρ = −0.798, p < 0.001), psychological health (ρ = −0.393, p < 0.001), social relationships (ρ = −0.353, p < 
0.001), and environmental domain (ρ = −0.420, p < 0.001) . The strongest correlation was observed between breathlessness 
severity and physical health. 
 
 Conclusion: 
Higher breathlessness severity showed a significant association with reduced WHOQOL-BREF domain scores among 
Jalgaon autorickshaw drivers, with the strongest relationship observed for physical health. These findings suggest that 
increasing breathlessness severity is related to changes in multiple dimensions of quality of life within this occupational 
group.

I. INTRODUCTION 
 
Chronic respiratory problems are long-term conditions 
that affect the airways and other structures of the lungs, 
leading to difficulti es in breathing and reduced lung 
function.[1] 
 
Respiratory symptoms are one of the main reasons for 
consultations at primary health care units. These include 
symptoms such as bleeding from the airways, Epistaxis, 
hemoptysis, Coughing, Breathing, abnormalit ies, Dyspnea, 
Wheezing, Excessive breathing, sneezing, Chest and throat 
discomfort.[2] Dyspnea is a common respiratory symptom; the 
Burden of Obstructive Lung Disease (BOLD) study reported 
dyspnea in approximately 27% of participants across 15 
countries.[3] 
 
Professional drivers are vulnerable to a variety of 
environmental, occupational, and health-related variables that 
could affect their quality of life. According to Sanchez et al., 
taxi and motorcycle taxi drivers experience occupational 
conditions involving physical and mental demands, prolonged 
working hours and other health -related challenges. [5] Since 
drivers are highly exposed to urban air pollution due to 
prolonged roadside work, it leads to increased rates of chronic 
respiratory symptoms including wheezing, persistent 
coughing, and dyspnea.[6] A study conducted by Adhikari and 
Gupta among autorickshaw drivers in Mangalore reported that 
28.7% experienced breathlessness, 19.7% had chronic cough, 
and 32.4% had low peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR) values. a 
conference proceedings.[4] Another study by Peter Stephen et 
al. (2018) among 297 autorickshaw drivers in urban 
Puducherry also reported a high prevalence of respiratory 
symptoms, with 68% of drivers experiencing breathlessness 
on exertion, highlighti ng the substantial occurrence of 
breathlessness in this occupational population. [1] A study 
examining changes in quality of life with the duration of 
chronic breathlessness reported that greater breathlessness 
severity was associated with reduced mobility, limitations in 
daily activities, and increased pain or discomfort. [9] 
 
Quality of life (QOL) refers to an individual’s 
perception of their position in life in relation to their cultural 
context, personal values, goals, expectations, standards, and 
concerns. Assessing Quality of Life involves looking at 
multiple aspects including health, psychological factors, 
environmental considerations, and social issues. [6] The 
WHOQOL-BREF is a 26 -item instrument developed by the 
World Health Organization to assess qu ality of life. It 
comprises four domains: physical health, psychological well-
being, social relationships, and environmental health. 
Responses are recorded on a five -point Likert scale, with 
higher scores indicating better perceived quality of life. The 
domain scores are transformed to a scale ranging from 20 to 
100. [7] 
 
Although respiratory symptoms among autorickshaw 
drivers have been studied in relation to respiratory health, 
limited research has examined the association between 
breathlessness severity and quality of life in this occupational 
group, particularly in Jalgaon. Therefore, the present study 
aimed to determine the association between breathlessness 
severity and quality of life among autorickshaw drivers in 
Jalgaon. 
 
II. MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY 
 
 Study Design 
An observational, cross -sectional study was conducted 
among autorickshaw drivers. 
 
 Study Setting 
The study was conducted among autorickshaw drivers 
at selected autorickshaw stands in Jalgaon city. 
 
 Sample Duration 
The study was carried out over a six-month period. 
 
 Sampling Method 
A convenience sampling method was used for 
participant selection. A total of 104 autorickshaw drivers 
participated in the study. 
 
 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria 
 
 Inclusion Criteria 
 
 Drivers of open -cabin three -wheelers engaged in 
passenger transportation for a minimum duration of three 
years. 
 20-65 years of age autorickshaw drivers. 
 Persons experiencing breathlessness for ≥3 years, with a 
dyspnea grade greater than 0 according to the ATS 
dyspnea scale. 
 
 Exclusion Criteria 
 
 Workers with physical disabilities. 
 Anyone who was not willing to participate. 
 Those with any variables that may affect the QOL Those 
with any variables that may affect the QOL such as past/ 
current respiratory illness, lung surgery etc. 
 
 Data Collection Procedure 
Participants meeting the eligibility criteria were 
screened and recruited for the study. The study objectives 
and procedures were explained to each participant, following 
which written informed consent was obtained. Demographic 
details were recorded using a structured data collection form. 
Breathlessness was then assessed for its presence and 
severity using the American Thoracic Society (ATS) dyspnea 
scale. Quality of life was subsequently evaluated using the 
WHOQOL-BREF questionnaire, com prising four domains: 
physical health, psychological health, social relationships, 
and environment. The questionnaire was administered to the 
participants in a standardized manner, and adequate 
clarification was provided whenever required without 
influencing their responses. All collected information was 
recorded systematically and checked for completeness. The 
data were subsequently coded, entered, and subjected to 
appropriate statistical analysis to assess the relationship

III. RESULTS 
 
Statistical analysis was performed using PSPP. Mean 
and standard deviation were calculated for descriptive 
analysis. The Shapiro –Wilk test was used to evaluate data 
distribution and indicated non -normality. Accordingly, non -
parametric methods were applied. Spearman’s rank 
correlation coefficient was used to examine the relationship 
between breathlessness severity and the four WHOQOL -
BREF domains. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

directly in mid -adulthood. The standard deviation indicates

IV. DISCUSSIONS 
 
The present study assessed the quality of life of 104 
Jalgaon autorickshaw drivers with chronic respiratory 
symptoms using the WHOQOL -BREF and examined its 
association with breathlessness severity using the ATS 
dyspnea grading. ATS Grade 1 was most common (45.2%), 
followed by Grade 2 (32.7%) and Grade 3 (22.1%), w ith no 
participants classified as Grade 4. 
 
The mean WHOQOL -BREF scores were 43.85 ± 7.56 
for physical health, 50.38 ± 6.81 for psychological health, 
54.50 ± 8.98 for social relationships, and 41.81 ± 8.14 for 
environment. The environmental domain had the lowest mean 
score, while social relationships had the highest. The 
relatively lower physical health score may be related to the 
effect of breathlessness on physical functioning and daily 
activities. This is consistent with Currow et al. (2020), who 
reported that increasing severity and duration of chronic 
breathlessness were associated with limitations in mobility 
and usual activities. [9] The psychological health findings may 
also be explained by the effect of persistent respiratory 
symptoms on daily functioning and well-being. Voll-Aanerud 
et al. (2010), using data from the European Community 
Respiratory Health Survey, reported that respiratory 
symptoms were associated with reduced health-related quality 
of life, covering both physical and psychological we ll-being, 
even among individuals without diagnosed asthma or 
COPD.[4]. 
 
The social relationships domain showed comparatively 
higher scores, with approximately equal proportions of 
participants classified as having good and poor social 
relationships. This m ay indicate that many participants 
maintained social support despite respiratory symptoms. The 
environmental domain had the lowest mean score, which may 
be related to occupational factors such as prolonged exposure 
to traffic-related air pollution, noise, vehicle vibrations and 
irregular working conditions. This finding is comparable with 
Sánchez et al., who reported lower environmental quality -of-
life scores among taxi drivers.[5] 
 
A statistically significant inverse correlation was 
identified between ATS dyspnoea grade and all four 
WHOQOL-BREF domains . The strongest association was 
observed with the physical health domain (ρ = −0.798, p < 
0.001), indicating a strong inverse relationship between 
breathlessness severity and physical quality of life. As the 
severity of breathlessness increased, physical health scores 
tended to decrease. This may be explained by the fact that 
increasing breathlessness can restrict physical activity, 
mobility, energy, sleep, and the ability to perform activities 
of daily living. Moderate negative correlations were observed 
between ATS grade and psychological health (ρ = −0.393, p 
< 0.001), social relationships (ρ = −0.353, p < 0.001), and 
environmental quality of life (ρ = −0.420, p < 0.001). The 
association with psychological hea lth suggests that greater 
breathlessness severity may be accompanied by poorer 
psychological well -being, potentially because persistent 
breathing difficulty can interfere with daily activities and 
contribute to emotional distress. Similarly, the negative 
association with social relationships may indicate that 
increasing breathlessness can limit participation in social and 
occupational activities. The association with the 
environmental domain may reflect difficulties in maintaining 
normal work and daily livi ng activities in the presence of 
more severe breathlessness. 
 
The comparatively stronger correlation with the 
physical health domain, compared with the other three 
domains, suggests that breathlessness severity is more closely 
related to the physical aspec ts of quality of life in this 
population. However, the significant associations across all 
four domains indicate that greater breathlessness severity is 
related not only to physical functioning but also to 
psychological, social, and environmental dimension s of 
quality of life. Similar associations between respiratory 
symptoms, chronic breathlessness and poorer quality of life 
have been reported by Matheson et al. [8] These findings 
support the importance of considering quality of life across 
multiple domain s when evaluating individuals experiencing 
chronic breathlessness. 
 
Overall, the results indicate that greater breathlessness 
severity is associated with lower quality of life across the 
physical, psychological, social, and environmental domains 
among the autorickshaw drivers included in the study. The 
observed inverse associations suggest that increasing severity 
of breathlessness may be accompanied by poorer quality -of-
life outcomes across multiple dimensions. Nevertheless, the 
cross-sectional nature of the study limits the interpretation of 
these relationships, as temporal sequence and causal 
relationships between breathlessness severity and quality of 
life cannot be established. Further research using objective 
measures of respiratory function, along wit h longitudinal 
study designs and follow-up assessments, may help to clarify 
the progression of these associations and provide a more 
comprehensive understanding of the relationship between 
breathlessness severity and quality of life among 
autorickshaw drivers. 
 
V. CONCLUSIONS 
 
The study concluded that chronic breathlessness was 
related to poorer quality of life among Jalgaon autorickshaw 
drivers, as demonstrated by significant negative correlations 
between ATS breathlessness grade and all four WHOQOL -
BREF doma ins, with the strongest association observed in 
physical health.

LIMITATIONS 
 
The study was conducted among autorickshaw drivers 
from Jalgaon, which may restrict the applicability of the 
findings to autorickshaw drivers from different geographical 
regions or to individuals engaged in other occupations. 
 
The study assessed quality of life using WHOQOL -
BREF but did not include objective pulmonary function 
measures to quantify respiratory impairment. 
 
Other factors that may influence quality of life, such as 
occupational exposure, duration of driving, smoking history, 
and socioeconomic factors, may not have been fully 
accounted for. 
 
FUTURE SCOPE 
 
Future research may consider recruiting a more diverse 
sample of autorickshaw drivers from multiple cities and 
geographical regions to improve the broader applicability of 
the findings. 
 
Future research can incorporate spirometry and other 
pulmonary function tests to objectively assess respiratory 
function. 
 
The relationship between quality of life and duration of 
occupation, daily driving hours, and years of exposure can be 
investigated.
