Gargling with Boiled Red Ginger (Zingiber Officinale Var. 
Rubrum) Water on Changes in Saliva pH 
 
Raisya Nabila1*, Elfi Zahara2 
1,2 Department of Dental Health, Poltekkes Kemenkes Aceh, Indonesia 
 
 
 
 
A B S T R A C T 
Salivary pH plays an important role in maintaining the balance of the oral 
environment and preventing dental caries. A decrease in salivary pH 
accelerates enamel demineralization; therefore, efforts to increase salivary pH 
are needed. Red ginger) contains bioactive compounds with antibacterial 
properties and can stimulate salivary secretion, making it a potential natural 
mouthwash. This study aimed to analyze the effect of gargling with boiled red 
ginger water on changes in salivary pH among residents of Dilib Lamteungoh 
Village, Aceh Besar Regency. Methods A quasi -experimental study with a 
pretest-posttest control group design was conducted involving 54 respondents 
selected through purposive sampling. Participants were divided into an 
intervention group and a control group. The intervention group gargled with 
10 ml of boiled red ginger water for 30 seconds, while the control group 
gargled with mineral water. Salivary pH was measured. before and after 
treatment using a digital pH meter. Data were analyzed using Paired t -test and 
Independent t -test with a significance level of 0.05. The mean salivary pH in 
the intervention group increased from 6.0 ± 0.2 to 7.1 ± 0.2, while the control 
group increased from 5.7 ± 0.5 to 6.6 ± 0.1. Statistical analysis showed a 
significant difference before and after treatment, with a greater increase in 
salivary pH observed in the intervention group (p=0.000). Gargling with boiled 
red ginger water significantly increased salivary pH and was more effective 
than gargling with mineral water. Boiled red ginger water may be considered a 
natural alternative for maintaining oral health. 
 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Oral health is an integral component of overall health that enables individuals to eat, speak, and 
interact socially without disruption. One of the primary factors contributing to various oral diseases is 
dental plaque, a soft deposit attached to the tooth surface composed of an accumulation of 
microorganisms within a cellular matrix (Pay et al., 2017) . Uncontrolled plaque accumulation increases 
the bacterial load in the oral cavity, thereby triggering enamel demineralization and eventually leading to 
dental caries (Sitanaya et al., 2023 ; Rahma et al., 2025) . The World Health Organization reported that 
approximately 3.5 billion people worldwide suffer from oral diseases, with approximately 2.3 billion 
individuals affected by permanent tooth caries and over 514 million children suffering from primary tooth 
caries (World Health Organization, 2022) . These data indicate that dental caries remains a major public 
health problem with a remarkably high prevalence. 
Indonesia continues to face a heavy burden of oral diseases. The results of the 2018 National Basic 
Health Research showed that 57.6% of the population experienced dental and oral health issues, with 
45.3% suffering from active caries and an overall caries experience prevalence reaching 88.8% 
(Kemenkes RI, 2018) . Aceh Province presents a similar trend, with a prevalence of oral health problems 
standing at 55%. An oral health examination conducted on the community of Dilib Lamteungoh Village 
revealed that 59 out of 65 respondents had caries, yielding a prevalence of 90.77% (Nazilla et al., 2025) . 
Furthermore, preliminary survey findings demonstrated an average salivary pH of 5.83 among residents, 
with 70% of respondents exhibiting salivary pH levels below the normal range (<6.7). An acidic salivary 
environment elevates the risk of enamel demineralization and accelerates the onset of dental caries 
(Sitanaya et al., 2023). 
Salivary pH plays an essential role in preserving the balance of the oral cavity ecosystem. Saliva 
functions as a buffer system, aids in the mechanical clearance of the oral cavity, and supplies essential 
minerals required for enamel remineralization (Annapoorna & Datta, 2020) . A decline in salivary pH to a 
critical threshold of approximately 5.5 –5.7 causes demineralization to proceed faster than

remineralization, thereby heightening caries risk (Sitanaya et al., 2023) . A neutral or slightly alkaline 
salivary pH suppresses the proliferation of cariogenic bacteria, including Streptococcus mutans, thereby 
reducing acid formation within dental plaque (Vahira et al., 2025). 
Plaque control is primarily achieved through tooth brushing, which can be augmented by the use of 
mouthwash. Chemical mouthwashes are proven effective in inhibiting microbial growth and reducing 
plaque accumulation; however, long-term use may induce adverse effects, such as mucosal irritation, taste 
alteration, and disruption of normal oral microflora balance (Deshpande et al., 2021) . Consequently, the 
utilization of natural ingredients as mouthwash alternatives has gained increasing attention, as they are 
considered safer, readily accessible, and associated with lower risks of side effects. Boiled red ginger 
(Zingiber officinale var. rubrum) water represents one such natural material with potential application in 
oral healthcare. 
Red ginger contains various bioactive compounds, such as gingerol, shogaol, flavonoids, oleoresin, 
and essential oils, which display antibacterial activity against various microorganisms responsible for 
plaque formation and oral diseases (Ahmed et al., 2022; Lukita, S. et al., 2021) . A study by (Isramilda & 
Nisa, 2024) demonstrated that red ginger extract effectively inhibits the growth of Staphylococcus aureus. 
Moreover, the distinct aroma of essential oils in red ginger can stimulate salivary flow rate, thereby 
assisting in maintaining oral pH balance (Sitanaya et al., 2023 ; Rahma et al., 2025) . Research by (Sitanaya 
et al., 2023) indicated that gargling with boiled red ginger water increased salivary pH, while (Oak et al., 
2023) reported an increase in mean salivary pH from 6.647 to 7.274 after gargling with infused red ginger 
water. 
Despite these benefits, the practical application of boiled red ginger water as a natural mouthwash 
in rural communities remains relatively limited. Residents of Dilib Lamteungoh Village generally 
recognize red ginger only as a warming beverage and a traditional remedy for colds, coughs, or pain, 
remaining unaware of its utility as a gargling solution to elevate salivary pH. Furthermore, studies 
investigating the effect of gargling with boiled red ginger water on salivary pH changes in this specific 
community have not been previously reported. This underscores the necessity for research to provide 
scientific evidence regarding the utilization of red ginger as a simple, safe, and viable promotive and 
preventive alternative for maintaining oral health. This study aimed to determine the effect of gargling 
with boiled red ginger (Zingiber officinale var. rubrum) water on changes in salivary pH among residents 
of Dilib Lamteungoh Village, Sukamakmur District, Aceh Besar Regency. The findings are expected to 
establish a foundation for utilizing natural materials as an accessible and safe strategy to increase salivary 
pH and support oral health maintenance in the community. 
 
METHOD 
This study employed a quasi -experimental design with a pretest -posttest control group approach 
to evaluate the effect of gargling with boiled red ginger ( Zingiber officinale var. rubrum) water on salivary 
pH changes. The investigation was conducted in Dilib Lamteungoh Village, Sukamakmur District, Aceh 
Besar Regency, from April 30 to May 2, 2026. The target population comprised productive -age individuals 
(15–64 years) meeting specified inclusion and exclusion criteria. A sample size of 54 respondents was 
selected using purposive sampling, calculated using Lemeshow’s formula with a finite population 
correction. Participants were assigned into two equal groups: an intervention group that gargled with 
boiled red ginger water and a control group that gargled with mineral water as a comparative standard. 
 
The boiled red ginger water was prepared by washing and crushing 50 g of fresh red ginger 
rhizomes, which were subsequently boiled in 500 mL of water for approximately 25 minutes. The 
preparation was filtered and allowed to cool to room temperature. Each participant in the intervention 
group gargled with 10 mL of the boiled red ginger solution for 30 seconds, while participants in the 
control group gargled with 10 mL of mineral water for the same duration. Salivary pH levels were 
measured immediately before (pretest) and after (posttest) intervention using a digital pH meter. Saliva 
collection was carried out using the spitting method, wherein respondents accumulated unstimulated 
saliva in the floor of the mouth and spat it into a sterile container for immediate pH evaluation.

The Paired t-test analysis in Table 6 demonstrates that the mean salivary pH in the intervention 
group increased from 6.0 ± 0.2 prior to treatment to 7.1 ± 0.2 after gargling with red ginger water, 
representing a mean increase of 1.08. This change was statistically significant ( p = 0.000). In the control 
group, the mean salivary pH also increased from 5.7 ± 0.5 to 6.6 ± 0.1 after gargling with mineral water, 
with a mean difference of 0.94 ( p = 0.000). Although both interventions yielded statistically significant 
increases, the magnitude of pH elevation was markedly higher in the red ginger intervention group, 
establishing its superior efficacy. 
 
DISCUSSION 
The results of this study demonstrate a significant increase in mean salivary pH after respondents 
gargled with boiled red ginger water compared to the control group gargling with mineral water. Shapiro –
Wilk testing confirmed the normal distribution of the data ( p > 0.05), justifying the application of 
parametric Independent t-tests. The statistical analysis yielded a p-value of 0.000 ( p < 0.05), confirming a 
significant difference in salivary pH changes between the intervention and control groups. These findings 
affirm that gargling with boiled red ginger water significantly enhances salivary pH among residents in 
Dilib Lamteungoh Village, Aceh Besar Regency. In the intervention group, baseline salivary pH averaged 
6.0 (acidic status). Post -intervention, the mean pH rose to 7.1, shifting into the neutral -to-slightly-alkaline 
range. While the control group also experienced an increase in mean pH from 5.7 to 6.6, the magnitude of 
change was less pronounced. This highlights that boiled red ginger water exhibits superior efficacy in 
elevating salivary pH compared to standard mineral water. 
The marked increase in salivary pH within the intervention group is attributed to active bioactive 
compounds in red ginger that stimulate salivary secretion, thereby enhancing salivary buffering capacity 
(Jumain et al., 2022) . Saliva houses essential buffer systems —principally bicarbonate, phosphate, and

protein systems—that act to maintain intraoral pH equilibrium. As salivary output increases, its capacity 
to neutralize organic acids improves, prompting a shift toward neutral or alkaline conditions (Can & 
Güvenç, 2025) . The characteristic pungency and thermal sensation of red ginger are presumed to act as 
gustatory stimuli that trigger salivary gland activity. Enhanced salivary flow rates elevate bicarbonate 
concentrations, strengthening buffer capacity and promoting intraoral stability . 
Furthermore, the bioactive constituents of red ginger —including gingerol, shogaol, flavonoids, 
and essential oils —exhibit distinct antibacterial properties capable of inhibiting acidogenic oral bacteria, 
notably Streptococcus mutans (Nuraini et al., 2024) . Suppressing acid-producing bacterial activity reduces 
acid accumulation in plaque, favoring a more neutral oral environment. Concurrently, the aroma of red 
ginger essential oils acts via olfactory and gustatory pathways to stimulate salivary flow. Higher flow rates 
systematically enhance the mechanical and chemical clearance of acids. These findings align with 
(Sitanaya et al., 2023) , who reported that gargling with boiled red ginger water successfully elevated 
intraoral salivary pH. Similarly, (Rahma et al., 2025) confirmed the efficacy of red ginger decoction as a 
natural mouthwash for increasing salivary pH. Thus, the current study reinforces existing evidence 
supporting the physiological benefits of red ginger in regulating oral pH. 
Maintaining optimal salivary pH is clinically relevant for dental caries prevention. An acidic 
intraoral environment accelerates enamel demineralization, elevating caries risk. Conversely, a neutral or 
slightly alkaline pH favors mineral precipitation, facilitating enamel remineralization and preserving hard 
tissue integrity (Can & Güvenç, 2025). Thus, utilizing red ginger decoction as a gargling agent represents a 
viable preventive strategy against dental caries in community settings. The slight pH increase observed in 
the control group can be attributed to mechanical rinsing action, which transiently stimulates minor 
salivary flow. However, lacking the bioactive and antimicrobial attributes of red ginger, mineral water 
could not produce an equivalent therapeutic effect. These outcomes highlight red ginger as a safe, cost -
effective, and accessible phytotherapeutic mouthwash alternative. 
 
CONCLUSION 
Based on the research findings, it can be concluded that Gargling with boiled red ginger ( Zingiber 
officinale var. rubrum) water significantly increases salivary pH among residents of Dilib Lamteungoh 
Village, Aceh Besar Regency.
