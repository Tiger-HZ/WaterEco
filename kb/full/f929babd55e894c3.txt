What if one of the most powerful performance tools in elite sport is not another technique, app, or mental drill—but a deeper relationship with nature? In this deeply personal and thought-provoking chapter, the reader is invited into mountains, glaciers, storms, Olympic memories, and silent moments of awe to explore how nature can become a living arena for mindfulness, acceptance, values, gratitude, and self-awareness. Drawing on decades of experience as a sport psychology practitioner, expedition traveler, and consultant in elite sport, the chapter shows how time in nature can sharpen presence, soften rumination, strengthen values, and help athletes reconnect with what truly matters. Through vivid stories from Spitsbergen, Greenland, Hardangervidda, and Olympic sport, the chapter makes a compelling case: the psychological states athletes seek in performance—focus, calm, courage, perspective, acceptance, and trust—are often the very states nature evokes most powerfully. Nature becomes more than recovery. It becomes a teacher, a mirror, and a training ground. The chapter also offers a refreshing and humane take on ACT and mindfulness in sport. Difficult concepts such as defusion, self-as-context, and acceptance are brought to life through lived experience rather than theory alone. Why do athletes struggle with acceptance? How can awe shift perspective? What happens when we stop fighting our thoughts and start observing them? And why might a mountain path, a campfire, or the sound of wind sometimes teach this better than a consulting room? This is a chapter for practitioners, coaches, and athletes who sense that thriving in elite sport requires more than mental control. It requires perspective, humility, and a way back to the present moment. Sometimes, that way back begins outdoors.
