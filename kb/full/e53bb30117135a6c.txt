Abstract 28 
Microcystins (MCs) are the most studied cyanotoxins. The uptake of MCs in cells and tissues of 29 
mammals and fish species is mostly mediated by organic anion-transporting polypeptides (OATPs in 30 
humans and rodents; Oatps in other species), and the Oatp1d1 appears to be a major tran sporter for 31 
MCs in fish. In this study, six MC congeners of varying physicochemical properties (MC-LR, -RR, -32 
YR, -LW, -LF, -LA) were tested by measuring their effect on the uptake of model Oatp1d1 33 
fluorescent substrate Lucifer yellow (LY) in HEK293T cells transiently or stably overexpressing 34 
zebrafish Oatp1d1. MC-LW and -LF showed the strongest interaction resulting in an almost complete 35 
inhibition of LY transport with IC50 values of 0.21 and 0.26 µM, while congeners -LR, -YR and -LA 36 
showed lower inhibitory effects. To discern between Oatp1d1 substrates and inhibitors, results were 37 
complemented by Michaelis–Menten kinetics and chemical analytical determinations of MCs uptake, 38 
along with molecular docking studies performed using the developed zebrafish Oatp1d 1 homology 39 
model. Therefore, our study showed that Oatp1d1-mediated transport of MCs could be largely 40 
dependent on their basic physicochemical properties, with log P OW being the most obvious 41 
determinant. Finally, apart from determination of the chemical composition of cynobacterial blooms, 42 
a reliable risk assessment should take into account the interaction of identified MC congeners with 43 
Oatp1d1 as their primary transporter, and herewith we demonstrated that such a comprehensive 44 
approach could be based on t he use of highly specific in vitro models, accompanied by chemical 45 
assessment and in silico molecular docking studies. 46

1. Introduction 51 
Cyanobacteria are marine, freshwater or terrestrial photosynthetic bacteria that possess the ability to 52 
produce a wide variety of secondary metabolites which enable them to modify their habitats and 53 
thrive under diverse environmental stress conditions (Sivonen and Jones, 1999; Dittmann et al., 54 
2013). Cyanobacterial secondary metabolites are in most cases highly bioactive substances, and some 55 
of them, called cyanotoxins, have been shown to present a significant risk to livestock, fish, other 56 
aquatic organisms and human health upon their release in to aquatic environments during so -called 57 
harmful algal blooms (HABs). HABs are a phenomenon that typically occurs in aquatic ecosystems 58 
in conditions of high nutrient input, eutrophication and increased temperature. They can be formed 59 
by various genera of cyanot oxin-producing cyanobacteria (toxic strains) and non -cyanotoxin-60 
producing cyanobacteria (non-toxic strains) (D’Anglada at al., 2016; Zohdi and Abbaspour, 2019). 61 
Considering the increased occurrence of cyanobacterial blooms, as well as human and animal 62 
poisonings (Wang et al., 2021), much effort has been put into revealing the toxic effects and molecular 63 
mechanisms of cyanobacterial toxicity (reviewed e.g., in Ferrão-Filho and Kozlowsky-Suzuki, 2011; 64 
Buratti et al., 2017 ). Among the many types of cyanotoxins, m icrocystins (MCs) are the most 65 
dominant in cyanobacterial blooms . They have been shown to be highly toxic and they are often 66 
associated with hepatotoxicity, nephropathy, neurotoxicity and other severe toxic effects, particularly 67 
upon harmful algal blooms (Chen et al., 2009). MCs are highly diverse in terms of molecular size, 68 
structure and physicochemical properties, with almost 280 congeners identified so far ( Bouaïcha et 69 
al., 2019 ). A typical MC structure consists of seven amino acids in a ring formation (cyclic 70 
heptapeptides) with an obligatory presence of a β-amino acid side chain (ADDA group) , and t heir 71 
nomenclature is primarily based on two variable amino acids that are always occupied by L -amino 72 
acids (Fig. S1 in Supplementary Material). 73 
The mechanisms of toxicity related to MCs are diverse, and the most studied one includes interactions 74 
with protein phosphatases PP1 and PP2A which in turn lead to the hyperphosphorylation of cellular 75 
proteins, DNA destruction, inflammation, apoptosis, hepatic hemorrhage and necrosis (MacKintosh 76 
et al., 1990). Induction of oxidative stress and interaction with cellular uptake mechanisms is also 77 
associated with exposure to MCs, causing cytoskeletal disruption, cancer cell invasio n and DNA 78 
damage (reviewed e.g., in McLellan and Manderville, 2017; Bouaïcha et al., 2019). As shown in 79 
several studies, the toxic potential of MCs depends on their toxicodynamics and toxicokinetics 80 
properties, and along with their inhibitory potency towards serine/threonine protein phosphatases 81 
(PPs), their uptake kinetics is probably a key determin ative of the organ and/or spe cies-specific 82 
toxicity of individual congeners. Interaction of MCs with serine/threonine PPs has been extensively 83 
addressed in numerous studies and data show that most toxic congeners frequently found in HABs 84 
have similar potency in the inhibition of PP1, PP2, and PP4-6, typically showing IC50 values in the 85 
range of 0.1–1 nM (Bouaïcha et al., 2019). The inhibition of the PPs activity by MCs results primarily 86 
from non -covalent interactions, although covalent interaction does occur between MCs and the 87 
catalytic sub-units of PPs. However, formation of the covalent bond occurs slowly, and is not essential 88 
for the inactivation of the PPs by MCs (Craig et al., 1996; Hastie et al., 2005; Perreira et al., 2011). 89 
Considering the uptake of MCs in various cells and tissues of mammals and fish species, it is mostly 90 
mediated by organic anion-transporting polypeptides (OATPs in humans and rodents; Oatps in fish 91 
and other species) (Fischer et al., 2010; Niedermeyer et al., 2014, Steiner et al., 2015). OATPs/Oatps 92 
are transmembrane proteins that typically consist of 12 transmembrane domains, and their primary 93 
physiological role appears to be the hepatic transport of steroid and thyroid hormones, bile salts, 94 
prostaglandins and oligopeptides. However, they have been shown to be involved in the transport of 95 
endo- and xenobiotics. They are typically polyspecific and expressed in most tissues, with specific 96 
members dominantly expressed in toxicologically important organs as the liver or kidney (Hagenbuch 97 
and Meier, 2003; Popović et al., 2010). Transport of MCs has been demonstrated for human 98 
OATP1B1 and OATP1B3, and rat and mice OATP1B2 (Fischer et al., 2005; Komatsu et al., 2007) . 99 
Members of the Oatp1 family were shown to interact with MCs in fish species such as the little skate, 100 
rainbow trout and zebrafish . Considering tissue distribution of Oatp1 transcripts , the level of their 101 
expression, and specificity towards MC congeners , the Oatp1d1 appears to be dominant transporter 102 
of MCs (Meier-Abt et al., 2007; Bury et al., 1998; Bieczynski et al., 2014; Steiner et al., 2015; 103 
Faltermann et al., 2016). 104 
From the ecotoxicological perspective, it is important to emphasize that the studies done so far 105 
indicate that the rate of MCs uptake, determined e.g., in zebrafish as a cyprinid fish model, varies for 106 
different congeners which may partially explain the observed differences in their toxic potential. 107 
Furthermore, as Oatp1d1 transporter appears to be highly relevant for uptake of MCs in zebrafish, it 108 
is important to better understand the potency and type of interaction of structurally and chemically 109 
different MC congeners with fish Oapt1d1 . If the interaction of various MCs with Oatp1d1 is 110 
significantly different, both in terms of their potency and type of interaction, the presence of highly 111 
toxic congeners in HABs might not represent high ecological risk if their Oatp1d1 transport is low . 112 
On the contrary, the presence of less toxic congeners with high rate of Oatp1d1 mediated transport 113 
could be highly deleterious. Therefore, in this study we addressed this topic using a zebrafish model: 114 
six MC congeners of varying physicochemical properties (MC -LR, -RR, -YR, -LW, -LF, -LA) that 115 
are frequently found in HABs were tested for their interaction with a target transporter using HEK 116 
293T cells transiently or stably expressing zebrafish Oatp1d1 cloned from zebrafish liver. Results of 117 
interaction studies were further verified by chemical analytical determinations of MC upt ake in 118 
transfected cells, and by molecular docking studies performed for specific congeners. 119

Concentrations of stock standard solutions made for analytical LC-MS analyses were 1 µg/µL, while 132

2.3. Oatp1d1 transport activity measurements 138 
Interaction of selected MCs with zebrafish Oatp1d1 transporter was determined by using the uptake 139 
transport assay with transiently transfected human embryonic kidney HEK239T cells overexpressing 140 
the Oatp1d1 uptake transporter cloned from zebrafish liver , as described previously (Popovic et al., 141 
2013; Maric et al ., 2017 ). Briefly, HEK293T cells overexpressing zebrafish Oatp1d1 were co-142 
exposed for 5 min to serial dilutions of MCs and LY as a model substrate , and the rate of Oatp1d1 143 
LY transport was determined. MCs were tested in the concentration range from 0.02 to 100 µM, the 144 
final concentration of Oatp1d1-specific substrate LY used in the assays was 10 µM, and the maximal 145 
amount of solvents (MeOH for MCs, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) for LY) never exceeded 0.1%. 146 
After incubation, the cells were washed twice with an ice-cold incubation medium (250 µL/well) and 147 
incubated with 250 µL/well of 0.1% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) for 30 min at 37 °C for cell lysis. 148 
Fluorescence of transport specific substrates was measured in 9 6-well black microplates using a 149 
microplate reader (Infinite M200, Tecan, Salzburg, Austria) at specif ic wavelengths of 425/540 nm. 150 
The eukaryotic vector pcDNA3.1(+) /His without cloned genes (mock -transfected cells) was also 151 
transfected into the HEK293T cells in order to determine non -transporter specific uptake. Oatp1d1-152 
transfected cells exposed only to incubation medium were used as an additional negative control. 153

2.4. Type of interaction of MCs with Oatp1d1 transporter 155 
2.4.1. Michaelis-Menten transport kinetics 156 
Stable expression of zebrafish Oatp1d1 in genetically engineered HEK293 Flp -In cells (Thermo 157 
Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA) was achieved using a non-clonal selection, by targeted integration 158 
of the oatp1d1 sequence subcloned into integration vector pcDNA5/FRT (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 159 
Waltham, USA) with combination of restriction enzymes HindIII/XhoI. pcDNA5/FRT/Oatp1d1 160 
constructs were specifically targeted into the genome of the Flp -InTM-293 cells following the 161 
manufacturer’s instructions, using basical ly the same protocol as described in Lončar and Smital 162 
(2018). Zebrafish Oatp1d1 stable transfectants were functionally verified by determination of the 163 
uptake rate of model fluorescent substrate LY in comparison to mock-transfected cells, and the stable 164 
cell line was further maintained in the same conditions as regular HEK293T cells. 165 
For the type of interaction experiments, HEK293T Flip-In/Oatp1d1 cells were seeded in 96-well 166 
plates at a density of 5 or 8 x 10 5 cells/mL in a final volume of 125 µL/well. Forty-eight hours after 167 
seeding, the growth medium was ex tracted from the cells and 100 µ L/well of incubation medium 168 
(145 mM NaCl, 3 mM KCl, 1 mM CaCl2, 0.5 mM MgCl2, 5 mM D-glucose, and 5 mM HEPES) was 169 
added. Preincubation lasted for 10 min after which 25 µL/well of medium was removed. The activity 170 
of the Oatp1d1 transporter was mea sured by exposing the cells to six concentrations of model 171 
substrate LY (5-300 µM) and two to three concentrations of MCs ranging from 0.05 – 100 µM. The 172 
exposure volume of LY and MCs added to the cells was 25 µL/well. The maximal amount of DMSO 173 
and MeOH used as solvents in all of the tested samples never exceeded 0.1%. A shift in the Km and 174 
Vmax values of Oatp1d1 transport was determined after 15 min incubation of LY with MCs , as the 175 
uptake of LY is linear during the first 15 min of incubation (Popovic et al., 2013) . After incubation, 176 
the cells were washed twice with 125 µL/well of cold incubation medium and subsequently incubated 177 
with 125 µL/well of 0.1% SDS for 30 min at 37 °C for cell lysis. As a control, the activity of the 178 
Oatp1d1 transporter in HEK293T Flip -In/Oatp1d1 cells was measured in the presence of model 179 
substrate LY only (six concentrations). The uptake into HEK293T Flip-In/mock cells was subtracted 180 
to obtain transporter -specific uptake. The Km increase and no changes in Vmax indicated MCs as 181 
substrates (competitive inhibition); no significant changes in Km and reduced Vmax pointed to non-182 
competitive inhibition; and reduced Km and Vmax indicated un -competitive inhibition. The 183 
measurements were done in duplicates and conducted in two to three independent experiments, and 184 
results of representative experiments are shown in the Results section. To obtain a linear calibration 185 
curve, the fluorescent dye LY was dissolved in 0.1% SDS, in the cell matrix dissolved in 0.1% SDS 186 
and in the incubation medium to obtain the linear calibration curves. Bradford assay was used to 187 
measure total protein concentration (Bradford, 1976). Based on total protein measurements and the 188 
obtained linear calibration curves, the uptake rate of LY was calculated and expressed as nmol of 189 
substrate per µg of protein per minute. 190

2.4.2. Chemical analytical verification of the type of interaction 193 
The MCs that showed significant interaction with zebrafish Oatp1d1 were preliminarily classified as 194 
substrates (competitive inhibitors) or non/un -competitive inhibitors based on results of Michaelis -195 
Menten kinetics experiments and were then further verified on the type of interaction by exposure of 196 
HEK293T Flip-In/Oatp1d1 stable transfectants and mock -transfected cells to MCs followed by a 197 
chemical analytical determination of the intracellular accumulation of the tested MCs by LC-MS 198 
analyses. Cells were seeded in 24-well plates at 8 x 10 5 cells/mL density in a final volume of 500 199 
µL/well and 48 h after seeding were exposed to different concentrations of MCs. After removing the 200 
growth medium above the cells, preincubati on was initiated by adding 400 µ L/well of incubation 201 
medium. Ten minutes later, HEK293T Flip -In/Oatp1d1 cells were exposed to three MC 202 
concentrations selected based on the initial transport activity determinations: 0.1, 0.5 and 1 µM for 203 
MC-LW and -LF; 0.5, 1 and 5 µM for MC-LA and -YR; 5, 20 and 50 µM for MC-LR. The maximal 204 
amount of MeOH used as solvent in all of the tested samples never exceeded 0.5%. Incubation with 205 
MCs lasted for 30 min after which cells were washed two times with 500 µL/well of incubation 206 
medium. The cells were then incubated in 500 µL/well of MeOH for 30 min at 37 °C in order to 207 
permeabilize the cells and extract the accumulated MCs. HEK293T Flip -In/mock cells were also 208 
exposed to the same concentrations of MCs in order to discern between t ransporter-specific and 209 
passive uptake of the tested MCs. After the final incubation in MeOH, cells were manually scraped 210 
from the bottom of the wells and technical duplicates from each concentration were merged into a 211 
single tube. Samples were the n centrifuged for 5 min at 1000 x g to remove cellular debris and 212 
proteins. Supernatant was transferred to a conical tube and 10 mL of MeOH was added. Samples 213 
were centrifuged for 5 min at 1000 x g, transferred to glass tubes and MeOH was evaporated under a 214 
nitrogen stream using a TurboVap system (Caliper Life Sciences, Hopkinton, MS, USA) at 40 °C 215 
until dryness. Residual dry matter was dissolved in 250 µL of MeOH. 216 
The prepared final solutions from the assays were analyzed by liquid chromatography -mass 217 
spectrometry (LC/MS). All analyses were performed using a Waters Acquity ultra -performance 218 
liquid chromatography (UPLC, Waters Corp., Milford, Massachusetts, USA) coupled to a Q -TOF 219 
Premier quadrupole -time-of-flight mass spectrometer (QTOF -MS; Waters Corp., Milford, 220 
Massachusetts, USA) equipped with an electrospray ionization source. The UPLC s ystem was 221 
equipped with a 1.7 µ BEH C18 column (100 x 4 mm) for chromatographic separation of MCs. The 222 
eluents A and B were acetonitrile with 0.1 % formic acid (v/v) and water with 0.1 % formic acid 223 
(v/v), respectively, and the flow rate was 0.4 ml/min. Th e sample acquisition was performed in 224 
positive ionization mode in the m/z range from 50 to 1100 Da. The details of the mass spectrometric 225 
settings were described elsewhere (Terzic and Ahel, 2011). For the quantitative assessment of 226 
individual MCs, the acquired full scan chromatograms were reconstructed using accurate masses of 227 
the corresponding [M+H]+ ions of MCs as follows: MC-LR (m/e 995.557), MC-YR (m/e 1045.536), 228 
MC-LW (m/e 1025.535), MC -LF (m/e 986.524) and MC -LA (m/e 910.493). The representative 229 
chromatograms are given in the Supplementary Material (Fig. S1) . The confirmation of peak 230 
identities as well as the quantitative assessment was performed using authentic standards as described 231 
above. 232

2.5. Homology modelling and molecular docking studies 234 
Biovia Discovery Studio Client v18.1. (Dassault Systèmes, Vélizy -Villacoublay, France) 235 
implemented Build Homology Models protocol was used to construct Oatp1d1 homology model 236 
based on alignment of the model sequence and the template structure - crystal str ucture of the 237 
glycerol-3-phosphate transporter from Escherichia coli (PDB ID: 1pw4) (Meier -Abt et al., 2006, 238 
Huang et al., 2003). Build Homology Models protocol uses the MODELER (Sali and Blundell, 1993) 239 
automodel to build homology models. To build an Oatp1d1 homology model, the input sequence 240 
alignment between the model sequence of Oatp1d1 and the sequence of glycerol -3-phosphate 241 
transporter was obtained using the Align Sequences protocol (Sequence Identity: 11.6 %, Sequence 242 
Similarity: 34,3 %). The alignm ent was analyzed and long insertions that could not b e modeled 243 
correctly were excised from the alignment in order to obtain a reliable model. The N-terminal variable 244 
region and the large extracellular and intracellular loops/regions that were judged to be unreliable and 245 
not important for ligand binding were excised. 246 
The remaining parameters in the Parameters Explorer of the Build Homology Models protocol were 247 
set as follows: Cut Overhangs was set to True to cut the terminal residues of the input model sequence 248 
that were not aligned with the templates ; Number of Models was set to 10 to specify the number of 249 
models to create from an initial structure with Optimization Level set to High to define the proportion 250 
of molecular dynamics with simulated annealing to perform. As to building refinement models on 251 
detected loop regions, i.e., the model sequence regions of at least 5 residues length which are not 252 
aligned with the template, Refine Loops was set to True. Build Homology Models protocol uses the 253 
DOPE (Discrete Optimized Protein Energy) (Shen and Sali, 2006) method to refine loops. Refine 254 
Loops Number of Models was set to 5 to define the number of models to be created by loop 255 
optimization, and Refine Loops Optimization Level was set to Low to define the number of models 256 
to be created by loop optimization. Refine Loops with Use Discrete Optimized Protein Energy 257 
(DOPE) Method was set to High Resolution. After running Build Homology Models protocol, the 258 
Best Model Structure Superimposed to Templates was selected from the generated output models for 259 
the final three -dimensional model structure of Oatp1d1. Finally, the selected model was manually 260 
adjusted and minimized using the Smart Mi nimizer algorithm. The dielectric constant used to 261 
minimize the Oatp1d1 model was set to 2 corresponding to the dielectric properties of saturated 262 
hydrocarbons as instructed when modelling a membrane system. 263 
Microcystin congeners to be docked in the homolo gy model of Oatp1d1 were created with 264 
ChemBio3D Ultra 13.0 (PerkinElmer, Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) and minimized using the Minimize 265 
Ligands protocol implemented in Biovia Discovery Studio Client v18.1. Flexible Docking protocol 266 
(Koska et al., 2008) was used for the molecular docking study with flexible amino acid side -chains 267 
which comprises the following steps: ChiFlex receptor conformations calculation, ligand 268 
conformations creation, LibDock docking of the ligand into active protein conformation sites, poses 269 
clustering to remove similar ligand poses, ChiRotor protein conformations rebuilding by refining 270 
selected protein side -chains in the presence of the rigid ligand, and a final CDOCKER ligand 271 
refinement. The selected homology model of Oatp1d1 was used as th e rigid receptor, while the 272 
binding site within the homology models was defined by a sphere (r=25 Å) surrounding the central 273 
pore. The rest of the parameters included in the FlexibleDocking protocol were set as follows. 274 
Maximum number of processed protein conformations was set to 100; Minimum angel, i.e. torsion 275 
angle cutoff (degrees) to determine whether the two side-chain chi1 angles are the same or not, was 276 
set to 30; Maximum number of residues for generating side chain conformations was set to 16 and 277 
these were the following: Met37, Lys38, Glu63, Ile70, Arg172, Glu176, Tyr199, Ile203 , Val206, 278 
Val347, Phe350, Ile351 , Ile354, Leu575, and A rg578; Conformation Method, i.e. algorithm for 279 
generating ligand conformations, was set to BEST; Maximum number of conformations to be created 280 
was set to 255; Energy Threshold, i.e. conformations of separate isomers are created inside this 281 
relative energy threshold (kcal/mol), was set to 20. LibDock ligand docking parameters inside 282 
FlexibleDocking protocol were set as foll ows: Number of Hotspots, i.e. number of polar or apolar 283 
receptor hotspots for conformer matching and Max Number to Save were set to 100 and 20, 284 
respectively with Tolerance for a hotspots matching algorithm for docking ligands set to 0.25; 285 
Maximum Number of Hits which defines the maximum number of hits saved for each ligand during 286 
hotspots matching prior to final pose minimization was set to 100 with Final Score Cutoff, i.e. 287 
fragment of the reported top scoring poses set to 0.5; Maximum number of poses to be kept per 288 
conformation was set to 30 with Maximum number of conformations for each ligand set to 1000; 289 
Steric Fraction specifying the number of clashes before the pose -hotspot alignment is discontinued 290 
was set to 0.10 inside the 0.5 Å Final Cluster Radius; maximum values for the Apolar SASA Cutoff 291 
and Polar SASA Cutoff were set to 15.0 Å and 5.0 Å, respectively. Lastly, a simulated annealing 292 
refinement was performed. 293

2.6. Statistical analysis 297 
All studies were performed in two to three independent experiments. Data from all experiments were 298 
analyzed and related calculations done using Microsoft Office Excel 2007 and GraphPad Prism 5 for 299 
Windows for statistical analysis, respectively. Data from Oatp1d1 transport activity measurements 300 
are expressed in percentages of LY uptake. Concentration -dependent LY uptake was calculated by 301 
using the equation (1): 302 
 303 
(1) Ii = ((Fi – Fm) / (Fc – Fm)) x 100 304 
 305 
where Ii is the percentage of inhibition for the test concentration i, Fi is the mean fluorescent value 306 
for test concentration i, Fm is the mean fluorescent value for mock and Fc is the mean fluorescent 307 
value for control. Serial dilutions of the test compounds were log transformed and results were 308 
analyzed by non -linear regress ion method used for obtaining dose -response curves, with 95% 309 
confidence intervals (CI). When possible and justified with respect to the intensity of the response, 310 
IC50 values that designate the concentrations that cause 50% of maximal observed inhibition were 311 
calculated from sigmoidal dose-response curves using the equation (2): 312 
 313 
 (2) y = b + (a – b) / (1 +10((LogIC50 – x) * h)) 314 
 315 
where y is the response, b is the minimum (bottom) of response, a represents the maximum (top) 316 
response, hillslope (h) is the slope of the curve, LogIC50 is the halfway response from bottom to top 317 
and x is the logarithm of inhibitor concentration. 318 
For Michaelis-Menten experiments , the obtained kinetic parameters, Km and Vmax values were 319 
calculated using the Michaelis–Menten equation (3), 320 
 321 
(3) V = (Vmax x S) / (S + Km) 322 
 323 
where V is the velocity (nanomoles of substrate per microgram of proteins per minute), Vmax is the 324 
maximal velocity, [ S] is the substrate concentration (micromoles) and Km is the Michaelis-Menten 325 
constant. Data obtained were fitted using nonlinear regression analysis with GraphPad Prism. 326 
For construction of Lineweaver-Burk plots (or double reciprocal), equation (4) was used: 327 
 328 
(4) 1/V = Km / Vmax (1 / [S]) + 1 / Vmax 329 
 330 
The plots are graphical representation of the of Oatp1d1 -mediated transport kinetics, with y -axis 331 
intercept as 1/Vmax and x -axis intercept as -1/Km. The type of inhibition was determined with 332 
analysis of lines convergence. Intersecting lines that converge at the y -axis indicate the competitive 333 
inhibition modality, whereas intersecting lines that converge to the left of the y-axis and on the x-axis 334 
indicate noncompetitive inhibition modality (Copeland, 2005). 335

3. Results 337 
3.1. Strength of interaction of tested MCs with zebrafish Oatp1d1 uptake transporter 338 
In the first step of our study we tested the strength of interaction of six selected MC congeners towards 339 
the zebrafish Oatp1d1 transporter, as determined by the inhibition of uptake of model Oatp1d1 340 
substrate Lucifer yellow (LY) in transiently transfected HEK293 FlpIn/Oatp1d1 cells. As can be seen 341 
on Fig. 1, the tested MC congeners markedly differed in their potency to inhibit LY transport. Apart 342 
from MC-RR, all other congeners significantly inhibited LY uptake in transiently tra nsfected cells 343 
upon a short, 5 min co-exposure with LY. The most potent interactors were MC -LW and -LF that 344 
resulted in an almost complete inhibition of LY uptake in transfected cells (95 and 82 %, respectively) 345 
with IC 50 values of 0.21 and 0.26 µM, respec tively. Significant inhibitory potency was also 346 
determined for MC congeners -LA, -LR and -YR, resulting in 38%, 20% and 16% inhibition of LY 347 
transport, respectively (Fig. 1). 348 
 349 
3.2. Determining the type of interaction of tested MCs with zebrafish Oatp1d1 uptake transporter 350 
Michaelis-Menten transport kinetics. 351 
All five MCs initially shown to interact with zebrafish Oatp1d1 with varying potency were further 352 
tested for their type of interaction. Our primary goal was to discern between substrates and inhibitors 353 
of zebrafish Oatp1d1, and in order to obtain more robust data and decrease variability among 354 
experiments that is often present when using transient expression protocols, related experiments were 355 
performed using HEK293 Flp -In cells stably overexpressing zebrafish Oatp1d1 (HEK293 Flip -356 
In/Oatp1d1) instead of the transient transfection protocol used for the initial determination of the 357 
interaction strength. 358 
Determinations of the type of interaction with zebrafish Oatp1d1 performed indirectly, using 359 
Michaelis-Menten transport kinetics experiments. Our data showed a clear pattern of Km increase and 360 
no significant changes in V max for MC congeners -LW and -LF, revealing that they are competitive 361 
inhibitors of LY transport mediated by zebrafish Oatp1d1. D ata were less conclusive for congener -362 
LA. Although the same pattern of the Km increase was nominally obtained, suggesting a competitive 363 
inhibition, both Michaelis -Menten kinetics (Fig. 2) and Lineweaver -Burk plots (Fig. S4) suggest 364 
rather a mixed type of inhibition. On the contrary, MC -LR and -YR appeared not to be zabrafish 365 
Oatp1d1 substrates as they rather acted as un-competitive inhibitors, showing a decrease in both K m 366 
and Vmax values (Table 1, Fig. 2). Yet, we have to note that in terms of statistical robustness, data for 367 
MC-LR were relatively weak, as a nominal decrease in K m was not statistically significant with 368

4. Discussion 478 
Based on the studies published so far, the uptake kinetics of various MC congeners is a factor that 479 
largely determines their bioavailability and toxic potential in exposed aquatic organisms, and Oatp 480 
transporters appeared to be major contributors relevant for the uptake of MCs in fish species (Meier-481 
Abt et al., 2007; Bury et al., 1998; Bieczynski et al., 2014; Faltermann et al., 2016; Steiner et al ., 482 
2016). Yet, more detailed studies performed recently utilizing zebrafish as a cyprinid fish model 483 
showed that the rate of MC uptake significantly varie s for different congeners. Furthermore, it has 484 
been shown that zebrafish Oatp1d1 acts as a ubiquitously expressed, multi -specific transporters of 485 
various MC congeners, while other MC transporting Oatps , like members of the Oatp1f subfamily , 486 
are expressed e xclusively in the kidney and transport only specific MC congeners (Steiner et al., 487 
2015). As the primary cause for this variability could be related to the strength and type of interaction 488 
(substrates versus inhibitors) of MC congeners with fish Oatp1d1 tr ansporter, it is logical to assume 489 
that these features may contribute to differences in the toxic potential of individual congeners towards 490 
fish species as one of the primary targets of cyanotoxins and subsequent toxic effects related to HABs. 491 
Therefore, it is highly relevant to better characterize the MC congeners frequently found in HABs 492 
with respect to their interaction with Oatp1d1. To do so, we selected six MC congeners that differ in 493 
terms of various structural properties and their log POW values (Table S1) and utilized HEK293T cells 494 
transiently or stably expressing zebrafish Oatp1d1 as a specific and sensitive assay system for testing 495 
their interaction with this transporter. 496 
In the first part of our study, we determined the strength of interaction of the tested MCs and found 497 
that most of them do interact with zebrafish Oatp1d1, inhibiting the uptake of model Oatp1d1 498 
substrate LY in transfected cells with varying potency. What was immediately obvious was that the 499 
strongest interactors were also the most li pophilic congeners -LW and -LF (Fig. 1 ), followed by 500 
congeners -LA, -LR and -YR, again in apparent correlation with their log P OW. Furthermore, as the 501 
only truly hydrophilic congener , with log P OW value of -0.2, MC-RR did not significantly interfere 502 
with LY transport by Oatp1d1. These results were similar to data reported earlier by Steiner et al. 503 
(2015), with the exception of MC-RR that was shown to be transported by zebrafish Oatp1d1, albeit 504 
at a slow rate and as the result of a prolonged 24 h exposure. 505 
However, our initial data on the interaction strength , although specific enough due to the use of 506 
Oatp1d1 overexpressing cells, could not reveal if the tested MC congeners are substances transported 507 
by the zebrafish Oatp1d1 transporter, or they inhibit LY transport due to an un- or non-competitive 508 
mode of inhibition. To get a better insight into the type of interaction, i n the next step of our study 509 
we performed Michaelis -Menten kinetics determinations of the type of interaction for five MC 510 
congeners shown to be Oatp1d1 interactors. Although Michaelis-Menten kinetics measurements are 511 
not a direct determination of substrate transport, we utilized it as an established experimental protocol 512 
to discern b etween substrates and inhibitors and showed that MC-LW and -LF clearly behaved as 513 
substrates (i.e. competitive inhibitors), while congeners -LR and -YR appeared not to be transported 514 
by Oatp1d1 . (Table 1, Fig s. 2 and S4). Considering the robustness of the data in respect to the 515 
calculated K m and V max values for LY transport , and their shifts in comparison to control values 516 
determined without addition of the tested MC congeners, the data obtained using this method 517 
appeared to be a good indication of congeners that are substrates versus those that act as Oatp1d1 518 
inhibitors. The exception s were MC-LA that were less conclusive and showed a mixed inhibiton 519 
pattern, and MC -LR where in term s of statistical robustness the data were relatively weak, as a 520 
nominal decrease in K m was not statistically significant with respect to data obtained for MC -LR at 521 
10 µm concentration (Table 1 , Figs. 2 and S4 ). This indicates that non -competitive inhibitio n 522 
(characterized by no changes in K m and a decrease in Vmax) could also have been a likely 523 
explanation. 524 
In addition to Michaelis-Menten kinetics data, the transport of selected MCs by zebrafish was further 525 
studied by direct chemical analytical determination of their accumulation rate in transfected and 526 
mock-transfected cells by LC -MS analysis. The obtained r esults were rather consistent with the 527 
results of kinetics determinations: significant increases in accumulati on of MC -LF, -LW, and -LA 528 
were determined in transfected over mock-transfected cells at all of the three concentrations of MCs, 529 
confirming them as substrates for zebrafish Oatp1d1 (Figs. 3, 4 and S2). Likewise, exposure to MC-530 
YR did not result in a significant fold-increase in accumulation over mock-transfected cells, showing 531 
that MC-YR was not transported by Oatp1d1. Yet, two irregularities were shown. The first is related 532 
to the lack of consistent dose re sponse for MC -LA, where the exposure of the cells t o the highest 533 
concentration actually resulted in lower accumulation of MC -LA in comparison to the two lower 534 
concentrations (Fig. 4). A likely explanation for this discrepancy might be related to suboptimal range 535 
of the concentrations used. For example, although MCs interact with cellular PPs primarily by non -536 
covalent, two -step mechanism involving rapid binding and inactivation of PPs, the process is 537 
followed by a slower (within hours) covalent interaction (Craig et al., 1996; Hastie et al. , 2005; 538 
Perreira et al., 2011). However, although a shorter, 30 min methanol extraction protocol was used in 539 
our study, it is possible that a more significant covalent interaction happened at the highest MC -LA 540 
concentration used, partly preventing extraction of accumulated MC-LA by methanol. Nevertheless, 541 
even at that concentration the accumulation of MC-LA was clearly much higher in transfected over 542 
mock-transfected cells showing basically that -LA congener is also a substrate transported by 543 
zebrafish Oatp1d1. 544 
Second inconsistency was related to MC -LR data. I n contrast to partially inconclusive Michaelis -545 
Menten analysis of MC -LR kinetics, chemical analytical determinations showed that MC -LR was 546 
transported into Oatp1d1 overexpressing cells, although at a lower rate (Figs. 3, 4, S2 and S4). These 547 
data are actually in agreement with previous reports that showed that MC-LR is an Oatp1d1 substrate 548 
in zebrafish (Steiner et al., 2015; Faltermann et al ., 2016). In terms of LC -LR toxicokinetics, the 549 
detailed study by Steiner and colleagues (2015) is particularly relevant, as it showed that intracellular 550 
transport of MC -LR by Oatp1d1 is much less favorable when compared to transport of more 551 
hydrophobic congeners like -LW or -LF, which are transported much faster. Consequently, although 552 
the authors correctly pointed out that kinetic values obtained in various studies using different 553 
exposure regimes, model substrates and/or detection methods should not be d irectly compared, we 554 
believe the overall pattern determined in our study is quite consistent with previous reports , which 555 
indicates that MC-LR is a slow, low affinity substrate for Oatp1d1 transporter. 556 
Finally, to confirm experimentally observed different modes of action among the tested compounds, 557 
a structural study was undertaken utilizing molecular modelling. Since a crystal structure of zebrafish 558 
Oatp1d1 is not available, a homology model was constructed based on a chosen template crystal 559 
structure of the glycerol -3-phosphate transporter from E. coli solved in a conformation with the 560 
central pore opening toward the intracellular side (F. Meier -Abt et al., 2006, Huang et al., 2003). 561 
Although the used template structure was not ideal in terms of the sequence similarity (Fig. S3), and 562 
long insertions that could not be modelled correctly were excised from the alignment in order to 563 
obtain a reliable model, all of the excised insertions were either from the N -terminal region or large 564 
extracellular/intracellular loops/regions not important for ligand binding. As a result, the final 565 
alignment of the model sequence and the template structure was of substantial quality, implying that 566 
the Oatp1d1 homology model constructed (Fig. 5) could be used for reliable molecul ar docking 567 
studies. It was further confirmed by successful docking of LY as a verified substrate for zebrafish 568 
Oatp1d1, which was docked deep in the central pore of zebrafish Oatp1d1 (Fig. 6AB). Moreover, LY 569 
was predicted to interact with Met37 via a hydro phobic π-alkyl non-covalent bond, and this residue 570 
corresponds to the position of Arg45 in the model glycerol-3-phosphate transporter described as one 571 
of the key residues for substrate binding, located at the closed end of the substrate -translocation 572 
pathway in the middle of the membrane (Huang et al., 2003). 573 
As to the docking studies of MC congeners, although all five MCs were studied and the most potent 574 
interactors MC -LF and MC -LW were successfully docked, others initially failed to fully 575 
accommodate inside the defined binding sphere. Such an outcome could have been expected for MC-576 
YR, which was shown both indirectly (Michaelis -Menten knetics) and directly (chemical analytical 577 
determinations) not to be transported by zebrafish Oatp1d1, but the docking of MC-LA and -LR 578 
inside the Oatp1d1 central pore, however, was expected. Nevertheless, after the refinement of the 579 
model by translation of the binding site sphere’s center closer to the opening of the central pore, MC-580 
LA and -LR were successfully docked. Inte restingly, it can be seen that MC -LR is placed higher 581 
above the central pore when compared with the predicted binding pose of MC -LF, and bulky MC -582 
LR is clearly prevented from easily entering the central pore. 583

Furthermore, although no crystal structures of the OATPs/Oatps are available and the transport 594 
mechanism of OATPs/Oatps has not been fully elucidated, the available in silico models imply that 595 
transport of their substrates most probably occur through a central, positively charged pore (Meier -596 
Abt et al., 2005). Consequently, as Oatps are primarily anion transporters, the presence of positively 597 
charged arginine residues in MC-LR, -YR, and -RR probably contribut ed to its lower affinities 598 
towards zebrafish Oatp1d1. Considering the strength and type of interaction of tested MCs versus 599 
their physicochemical descriptors, our data support earlier observations (Karlgren et al., 2012 ; 600 
Wolman et al., 2013) which indicated that apart from lipophilicity, properties like topological polar 601 
surface area and hydrogen bond features correlate with the potency of various substances, including 602 
MCs (Table S1), for interaction with OATPs/Oatps, including zebrafish Oatp1d1. 603 
 604 
5. Conclusion 605 
Taken together, the data obtained in this study support results by similar studies implying that the 606 
zebrafish Oatp1d1 is a membrane transporter that could be a rate limiting step for the uptake of 607 
microcystins in cyprininds, and possibly other teleosts. However, although a wide substrate 608 
preference and a rather large binding region of zebrafish Oatp1d1 do enable the transport of 609 
structurally and chemically different MC congeners, it seems to be highly plausible that the transport 610 
of MCs could be largely dependent on their basic physicochemical properties, with log POW being the 611 
most obvious determinant. 612 
Secondly, as previously suggested by Stainer et al. (2015), the presence of nominally highly toxic 613 
MC congeners (e.g., MC -LR) in cyanobacterial blooms does not necessarily translate to high 614 
ecological risk if their capacity for Oatp1d1 transport is low. And vice versa, the presence of 615 
nominally less toxic MCs (e.g., MC-LF or -LA) that are in contrast readily taken up by Oatp1d1 can 616 
result in highly relevant deleterious effects in exposed fish, especially neurotoxicity and renal toxicity 617 
in organs and tissues characterized by significant expression of Oatp1d1 or related transporters with 618 
overlapping substrate specificities. 619 
Consequently, apart from the determination of chemical composition of HABs in relation to MCs, a 620 
reliable risk assessment should take into account the interaction of identified MC congeners with 621 
Oatp1d1 as their primary transporter in fish species. As demonstrated, the use of highly specific in 622 
vitro models in combination with uptake and kinetics determinations, chemical analytical verification 623 
and in silico molecular docking studies could be used as a reliable experimental setup for this goal. 624
