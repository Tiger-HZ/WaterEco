Abstract Phosphorus-rich wastewater from pig farming poses a major threat to aquatic ecosystems due to its contribution to eutrophication. This study investigated the removal of phosphate from actual piggery wastewater using electrocoagulation, aiming to optimize operational parameters for maximum treatment efficiency. A Central Composite Design (CCD) under Response Surface Methodology (RSM) was employed to evaluate the combined effects of pH, current intensity, and reaction time on phosphate removal. Experimental results revealed that pH played the most crucial role, significantly influencing the hydrolysis of aluminum, which is essential for effective phosphate adsorption and co-precipitation.
