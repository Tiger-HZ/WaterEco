Effluents from automobile industries are complex mixtures of industrial discharge, usually composed of various chemical constituents, and hence, they require sustainable and biologically driven remediation strategies. This study is the first integrated evaluation of microalgal phycoremediation on automobile industry wastewater that analyzed the phycoremediation potential of four microalgal monocultures, namely, Chlorella sp., Scenedesmus sp., Chlorococcum sp., and Desmococcus sp., grown in automobile effluent under laboratory conditions for 15 days to evaluate the physicochemical changes, growth kinetics, and biochemical changes. Phycoremediation significantly improved the water quality, with reductions of approximately 33%–35% in total dissolved solids (TDS), 28%–30% in conductivity, and 39%–41% in salinity. Chlorella sp. and Scenedesmus sp. showed the best performance of remediation process, accompanied with the specific growth rate (µ) of 0.148 days -1 and 0.136 days -1 , respectively, with the remarkable growth of the dry biomass (1.63 ± 0.03 g/L, 1.76 ± 0.03 g/L) and the concentration of chlorophyll a (10.6 ± 0.18 μg/mL, 11.5 ± 0.18 μg/mL). Furthermore, biochemical analyses showed increase in the protein (up to 41.73% dry weight), carbohydrate (36% dry weight), and lipid (34.03% dry weight) contents, reflecting the increase in the cellular metabolism even under effluent stress. These results confirm the feasibility of Chlorella sp. and Scenedesmus sp. that can be used for transforming automobile effluent into a bio-based feedstock for a sustainable biorefinery aligning with the sustainable development goals such as SDG-6 (clean water and sanitation), SDG-13 (climate action), and SDG-7 (affordable and clean energy). This study provides a foundational framework for integrated phycoremediation biomass valorization systems.
