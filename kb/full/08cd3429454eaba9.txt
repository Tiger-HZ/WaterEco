ABSTRACT: Light sierozems are widely used for irrigated agriculture in southern Kazakhstan, but their low 
organic matter content and susceptibility to secondary salinization make soil fertility sensitive to land use. 
This study compared the humus status and agrochemical properties of irrigated light sierozems under 
contrasting land -use types in the K yzylorda Region. Tree soil profiles were studied within a third -year 
alfalfa field, irrigated cropland under melon production, and land abandoned for more than 15 years. 
Samples were collected by genetic horizon and analyzed for particle -size composition, soil organic carbon 
(SOC), humus fractions, available nutrients, pH, and exchangeable cations. All soils had a light loam 
texture dominated by fine sand. Surface SOC content was 0.96% under alfalfa, 0.72% in irrigated cropland, 
and 0.65% in the fallow soil . The alfalfa soil contained more non -hydrolysable residue and humic acids. 
The ΣHA/ΣFA ratio was 0.6 in all soils. Nitrate nitrogen and available sulfur accumulated in the middle 
horizons under alfalfa and irrigated cropland, whereas available phosphorus and exchangeable potassium 
were concentrated mainly in the upper horizons. Soil pH ranged from 8.76 to 9.07. The soil under third -
year alfalfa had the highest SOC content and a greater contribution of stable humus fractions. At the 
studied site, more than 15 years of unmanaged fallow did not restore a comparable stable organic matter 
pool.

INTRODUCTION 
The rational use of land resources and the preservation of soil fertility are crucial for the 
sustainable functioning of irrigated agriculture. For the arid zone of southern Kazakhstan, this issue 
is especially relevant because the combination of intensive agricultural use an d natural 
characteristics of the area leads to accelerated mineralization of organic matter, deterioration of soil 
physical and chemical properties, and development of secondary salinization processes. 
Light sierozem soils are one of the main soil resource s in the arid zone of southern 
Kazakhstan. They develop under conditions of high heat availability and insufficient rainfall, which 
determine their specific properties. Light sierozem soils are characterized by a high level of 
biological activity, intensive organic matter mineralization, and relatively low humus content (Soils 
of the Kazakh SSR, 1988). As a result, light sierozem soils show high variability in humus levels 
and agrochemical properties depending on the type of land use. 
For the Kyzylorda Regi on, soil fertility preservation is becoming especially important. The 
results of long-term studies confirm the conclusion that in irrigated sierozem soils, agricultural use 
leads to a 40 -50% decrease in humus content and mobile forms of nutrients over a re latively short 
period (Suleimenov et al., 2013). At the same time, under irrigation poor drainage, rising 
groundwater levels, and high evaporation can increase the capillary rise of salts and development of 
secondary soil salinization (Suleimenov et al., 2009). 
Along with changes in water -salt balance, one of the most vulnerable components of 
irrigated sierozem soils is organic matter, including humus, is one of the most vulnerable 
components of irrigated sierozem soils which are considered the key componen ts of soil fertility 
and determine soil stability. Therefore, for light sierozem soils, even small changes in the direction 
of humus formation and humus content can noticeably affect water retention, buffering capacity, 
and the ability to maintain producti ve potential of agroecosystems. This is especially important for 
light sierozem soils with a low initial humus content. Even minor quantitative and qualitative 
changes in organic matter significantly influence the resilience of agrocenoses to unfavorable 
factors (Mamontov et al., 2023). 
In irrigated agriculture, when there are risks of secondary salinization, both the availability 
of soil nutrients and the physical and chemical properties of the soils are important, which are 
largely determined by the state of the soil exchange complex. Changes in the balance of 
exchangeable cations, primarily calcium, magnesium, and sodium, can significantly affect soil 
structure, its water -physical properties, pH level, and direction of humus formation processes 
(Orlov, 1992; Vorobyeva, 1998). 
Despite a significant number of studies on the fertility of sierozems, most of them focus on 
specific indicators of their condition. At the same time, comparative assessment of humus status, 
nutrient availability, and cation exchange composition of light sierozems under different land use 
types has been insufficiently studied. There are practically no data comparing the properties of 
irrigated arable land, perennial legume grasses, and long -term fallow in the arid zone of 
Kazakhstan. 
In this regard, the aim of the study was to comparatively assess humus status, agrochemical 
properties, and composition of exchangeable cations in light sierozems under different types of land 
use in the arid zone of Kazakhstan, using the Kyzylorda region as an example. Special attention was 
paid to a comprehensive study of organic carbon content, the group and fractional composition of 
humus, soil fertility in terms of available nutrients, and patterns of exchangeable cation distribution 
in the soil profile under irrigated arable land, alfalfa crops, and long -term fallow. This made it 
possible to carry out a comprehensive comparative assessment and clarify specific features of 
functioning of light sierozems in the arid zone of Kazakhstan.

The profile distribution of exchangeable b ases depended on land use type. Under alfalfa, 
calcium content generally decreased with depth; in arable land, it increased in the middle part of 
profile; and on fallow land, it sharply increased in the lower horizons, reaching 19.647 mmol (+) 
per 100 g of soil. 
Magnesium content changed less noticeably, but its accumulation in the lower part of profile 
was also most pronounced on fallow land. Exchangeable sodium was present in small amounts in 
all cases and did not exceed 0.280 mmol/100 g, indicating its low contribution to the soil exchange 
complex. 
For a more detailed analysis of the distribution of agrochemical indicators across the soil 
profiles and the relationships among them, correlation analysis was carried out using Spearman's 
rank correlation coefficient for all sampled horizons (n=12). 
It was found that the content of nitrate nitrogen had strong positive correlation with available 
sulfur (ρ = 0.93; p < 0.001) and negative correlation with pH ( ρ = –0.75; p = 0.0046) and available 
phosphorus (ρ = –0.61; p = 0.036). This reflects differences in profile distribution and behavior of 
soil nutrients. 
Available phosphorus is positively correlated with exchangeable potassium (ρ = 0.80; p = 
0.0016) and proportion of “physical clay” (<0.01 mm) ( ρ = 0.77; p = 0.0034), highlighting the role 
of fine fractions and topsoil layers in accumulation and retention of P and K. In contrast, available 
sulfur is negatively associated with “physical clay” (ρ = –0.84; p = 0.0006) and available 
phosphorus (ρ = –0.79; p = 0. 0022), which aligns with its higher mobility and accumulation in sub 
plow/lower horizons. The Ca and Mg correlation (ρ = 0.75; p = 0.0052) reflects coordinated 
changes in composition of absorbed cations across the studied profiles. 
Additionally, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients (ρ) were calculated for the topsoil 
horizons (0–30/35 cm), including soil organic carbon and humus fractions (ΣHA and ΣFA). (Fig. 3). 
Due to the small number of observations (n = 3), these coefficients were used only for a 
tentative assessment of the direction of the relationships and were not interpreted as statistically 
significant. 
 
DISCUSSION 
The analysis of changes in particle size distribution of soils showed that observed 
differences are likely due to combined influenc e of land use features and natural spatial variability 
of the particle size distribution of the studied light sierozem soils.

The obtained data align with the known characteristics of light -textured, light -colored 
sierozems, which are distinguished by high water permeability, relatively low water -holding 
capacity, and limited sorption capacity.

Under these conditions, the most mobile compounds –primarily nitrates and sulfates – 
undergo easier redistribution throughout soil profile, whereas phosphorus and significant portion of 
potassium concentrate predominantly in the upper horizons. The predominance of fine -sand 
fraction, combined with low content of silt -sized particles, reflects formation characteristics of the 
studied light-colored sierozems on Quaternary deluvial-proluvial deposits (Borovsky et al., 1971). 
The results of assessing humus condition of light sierozem soils are consistent with 
numerous data on po sitive effect of long -term legume crops on soil organic matter accumulation. 
The higher organic carbon content in the soil under third -year alfalfa may be associated with its 
well-developed root system and annual input of root and crop residues. 
Symbiotic nitrogen fixation also plays important role, ensuring formation of plant biomass 
with higher nitrogen content and creating favorable conditions for microbial transformation of plant 
residues and subsequent humus formation (Zhou et al., 2015; Jensen et al. , 2012; Drinkwater et al., 
1998; Song et al., 2021Zhang et al., 2023). 
The content of non -hydrolysable residue, which characterizes the most resistant part of 
organic matter to biochemical transformation is of particular interest. 
The results suggest that even after a long fallow period, the most stable pool of humus 
substances has not recovered. Probably, in arid climates, absence of soil cultivating doesn’t provide 
accumulation of stable humus compounds, due to limited input of organic residues and low 
productivity of natural vegetation. This aligns with findings that in semi -arid conditions, the 
recovery of organic carbon supplies after stopping cultivation can be slow and doesn’t always reach 
the original level even after 10 –15 years without active resto ration of plant cover (Chang et al., 
2017).

The total content of organic carbon does not reflect the qualitative composition of humic 
substances or their stability. Therefore, to get a better idea of humus condition, group and fractional 
composition of humus was analyzed. 
The ΣHA/ΣFA ratio of 0.6 indicates the predominance of fulvic acids over humic acids and 
corresponds to a fulvate -humate type of humus typical of light sierozems in the arid zone. Despite 
the same ΣHA/ΣFA ratio 0.6, the land -use types diff ered in the total content and distribution of 
individual humic substance fractions (Ozhovan, 2013; Skrylnyk et al., 2018). 
Fractional composition of humus changed depending on land use type. In soils of three 
plots, fraction HA III dominated in composition of humic acids, represented by humic substances 
strongly bound to mineral part of soil and considered more resistant to decomposition (Ponomareva 
et al., 1980; Orlov, 1990). 
The data obtained indicate a higher proportion of organic matter associated with mineral part 
of soil, which may reflect increase in the processes of forming stable organo -mineral complexes 
(Alekseeva, 2021; Bezuglova et al., 2024). 
The predominance of the FA I fraction indicates a relatively higher proportion of mobile and 
less stable c omponents of organic matter in the fallow soil. Therefore, simply stopping soil 
cultivation by itself did not ensure restoration of stable humus pools; in arid conditions, this process 
is likely limited by poor development of natural vegetation cover and i nsufficient input of organic 
residues (Timofeeva et al., 2021; Burdukovskii et al., 2020; Mockeviciene et al., 2022). 
Thus, the soil under third -year alfalfa was characterized by a higher humic acid content, a 
greater proportion of humic substances associa ted with the soil mineral matrix, and more favorable 
indicators of humus status. The results show that character of land use affects not only the amount 
but also the quality of organic matter in light sierozem soils, determining its level of humification 
and stability. 
In irrigated fields, in application of nitrogen two opposing processes are possible. On the 
one hand, higher crop productivity can increase the input of plant residues and carbon into soil. On 
the other hand, tillage, irrigation, and nitrogen fertilization can boost mineralization of organic 
matter, cause losses of soluble organic carbon with drainage water, enhance microbial 
transformation of organic compounds, and involve previously stabilized forms of organic matter in 
decomposition processes (Mládková et al., 2006; Zheng et al., 2024). So, soil nitrogen background 
definitely needs to be considered when studying organic matter transformation in irrigated farming. 
In conditions with light granulometric composition and regular irrigation, nitr ate nitrogen 
easily migrates down soil profile, which promotes its downward movement and accumulation in B 
horizon. At the same time, sources of its accumulation in these areas differ. In arable land, nitrate 
nitrogen accumulation may be associated with th e mineralization of organic matter, use of nitrogen 
fertilizers and crop residues. The higher nitrate nitrogen content under alfalfa may be indirectly 
related to symbiotic nitrogen fixation follow by the input, mineralization, and nitrification of 
nitrogen-containing plant residues (Kebede, 2021). 
The minimal content of nitrate nitrogen in all horizons of fallow soil is probably due to the 
absence of additional nitrogen input and its more active involvement in natural biological cycle. 
Thus, under alfalfa and in arable land, nitrate nitrogen increased from the surface horizon to 
the middle part of the profile and then decreased in the deeper horizon. This pattern should be 
considered when determining the forms, rates, and timing of nitrogen fertilizer appli cation to avoid 
unproductive losses. 
High content of available phosphorus in topsoil is linked to its low mobility and its binding 
in carbonate soils by calcium and magnesium. High concentrations of available phosphorus in the 
upper layers can also be due to accumulation of organic matter and localization of main mass of 
roots in the plow layer (Hinsinger, 2001; Murad et al., 2024; York et al., 2013; Tang et al., 2023; 
Peterburgskii, 1981). 
Similarly to phosphorus, the higher availability of exchangeable po tassium in topsoil is 
linked to biogenic accumulation of the element, as well as higher levels of fine particles and organic

matter in the upper soil layers. In deeper layers, where the content of clay and organic matter 
decreases, soil retaining ability in relation to potassium also decreases (Mengel et al., 2001). 
One of the indicators of soil agrochemical condition is the content of available sulfur, which 
plays an important role in nitrogen -use efficiency. The profile distribution of the element showed 
that the maximum sulfur concentration in the B horizon is typical for soils in areas with perennial 
alfalfa and arable land, while in fallow area, increase in sulfur content is observed at the depth 
where parent rock is located. 
The profile distribution of available sulfur occupied an intermediate position between those 
of nitrate nitrogen and available phosphorus. 
The higher sulfur content under alfalfa may be related to accumulation of organic matter, 
activation of microbiological processes, and input of sulfur-containing compounds from residues of 
perennial crops (Eriksen et al., 1998; Tabatabai, 1996). 
At the same time, the increase in available sulfur in the lower horizons shows the high 
mobility of sulfate ions under conditions of light soil texture and periodic leaching. 
The distribution of available nutrients in the soil profile is closely related not only to the 
particle size composition but also to soil reaction (pH), which affects the mobility, availability, and 
chemical forms of nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur, and exchangeable cations. 
To characterize the physicochemical state of light sierozems under different land -use 
conditions, the composition of exchangeable cations in the soil exchange complex was analyzed. 
In general, the studied light siero zems differed among land -use types mainly in the profile 
distribution of calcium and magnesium rather than in sodium content. 
 
CONCLUSION 
The studied light sierozem soils were characterized by light loamy texture with 
predominance of fine sandy particles. The highest physical clay content was found in the fallow 
soil, and the lowest in the irrigated field under melon crops; the soil under perennial alfalfa 
occupied an intermediate position. Differences in distribution of fine particles in profile are 
probably due to combination of natural spatial heterogeneity of soil surface and land use practices. 
The most favorable indicators of humus status were found under perennial alfalfa. The 
organic carbon content here was 0.96%, while in irrigated arable land and f allow plots it was 0.72% 
and 0.65%, respectively. The soil under alfalfa had the highest content of non -hydrolysable residue, 
a higher proportion of humic acids, and a larger HA III fraction associated with mineral part of the 
soil. 
In all variants, the ra tio ΣHA/ΣFA was 0.6, while fulvic acids were dominating in humus 
composition. The results indicate that stopping agricultural land use for more than 15 years in arid 
climate conditions alone did not ensure the restoration of stable organic matter pool. 
The distribution of available forms of nutrients showed a clear profile differentiation. Under 
alfalfa and on irrigated arable land, the maximum content of nitrate nitrogen and available sulfur 
was observed in the middle part of the profile, indicating redist ribution of these elements 
throughout the profile and their accumulation in the middle part under conditions of irrigation and 
light soil texture. In contrast, available phosphorus and exchangeable potassium were mainly 
concentrated in the upper horizons. 
On the fallow plot, nitrate nitrogen content was minimal throughout the entire profile. 
The soils of all the studied sites were characterized by strongly alkaline reaction and 
predominance of exchangeable calcium and magnesium. Exchangeable sodium was present in small 
amounts, indicating a low contribution of sodium to the soil exchange complex. 
The main differences between land use types were seen in profile distribution of calcium 
and magnesium – under alfalfa, calcium content generally decreased with d epth; in arable land, it 
increased in the middle of profile; and in fallow land, there was a noticeable accumulation of both 
cations in the lower horizons. 
Correlation analysis revealed relationships between particle size and agrochemical 
properties of soi ls studied. The strongest positive correlation was found between nitrate nitrogen

The results showed that the soil unde r third-year alfalfa had a higher organic carbon content

soils. 
To maintain fertility of irrigated light sierozem soils, it is necessary to ensure the suppl y of 
organic matter and take into account the possibility of nitrate and sulfate migration when 
determining forms, doses, and timing of fertilizer application. 
At the studies site, leaving irrigated light sierozem soil as unmanaged fallow for more than 
15 years did not restore its organic carbon content to the level recorded under alfalfa. When land is 
temporarily withdrawn from agricultural use, measures are necessary to preserve productive 
vegetation cover, ensure a regular input of organic matter, and mo nitor humus status and available 
nutrient contents.

РЕЗЮМЕ 
Светлые серозёмы широко используются в орошаемом земледелии юга Казахстана, но 
низкое содержание органического вещества и риски проявления вторичного засоления в 
зависимости от характера землепользования значительно влияют на плодородия этих почв. 
Цель исследо вания заключалась в сравнительной оценке гумусного состояния и 
агрохимических свойств орошаемых светлых серозёмов при контрастных типах 
землепользования в Кызылординской области. 
Исследованы три почвенных разреза, заложенных на поле люцерны третьего года 
жизни, орошаемой пашне под бахчевыми культурами и участке, не использовавшемся в 
сельском хозяйстве более 15 лет. Образцы отбирали по генетическим горизонтам, 
определяли гранулометрический состав, содержание органического углерода почвы (SOC), 
фракционный состав гумуса, подвижные формы элементов питания, pH и состав обменных 
катионов. 
Для всех почв характерным являлся легкосуглинистый гранулометрический состав с 
преобладанием мелкопесчаной фракции. Содержание SOC в верхнем горизонте составляло 
0,96% под люце рной, 0,72% на орошаемой пашне и 0,65% в залежной почве. Почва под 
люцерной содержала больше негидролизуемого остатка и гуминовых кислот. Во всех почвах 
отношение ΣHA/ΣFA соста вляло 0,6. Нитратный азот и подвижная сера накапливались в 
средней части профиля под люцерной и на орошаемой пашне, тогда как доступный фосфор и 
обменный калий концентрировались преимущественно в верхних горизонтах. Значения pH 
находились в пределах 8,76–9,07. 
Почва под люцерной третьего года жизни характеризовалась наибольшим 
содержанием SOC и более высокой долей устойчивых фракций гумуса. На обследованном 
участке более чем 15 -летнее залежное состояние не обеспечило восстановления 
сопоставимого устойчивого пула органического вещества.
