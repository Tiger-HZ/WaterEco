The modern conceptualization of great power competition has perceived external forces like military power and economy size, leaving out the influence of domestic political bases on the outcome of strategies formulated by great powers. This paper proposes a construct of a hidden hierarchy to explore the implications of internal cohesion and the quality of governance on the level of strategic performance of great powers. The study applies a qualitative comparative case study, compares and contrasts the United States, China, and Russia, and is based on process tracing and cross-case analysis data, using the indicators of governance, data on political stability, and strategic performance.
