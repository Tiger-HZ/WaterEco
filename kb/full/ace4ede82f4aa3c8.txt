Abstract This paper studies how pre-trade transparency is associated with market quality in corporate bond markets. We examine the Swiss corporate bond market, which features a central limit order book (CLOB) with publicly visible quotes alongside a coexisting over-the-counter (OTC) market, and compare it to the predominantly OTC-driven U.S. market. Using a comprehensive transaction-level dataset, we document that while the Swiss market is substantially smaller and exhibits lower trading activity, it displays significantly more favorable price-based liquidity measures, with lower transaction costs, price dispersion, and price impact. Importantly, these differences extend beyond CLOB-trading: even OTC transactions are executed at more competitive prices relative to the U.S. market. Moreover
