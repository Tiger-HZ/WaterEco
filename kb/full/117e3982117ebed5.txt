ABSTRACT Water-efficient fixtures provide water, carbon, and cost benefits, but certification, monitoring, incentives, and education gaps limit effectiveness. Climate-driven droughts and water scarcity have heightened global attention to tap water conservation policies. This study evaluates South Korea's water conservation management system. It proposes policy improvements through: (1)analysis of domestic legislation and ordinances, (2)international comparison of water conservation policies with the United States, Australia, Singapore, and Japan, and (3)integrated quantitative analysis of the water-saving, economic, and greenhouse-gas reduction effects of water-efficient fixtures.
