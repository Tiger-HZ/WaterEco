Runway incursions remain a persistent safety challenge in airport surface operations, with a small subset of events associated with severe outcomes. Existing analyses often emphasize event frequency or categorical severity, providing limited insight into how high-severity outcomes are distributed across operational configurations. This study uses more than two decades of FAA ASIAS runway incursion records to test an event-level, domain-guided Bayesian network for conditional severity characterization. The model integrates operational interaction pairs, surface-exposure indicators, aircraft-sector involvement, and FAA-assigned cause/context groups. Scenario-based inference was used to estimate how posterior high-severity probability varied under different operational and contextual evidence conditions. Results show that high-severity outcomes were more concentrated in configurations involving surface exposure, mixed operating sectors, and coordination-intensive contexts. Low-frequency pedestrian-related scenarios produced large numerical shifts but required cautious interpretation, whereas better-supported scenarios, such as GA + ATC-related context, GA + vehicle, ATC-related context, and mixed aircraft sectors, showed recurring conditional severity patterns. The study demonstrates how a TVC-informed Bayesian network can support interaction-aware runway safety analysis by moving beyond aggregate counts toward interpretable comparison of high-severity concentration across operational scenarios.
