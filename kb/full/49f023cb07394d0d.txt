This study examines the customs clearance stage, which has remained largely outside the analytical scope of halal logistics literature that predominantly focuses on transportation and storage activities. Drawing on qualitative data obtained through semi-structured interviews with customs officers, inspectors, private sector representatives, standardization bodies, and Islamic scholars, the study employs thematic analysis to explore how halal integrity is addressed within customs environments.The findings indicate that mandatory public interventions at customs---such as inspection, sampling, search procedures, and temporary storage---introduce integrity-related risks distinct from those associated with production and conventional logistics processes.
