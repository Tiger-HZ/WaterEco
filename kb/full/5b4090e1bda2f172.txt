Public communication plays a critical role in shaping how emerging technologies are contested and governed. This study examines the contestation patterns in media and official communications on genome-engineered biotechnologies in New Zealand, asking who dominates public debate, what themes structure debates and how robust the quality of communication is. Using an inductive research design, the analysis operationalises framings at the micro level through communication units, enabling systematic linkage between framing devices, actor positioning and communication quality across 124 media items and key policy documents published between 2017 and 2024. The findings reveal five communication domains, dominated by regulations, science and applications. They also show pronounced asymmetries in contestation patterns: pro-biotechnology actors dominate both media and policy communications, foregrounding innovation-driven deregulation and techno-optimistic futures, while omitting key scientific aspects. Actors critical of high-risk applications emphasise precaution, interactive public engagement, Māori perspectives and comprehensive scientific assessments. Quality deficient communications are concentrated among pro-biotechnology actors and sometimes amplified by journalists. The study advances research on communication by integrating framing scope, communication quality and actor power, highlighting implications for informed democratic deliberation and regulatory accountability.
