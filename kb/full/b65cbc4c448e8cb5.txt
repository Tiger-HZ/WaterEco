Abstract Water quality degradation and biological invasions represent major drivers of global ecosystem degradation, yet their interactive effects remain poorly understood, especially in anthropogenically disturbed estuarine ecosystems. Here, we employed environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding to characterize multi-trophic biological communities in a subtropical estuary and to assess how water-quality degradation influences the invasion success of non-native fishes. The results show, firstly, the relative sequence abundance of non-native fish, rather than their species richness, emerged as the primary factor negatively associated with native fish diversity.
