Background: Blackberry (Rubus spp.) is a valuable functional food crop rich in phenolics, anthocyanins, flavonoids, vitamin C, and other bioactive compounds. Although micropropagation enables rapid production of uniform planting material, its effects on multi-year field performance and fruit biochemical quality remain insufficiently documented under specific agroecological conditions. Objective: This study compared micropropagated (MP) and conventionally propagated (CP) plants of four thornless blackberry cultivars (‘Doyle’, ‘Loch Ness’, ‘Natchez’, and ‘Freedom’) under Armenian conditions, focusing on field performance, disease incidence, and fruit biochemical quality relevant to functional food applications.
