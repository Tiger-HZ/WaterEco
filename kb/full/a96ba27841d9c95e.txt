Marine protected areas (MPAs) are widely implemented to protect or rebuild exploited populations, yet their population-level effectiveness remains variable. One unresolved question is how the spatial placement of no-take zones interacts with larval dispersal directionality, particularly in linear coastal systems where connectivity is often asymmetric. For sedentary species with planktonic larvae, such as European lobster, it remains unclear when reserve placement most strongly affects demographic recovery after exploitation. Here, we use a spatially explicit individual-based model parameterized for European lobster in a generic linear coastal system.
