Agricultural standards are increasingly vital for global trade, yet they present a complex paradox as both trade enablers and significant barriers for developing countries. This study examines the food safety and quality regulatory frameworks in Tanzania, focusing on the horticultural sector’s engagement with intra-African and European Union (EU) markets. Despite horticulture being Tanzania’s fastest-growing agricultural sub-sector, accessing export markets remains a challenge, with only 0.3% of the produce reaching international markets. Findings reveal critical gaps in national standards. For instance, while the Tanzania Bureau of Standards (TBS) has developed over 3,000 standards, those relating to fruits and vegetables often lack specific safety limits for different hazards.
