Abstract Tight reservoirs are characterized by low porosity, low permeability and complex pore structures, resulting in poor waterflood recovery efficiency. Existing microscale two-phase flow studies are mostly based on two-dimensional geometric models, and the physical mechanism of fluid–solid coupling effect on flow behavior remains insufficiently understood. In this study, a three-dimensional digital core model of real tight sandstone was constructed using CT scanning and digital image processing techniques, and a phase-field mathematical model of oil–water two-phase flow considering bidirectional fluid–solid coupling was established, with its accuracy verified by classical capillary imbibition experiments (maximum relative error &lt;3.5%).
