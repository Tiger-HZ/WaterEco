Abstract Background Glioblastoma (GBM) is the deadliest brain tumor. Generally, male GBM occur at higher incidence and demonstrate shorter overall survival compared to females. Targetable drivers of this phenomenon remain undisclosed, preventing the integration of sex differences into the design of targeted therapeutics. Methods Here, we utilized metabolite tracing, pharmacological blockade, and nutrient deprivation to profile and target putative sex differences in cellular proliferation driven by the synthesis of glycine and nucleotides from glucose. Results Utilizing an established murine model of GBM (Nf1-/-DNp53), we observed male biases in de novo glycine biosynthesis and in the cellular response to inhibition of this pathway in nutrient-replete media.
