Abstract Background Breast cancer remains a major global and regional health concern. Although early detection and advanced therapies have extended survival, many breast cancer survivors (BCS) continue to experience persistent symptom burden and functional limitations. Online health information seeking (OHIS) and electronic health literacy (eHL) may support symptom self-management, yet their independent contributions to health-related quality of life (QoL) remain unclear. This study examined how eHL, OHIS, and exercise task self-efficacy (ESE) relate to QoL among BCS.
