The Roraima savanna is part of the Amazon basin and is located near the state capital Boa Vista in the Rio Branco-Rupununi basin, an area of ​​54,000 km2 shared by Brazil (40,000 km2), Guyana and Venezuela. The many swamps, streams and lakes are important in maintaining the water balance and recharging the streams, which are presently under intense pressure from urban sprawl. We surveyed the aquatic macrophytes of a natural lake in the savanna ecosystem. Trainer drainage, the lake’s diameter is about 1,450 m, with the maximum depth ranging from 0.5 m in the dry season to 4.0 m in the rainy season. The climate is tropical humid type A subtype Awi (Köppen classification), with mean monthly temperatures between 23 and 31 °C and mean annual rainfall 1750 mm. Samples were taken six times from September 2006 (end of rainy season) through March 2007 (end of dry season), and plant material collected for species identification was deposited in the Herbarium of the University of Roraima. We collected a total of 30 species in 16 families: Alismataceae, Araceae, Cyperaceae, Eriocaulaceae, Euphorbiaceae, Fabaceae, Lamiaceae, Lentibulariaceae, Linderniaceae, Melastomataceae, Menyanthaceae, Nymphaeaceae, Plantaginaceae, Poaceae, Rubiaceae, Xyridaceae, all with representatives in both seasons. The species richness recorded was high compared with other studies conducted in Brazil, highlighting the need for further investigations of the aquatic flora of Roraima. The family Cyperaceae was the most important in number of species and extent of colonization of the lake, especially along the shore. The complex underground system of sedges makes them indispensable in stabilizing sediments, and an important element for restoration of impacted areas.
