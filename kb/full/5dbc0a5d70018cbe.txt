Abstract
Nanoparticles (NPs) in both water and sediments can interact with other contaminants. Increasing
production and using NPs by discharge into the aquatic environment can be a threat to aquatic
organisms. This study aimed to consider the ecotoxicity and binary effect of arsenic (III) and silica
nanoparticles (nSiO2), as independent variables, individually and simultaneously on the mortality rate of
Zebrash (Danio rerio), as a response, by response surface methodology (RSM) under central composite
design (CCD). The results revealed that in the treatments within the concentration range of 1 to 5 mg L-1
arsenic and 1-100 mg L-1 nSiO2, no mortality was observed after 96 h. The optimal conditions to
achieving the lowest effect of simultaneous toxicity in the concentration range of nSiO2 and arsenic were
87.2 and 7 mg L-1, respectively. Accordingly, the desirable function of the predicted model was found to
be 0.78. According to these results, arsenic is toxic for zebrash. On the other hand, individual exposure
to nSiO2 did not cause acute toxicity in the studied species, while the arsenic toxicity was decreased by
increasing the concentration of nSiO2.
1. Introduction
Engineered nanoparticles (ENPs) with application in various sectors may be released into the aquatic
environment in the form of individual particles or aggregates/agglomerates, which are still prone to break
up in certain environmental conditions, and thus cause nanotoxicity to aquatic organisms (Minetto, Volpi
Ghirardini et al. 2016, Hu, Guo et al. 2017, Xu, Wang et al. 2020). On the other hand, there are several
substantial applications of ENPs which lead to their intentional release into aquatic environments, such
as environmental remediation, agricultural fertilization, pesticides, or herbicides, as well as aquaculture
enhancement (Onuegbu, Aggarwal et al. 2018, Barone, Hayes et al. 2019, Dawit Moges, Patel et al. 2020).
Among various ENPs, silica nanoparticles (nSiO2) are commonly used nanomaterial in different
industries (Kaur and Gupta 2009, Dumitrescu, Karunaratne et al. 2017). However, many studies have
reported some side effects of nSiO2 in living organisms, especially aquatic organisms (Liu, Wu et al.
2015, Dumitrescu, Karunaratne et al. 2017, Duan, Liang et al. 2018, Zhu, He et al. 2019), while Fent et al.
(Fent, Weisbrod et al. 2010) exposed zebrash embryos to nSiO2 less than six hours after fertilization.
Their results showed that there was not any absorption, movement and mortality in embryo mainly
because of nSiO2 agglomeration on the surface of the chorion.
According to the agency for toxic substance and disease registry (ATSDR), arsenic (As) is ranking among
the top ten hazardous substances (Sarkar, Mukherjee et al. 2014), which has severe health effects on the
environment and the life of a human (Shankar, Shanker et al. 2014, Bambino, Zhang et al. 2018). This
toxic element releases into aquatic environments from natural and anthropogenic sources, e.g., volcanic
sediments, coal, pesticides, dyes, and weapons (Garelick, Jones et al. 2008, Gao, Baisch et al. 2018).
Exposure to arsenic can cause harmful effects on human health and leading to various cancers and renal
and neurological problems (Dong, Liu et al. 2016, Yan, Naidu et al. 2018). Many studies have investigated
the As toxicity and its bioaccumulation in aquatic life, and how it can be harmful to their health (Samuel,
Page 3/17
Kathirvel et al. 2005, Dong, Liu et al. 2015, Kumar, Gupta et al. 2019, Ray, Shelly et al. 2020). Among
various sh, because of similar organs and cell types to other vertebrates, facile keeping laboratory
conditions, and fast development (Rubinstein 2003), zebrash has been regarded as a brilliant standard
and common model for toxicological evaluation of drugs, chemicals, and ecotoxicology (Scholz, Fischer
et al. 2008, Lin, Zhao et al. 2013, Chun, Park et al. 2017, Bai and Tang 2019). Therefore, zebrash was
selected as a case study for investigating both the individual and binary toxicity of nSiO2 and arsenic (III).
Hence, the response surface methodology (RSM), an affordable method for decreasing the experimental
phases (Sanati, Kamari et al. 2019), was employed for designing the experiment by the mathematical and
statistical methods, which analyzed the interactional and individual effects of nSiO2 and arsenic on
zebrash mortality.
In the current study, the mortality rate of zebrash was considered by two independent variables, nSiO2
and arsenic (III), where the experimental stages were designed by central composite design (CCD) under
RSM methodology using Design-Expert software. Furthermore, the binary effects and toxicity of
independent variables, were investigated on zebrash mortality as the response.
2. Materials And Methods
2.1. Model aquatic species
The aquatic species used in the present study was RFP-type zebrash (Danio rerio), each weighted in the
range of 1.18 ± 0.45 g. The sh were kept in 200 L aquariums to be adapted with laboratory conditions
for at least ten days at Aquatic Nanobiotechnology Laboratory at the University of Kurdistan.
2.2. Stock solutions and dispersions
2.2.1 Preparation of As(III) stock solution
For the preparation of 1000 mg L-1 arsenite stock solution, 4.16 g of sodium arsenite (NaAsO2) purchased
from Sigma Aldrich company was dissolved in deionized water and reached 1000 mL. The stock solution
was stored in a plastic container at 4 ° C and, during the experiments, was diluted to the desired arsenite
concentrations.
2.2.2 Preparation of silica nanoparticles suspension
Silica nanoparticle suspension was prepared by adding 1 g of nSiO2, supplied by US Research
Nanomaterial company, to 100 mL deionized water. Dispersion was sonicated, by FAPAN 150UT
sonicator (FAPAN CO. Ltd.), three times for 60 min while 400 mL and 500 mL of deionized water were
added to the solution in the second and third stages of sonication processes, respectively.
In addition, the morphology, size, and shape of nSiO2 have characterized by scanning electron
microscope (SEM) images (TESCAN microscope, Czech Republic) and transmission electron microscope
Page 4/17
(TEM) images (Zeiss EM900, Germany).
2.3 Investigation of individual and binary toxicity of As (III)
and nSiO2
To evaluate the acute toxicity of arsenic, lethal concentrations (LCs) were determined in 2 L exposure
vessels lled with 1 L fully aerated water during 96 h. In these experiments, As (III) was provided at the
concentration range of 2 to 60 mg L-1. Lethal concentration ranges in individual experiments of As (III) or
nSiO2 were determined by testing the toxicity of arsenite at eight concentrations including 5, 9, 11, 13, 15,
25, 32.5, and 35 mg L-1, and three concentrations of nSiO2 including 1, 10, and 100 mg L-1 added
individually to glass breakers. Then, seven healthy shes were transferred to each breaker. Feeding of
shes was stopped 24 h before commencing the toxicity experiment. Furthermore, in all experiments,
after the shes were exposed to chemical compounds, the number of dead shes during 96 h and every
24 h were counted and recorded, and the dead shes were taken out of the water. Finally, to estimate
lethal concentrations, results from counting the dead sh were analyzed using the EPA Probit Analysis
program (version 1.5, released by US Environment Protection Agency).
In binary toxicity tests, 13 different mixture concentrations of arsenic and nSiO2 were prepared according
to CCD (Table 2) in 1 L fully aerated water, added to glass breakers.

3. Results And Discussion
3.1. Characterization
The morphology and size information of nSiO2 were characterized by scanning electron microscope
(SEM) and transmission electron microscope (TEM), as shown in Fig. 1. The SEM images (Fig. 1.a)
revealed that the nSiO2 were in a spherical shape (Zargar, Hartanto et al. 2017), and also the TEM image
(Fig. 1.b) is also approved the SEM results. The particle size of nSiO2 was calculated by Axio Vision Rel.
4.8 software using TEM image. According to Fig. 1.c, the average particle size of nSiO2 was 17.29 nm.
2.3. Lethal concentration of As(III)
Table (3) shows the results of acute toxicity tests for determining the lethal concentrations of As (III) in
the zebrash. According to the obtained results, the control treatment did not experience any mortality
after 96 h. The results showed that in treatments with As (III) concentrations of 1 to 5 mg L-1 of arsenic,
no death was observed after 96 h. Accordingly, the more the concentration of arsenic would be increased,
the dead sh could be obtained.

In the toxicity test of pollutants for aquatics species, determining acute toxicity is the rst stage
investigating the median lethal concentration of toxicants in water. The results of acute sh toxicity
experiments are not compatible with most current animal welfare laws because mortality is the primary
endpoint, and it is often supposed that sh have got distressed and possibly in pain by exposure to the
toxicant (Lammer, Carr et al. 2009). Sun and colleagues, who studied the effect of low concentration of
As (III) on zebrash larvae, showed that at As (III) concentrations  ≤  150 µg L-1, the survival rate of the
larvae was declined due to oxidative stress (Sun, Zhang et al. 2019). In another study, Kabir and
colleagues found that abnormal embryonic development such as heart defect and abnormal head
development in zebrash embryos could have occurred in the presence of 2mM arsenic exposure (Kabir,
Anwar et al. 2020).
3.3. Effect of the nSiO2
The results of acute toxicity experiments based on a single exposure to nSiO2 in zebrash revealed no
mortality in sh with the nSiO2 concentration range between 1 and 100 mg L-1 after 96 h. The initial
studies for investigating NPs toxicity on aquatic species were conducted in 2000 by (Frenkel, Kimmel et
al. 2000), where the effect of silver chloride (AgCl) NPs was investigated on sh skin. Vranic et al. (Vranic,
Shimada et al. 2019) showed that silica NPs did not affect the survival rate, morphology, and other
physiological aspects of zebrash. In addition, they found that chorion plays an important inhibiting role
against penetration of nSiO2 within the cell, while in the absence of chorion, nSiO2 decreased the survival
rate dramatically. In another study, Liu et al. revealed that there was no a noticeable alteration in the
cardiovascular system of zebrash larvae in the presence of a low concentration of nSiO2 (~ 25 µg mL-1).
On the contrary, the adverse effects of CdCl2 were increased substantially in the presence of nSiO2 in
Page 8/17
zebrash as the blood pressure, and apoptosis increased and heart rate decreased consequently (Liu,
Wang et al. 2021).
3.4 Evaluation of binary toxicity of nSiO2 and As (III)
3.4.1 Statistical analysis and lack of tness
Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to investigate the binary effect of variables on zebrash
mortality. By using multivariate regression analysis on the available data, the relationship between the
responses and the independent variables was obtained through linear (24 and 48 hours) or quadratic (72
and 96 hours) equations that suggested by Design-Expert software (Eqs. 4 to 7). The results of each
independent variable and their interaction are shown in Tables S-1 to S-4 using the applied models in the
form of analysis of variance. Using the F and p values, the signicance of the correlations between each
variable and the mortality (response) was determined. Regression models for 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, and 96 h,
yielded p values of 0.015, 0.032, 0.002 and 0.002 and F values of 6.47, 4.93, 13.33 and 12.64,
respectively. The results showed that the models are suitable for spatial design and describing the
relationship between independent variables and response. In addition, ANOVA studies showed that when
the value of the sum of squares increases, the signicance of that variable increases again.
Furthermore, the value of the coecient of determination (R2), which checked the t of the model, in 24 h,
48 h, 72 h, and 96 h were 0.56, 0.49, 0.90, and 0.90, respectively. The results indicate that 90% of the
experimental data can be described by the predicated model. The lack of t values of F-value after 24 h,
48 h, 72 h, and 96 h are equal to 0.51, 1.36, 0.39, and 0.64, respectively. Figure 2 shows the correlation
between the predicted and the actual (experimental) values. Observed that, the correlation between actual
and predicted values increases dramatically with increasing sh exposure time. So, this relationship
becomes completely linear after 96 hours.

The interaction of independent variables including As (III) and nSiO2 with mortality after 24 h, 48 h, 72 h
and 96 h are shown in Fig. 3 using 3D and contour plots. According to these results, by decreasing the

100 mg L-1, after 24 h, 48 h, 72 h and 96 h the mortality rate decreased from 74.5%, 79.8%, 82.6%, and

3.4.3 Analysis of independent variables optimization
The main aim of the present study is to achieve the optimal conditions of crucial factors involved in the
experiment to maximize the effect of silica nanoparticle concentration on decreasing the mortality effect
of arsenic using the mathematical model developed. For this purpose, the nSiO2, arsenic solutions, and
mortality rate of zebrash were set for achieving the maximum desirability from zero to one using the
numerical optimization menu of Design-Expert Software. To reach the highest desirability, the maximum
and minimum ranges for nSiO2 and arsenic solutions were selected from 1 to 100 mg L-1 and 2 to 60 mg
L-1, respectively. Figure 4 shows the desirability ramp for silica nanoparticles. Consequently, the optimal
concentrations of nSiO2 and arsenic solutions were obtained by 87.2 mg L-1 and 7.0 mg L-1, respectively.
In addition, the 0.78 desirability function showed that the response surface methodology (RSM) is
suitable for optimum condition determination.
4. Conclusion
Heavy metals such as arsenic are considered a class of crucial pollutants in aquatic environments, as
these pollutants, in addition to their lethal toxicity, have a high potential for bioaccumulation in various
tissues of aquatic species. Besides, nSiO2, such as heavy metals, would have had harmful effects on the
environment, which have been discharged to the aquatic bodies in various ways. Based on the study’s
aims, the correlation and acute toxicity of various concentrations of arsenic and nSiO2 as independent
variables on the mortality rate of zebrash as a response was determined and optimized by central
composite design (CCD) under response surface methodology (RSM). Accordingly, the results revealed
that by increasing the arsenic, the mortality rate of zebrash is increased, while the toxicity of arsenic is
decreased in the presence of the nSiO2; however, the effect of the nSiO2 on the mortality rate is minor.
Consequently, ndings showed that arsenic is signicantly harmful to zebrash, and the low
concentrations of this can lead to massive losses in the aquatic environments.
