Topography is widely recognized as a primary control on groundwater flow organization in mountain settings along with Recharge (R) and Hydraulic conductivity (K), yet groundwater simultaneously modifies topography through seepage-driven erosion and sapping. This bidirectional coupling implies that landscapes should record the architecture of subsurface flow. However, the diagnostic value of geomorphic indicators remains poorly constrained and rarely used as a proxy for understanding groundwater flow organization in mountain systems.
