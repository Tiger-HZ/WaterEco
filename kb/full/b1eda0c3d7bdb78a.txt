Introduction Soil salinity severely constrains crop productivity in coastal agricultural regions by impairing soil water availability, nutrient acquisition, and root-zone conditions. Straw-based soil management practices have been widely proposed for saline soil improvement, but their comparative effectiveness and underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Methods A two-year container experiment with barley ( Hordeum vulgare L.) was conducted during the 2023 and 2024 growing seasons to compare the effects of surface straw mulching (SM), straw incorporation (SI), and straw-derived biochar incorporation (BC) with an unamended control (CT) on soil physicochemical properties, crop growth, and yield formation in coastal saline soil.
