Pre-computed embeddings from geospatial foundation models turn the most expensive step of satellite analysis into shared data. We test how much analysis this enables for one country, using TESSERA, which publishes a 128-dimensional embedding for every 10 m pixel and year. An audit of Kenya shows that all 4,766 tiles centred in the country carry all nine years (2017–2025), but that the landmask removes the sea and not inland water. We then map Lake Baringo, a Rift Valley lake that has flooded its shores, by assigning each pixel to the nearer of two reference embeddings (open water and urban land), with no training.
