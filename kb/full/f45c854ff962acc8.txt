Abstract The entropy surface density at the event horizon of spherically symmetric black holes is calculated in this paper. The results show that this entropy density is constant, independent of the radius of event horizon location. This explains why the black hole entropy is proportional to the horizon area. The number of quantum states per unit horizon area is constant, explains the proportionality between black hole entropy and horizon area. Further results show that the degrees of freedom may be encoded by the horizon. The quantum states of spherically symmetric black holes are uniformly distributed near the horizon, supporting the holographic principle.
