A total of 33 isolate (12 from borehole and 21 from well water) comprising Eight (8) different bacterial species were isolated and identified: Escherichia coli, Klebsiella spp, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Salmonella typhi, Proteus mirabilis, Bacillus cereus, Enterobacter aerogenes, and Staphylococcus aureus. Specific antibiotics were tested against their corresponding type or group of bacteria isolated using disc diffusion method. The isolated bacteria that were found to be susceptible to all the antibiotics were 4(33.3%) and 3(14.3%), those that showed resistance to 1 or 2 antibiotics are 6(50.0%) and 10(47.6%) and those resistant to 3 and above antibiotics are 2(12.5%) and 8(38.1%) for boreholes and wells respectively. Two (2) species of P. aeruginosa from the borehole water displayed 100% Multiple Antibiotic Resistance (MAR). Similarly, four (4) species of P. aeruginosa from well water exhibited 75% resistance to multiple antibiotics; three (3) species of E. coli showed 100% resistance to multiple antibiotics, two (2) K. pneumoniae showed 100% resistance to multiple antibiotics and two (2) Bacillus cereus indicated 50% resistance to multiple antibiotics. Molecular detection of ESBL genes showed negative result for all of the isolates moreover, quinolone resistance genes (qnrA, qnrS, and qnrB) was seen in four (4) isolates among the test bacteria. Therefore, multiple antibiotic-resistant bacteria can be found in water and may provide a way through which MAR-bacteria get into human and animal bodies. It is therefore recommended that antibiotics should be enhanced to promote their effectiveness on MAR species.
