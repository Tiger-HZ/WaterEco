ABSTRACT River regulation following damming is often associated with deleterious downstream effects, in large part due to reduced total discharge and disruption of seasonal flooding. These effects would be expected to be exacerbated by drought, particularly extended drought. River regulation also enables flow engineering that has the potential to partially mitigate downstream effects. We examined Tuolumne River benthos, in the reach below the iconic Hetch Hetchy Reservoir and within Yosemite National Park (California, USA), in comparison with two reference reaches, above the reservoir and on the Merced River, across 5 years (2015–2019) during the extreme California drought of the 2010s. One wet year (2017) facilitated large engineered floods that contrasted strongly with antecedent flows.
