Abstract Tropical cyclones (TCs) often produce satellite-observed surface chlorophyll-a blooms, which are commonly interpreted as increased phytoplankton biomass. Using 44 cyclones in the Bay of Bengal (BoB), we found that surface chlorophyll-a increased rapidly during TC passage but declined within a few days, while the basin-scale composite showed no significant increase in chlorophyll-a integrated over the upper 100 m. The surface chlorophyll-a response was weaker in the central deep basin than along the northwestern shelf and off Sri Lanka. These regional differences were associated with freshwater stratification, barrier-layer structure, coastal transport, and upwelling.
