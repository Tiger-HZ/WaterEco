Evapotranspiration (ET) is a major component of the water and energy cycle, influencing hydrologic processes, agricultural management, groundwater recharge, and land surface–atmosphere interactions. Water balance methods for estimating ET are widely used because of their direct connection to the conservation of mass and their applicability across scales. This review examines the theoretical basis and recent developments in water balance approaches for estimating ET across different hydroclimatic regimes. It summarizes classic soil water balance methods, physically based hydrologic models, remote-sensing approaches, and integrated machine learning techniques.
