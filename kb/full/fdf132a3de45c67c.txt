We utilized both hydropeaking and experimental flows to quantify responses of macroinvertebrate drift, benthic assemblages, and fish consumption to double-peak release patterns. Our results suggest that changes in discharge may have a greater impact on macroinvertebrate drift than absolute flow levels, such that mean daily drift biomass was significantly higher during double-peaking; however, drift increases were sustained for only 30–60 days despite ongoing hydropeaking. Drift increases were proportional to peak magnitude, with drift biomass peaking during the rising limb of the hydrograph and declining prior to the cessation of peak flows.
