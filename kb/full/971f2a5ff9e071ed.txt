Inroduction Source credibility is fundamental to how medical information is processed,yet the underlying neurocognitive mechanisms remain poorly understood. We investigated how two credibility cues — a doctor’s expertise (years of work experience) and aggregate patient ratings (star ratings) — modulate brain responses to veracity cues (‘True’ vs. ‘False’) presented after health- related headlines. Methods We recorded EEG from 36 participants while they evaluated true and false headlines across various medical topics (e.g., vitamins, GMOs, vaccination). Each headline was paired with a fictional doctor’s profile that systematically varied in expertise (work experience) and patient ratings (star ratings).
