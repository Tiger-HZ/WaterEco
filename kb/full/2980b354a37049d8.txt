Reservoir sedimentation threatens storage capacity and environmental safety in the Three Gorges Reservoir (TGR), yet dredging remains constrained by suspended particulate matter (SPM) release, heavy metal migration, sediment-treatment costs, and uncertain reuse benefits. This study developed an integrated source–process–end framework for the first in-situ dredging pilot in the TGR by combining long-term sediment budgeting, geoaccumulation-index assessment, hydrodynamic–SPM simulation, sediment separation and dewatering, resource utilization, and economic sensitivity analysis. From June 2003 to December 2022, monitored suspended-sediment inflow and outflow were 2.6943 and 0.6350 billion tonnes, respectively, giving a conservative net deposition estimate of 2.0593 billion tonnes. Deposition was concentrated in the permanent backwater zone, especially from Fuling to Fengjie. The geoaccumulation-index results showed relatively low sediment-associated heavy metal risks at Fuling and Zhongxian; both sections had no moderate or higher contamination. Simulations showed that pneumatic dredging generated the smallest SPM plume because its enclosed suction-and-conveyance process reduced open-water disturbance. Under an inflow of 5000 m 3 /s and a water level of 150 m, the SPM increment at Fuling remained below the 10 mg/L control threshold. Sediment sorting and dewatering enabled coarse particles to be used as aggregates and fine sediment to be used for fired bricks and mine-restoration soil. The pilot-scale direct economic balance was negative, with a deficit of 1.055 million yuan, but sensitivity analysis indicated that higher coarse-particle recovery, higher-value fine-sediment products, and larger scale could improve feasibility. The framework supports pollution-controlled dredging and sediment reuse in large reservoirs.
