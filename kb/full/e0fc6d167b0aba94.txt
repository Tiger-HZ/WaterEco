Recent advances have been made in treating hypersaline brines (salinity >10 wt%) from the water,
energy, and agriculture industries. One of the emerging solutions is solar-driven interfacial
evaporation, while its application is limited by persistent salt accumulation. Here, we report a solar
interfacial evaporator-crystallizer comprising a bilayer, two-support-span architecture that integrates
a nanostructured titanium (Ti) mesh with a photothermal b ‑PDMS layer. Localized top-surface light
absorption and unidirectional vapor generation promote edge-directed salt crystallization, mitigating
surface fouling. Under extreme salinity (20 wt%), the device maintains net solar-driven evaporation
ﬂux ≥1.2 kg·m‒2 h‒1, redistributing salt to span edges, limiting crust coverage to <10% during the 12 h
illumination test. Owing to the b-PDMS layer, low interfacial adhesion enables passive salt shedding
overnight, supporting cyclic stability. Time-lapse imaging and environmental scanning electron
microscopy elucidate the coupled dynamics of evaporation, crystallization, dissolution, and shedding
across diurnal cycles. Furthermore, indoor cyclic and outdoor tests using real seawater brine
demonstrate operational robustness and scalability, establishing this platform as an effective, zero-
liquid-discharge solution for hypersaline brine management.
Freshwater scarcity in arid and semi-arid regions has driven a rapid
expansion of desalination technologies, particularly reverse osmosis (RO)
membranes, multi-stageﬂash (MSF) or multi-effect distillation (MED)1–5.
While effective, these systems are hindered by high costs, complex infra-
structure, and the persistent challenge of hypersaline brine disposal6.R O
generates smaller volumes of highly concentrated brine, whereas thermal
desalination (MSF and MED) produces larger volumes of more dilute
streams; however, in both cases, brine disposal remains an unresolved
challenge7,8. Conventional disposal methods, including marine outfalls,
deep-well injection, and evaporation ponds, are costly, land-intensive, and
environmentally burdensome9. Similar challenges also affect the treatment
of produced water and saline wastew aters from the energy, agriculture,
mining, and manufacturing sectors. Consequently, there is growing demand
for decentralized and sustainable technologies capable of treating diverse
hypersaline streams while advancing minimal and zero-liquid-discharge
(ZLD) strategies10–12.
Despite substantial advances in brine treatment and water-recovery
technologies, managing hypersali ne wastewater and produced water
remains constrained by trade-offs among energy consumption, cost, land
requirements, and residual brine disposal. Membrane distillation can treat
highly concentrated streams with near-complete salt rejection, but its per-
formance is often limited by membrane wetting, scaling, andﬂux decline at
elevated salinities13. Hybrid membrane systems integrating RO with com-
plementary separation processes can enhance water recovery, yet their
effectiveness declines as osmotic pressure rises and concentrate manage-
ment becomes more challenging14. Thermal crystallizers achieve ZLD by
converting residual brine into solid salts, but remain constrained by high
capital and energy requirements 15. Evaporation ponds provide a com-
paratively simple disposal option, y et require large land areas, depend
heavily on climatic conditions, and pose environmental risks associated with
salt accumulation and seepage6. Collectively, these approaches highlight a
persistent trade-off among water recovery, energy demand, land footprint,
and economic viability.
To address these limitations, atte ntion has recently been directed
toward solar-driven and nature-inspired interfacial evaporation systems for
selective water transport and salt rejection, with recent research increasingly
shifting from maximizingevaporation rates toward enabling ZLD treatment
through rational control of crystallization pathways16–19. Recent studies have

The results presented in Figs.2 and 3 demonstrate that conventional
bidirectional evaporators are well suited for desalination of relatively
low-salinity feedwaters, such as brackish water and seawater, where salt
accumulation is limited. However, under hypersaline conditions, pro-
gressive salt deposition on the illuminated surface increasingly obstructs
evaporation sites and degrades long-term performance. The proposed
unidirectional evaporator overcomes this limitation by con ﬁning eva-
poration to the bottom surface while maintaining a clean photothermal
surface through edge-directed salt crystallization. This behavior arises
from the coupled effects of localized evaporation, capillary ﬂow, and ion
transport rather than the temperature distribution alone. Because the top
b-PDMS layer is impermeable, it serves exclusively as the photothermal
absorber, whereas evaporation occurs only from the exposed bottom
surface of the suspended Ti mesh. Although evaporation is most intense
beneath the illuminated region, continuous water replenishment sup-
presses sustained supersaturation in the central span, and transports
concentrated brine toward the boundaries. As the ﬂow approaches the
junction between the suspended span and the supports, the liquid
velocity decreases while ion concentration continues to increase, leading
to preferential supersaturation and crystal growth at the edges. Once
established, the edge crystals continue to grow by incorporating ions
delivered through capillary ﬂow, while the central evaporation region
remains continuously wetted and largely free of persistent salt accu-
mulation. This mechanism is consistent with our previously developed
capillary transport-evaporation model 35, which predicts that the pre-
cipitation front is governed by the balance between evaporation-induced
concentration gradients and capillary-driven liquid transport rather than
by the location of maximum temperature alone.
Mechanisms of salt crystallization and deposition on directional
evaporators
Figure 4a, b illustrate the crystallization and subsequent salt deposition
behaviors observed in both bidirectional and unidirectional evaporators in
different saline conditions. In the bidirectional con ﬁguration, the water
supply through both supports continuously transports saline solution to the
span owing to capillary force and evaporation, effectively pushing crystal-
lized salt toward the edges. This mechanism promotes directional edge
crystallization, particularly when using water with low salinity, such as
simulated seawater. As a result, less than 4% of the top surface area becomes
covered with salt after 12 h of illumination under one sun, with deposition
localized primarily at the edges (Fig. 4c). However, under hypersaline
conditions (salinity >10 wt%), the water supply becomes insuf ﬁcient to
displace all crystallized salt patche s from the central region of the span.
Consequently, salt patches begin to accumulate on both the top and bottom
surfaces, obstructing the active evaporation sites. This ef fect is especially
pronounced in the bidirectional evaporator, where more than 70% and 95%
of the surface area becomes blocked after 8 and 6 h of operation, respectively,
when using solutions with salinities of 12 and 20 wt% (Fig.4d, e). In contrast,
the unidirectional evaporator consi stently maintains directional edge
crystallization across all salinity levels, with salt coverage remaining below
10% throughout the experiment.

evaporator.
Cyclic and outdoor performance
Indoor cyclic experiments and outdoor evaporation tests with real-time
brine solutions were conducted over 12-h periods under realistic weather
conditions, which are essential for evaluating the practical performance
of the unidirectional evaporator. Different from most existing literature
focusing on controlled laboratory conditions and simulated seawater,
this study emphasizes the real-world applicability of the proposed
solution. To assess the device ’s durability and long-term operational
capability in treating hypersaline brine, actual seawater with a measured
salinity of 4.2 wt% was collected from Al Hudayriat Island, Abu Dhabi,
UAE (Fig. 5a). A 12 wt% brine solution was then prepared by evaporating
excess water from the collected seawater. Indoor cyclic experiments
using this hypersaline brine were conducted over four consecutive days,
with each cycle consisting of 12 h of 1-sun illumination followed by 12 h

In short, this study introduces a robust evaporator that addresses the
persistent challenge of salt accumulation during hypersaline brine
treatment. By engineering a bilayer architecture composed of nanos-
tructured titanium mesh and a photothermal b-PDMS coating, the
device enables unidirectional vapor evaporation and prevents top-side
salt crystallization. This design innovation promotes directional salt
crystallization toward the span edges, minimizing the obstruction of the
span area and maintaining high evaporation performance. Under
extreme salinity conditions (up to 20 wt%), the device consistently
achieves stable evaporation ﬂuxes over 1.01 kg·m⁻²·h⁻¹, with salt coverage
remaining below 10% after 12 h of operation. The low adhesion of salt on
the b-PDMS surface facilitates passive salt shedding, enabling unin-
terrupted cyclic operation during the day and night cycles. Indoor cyclic
experiments and outdoor ﬁeld tests using real seawater-derived brine
further validate the device ’s operational durability and adaptability
under ﬂuctuating environmental conditions. Collectively, these ﬁndings
demonstrate the unidirectional evaporator as a sustainable and ef ﬁcient
solution for hypersaline water desalination and produced water treat-
ment without liquid discharge for real-world large-scale deployment.

Methods
Materials processing and fabrication of evaporator
Titanium mesh was chemically oxidized by hydrothermal treatment in 1 M
sodium hydroxide (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.9% purity) solution at 220 °C for 4 h
after cleaning with ethanol and deionized water, followed by rinsing with
deionized water. The oxidized Ti (oxi. Ti) mesh had an effective porosity of
~0.4 and was folded into a two-support-span conﬁguration. To fabricate the
unidirectional evaporator, a photothermal barrier layer consisting of carbon
black-loaded PDMS (b-PDMS; PDMS base/curing agent = 9:1 with 0.1 wt%
carbon black) was prepared, degassed for 15 min, and selectively transferred
onto the upper span of the inverted mesh using a bottom-up deposition
method. Brieﬂy, 2 mL of b-PDMS was spread on a Teﬂon sheet at 120 °C,
partially cured for 60 s, and the inverted oxi. Ti mesh was pressed onto the
semi-cured ﬁlm. This process coated (~300 μm) only the top surface, pre-
venting polymer penetration through the mesh cross-section. Ti mesh
without the b-PDMS layer served as the bidirectional evaporator.
Characterization

Lambda 1050 spectrophotometer, wettability by contact-angle goniometry
(Kyowa), and surface morphology by SEM (Phenom XL). Salt dissolution
and crystallization were investigated using environmental SEM (Quattro
ESEM) under low-vacuum conditions with a Peltier cooling stage. The salt-
crystal adhesion force was quantiﬁed using a digital force gauge (AMF-5,
