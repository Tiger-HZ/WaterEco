Abstract
Zirconium-based porphyrin metal organic framework (Zr-PMOF) is considered as a promising
photocatalytic material due to its large specic surface area, wide visible light absorption capacity, good
water stability and chemical stability. However, the recombination of photogenerated carriers of monomer
PMOF limits its performance of photocatalytic organic pollutants degradation. Metal sulde has a
suitable visible band gap, which can form a heterojunction with MOF materials to enhance the
photocatalytic eciency of MOF. Therefore, a typical metal sulde semiconductor ZnIn2S4 (ZIS) is
introduced into a Zr-MOF (MOF-525) by solvothermal method to prepare the ZIS/MOF-525 composite
photocatalyst in this work. The results of characterization analysis, optical analysis and electrochemical
analysis show that the interface of ZIS/MOF-525 forms a typical type- heterojunction, which accelerates
the electron transport rate and effectively inhibits the recombination of photogenerated e- and h+ in MOF-
525. The optimal removal eciency of tetracycline (TC) by ZIS/MOF-525-3 can reach 93.8% under 60 min
visible light illumination, which is greater than that of pure MOF-525 (37.2%) and ZnIn2S4 (70.0%), and it
can still maintain good stability after ve cycles reusing experiment. This work provides feasible insight
for the preparation of novel and ecient PMOF-based photocatalysts in the future.
1. Introduction
Antibiotics have saved countless lives since their discovery and application, which is one of the major
achievements in the history of human medicine (Liu et al. 2021, Shao et al. 2022b). Among them,
tetracycline (TC) has become a widely used antibiotic because of its strong antibacterial effect and low
price. However, people gradually found that the use of TC has unignored side effects (Liang et al. 2021a,
Shao et al. 2022a, Wu et al. 2022). From a therapeutic point of view, the effectiveness of TC is
undermined by the increasing number of antibiotic-resistant pathogens. From the perspective of an
ecosystem and natural environment, antibiotic pollution caused by the extensive use and discharge of TC
also needs social attention (Shao et al. 2020). Researchers have tried to solve the problem of
environmental antibiotic pollution through adsorption, photocatalysis, biodegradation, electrochemical
treatment and other methods, and have made good progress (Choudhary et al. 2021, Dutta &Mala 2020,
Liang et al. 2019, Pan et al. 2020, Wu et al. 2020). Among them, photocatalysis technology has the
advantages of green, high eciency, economy and environmental protection, which has attracted
extensive attention from scientists (Jin et al. 2021, Luo et al. 2020, Luo et al. 2021, Nguyen et al. 2020,
Tong et al. 2018). The conversion and utilization of solar energy have also made photocatalysis one of
the hottest topics in the past decades. At present, various photocatalysts have been used to degrade
antibiotics, but they are still in the laboratory stage. The low specic surface area, poor stability and easy
recombination of photogenerated carriers limit the practical application of catalysts. Therefore, it is
necessary to further explore a more stable and ecient photocatalytic degradation system of antibiotics
(Ge et al. 2022, He et al. 2021, Liang et al. 2021b).
Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) possess a high potential for photocatalytic degradation of organic
pollutants due to their semiconductor characteristics (Hussain et al. 2021, Li et al. 2019b). MOF was rst
Page 4/27
demonstrated to have the ability of photocatalytic degradation of pollutants in 2007, Garcia et al.
(Xamena et al. 2007) found that MOF-5 exhibited photocatalytic degradation activity for phenol. In the
following ten years, a variety of MOFs with photocatalytic degradation ability has been successively
found (Jing et al. 2017, Liang et al. 2015, Masoomi et al. 2016, Zhao et al. 2017). Adequate thermal
stability and chemical stability can largely assist MOFs with photocatalytic reactions. The stability of
MOFs is determined by metal nodes and organic ligands. It is generally considered that MOF formed by
combining metal ions with high charge/radius ratio as metal nodes and organic ligands with carboxylic
acid usually have excellent chemical stability and thermal stability (Fateeva et al. 2015, Xu et al. 2016).
Zr-porphyrin MOFs (Zr-PMOF) is a kind of Zr-MOF synthesized with non-metallic porphyrin as a ligand.
The high valence state of Zr4+, the high connection number of secondary building units (SBUs), and the
carboxylic acid terminal group of porphyrin ligand together give Zr-PMOF excellent stability. In addition,
the porphyrins-based ligand in Zr-PMOF has a wide range of visible light absorption capacity, which is the
main reason for the excellent visible light catalytic performance of Zr-PMOF (Lin et al. 2021, Qin et al.
2021, Shi et al. 2018). On the other hand, Zr-O metal nodes orderly arrange the porphyrin ligands to form
a three-dimensional (3D) structure, preventing the stacking and aggregation of porphyrins, making Zr-
PMOF exhibit better photocatalytic ability than porphyrin monomers (Wang et al. 2019). So far, a series of
Zr-PMOF have been synthesized and applied in the eld of photocatalysis, such as PCN-221 (Wu et al.
2019), PCN-222 (Sharma et al. 2020), PCN-224 (Shi et al. 2018), MOF-525 (Huang et al. 2019), etc.
Although pure PMOF has a very high visible light absorption capacity, its ability to photodegrade
pollutants is limited by the easy recombination of photogenerated e−-h+ pairs. Recent studies have shown
that the combination of Zr-PMOF with other semiconductors can form heterojunctions, accelerate the
generation of photogenerated carriers, and effectively inhibit their recombination, thus improving the
photocatalytic performance of Zr-PMOF (Bu et al. 2016, Ye et al. 2016, Zhang et al. 2016b). For instance,
Jin et al. (Jin et al. 2021) constructed a hierarchical ZnIn2S4@PCN-224 Z-scheme heterojunction to
dominate visible photocatalytic degradation of TCH. The results showed that TCH (20 mg L− 1) was
almost completely degraded after 60 min with visible light illumination, which was signicantly higher
than that of PCN-224 monomer and other MOF-based photocatalysts (Chen et al. 2022, Dong et al. 2019,
Fakhri &Bagheri 2020). Wu et al. (Wu et al. 2019) encapsulated CH3NH3PbI3 (MAPbI3) perovskite
quantum dots (QDs) in the holes of PCN-224(Fe) by a sequential deposition route then constructed
MAPbI3@PCN-221(Fe) composite photocatalyst. The photocatalytic test results showed that
MAPbI3@PCN-221(Fe) exhibited obviously improved yields for CO2 reduction, which was 25–38 times
higher than those of corresponding pure PCN-221(Fe).
As a typical Zr-PMOF, MOF-525 (Fig. S1) has a smaller size and larger specic surface area than other Zr-
PMOF. In addition, MOF-525 shows a wide and intense absorption in the region of 350–750 nm, which
indicates that MOF-525 has the potential as a highly ecient photocatalyst (Morris et al. 2012). Toyao et
al. (Toyao et al. 2015) rstly proved that MOF-525 possessed the ability to catalyze the degradation of
arylboronic acid under visible light. Liu et al. (Liu &Iop 2020) compared the photocatalytic degradation of
2-chloroethyl ethyl sulde (CEES) by MOF-525, NTU-9 and ZIF-8. The results showed that CEES could be
Page 5/27
completely degraded to 2-chloroethyl sulde (CEESO) when MOF-525 was used as a photocatalyst, and a
certain proportion of CEES could be degraded to CEESO when NTU-9 was used as the photocatalyst,
while ZIF-8 had no ability to degrade CEESO, indicating that MOF-525 had stronger photocatalytic
reduction ability than other MOFs. Boyer et al. (Boyer et al. 2020) studied the photocatalytic activity of
methyl acrylate (MA) polymerization mediated by using MOF-525(Zn), PCN-222(Zn), PCN-223(Zn) and
PCN-224(Zn) as photocatalysts respectively. The results showed that compared with other Zr-PMOF,
MOF-525(Zn) had a smaller size and larger specic surface area, thus showing the best photocatalytic
performance.
However, similar to other Zr-PMOF, the photocatalytic eciency of pure MOF-525 was still limited
because of the easy recombination of photogenerated e−-h+ pairs (Zhang et al. 2016a). Therefore, it is
necessary to nd effective strategies to improve the utilization of photogenerated carriers in MOF-525. As
shown above, forming a heterojunction structure with other materials is a feasible way to improve the
separation eciency of photogenerated e−-h+ of Zr-PMOF. For MOF-525, there are few studies in this area,
so it has broad research prospects. As a typic metal sulde, ZnIn2S4 has a suitable visible band gap,
which can form a heterojunction with MOF materials to enhance the photocatalytic performance of MOF
(Chen et al. 2019, Liu et al. 2018). In this paper, a typical metal sulde semiconductor ZnIn2S4 was
introduced into MOF-525 via a facile solvothermal method, and a ZIS/MOF-525 double electron transfer
system was constructed to solve the problem of easy recombination of photogenerated carriers in MOF-
525. The improvement of the photocatalytic effect of ZIS/MOF-525 was veried by the degradation
experiment of TC. Through a series of optical and electrochemical properties, it was veried that
ZIS/MOF-525 was a kind of photocatalytic material with the high catalytic ability and strong stability.
2. Experimental Section
2.1 Materials
Zirconyl chloride octahydrate (ZrOCl2∙ 8H2O), Thioacetamide (TAA, CH3CSNH2), dimethylformamide
(DMF, HCON(CH)), tetrais(4-carboxyphenyl)porphyrin (TCPP), benzoic acid (C6H5COOH), acetone
(CH3COCH3), ethanol (C2H5OH), indium (III) nitrate hydrate (In(NO3)3·xH2O), glycerol (CH3H8O3) and TC
were purchased from Aladdin or Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. The deionized water (18.25 M Ω
cm− 1) was produced from the UP ultrapure machine and used throughout the experiment.
2.2 Synthesis
The MOF-525 was synthesized according to the method previously reported (Boyer et al. 2020).
Specically, 1.3 mmol of ZrOCl2·8H2O and 0.04 mol benzoic acid were dissolved in a threaded
scintillation bottle containing 8 mL of DMF solution. The bottle was then sealed and placed in an oven at
80 ℃ for 2 h. After the solution was cooled to room temperature, 47 mg TCPP (0.6 mol) was added to the
solution and treated with ultrasound for 20 min. Finally, it was placed in the oven at 80 ℃ for 24 h.
Page 6/27
Finally, the samples were centrifuged and washed with DMF and acetone, and then dried in a vacuum
oven (120 ℃ ) for 10 h.
ZnIn2S4/MOF-525 nanocomposites were prepared by a one-step solvothermal method (Scheme 1).
Specically, the prepared MOF-525 powder (0 mg, 10 mg, 20 mg, 40 mg, 60 mg) was ultrasonically
dispersed into a mixed solution containing 15 mL of DMF and 5 mL of glycerol, then 0.135 g of ZnCl2,
0.586 g of In(NO3)3·3H2O and 0.301 g of thioacetamide were added and stirred for 1 h. The collected
suspension was transferred to a 50 mL polytetrauoroethylene lined stainless steel autoclave and
maintained in an oven at 160 ℃ for 16 h. After the sample was cooled, it was centrifuged and dried in a
60 ℃ vacuum drying oven for 6 h to obtain samples ZnIn2S4, ZIS/MOF-525-1, ZIS/MOF-525-2, ZIS/MOF-
525-3 and ZIS/MOF-525-4 respectively.
2.3 Characterization
The phase structure was assessed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker AXS D8). The nanostructure and
crystal structure of samples were examined by scanning electron microscope (SEM, TESCAN MIRA LMS)
and transmission electron microscope (TEM, Titan G260-300). The functional groups of samples were
studied by Fourier transform infrared (FTIR, Nicolet iS10) with KBr pellet method. The surface
compositions were obtained by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientic K-Alpha). The
Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) specic surface area was measured by Quantachrome apparatus with N2
adsorption-desorption measurements. Photoluminescence (PL) spectra was recorded by a uorescence
spectrometer (Hitachi F-7000, Japan). The total organic carbon (TOC) was evaluated by a Total Organic
Carbon Analyzer (TOC-L shimazu, Japan). The electrochemical analyses were measured using an
electrochemical workstation (CHI 660 C, China) in a three-electrode system. The ESR signals were
measured on a spectrometer (Bruker, Germany).
2.4 Photocatalytic performance
The photocatalytic performance of the prepared samples was assessed by measuring their ability to
degrade TC solution (20 mg L1) under visible light conditions. In the experiment, 15 mg of synthesized
catalysts were added and dispersed in 100 mL TC solution. Each suspension was stirred for 40 min in
dark room to obtain the adsorption-desorption equilibrium. After dark adsorption, the photocatalytic
experiments were conducted under visible light ( λ  > 420 nm, 300W Xenon lamp was used as light source).
During the degradation process, 1.0 mL solution mixtures were withdrawn at every 10 min time interval
and the content of TC was determined by UV-vis spectrophotometer. The maximum adsorption
wavelength of tetracycline was 357 nm.

To investigate the crystal structure of MOF-525, ZnIn2S4 and ZIS/MOF-525 composites, the XRD was
performed. The displayed characteristic diffraction peaks appeared at 2 θ  = 4.5, 6.5, 7.9 and 8.2 which

525 in the composite was not obvious. The FTIR analysis was performed to further search the impact on
the structure of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 after compounding. The traditional characteristic absorption peak
of ZnIn2S4 was clearly visible in Fig. 1c, the absorption peak near 1614 cm− 1 corresponded to the water
molecules adsorbed on the ZnIn2S4 surface. The absorption peak around 1400 cm− 1 corresponded to the
hydroxyl groups adsorbed on the surface (Gao et al. 2013, Liu et al. 2018). In the MOF-525 spectral line,
the peak near 650 cm− 1 to 750 cm− 1 and 1100 cm− 1 to 1200 cm− 1 were attributed to the Zr-O in MOF-
525 (Chen et al. 2021), and the peak at 1603 cm− 1 was caused by the in-phase stretching vibration of

peaks of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4, indicating that the composite of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 had no effect on
their structures.
The morphology of the synthesized monomers and complexes was characterized by SEM and TEM.
MOF-525 presented a typical cubic structure (Fig. 2a) with a side length of about 0.22 µm, ZnIn2S4
showed a typical petal-like structure (Fig. 2b), which was consistent with the previous reports, indicating
the successful synthesis of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 (Boyer et al. 2020, Liang et al. 2021c). The SEM image
of ZIS/MOF-525 showed that ZnIn2S4 was evenly embedded on the surface of MOF-525 (Fig. 2(c-d)). The
samples were scanned by TEM to further explore the microstructure and morphology of ZIS/MOF-525
composite. As shown in Fig. 3(a-b), ZnIn2S4 was wrapped on the surface of MOF-525, making ZIS/MOF-
525 still present a cube structure. The composite boundary between ZnIn2S4 and MOF-525 could be seen
in Fig. 3c, which was very important for effective charge transfer between ZnIn2S4 and MOF-525.
According to Fig. 3d, it could be determined that the composite contained C, O, N, Zr, Zn, in and S, which
corresponded to the element composition of ZnIn2S4 and MOF-525. The above results all proved that the
ZIS/MOF-525 composite was successfully synthesized. The spatial structure of ZnIn2S4 and MOF-525
was not affected, and the main body was cubic structure. ZnIn2S4 was embedded on the surface of MOF-
525, which was conducive to the migration of photogenerated electrons.

successfully embedded on the surface of MOF-525.
3.2 Optical property and electrochemical analysis
The optical properties of pure MOF-525, ZnIn2S4, and ZIS/MOF-525 composites were determined by the
UV–vis DRS. As indicated in Fig. 6a, MOF-525 had two main absorption edges, located at about 450 and
750 nm respectively, corresponding to two absorption bands of porphyrin ligand, Soret absorption band
of porphyrin at 500 nm and Q absorption band of porphyrin at 750 nm (Morris et al. 2012, Toyao et al.
2015). The absorption edge of ZnIn2S4 was about 500 nm, which was attributed to the intrinsic band gap
absorption. Compared with pure ZnIn2S4, ZIS/MOF-525 with different proportions had a longer
wavelength absorption edge, and the absorption boundary reached about 650 nm. It was proved that the
introduction of MOF-525 could effectively enhanced the absorption boundary of ZnIn2S4, so as to obtain
more visible light for visible photocatalysis. The energy band gaps (Eg) of MOF-525, ZIS/MOF-525-1,
ZIS/MOF-525-2, ZIS/MOF-525-3, ZIS/MOF-525-4 and ZnIn2S4 were calculated to be about 1.67 eV, 1.89
eV, 1.88 eV, 1.85 eV, 1.79 eV and 2.65 eV by the Kubelka-Munk method, respectively (Fig. 6b). The Mott
Schottky curves of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 were tested to understand the valence band structure of
synthesized samples. As shown in Fig. 6c, the slopes of the straight-line parts of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4
curves were positive, which meant that MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 were typical n-type semiconductors. The
at band potentials of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 were − 0.63 V and − 0.49 V respectively. The at band
Page 9/27
potential is approximately the minimum conduction band value (CBM) for n-type semiconductors (Li et
al. 2019a). Therefore, through the CBM value and the above obtained Eg value, the conduction band and
valence band distribution structure corresponding to MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 could be obtained, as shown
in Fig. 6d.
The electrochemical analysis is an effective technique for evaluating photocurrent response and carrier
separation and transfer eciency of photocatalysts (Liu et al. 2021, Shao et al. 2019a, Shao et al.
2019b). The excited states and uorescence intensity of the synthesized samples were studied by
photoluminescence (PL) emission spectra at the excitation wavelength of 385 nm. As was shown in
Fig. 7a, ZnIn2S4 showed a strong emission signal at about 390 nm, which indicated that ZnIn2S4 had a
high charge recombination rate. After the introduction of MOF-525, the emission signal was signicantly
weakened, indicating that the addition of MOF-525 could effectively inhibit the recombination of
photogenerated e−-h+ pairs. The fact that the signal of ZIS/MOF-525-4 was stronger than that of
ZIS/MOF-525-3 indicated that excessive addition of MOF-525 inhibited the photocatalytic eciency of
ZIS/MOF-525-4, this was consistent with the above photocatalytic degradation results. The transient
photocurrent spectrum in Fig. 7b further veried the results of PL spectrum, the order of photocurrent
intensity was ZIS/MOF-525-3 > MOF-525 > ZIS/MOF-525-4 > ZnIn2S4, illustrating that ZIS/MOF-525-3
excited more photogenerated carriers to participate in the photocatalytic reaction than other samples
under the condition of visible light. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) diagram in Fig. 7c
showed that compared with the original MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4, the arc radius of ZIS/MOF525-3 was
signicantly smaller, which indicated that the electron transfer resistance of ZIS/MOF525-3 was weaker,
which was more conducive to the transmission of photogenerated carriers (Shen et al. 2020, Sun et al.
2019, Zhang et al. 2018). The transient PL spectrum in Fig. 7d could visually observed the electron-hole
recombination time, indicating that the average attenuation time of ZIS/MOF-525 composite was longer
than MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4, indicating that ZIS/MOF-525 composites could effectively accelerated the
transfer of photogenerated charges and effectively improved the utilization rate of photogenerated
charges (Liang et al. 2021c). The above results consistently presented that there was a synergistic effect
between MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 in the ZIS/MOF-525 composite photocatalyst, which made the
performance of the ZIS/MOF-525 photocatalyst exceed that of monomer photocatalysts.
3.3 Photocatalytic activity
TC was used as the target pollutant to verify the photocatalytic performance of the synthesized
photocatalyst. The photocatalysis experiment was determined at room temperature with a 300W Xenon
lamp as the light source and 420 nm as the lter. As was shown in Fig. 8a, the removal rates of TC by
monomer MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 were only 37.2% and 70.0% within 60 min, respectively. Compared with
the original MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4, the composite ZIS/MOF-525 not only improved the photocatalytic
degradation capacity but also enhanced the adsorption capacity of TC. In general, the degradation
performance of TC was greatly improved. The degradation eciency was in the order of ZIS/MOF-525-3 
= ZIS/MOF-525-4 > ZIS/MOF-525-2 > ZIS/MOF-525-1. The degradation eciency of ZIS/MOF-525-3
Page 10/27
reached 93.8% under the condition of optimal catalyst addition amount (Fig. S3). Then, the content of
MOF-525 increased again, the adsorption capacity was improved, the photocatalytic capacity was
decreased, and the total degradation eciency remained unchanged. The degradation eciency of all
complexes was signicantly higher than that of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 which proved the synergistic
effect of MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 on the enhancement of photocatalytic effect. Figure 8b showed that the
degradation process of ZIS/MOF-525 composites accorded with the pseudo-second-order kinetic model.
Figure 8c presented the mineralization eciency of different catalysts. The results showed that
compared with monomer MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4, ZIS/MOF-525-3 had the lowest total organic carbon
(TOC) content and the highest mineralization eciency after photocatalytic degradation. The above
results showed that there was a synergistic effect between ZnIn2S4 and MOF-525, which jointly improved
the ability of photocatalytic degradation of TC. Several circulation experiments were carried out to
investigate the stability and reusability of ZIS/MOF-525-3 photocatalyst. Specically, ZIS/MOF-525-3 with
the best degradation performance participated in the reaction. After each degradation experiment, the
catalyst was simply ltered and reused, washed with pure water, put into the oven for drying at 80°C, and
then the degradation experiment was repeated. The results showed that the degradation eciency of the
catalyst decreased slightly after the rst cycle, but it still maintained a degradation eciency of 84%
(Fig. 8d). It was because the TC adsorbed on the surface of the ZIS/MOF525-3 material was not
completely removed when the material was recovered, resulting in the decline of the adsorption and
removal ability of the subsequent degradation experiments. However, the photocatalytic eciency of
ZIS/MOF525-3 did not decrease signicantly in ve cycles, and the degradation eciency did not change
signicantly after the rst cycle and four cycles, indicating that the ZIS/MOF525-3 photocatalyst had
good stability and could be used for multiple cycles.
3.4 Photocatalytic mechanism
To fully speculate the photocatalytic mechanism of ZIS/MOF-525 photocatalysts and analyze the
synergistic effect between MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4, it is necessary to analyze the active substances
produced in the photocatalytic process of ZIS/MOF-525. Firstly, the radical quenching experiment was
carried out. 4-hydroxy-TEMPO, EDTA-2Na and isopropanol (IPA) were used as quenchers for superoxide
radical (•O2−), hole radical (h+) and hydroxyl radical (•OH) (Fig. 9a). The results showed that adding 1
mmol isopropanol into the reaction solution did not affect the nal degradation eciency, indicating that
•OH was not an active species for photocatalytic degradation of TC by ZIS/MOF-525-3 material. However,
when 1 mmol of 4-hydroxy-TEMPO was added, the photocatalytic degradation eciency was
signicantly reduced, and the degradation eciency after 60 min of illumination was 40.1%, which was
mainly adsorption degradation, indicating that the photocatalytic performance of ZIS/MOF-3 was greatly
lost after the removal of •O2− in the reaction solution. Therefore, it can be concluded that •O2− active
species played a major role in the photocatalytic degradation of TC by ZIS/MOF-525-3. The addition of
EDTA-2Na also reduced the degradation eciency of ZIS/MOF-525-3 from 93.8–70.02%, indicating that
h+ also played a role in the catalytic reaction. To further clarify the active substances produced in the

photocatalytic process, the ZIS/MOF-525-3 samples were characterized by ESR spin trap spectroscopy.
Figure 9b showed that the characteristic signal of DMPO-•O2− could not be detected under dark
conditions. Under the illumination of visible light, four characteristic signals of DMPO-•O2− were found
and the signal intensity ratio was 1:1:1:1. With the extension of irradiation time, the signal intensity
became stronger, indicating that the generation of •O2− was consistent with the experimental results of
free radical quenching. In addition, the signal peak with the intensity ratio of 1:2:2:1 in Fig. 9c
corresponds to DMPO-•OH, which conrmed the formation of •OH, although the number was small (Wu et
al. 2021). The above results showed that •O2− and h+ played a leading role in the degradation of TC by
ZIS/MOF-525-3 while the effect of •OH was not obvious.
Based on the above conclusions, the possible photocatalytic mechanism is deduced and proposed. In
general, the photocatalysis system of ZIS/MOF-525 composite forms heterojunctions to realize double
electron transfer. According to the above research results, there is a typical type II heterojunction between
ZIS/MOF-525. Under visible light illumination, both MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4 will separate electrons and
holes (Eq. 1–2). Subsequently, the electrons on the MOF-525 conduction band gather on ZnIn2S4, and the
conduction band potential of ZnIn2S4 (-0.49 V vs NHE) is more negative than the standard potential (-0.33
V vs NHE) of O2/•O2−, so the electrons gathered on the ZnIn2S4 conduction band react with the O2
enriched on the catalyst surface to form •O2− (Eq. 3). After the electrons are consumed, the h+ produced in
the valence band of ZnIn2S4 converges to the valence band of MOF-525. Finally, these generated •O2−
and h+ participate in the oxidation-reduction reaction of TC and degrade TC into H2O, CO2 and other small
molecular substances (Eq. 4). The corresponding photocatalytic degradation mechanism is shown in
Fig. 10.

In summary, a novel visible-light responsive PMOF-based photocatalysts ZIS/MOF-525 was constructed
by introducing ZnIn2S4 onto the surface of MOF-525 through solvothermal method. Petal-like ZnIn2S4
grew on the surface of MOF-525 and had strong interface contact with MOF-525. Compared with the pure
MOF-525 and ZnIn2S4, the photocatalytic degradation ability of ZIS/MOF-525 photocatalyst was
signicantly improved. ZIS/MOF-525-3 exhibited 93.8% TC degradation eciency within 60 min under
visible light irradiation, which was 2.52 times than that of pure MOF-525. In addition, the high
photocatalytic eciency of ZIS/MOF-525 photocatalyst was still maintained during ve cycles, indicating
that the catalyst had high stability and recoverability. A series of characterization techniques exhibited
that the ZIS/MOF-525-3 had outstanding structure, optical properties and electrochemical features.
Mechanism analysis revealed that there was a typical type II heterojunction in ZIS/MOF-525
photocatalysts, which improved the electron transport eciency within the photocatalyst and
signicantly inhibited the recombination of photogenerated e− and h+. In this work, a method was
proposed to solve the problem of easy recombination of photogenerated e−-h+ pairs in PMOF, which
provided a reference for the preparation of new PMOF-based photocatalysts in the future.
