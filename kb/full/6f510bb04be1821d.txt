Coordination between new-type urbanization (NTU) and carbon mitigation is a critical lever for sustainable development. This study examines 71 cities across the three major urban agglomerations (UAs) in the Yangtze River Economic Belt (YREB) from 2009 to 2024. We measure NTU and carbon emission intensity (CEI) using an entropy-weight TOPSIS approach and a bias-corrected downscaling model. A modified coupling coordination model is then applied to derive the coupling coordination degree (CCD). Furthermore, we develop an XGBoost–SHAP framework to identify the nonlinear and interaction effects of Scale–Industry–Energy (S–I–E) factors on the CCD. Based on this, we specify differentiated scenarios to forecast the evolutionary trends of the CCD in these UAs through 2035. The results show that: (1) NTU and CEI across the three UAs have been progressively optimized, leading to a continuous rise in the CCD. While inter-agglomeration disparities have narrowed, marked intra-agglomeration heterogeneity persists. (2) S–I–E pathways exhibit significant nonlinear effects on CCD, with scale, industrial, and energy dimensions jointly shaping coordination patterns, while their relative importance varies across urban agglomerations. Scale pathways dominate in the CCUA, industrial pathways play a more prominent role in the UAMR, and the YRD shows a more balanced contribution with relatively stronger effects from industrial and energy dimensions. Strong interaction effects are observed among pathways, indicating that CCD evolution is associated with structured interdependencies rather than isolated factors. (3) Scenario-based projections reveal that no single pathway is uniformly superior. However, compared with extensive scale expansion, prioritizing ecological protection and industrial upgrading is more conducive to fostering regional convergence and narrowing development gaps. These findings provide empirical evidence and decision support for place-based strategies to promote green urbanization and low-carbon coordination in global urban clusters.
