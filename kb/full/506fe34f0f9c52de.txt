Potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) is the third most important food crop worldwide; however, its production continues to face major challenges from pathogen pressure, environmental stress, and dependence on agrochemicals. Micropropagation provides disease-free planting material, but plantlets often show low survival during acclimatization due to physiological and microbial imbalances. Plant growth-promoting bacteria (PGPB) offer a bio-based solution by enhancing plant establishment, nutrient uptake, stress tolerance, and yield. This chapter explores the potential of Bacillus, Burkholderia, and Streptomyces strains as biological agents to improve the acclimatization and tuberization of micropropagated potato plants. Drawing on experimental research and recent literature, it discusses key PGPB mechanisms, including phytohormone and siderophore production, phosphate solubilization, and biocontrol activity, as well as their integration into sustainable potato production systems. Under greenhouse conditions, bacterial inoculation resulted in a 100% survival rate during the acclimatization phase. Furthermore, the experimental data revealed enhanced tuberization, particularly with the Burkholderia strain and PGPB consortia, showing significant increases in both the number and weight of tubers per plant compared to the control. Overall, the findings suggest that the early integration of these bacteria, either individually or in combination, into sustainable production systems represents a promising approach to improving acclimatization efficiency and tuber yield of micropropagated potato plants.
