Effect of long-term fertilization on nutrient uptake and
harvest index under pearl millet-mustard cropping
sequence in the Gird zone of Madhya Pradesh

A BSTRACT
A long-term fertilizer experiment initiated in 2002 on a sandy clay loam Typic Ustochrepts at Gwalior,
Madhya Pradesh, was used to quantify the effect of twelve nutrient-management treatments on nutrient
uptake and yield of a pearl millet–mustard cropping sequence during 2024–2026. The experiment was
laid out in a randomized block design with three replications. Integrated nutrient management consis-
tently outperformed sole chemical fertilization and the unfertilized control. The treatment receiving
100% NPK + FYM @ 10 t ha−1 + seed inoculation with Azotobacter and PSB recorded the highest N, P
and K uptake in both crops: pearl millet grain and straw N uptake of 65.37 and 55.77 kg ha−1 (versus
25.48 and 22.48 kg ha−1 in the control), P uptake of 12.98 and 15.00 kg ha−1 (versus 3.73 and 3.03 kg
ha−1) and K uptake of 23.69 and 115.53 kg ha−1 (versus 9.77 and 34.11 kg ha−1). In mustard, the same
treatment produced the highest grain and straw N (83.32 and 45.47 kg ha−1), P (15.33 and 16.50 kg
ha−1) and K (21.67 and 88.32 kg ha−1) uptake against control values of 15.54 and 9.68, 2.66 and 2.95,
and 4.72 and 14.49 kg ha−1, respectively. Harvest index was highest under the integrated treatment in
both crops (35.84% in pearl millet and 27.84% in mustard) against 30.34% and 21.40% in the control.
The results demonstrate that combining organic manure and biofertilizers with the recommended fertil-
izer dose improves nutrient uptake and photosynthate partitioning more effectively than chemical fertil-
ization alone, even at elevated doses and that FYM-integrated treatments sustain productivity through
improved soil health rather than higher nutrient input (Paramesh et al., 2023; Ranjan et al., 2023).
Keywords: Integrated nutrient management, Nutrient uptake, Harvest index
Introduction
Pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum L.) and Indian mustard (Brassica juncea L.) form one of the most impor-
tant cropping sequences of the semi-arid and rainfed regions of central India, particularly in the Gird zone of
Madhya Pradesh, where an Inceptisol-dominated, low-organic-matter soil base and erratic rainfall (600-
800 mm annually) constrain sustained productivity. Continuous cultivation with imbalanced or inadequate
fertilization over decades has led to widespread nutrient mining, declining native soil fertility, and yield stag-
nation in cereal-oilseed systems across this region (Khambalkar et al., 2012) [1].
Pearl millet, though hardy and drought-tolerant, is an exhaustive feeder that removes substantial quantities of
nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K) from the soil, while mustard, being an oilseed crop with a
high sulfur and boron requirement in addition to NPK, imposes further nutrient demand on the same land
within a single agricultural year. Under such intensive double-cropping, the quantum and efficiency of nutri-
ent uptake by both crops become critical determinants of system productivity and long-term soil sustainabil-
ity. Long-term fertilizer experiments (LTFEs) conducted under the All India Coordinated Research Project
on Long-Term Fertilizer Experiments have consistently demonstrated that balanced and integrated nutrient
management- combining recommended NPK doses with organic manures such as farmyard manure (FYM)
and biofertilizers (Azotobacter and phosphate-solubilizing bacteria) substantially improves nutrient uptake,
yield and soil nutrient status compared to unfertilized control or fertilizer-only treatments. At Gwalior, a
long-term permanent manurial trial on the pearl millet–mustard system reported that integrated application
of recommended NPK, FYM (10 t ha−1 yr−1), and biofertilizers increased yields of pearl millet and mustard
by over 100% and 130%, respectively, relative to the unfertilized control, while also improving available soil
N and P (Khambalkar et al., 2012) [1].
Similarly, studies on pearl millet under long-term organic and inorganic fertilization have shown that contin-
uous FYM application, particularly across both cropping seasons, significantly enhances grain and stover
nutrient concentration (N, P, K) and total nutrient uptake compared with fertilizer nitrogen alone, underscor-
ing the complementary role of organic sources in sustaining nutrient availability over successive cropping

ing region-specific nutrient management recommendations and ensur-
ing the long-term sustainability of this cropping sequence. Keeping
this in view, the present investigation was undertaken to quantify the
major nutrient uptake and yield of pearl millet and mustard as influ-
enced by long-term fertilization practices in the Gird zone of Madhya
Pradesh.
2. Material and Methods
The present study was conducted during 2024–2026 under an ongoing
long-term fertilizer experiment initiated in 2002 at the research farm,
College of Agriculture, Rajmata Vijayaraje Scindia Krishi Vishwa
Vidyalaya, Gwalior. The site (26°13′N, 76°10′E; 197 m above mean
sea level) lies in the Gird agro-climatic zone on a uniformly levelled,
well-drained field. The climate is semi-arid and sub-tropical, with
summer temperatures reaching 43 °C and winter lows of 3–4 °C; the
mean annual rainfall is about 730 mm, mostly received from late June
to mid-September. The soil of the experimental field is alluvial sandy
clay loam, classified as Typic Ustochrepts.
The experiment was laid out in a randomized block design with
twelve treatments and three replications, giving 36 plots with a net
plot size of 4.4 × 2.4 m. The twelve treatments, applied identically to
both crops, were: T1, control; T2, 75% RDF-NPK; T3, 100% RDF-
NPK; T4, 150% RDF-NPK; T5, 100% RDF-NP (without K); T6,
100% RDF-N (excluding P and K); T7, 100% NPK + 25 kg ZnSO4
ha−1; T8, 100% NPK + 50 kg FeSO4 ha−1; T9, 100% NPK + 1% fo-
liar spray of ZnSO4 and FeSO4 at 25 and 45 days after sowing; T10,
75% NPK + FYM @ 10 t ha−1; T11, 100% NPK + FYM @ 10 t ha−1;
and T12, 100% NPK + FYM @ 10 t ha−1 + seed inoculation with
Azotobacter and PSB. Pearl millet hybrid 86M90 (kharif) was fertil-
ized at 80:40:20 kg N: P2O5: K2O ha−1 and mustard hybrid Giriraj
(rabi) at 100:60:40 kg N: P2O5: K2O ha−1 according to the treatment
schedule, while FYM @ 10 t ha−1 was applied only in the FYM-inte-
grated treatments (T10–T12).
Crops were harvested at maturity after excluding border rows and
30 cm from each plot end to eliminate border effects. Five tagged
plants from each plot were harvested separately for post-harvest ob-
servations. Bundles were sun-dried for 3–4 days and weighed to
record biological yield; the produce was then threshed manually, win-
nowed and cleaned, and grain yield was recorded, while straw/stover
yield was derived by subtracting grain yield from biological yield.
Harvest index (%) was computed as the ratio of grain (economic)
yield to biological yield, expressed as a percentage.
Representative grain and straw samples were oven-dried at 65 ± 5 °C
to constant weight, ground, and subjected to wet digestion. Nitrogen
was determined by the micro-Kjeldahl method, phosphorus by the
vanado-molybdate yellow colour method using a spectrophotometer,
and potassium by flame photometry. Nutrient uptake (kg ha−1) by
grain and straw of each crop was calculated by multiplying the nutri-
ent concentration (%) by the respective dry matter yield (kg ha−1) and
dividing by 100, and the total uptake was obtained by summing the
grain and straw values.
The data were subjected to analysis of variance for the randomized
block design, and treatment means were compared using the critical
difference at 5% probability (p ≤ 0.05).
3. Results
3.1. Total nitrogen uptake (kg ha−1)
Nitrogen uptake varied significantly across nutrient management
treatments in both crops. In pearl millet, grain N uptake ranged from
25.48–65.37 kg ha−1 and straw from 22.48–55.77 kg ha−1, with 100%
NPK + FYM + Azotobacter + PSB seed treatment giving the highest
uptake in both grain (65.37) and straw (55.77), followed by 100%
NPK + FYM, 150% NPK, and 75% NPK + FYM, all significantly
higher than the control (25.48/22.48 kg ha−1).
In mustard, grain N uptake ranged from 15.54–83.32 kg ha−1 and
straw from 9.68–45.47 kg ha−1. The same integrated treatment (100%
NPK + FYM + Azotobacter + PSB) again produced the highest up-
take (83.32 grain, 45.47 straw), with 150% NPK slightly outperform-
ing 100% NPK + FYM in grain uptake, while straw uptake followed
the order 100% NPK + FYM > 150% NPK > 75% NPK + FYM, all
significantly exceeding the control (15.54/9.68 kg ha−1).
Overall, integrated nutrient management consistently enhanced N up-
take over sole chemical or control treatments.
3.2 Total phosphorus uptake (kg ha−1)
Total P uptake showed significant variation across treatments in both
crops. In pearl millet, grain P uptake ranged from 3.73–12.98 kg ha−1
and straw from 3.03–15.00 kg ha−1, with 100% NPK + FYM +
Azotobacter + PSB seed treatment recording the highest uptake in
both grain (12.98) and straw (15.00), followed by 100% NPK +
FYM, 150% NPK, and 75% NPK + FYM, all significantly superior to
the control (3.73/3.03 kg ha−1).
In mustard, grain P uptake ranged from 2.66–15.33 kg ha−1 and straw
from 2.95–16.50 kg ha−1. The integrated treatment (100% NPK +
FYM + Azotobacter + PSB) again gave the highest uptake (15.33
grain, 16.50 straw), with 150% NPK marginally exceeding 100%
NPK + FYM in grain P uptake, while straw uptake followed the order
100% NPK + FYM > 150% NPK > 75% NPK + FYM, all signifi-
cantly higher than the control (2.66/2.95 kg ha−1).
Overall, integrated nutrient management consistently maximized P
uptake in both crops.
3.3. Total potassium uptake (kg ha−1)
Total K uptake showed significant variation across treatments in both
crops. In pearl millet, grain K uptake ranged from 9.77–23.69 kg ha−1
and straw from 34.11–115.53 kg ha−1, with 100% NPK + FYM +
Azotobacter + PSB seed treatment recording the highest uptake in
both grain (23.69) and straw (115.53), followed by 100% NPK +
FYM, 150% NPK, and 75% NPK + FYM, all significantly superior to
the control (9.77/34.11 kg ha−1).
In mustard, grain K uptake ranged from 4.72–21.67 kg ha−1 and straw
from 14.49–88.32 kg ha−1. The integrated treatment (100% NPK +
FYM + Azotobacter + PSB) again gave the highest uptake (21.67
grain, 88.32 straw), with 150% NPK marginally exceeding 100%
NPK + FYM in grain K uptake, while in straw, 150% NPK slightly
surpassed 100% NPK + FYM, followed by 75% NPK + FYM, all sig-
nificantly higher than the control (4.72/14.49 kg ha−1).
Overall, integrated nutrient management consistently maximized K
uptake in both crops.
3.5. Harvest index (HI)
Harvest index (HI) varied significantly across treatments in both
crops. In pearl millet, HI ranged from 30.34–35.84%, with the highest
value recorded under 100% NPK + FYM + Azotobacter + PSB seed
treatment (35.84%), followed equally by 150% NPK and 100% NPK
+ FYM (35.33% each), then 100% NPK (34.28%) and 75% NPK +
FYM (34.23%), all significantly higher than the control (30.34%).
In mustard, HI ranged from 21.40–27.84%, with the integrated treat-
ment (100% NPK + FYM + Azotobacter + PSB) again giving the
highest value (27.84%), closely followed by 150% NPK (27.71%),
75% NPK + FYM (26.92%), and 100% NPK + FYM (26.81%), all
significantly exceeding the control (21.40%).

4. Discussions
4.1 Total nitrogen uptake
Nitrogen uptake in grain and straw of both crops increased signifi-
cantly with fertilization, being highest under 100% NPK + FYM +
Azotobacter + PSB seed treatment (65.37 kg ha−1 in grain and
55.77 kg ha−1 in straw in pearl millet; 83.32 and 45.47 kg ha−1 in
mustard) and lowest under the unfertilized control (25.48/22.48 and
15.54/9.68 kg ha−1, respectively). Nutrient uptake being a function of
both nutrient concentration and dry matter production, the markedly
higher N uptake under the integrated treatment resulted from the com-
bination of higher N content and significantly higher grain and straw
yields recorded in these plots (Khambalkar et al., 2012) [1]. The
greater N uptake under FYM-integrated treatments may be attributed

to the sustained, gradual release of nitrogen through mineralization of
organic manure, which maintained a continuous supply of N through-
out the crop growth period (Meena et al., 2025) [3], and to biological
nitrogen fixation by Azotobacter, which augments the soil mineral N
pool and thereby enhances plant absorption (Aasfar et al., 2021) [4].
Long-term organic and N fertilization studies on pearl millet have
similarly demonstrated that continuous FYM application, particularly
in both cropping seasons, significantly enhances grain and stover N
concentration and total N uptake compared with fertilizer nitrogen
alone (Sheoran et al., 2024) [2]. In mustard, 150% NPK slightly out-
performed 100% NPK + FYM in grain N uptake, indicating that the
readily available mineral nitrogen from the higher fertilizer dose was
efficiently absorbed during the reproductive phase; nevertheless, the
FYM-based treatments maintained a comparable and often superior
straw N uptake, reflecting slower, more synchronized N release from
organic sources and reduced losses (Singh, 2020) [5].
4.2 Total phosphorus uptake
Phosphorus uptake ranged from 3.73 to 12.98 kg ha−1 grain and 3.03
to 15.00 kg ha−1 straw in pearl millet and from 2.66 to 15.33 and 2.95
to 16.50 kg ha−1 in mustard, with the highest values under 100%
NPK + FYM + Azotobacter + PSB (12.98/15.00 kg ha−1 in pearl mil-
let; 15.33/16.50 kg ha−1 in mustard) and the lowest under control.
The enhanced P uptake in the integrated and biofertilizer treatments
may be ascribed to the higher availability of P in the root zone: phos-
phate-solubilizing bacteria release insoluble native and applied phos-
phates through acidification, chelation and exchange reactions (Alladi
et al., 2017; Jilani et al., 2007) [6, 7], while organic acids released dur-
ing FYM decomposition reduce P fixation on soil colloids and stimu-
late the microbial mineralization of organic phosphorus (Bharti et al.,
2017) [8]. In mustard, 150% NPK recorded marginally higher grain P
uptake than 100% NPK + FYM, as expected from the larger mineral
P dose applied, while the FYM-integrated treatments again dominated
straw P uptake, corroborating the longer-term P-supplying capacity of
organic amendments (Khambalkar et al., 2012) [1]. The conspicuously
low P uptake under the control (3.73 kg ha−1 in pearl millet grain) re-
flects the continued removal of P without any external replenishment
and the high P-fixing tendency of the soil, which progressively limits
both soil available P and crop uptake (Rahaman et al., 2024) [9].
4.3 Total potassium uptake
Potassium uptake ranged from 9.77 to 23.69 kg ha−1 grain and 34.11
to 115.53 kg ha−1 straw in pearl millet and from 4.72 to 21.67 and
14.49 to 88.32 kg ha−1 in mustard, being highest under 100% NPK +
FYM + Azotobacter + PSB (23.69/115.53 kg ha−1 in pearl millet;
21.67/88.32 kg ha−1 in mustard) and lowest under control. The pre-
dominance of K uptake in straw (e.g., 115.53 vs 23.69 kg ha−1 under
the integrated treatment in pearl millet) reflects the high K accumula-
tion in vegetative tissue, particularly of pearl millet stover, consistent
with the well-known luxury consumption of potassium by this crop
(Singh, 2020) [5]. The higher K uptake under FYM-amended treat-
ments may be attributed to the direct addition of K through manure,
its release during mineralization of organic residues, and the improve-
ment in soil cation exchange capacity and K retention, which sustains
a larger pool of readily available potassium in soil (Pradhan et al.,
2024; Yadav et al., 2019) [10, 11]. Imbalanced and unfertilized treat-
ments, by contrast, showed the lowest K uptake, indicating progres-
sive depletion of the soil K reserve under continuous cropping with-
out adequate replenishment (Rahaman et al., 2024) [9].
4.5 Harvest index
The harvest index of pearl millet ranged from 30.34 (control) to
35.84% (100% NPK + FYM + Azotobacter + PSB), while that of
mustard ranged from 21.40 (control) to 27.84% (the integrated treat-
ment), closely followed by 150% NPK (27.71%). The improvement
in harvest index under fertilized and integrated treatments indicates
better partitioning and translocation of photosynthates towards the
economic produce as a result of balanced and timely nutrient supply,
which enhanced grain formation and filling. The highest harvest in-
dex under the integrated treatment, combined with the highest grain
and straw yields, confirms that INM improved both total biomass pro-
duction and its efficient conversion into grain, a pattern observed con-
sistently in both a cereal and an oilseed crop (Paramesh et al., 2023)
[12].
5. Summary and Conclusions
The study demonstrates that integrated nutrient management consis-
tently outperformed sole chemical fertilization or the unfertilized con-
trol across both pearl millet and mustard, for every parameter as-
sessed nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium uptake, grain and straw
yield, and harvest index. The treatment combining 100% NPK with
FYM and Azotobacter + PSB seed treatment emerged as the superior
option in nearly every case, recording the highest values for grain and
straw N, P, and K uptake, the highest grain and straw yields, and the
best harvest index in both crops. The 150% NPK and 100% NPK +
FYM treatments followed closely behind, often trading second and
third place depending on the nutrient and crop, while 75% NPK +
FYM consistently ranked above the control but below the higher-in-
put treatments. The control recorded the lowest values throughout,
confirming the baseline nutrient-limited condition of the soil.
These results indicate that combining organic manure with biofertiliz-
ers alongside recommended chemical fertilizer doses improves nutri-
ent availability, uptake efficiency, and photosynthate partitioning
more effectively than chemical fertilization alone, even at elevated
(150%) doses. The consistency of this pattern across two structurally
different crops a cereal and an oilseed strengthen confidence that the
benefit stems from improved soil biological activity and nutrient syn-
chronization rather than crop-specific response. Long-term multi-sea-
son trials are needed to confirm the sustainability of these gains and
their effect on soil health indicators. Economic analysis (cost-benefit
ratio) should accompany yield data. Further studies could explore op-
timized Azotobacter–PSB strain combinations, split application tim-
ing, and performance under varying soil types and rainfall conditions.
