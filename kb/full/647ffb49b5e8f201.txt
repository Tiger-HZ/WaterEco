Thin polymer films exhibit thickness-dependent viscoelastic behavior attributed to enhanced segmental mobility near free surfaces, a phenomenon recently linked to surface-induced constraint release (SICR) in entangled polymer melts. While self-consistent theoretical treatments of this mechanism exist, computationally lightweight phenomenological models that isolate the role of specific structural assumptions remain comparatively underexplored. This work showcases a developed closed-form, depth-resolved generalized Maxwell model in which local relaxation times are derived from a depth-dependent entanglement molecular weight profile, motivated by reptation scaling () and literature-reported surface entanglement enhancement ratios.
