Effect of weed control practices on nutrient content of
maize (Zea Mays L.)
Harshita Mishra1, HS Kushwaha2, Vikas Tiwari3, Jyoti Kahar4, Ajay Kumar Kashyap5
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i10b.6757
A BSTRACT
The field investigation was carried out during two consecutive kharif seasons of 2023–24 and 2024–25
at the Agricultural Research Farm, Mahatma Gandhi Chitrakoot Gramodaya Vishwavidyalaya,
Chitrakoot, Satna (M.P.) to evaluate the effect of various weed control practices on the nitrogen, phos-
phorus and potassium content in grain and stover of maize (Zea mays L.). Results revealed that among
the weed management options, the maximum nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium contents in both
grain and stover were recorded under the weed-free check, registering pooled values of 1.61% N, 0.47%
P and 0.59% K in grain and 0.65% N, 0.25% P and 1.63% K in stover. Among the harbicidal treatments,
Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb mesotrione + atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS and Pyroxasulfone @
127 g ha−1 PE fb tembotrione @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS recorded the highest macro-nutrient content,
both achieving pooled values of 1.59%, 0.47% and 0.59% N, P and K in grain, while 0.65% N, 0.25% P
and 1.62% K in stover and 1.59% N, 0.47% P and .08% K in grain and stover respectively, Conversely,
the significantly lowest nutrient contents across both experimental years and on a pooled basis were ob-
served under the Weedy check (T1), recording pooled values of 1.48% N, 0.43% P and 0.51% K in grain
and 0.56% N, 0.22% P and 1.49% K in stover, followed by Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g ha−1 PE fb halosul-
furon methyl @ 67 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS (T9). Systematic weed suppression during critical crop growth
stages effectively minimized crop-weed competition, thereby improving root absorption efficiency and
facilitating greater translocation of N, P and K into grain and stover.
Keywords: Nitrogen content, Phosphorus content, Potassium content, Herbicides
Introduction
Maize (Zea mays L.) is a major cereal crop globally and in India high . Its productivity and quality are
strongly influenced by weed interference, particularly during the early growth stages when crop-weed com-
petition for soil nutrients is most critical (Kakade et al., 2016) [1]. Weeds compete directly with maize for es-
sential macronutrients like nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K), often absorbing a higher propor-
tion of available nutrients than the crop itself under unmanaged conditions, leading to severe "nutrient drain"
(Sanketh, 2021) [2].
Efficient weed control options including timely hand weeding, pre- and post-emergence herbicide applica-
tions and integrated weed management strategies significantly reduce weed density and dry biomass
(Sardaret al., 2020) [3]. This reduction minimizes nutrient depletion by weeds and enhances nutrient avail-
ability and uptake by the maize crop (Samanth et al., 2015) [4]. Proper weed management promotes greater
accumulation of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium in maize grain and stover compared to unweeded con-
trols, contributing to better nutrient partitioning toward yield components (Kumar et al., 2017) [5].
Conversely, unmanaged weed growth leads to severe crop-weed competition, resulting in reduced nutrient
content in grain and stover, lower crop yield and poor nutrient use efficiency (Malviya et al., 2007) [6].
Under the agro-ecological conditions of the Kymore Plateau region of Central India (Chitrakoot, Satna,
M.P.), characterized by a semi-arid sub-tropical climate, sandy loam soils and distinct seasonal weed flora,
nutrient losses due to weed competition pose a major constraint to maize production. However, region-spe-
cific information on the effect of various pre- and post-emergence herbicide combinations and hand weeding
practices on the nutrient content of maize remains limited. Understanding nutrient dynamics under different
weed management practices is essential for optimizing input efficiency and ensuring sustainable crop pro-
duction. Hence, a study was under taken to assess the effect of weed management practices on nitrogen,
phosphorus and potassium content in grain and stover of maize.

Madhya Pradesh. Situated within the Kymore Plateau region of Northern M.P., the site lies at 25°10′ N lati-
tude and 80°52′ E longitude, with an elevation of 190 to 210 m above mean sea level. The experimental soil

options applied to maize, namely T1: weedy check, T2: weed-free

T5: Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb Halosulfuron methyl @ 67 g ha−1 at

Mesotrione + Atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at 25-30 DAS. These twelve
treatments were evaluated in a randomized block design (RBD) repli-
cated three times.
The maize (cv. JM – 216) was sown on 19 July 2023 and 16 July
2024 at a row spacing of 60 cm in furrows apart with seed rate 20 kg
ha−1. After 15 DAS in the first year and 12 DAS in the second year,
the plant spacing of maize was maintained at 20 cm apart with thin-
ning and gap filling operations. The maize crop was fertilized at 120:
60: 30 kg N: P2O5: K2O ha−1. Half quantity of N + full quantity of
P2O5 and K2O was basally applied in furrows at sowing and the re-
maining half nitrogen was applied in two equal split doses as top
dressing at 21 and 46 DAS during 2023-24 and at 20 and 44 DAS
during 2024-25. The maize crop was harvested on 20 October 2023
and 11 October 2024 during two consecutive years. The nitrogen,
phosphorus and potassium content in grain and stover of maize at har-
vest was estimated by adopting the Kjeldahl method using the KEL
PLUS nitrogen estimation system for nitrogen, the Colorimetric
(Spectrophotometer) method for phosphorus as described by Jackson
(1973) [7] and the Flame photometer method for potassium as de-
scribed by Jackson (1973) [7]. Analysis of variance technique appro-
priate to randomized block design and valid conclusions were drawn
as suggested by Fisher et al. (1967) [8]. The treatment differences
were tested for significance by 'F' test and for the data in which the
treatment effects were significant, the appropriate standard error of
mean 𝑆𝐸m ± and the critical difference (CD) were worked out at 5%
level of significance.
Result and Discussion
Nitrogen content (%) in grain and straw of maize
Nitrogen content in grain of maize was significantly improved with
the use of all weed control treatments over the weedy check plot on
the basis of pooled data. It was recorded significantly higher under
Weed-free check viz. 1.62% (2023–24), 1.59% (2024–25) and 1.61%
(pooled) followed by Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb Mesotrione +
Atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS viz. 1.61% (2023–24), 1.58%
(2024–25), 1.59% (pooled) and Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g ha−1 PE fb
Tembotrione @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS viz. 1.61% (2023–24),
1.57% (2024–25), 1.59% (pooled) respectively. Conversely, the low-
est nitrogen content in grain was recorded under Weedy check viz.
1.51% (2023–24), 1.45% (2024–25) and 1.48% (pooled). The nitro-
gen content in stover of maize was significantly enhanced with all
weed control treatments as compared to the weedy check on the basis
of pooled data. It was found significantly maximum under Weed-free
check i.e. 0.61% (2023–24), 0.70% (2024–25) and 0.65% (pooled)
followed by Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb Mesotrione + Atrazine @
300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS i.e. 0.60% (2023–24), 0.69% (2024–25)
and 0.65% (pooled) and Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g ha−1 PE fb
Tembotrione @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS viz. 0.60% (2023–24),
0.68% (2024–25) and 0.64% (pooled). Meanwhile, the lowest nitro-
gen content in stover was observed under Weedy check i.e. 0.54%
(2023–24), 0.59% (2024–25) and 0.56% (pooled). This was due to the
effective control and systematic suppression of weeds throughout the
critical growth stages, which significantly reduced crop-weed compe-
tition for space, moisture, light and nutrients. The lower weed density
and higher weed control efficiency in the weed-free check and
Sequential herbicidal treatments—particularly Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1
PE fb Mesotrione + Atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS and
Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g ha−1 PE fb Tembotrione @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–
30 DAS facilitated unhindered root expansion and better soil nutrient
availability. This enhanced the root absorption capacity and nutrient
uptake efficiency of maize, thereby promoting vigorous vegetative
development, higher dry matter accumulation and greater transloca-
tion and accumulation of nitrogen into the developing grain and
stover tissue. Conversely, heavy weed infestation in the weedy check
caused severe nutrient competition, resulting in restricted plant
growth and lower tissue nitrogen content. These findings are in close
conformity with the results reported by Gul et al. (2015) [9].
Phosphorous content (%) in grain and straw of maize
Various weed management treatments significantly improved the
phosphorus content in grain of maize over the weedy check plot on
the basis of pooled data. It was recorded significantly higher under
Weed-free check viz. 0.48% (2023–24), 0.47% (2024–25) and 0.47%
(pooled) followed by Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb Mesotrione +
Atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS and Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g
ha−1 PE fb Tembotrione @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS viz. 0.48%
(2023–24), 0.46% (2024–25), 0.47% (pooled) and 0.48% (2023–24),
0.46% (2024–25), 0.47% (pooled), respectively. Conversely, the low-
est phosphorus content in grain was recorded under Weedy check viz.
0.44% (2023–24), 0.41% (2024–25) and 0.43% (pooled). The phos-
phorus content in stover of maize was significantly enhanced with all
weed control treatments as compared to the weedy check on the basis
of pooled data. It was found significantly maximum under Weed-free
check i.e. 0.24% (2023–24), 0.26% (2024–25) and 0.25% (pooled)
followed by Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb Mesotrione + Atrazine @
300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS i.e. 0.24% (2023–24), 0.26% (2024–25)
and 0.25% (pooled) and Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g ha−1 PE fb
Tembotrione @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS viz. 0.24% (2023–24),
0.25% (2024–25) and 0.25% (pooled). Meanwhile, the lowest phos-
phorus content in stover was observed under Weedy check i.e. 0.21%
(2023–24), 0.22% (2024–25) and 0.22% (pooled). The improvement
in phosphorus concentration within both grain and stover under effec-
tive weed management practices can be attributed to the successful
elimination of crop-weed competition during critical growth stages. In
the weed-free check and superior herbicidal treatments notably
Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb Mesotrione + Atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at
25–30 DAS and Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g ha−1 PE fb Tembotrione @
120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS, the timely control of weeds prevented the
depletion of soil moisture and bioavailable nutrients. Consequently,
the maize crop developed a more extensive root system and robust
vegetative structure, facilitating higher absorption of native and ap-
plied phosphorus from the soil solution. This optimized nutrient up-
take directly supported primary physiological processes, energy trans-
fer and efficient translocation of phosphorus compounds into sink tis-
sues during reproductive development. Conversely, unmanaged weed
growth in the weedy check severely restricted root growth and soil
nutrient availability, leading to diminished tissue phosphorus levels.
These results are in close agreement with the findings reported by
Srinivasan et al. (2024) [10].

Potassium content (%) in grain and straw of maize
Potassium content in grain of maize was significantly influenced by
different weed management practices, showing a marked improve-
ment across all weed control treatments compared to the weedy check
on a pooled data basis. The highest accumulation was registered in
the Weed-free check, recording 0.58% (2023–24), 0.61% (2024–25)
and 0.59% (pooled), which was closely followed by Atrazine @ 500 g
ha−1 PE fb Mesotrione + Atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS viz.
0.58% (2023–24), 0.60% (2024–25), 0.59% (pooled) and Atrazine @
500 g ha−1 PE + hand weeding at 25-30 DAS viz. 0.57% (2023–24),
0.58% (2024–25), 0.58% (pooled), respectively. Conversely, the low-
est potassium content in grain was recorded under Weedy check viz.
0.54% (2023–24), 0.49% (2024–25) and 0.51% (pooled). Regarding
potassium content in stover of maize, all herbicidal and manual weed
management treatments proved significantly superior over the un-
weeded control based on the pooled results. The maximum values
were observed under Weed-free check i.e. 1.64% (2023–24), 1.62%
(2024–25) and 1.63% (pooled) followed by Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1
PE fb Mesotrione + Atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS i.e. 1.63%
(2023–24), 1.60% (2024–25) and 1.62% (pooled) and Pyroxasulfone
@ 127 g ha−1 PE fb Tembotrione @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS viz.
1.63% (2023–24), 1.60% (2024–25) and 1.61% (pooled). Meanwhile,
the lowest potassium content in stover was observed under Weedy
check i.e. 1.52% (2023–24), 1.46% (2024–25) and 1.49% (pooled).
The significant enhancement in potassium content in maize grain and
stover under weed-free and sequential herbicidal treatments was pri-
marily due to the effective suppression of weed competition for soil
moisture and nutrients. Mitigating crop-weed interference allowed for
unrestricted root extension, enabling enhanced potassium absorption
from the root zone. This unimpeded nutrient uptake promoted supe-
rior vegetative vigor and dry matter production, which in turn facili-
tated efficient partitioning and accumulation of potassium into plant
tissues. Conversely, high weed density in the weedy check severely
restricted nutrient availability, resulting in lower tissue potassium
concentrations. These findings are in close agreement with the results
reported by Deshmukhet al. (2008) [11].
Conclusion
Thus, it can be concluded that the pre-emergence application of
Atrazine @ 500 g ha−1 PE fb mesotrione + atrazine @ 300 g ha−1 at
25–30 DAS, as well as Pyroxasulfone @ 127 g ha−1 PE fb tembotri-
one @ 120 g ha−1 at 25–30 DAS, were found to be the most effective
herbicidal treatments for achieving efficient weed suppression, mini-
mizing crop-weed competition and significantly enhancing the nitro-
gen, phosphorus and potassium concentrations in both grain and
stover of maize, remaining statistically at par with the weed-free
check.
