Growing solid waste generation places increasing pressure on urban land systems by consuming scarce spatial resources, constraining environmental capacity, and increasing management burdens. This study exploits the implementation of the Zero-Waste City Policy (ZWCP) as an exogenous policy intervention. It first measures urban land resource allocation efficiency with the super-efficiency SBM model and then applies a double machine learning framework to mitigate high-dimensional confounding, estimate the policy effect, and explore potential transmission factors. The results indicate that the ZWCP leads to a significant improvement in efficiency of urban land allocation. Further evidence suggests that the policy effect operates through enhanced green innovation, accelerated industrial up-grading, and increased governmental attention to environmental governance. Heterogeneity analysis further reveals that the positive effect is more evident in central and western regions and in small and medium-sized cities. This study extends the literature on the economic consequences of solid waste governance from the perspective of land resource allocation and provides empirical evidence for advancing integrated waste management and urban green development.
