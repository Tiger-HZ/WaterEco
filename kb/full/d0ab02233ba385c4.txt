Abstract The EU Drinking Water Directive (2020/2184) mandates monitoring of 20 priority per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) from January 2026, yet predicting which PFAS structures pose the greatest groundwater contamination risk remains challenging. We present a machine learning framework that simultaneously predicts the three physicochemical properties governing PFAS subsurface mobility─soil sorption (log KOC, R2 = 0.90), water solubility (log Sw, R2 = 0.90), and ionization class (93% accuracy)─from molecular structure alone. The models were trained on 122 PFAS spanning 37 subclasses using 44 molecular descriptors, including nine that encode the segmentation of the fluorinated backbone by ether linkages, and were validated by cross-validation, external test sets, leave-one-subclass
