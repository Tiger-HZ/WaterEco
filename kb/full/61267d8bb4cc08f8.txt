With global warming, ice avalanche hazards have escalated, becoming one of the most severe cryospheric disasters on the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau. To date, research on ice avalanches remains limited, focusing largely on individual glaciers and lacking a regional-scale understanding of their overall activity. In this study, an ice avalanche inventory was established based on multi-source remote sensing data, and a geographically weighted random forest (GWRF) model was developed for regional-scale ice avalanche hazard assessment by integrating multiple topographic, climatic, and glacier-related conditioning factors while considering their spatially varying relationships with ice avalanche occurrence.
