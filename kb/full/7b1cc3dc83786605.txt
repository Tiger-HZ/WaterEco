Construction project performance remains highly sensitive to contextual determinants that extend beyond traditional project-level management variables. This systematic literature review synthesises evidence from 36 empirical studies published between 2015 and 2025 to examine: (RQ1) which contextual determinants have been investigated and how they are operationalised; (RQ2) how project performance is measured; (RQ3) how governance and administrative capacity are conceptualised; and (RQ4) what methodological patterns and gaps characterise the field. The review covers building, infrastructure, and PPP projects across developing and emerging economies, with a smaller representation from advanced contexts. Findings show that economic/financial (e.g., inflation, payment delays, financing cost), institutional/regulatory (e.g., procurement systems, political interference, rule of law), social/workforce (e.g., labour productivity, skill shortages), technical/managerial (e.g., planning quality, design changes), and environmental/external shock determinants are recurrent across studies. Cost and time overruns dominate performance operationalisation, while quality, safety, satisfaction, and survival outcomes are comparatively under-examined. Governance appears in three forms: external institutional quality, project/contract governance mechanisms, and organisational capability. The strongest evidence indicates that governance functions as a buffering and mediating mechanism that shapes how contextual pressures translate into performance outcomes. However, the literature is dominated by cross-sectional perception-based designs, limiting causal inference and interaction modelling. The review concludes that construction performance is best understood as a multi-level phenomenon in which contextual volatility interacts with project-system and governance capacity. Future research should prioritise longitudinal, multi-level, and interaction-based modelling to quantify governance buffering effects and enhance policy-relevant insights.
