Large ungulates significantly influence forest ecosystems through shoot biting and bark stripping, but their impact on the dynamics of invasive plant species still remains insufficiently investigated. The North American black cherry (Prunus serotina Ehrh.) is a widespread invasive species in European forests, known for its strong defensive capabilities against herbivores. Despite this, recent observations indicate that deer occasionally utilize its bark and phloem as a food source. This study aimed to determine the extent of deer bark stripping on P. serotina across different age/size classes and evaluate the associated nutrient content in plant tissues.
