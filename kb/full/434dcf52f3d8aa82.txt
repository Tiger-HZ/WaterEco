The North China Plain (NCP) has entered a “New Water Regime” driven by water diversions, stringent groundwater management, and climatic fluctuations, resulting in the widespread recovery of regional groundwater levels. How land subsidence has responded spatiotemporally to these interventions remains unclear. To quantify the regional hydrogeological responses, we integrated Time-Series Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (TS-InSAR) observations spanning 2016–2023 with weighted centroid tracking and Emerging Hot Spot Analysis (EHA) based on a Space-Time Cube. Centroid tracking indicated that the regional subsidence center remained spatially stable without significant directional drift.
