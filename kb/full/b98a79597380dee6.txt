ABSTRACT Pickering emulsion polymerization employing inorganic nanoparticles as stabilizers, without the use of small molecular surfactants, can create high-performance waterborne polymer dispersions ideal for industrial purposes including coatings, inks, and adhesives. In addition to silica nanoparticles, Laponite clays can be integrated into emulsion latexes, as their layered structures are anticipated to improve barrier properties. This study explores the function of Laponite clays as the sole stabilizers in both single-batch and semi-continuous emulsion polymerization processes, examining their effects on the final emulsion latexes. Using styrene (St) as a representative hydrophobic monomer, we show that Laponite clay-stabilized latexes can be synthesized with single-batch monomer addition, while latex size can be managed through continuous monomer addition. Films that are transparent and water-resistant can be produced using 1.0 wt% Laponite clays as stabilizers. Notably, the elastic modulus of films made from 1.0 wt% Laponite clay-stabilized latexes is over ten times greater than that of surfactant-stabilized latexes. When switching the monomer to the less hydrophobic methyl methacrylate (MMA), Laponite clays maintain stability in PMMA emulsion latexes. Additionally, hybrid emulsion latexes created through continuous monomer addition can yield shape-retaining hydrogels.
