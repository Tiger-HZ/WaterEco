The study explored the bacteriological and nutritional composition of akamu fermented from a combination of Zea mays, Sorghum bicolor and Pennisetum glaucum cereals. Akamu was fermented in four (4) setups (white Zea mays and Sorghum bicolor combination, yellow Zea mays and Sorghum bicolor combination, white Zea mays and Pennisetum glaucum combination, yellow Zea mays and Pennisetum glaucum combination). The akamu was allowed to ferment by steeping in distilled for 4days. The effluents generated from the steeping water were collected daily for microbiological analyses. The fermentation process lasted for a period of 4 days and after that the steeped grains were wet milled, sieved, allowed to sediment, decanted to produce the semi-solid akamu.
