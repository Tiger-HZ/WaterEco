Water hyssop (Bacopa monnieri (L.) Wettst.) is a water-demanding medicinal plant whose responses to declining soil moisture remain insufficiently characterized, particularly in relation to phenolic accumulation. This study investigated the morphological, physiolog-ical, and anatomical responses of water hyssop to water limitation and identified the soil moisture conditions associated with enhanced phenolic accumulation. Short-term water limitation (11 days at 9.06% soil moisture) maximized total phenolic content (TPC), which reached a peak of 195.69 µg mL−1. Physiological stress was initially detected at 21–22% soil moisture, evidenced by a decline in relative water content (RWC).
