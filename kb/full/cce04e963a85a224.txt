Current reusable launch vehicle infrastructure treats the launch pad and the landing site asymmetrically.Launch pads at high-thrust sites use water deluge systems to manage acoustic reflection and heat duringignition and liftoff. No equivalent system exists on the landing side. Vehicles that land, whether ondroneships, dedicated landing zones, or directly into a catch tower, are received onto open, weatherexposed infrastructure and are only brought into a controlled environment once transported to a separaterefurbishment hangar. This paper proposes a conceptual, ground-based enclosure positioned at the landingsite itself, intended to close that gap using dry, tiered containment rather than water. The system relies ona rigid, telescoping wall tier for direct post-landing exposure, a flexible, roll-stored wall tier for the lowerintensity tail of off-gassing and residual heat, predictively pre-staged ventilation tied to descent telemetry,and a sensor-driven unified control system governing timing across all components. A secondarycontribution reframes debris shed during landing, typically treated as pad damage requiring cleanup, aspotential diagnostic signal, drawing on established analogs such as aircraft engine oil analysis andNASA's use of shed thermal protection material as an engineering indicator during the Shuttle program.This paper situates the concept against the closest identified prior art, states plainly which elementsappear novel and which do not, and identifies the specific engineering unknowns that would need to be resolved before the concept could move from design reasoning to a buildable specification. It does notclaim to have built, tested, or formally validated the concept described.
