EDITORIAL
Advancing environmental toxicology through sustainable 
technologies, intelligent systems, and forensic innovations
Kumud Kant Awasthi1 · Mahipal Singh Sankhla1 · Rajeev Kumar2

Environmental toxicology stands at the intersection of 
research, sustainability, and societal imperative. Environ-
mental danger is rapidly altering due to industrialization, 
climate change, and emerging poisons. This Special Issue is 
based on the compilation “Advancements in Environmental 
Toxicology: Assessing Human Exposure and Health Risks” 
and comprises a range of interconnected studies that tackle 
pressing concerns in environmental contamination, monitor-
ing, remediation, and sustainable technological progress. It 
emphasizes integrated approaches for risk evaluation and 
environmental surveillance. Collectively, these contributions 
provide critical insights for advancing research and policy in 
environmental toxicity.
A prominent subject in this collection is the shift towards 
sustainable resource utilization and eco-friendly technical 
solutions. Research on the thermal pretreatment of water 
hyacinth suggests the feasibility of utilizing invasive bio-
mass to produce value-added biofuels, including bioethanol 
and biomethanol. It exemplifies a circular economy by inte-
grating waste management with renewable energy genera -
tion. Furthermore, advancements in sustainable machining 
through nanofluid-assisted minimum quantity lubrication 
demonstrate how technological innovations can significantly 
reduce environmental impact without compromising indus-
trial output.
Environmental toxicology remains concentrated on the 
management of water resources and the monitoring of envi-
ronmental conditions. The application of GIS technologies 
and multi-criteria evaluation for assessing groundwater 
potential in the watershed provides a solid foundation for 
sustainable water resource planning. The examination of 
land cover alteration and the impact of slope gradient on 
soil physicochemical properties yields critical insights into 
the effects of anthropogenic and geomorphological influ-
ences on soil health and contaminant dynamics. Another 
significant component of this collection is the domain of 
developing contaminants and novel materials. A comprehen-
sive evaluation of lithium in the Anthropocene underscores 
remediation strategies while also expressing apprehensions 
regarding the escalating accumulation, bioavailability, and 
health ramifications of lithium in the environment. The syn-
thesis of MWCNT including Cu-BDC metal–organic frame-
works for the photocatalytic degradation of methylene blue 
dye demonstrates the potential of nanostructured materials 
in addressing industrial wastewater pollution. Artificial intel-
ligence and data-centric methodologies are revolutionizing 
environmental toxicology. The utilization of explainable 
deep reinforcement learning in climate forecasting provides 
a transparent and adaptive modeling framework, while AI/
ML-driven computational models for toxicity prediction 
signify a transformative shift towards predictive toxicol-
ogy, departing from conventional experimental techniques 
and facilitating expedited risk assessment. The compilation 
is a notably innovative contribution to the domain of green 
forensics. This study presents the effective use of an eco-
friendly eggshell-derived reagent for the detection of latent 
fingerprints on objects immersed in water, highlighting the 
potential of sustainable materials in forensic science and 
bridging green chemistry with forensic investigation.

strategy that integrates environmental chemistry, engineer -
ing, data science, and forensic methodologies. The research

focuses on the biological 
effects of nanoparticles, heavy 
metal contamination, oxidative

tainable strategies for soil and 
water remediation. He also 
works on nano-enabled biosen -
sors and forensic applications of nanotechnology. Dr. Awasthi has

ries in life sciences and forensic science. He actively contributes 
to the scientific community as an editor, guest editor, and reviewer

mental challenges through innovative, sustainable, and interdisci-
plinary scientific approaches.
