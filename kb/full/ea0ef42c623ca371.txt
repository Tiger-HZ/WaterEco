ABSTRACT Amphibian populations are in global decline, with 41% of species considered threatened, driven by habitat loss, land use change, climate change and diseases, particularly chytridiomycosis. Larval stages are confined to aquatic environments making tadpoles reliable bioindicators of ecosystem health due to high sensitivity to both water quality and surrounding land use. This systematic review synthesizes evidence from 58 studies published between 2005 and 2025 that examined the effects of land use and water quality characteristics on anuran tadpole assemblages. Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, a structured literature search was conducted across Web of Science, Scopus and Google Scholar in December 2025.
