The evaluation of plant landscape aesthetic quality in national wetland parks, when viewed through the lens of urban–rural integrated leisure experience, constitutes a nuanced multi-attribute decision-making problem rather than a purely visual assessment. Given the perceptual uncertainty inherent in leisure contexts, this study adopts probabilistic hesitant fuzzy sets (PHFSs), which more effectively capture ambiguity and evaluative hesitation. Attribute weights are derived using an entropy-based method, thereby enhancing objectivity and reducing subjective bias. Moreover, a probabilistic hesitant fuzzy grey relational analysis (PHF-GRA) model is developed, integrating grey system theory with fuzzy uncertainty modeling to strengthen analytical robustness. The framework is validated through a case study of a national wetland park, and comparative results indicate greater adaptability and discriminative power, offering practical implications for ecological planning and rural revitalization.
