ABSTRACT The growing concerns over the economic, health, and environmental impacts of traditional chemical coagulants have sparked a global interest in finding more sustainable alternatives for water treatment. This study explores and compares two locally available Ethiopian plant-based coagulants, cactus (Opuntia ficus-indica) and Moringa stenopetala, to determine their effectiveness in purifying contaminated surface water. Using response surface methodology with a face-centered central composite design, we examined how four critical operational factors – pH, coagulant dosage, stirring speed, and stirring time – influence the removal of color, chemical oxygen demand, and turbidity from water.
