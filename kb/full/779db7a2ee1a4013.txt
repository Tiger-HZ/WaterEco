Abstract Soil physiochemistry controls phosphorus (P) biogeochemistry and, by extension, plant productivity in terrestrial environments. Often, P is split into plant-available and bound pools. Iron-bound P, specifically, is traditionally considered unavailable to plants. Here we show iron-rich soil layers can hold the majority (60 to 78 percent) of phosphorus in the top meter of soil and, more importantly, when the rise of a seasonal water table reaches this layer the reductive dissolution of iron oxides releases high concentrations of P into soil pore water.
