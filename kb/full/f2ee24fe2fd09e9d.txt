Phenology-synchronized nano-biostimulant consortia
enhance productivity and multi-nutrient-use efficiency of
early-, medium and late-maturing maize (Zea mays L.)
hybrids under contrasting nitrogen environments
B Arjun Rao1, Chittimothu Suresh Babu2
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/26174693.2026.v10.i9Sac.10168
A BSTRACT
Nitrogen management in modern maize (Zea mays L.) production systems is complicated by hybrid-
specific differences in phenological duration and nutrient-demand dynamics, while the integration of
biostimulants into precision, phenology-based nutrient schedules remains poorly characterized. A field
experiment was conducted during the 2025–26 kharif season at BEST Innovation University, Andhra
Pradesh, India, in a Randomized Block Design with three replications, to evaluate the interactive effects
of three maize hybrid maturity groups (early, H1; medium, H2; late, H3), two nitrogen environments
(low-N, 60 kg N ha−1; recommended-N, 120 kg N ha−1) and a phenology-synchronized ternary nano-
biostimulant consortium (seaweed extract + plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria + humic acid encap-
sulated in a chitosan nanocarrier; B0 = control, B1 = nano-consortium applied at the V6, VT and R2
stages of each hybrid) on growth, yield, grain quality, soil nutrient dynamics and multi-nutrient-use effi-
ciency. Grain yield increased significantly with hybrid maturity duration (3973, 4942 and 5936 kg ha−1
for H1, H2 and H3, respectively), nitrogen level (5770 vs. 4130 kg ha−1) and nano-biostimulant applica-
tion (5356 vs. 4545 kg ha−1; +17.8%), with the highest yield (7724 kg ha−1) recorded for the late-ma-
turing hybrid under recommended nitrogen with the nano-consortium (T12) — a 145.3% gain over the
lowest-yielding treatment. Significant hybrid × biostimulant interactions for grain yield and for partial
factor productivity of nitrogen, phosphorus-use efficiency and potassium-use efficiency indicated that
the absolute magnitude of the biostimulant response scaled with hybrid maturity duration, being largest
in the late-maturing hybrid. Nano-biostimulant application did not alter the calendar duration to physio-
logical maturity, and it significantly enhanced plant height, leaf area index, SPAD chlorophyll index,
kernel weight, grain protein content, soil available phosphorus and potassium, and microbial biomass
carbon. These results demonstrate that stage-scouted, phenology-synchronized nano-biostimulant appli-
cation is an agronomically robust strategy for improving maize productivity and nutrient-use efficiency,
with the largest returns obtained in longer-duration hybrids under adequate nitrogen supply.

1. Introduction
Maize (Zea mays L.) is among the most productive and versatile cereal crops in the world, with global pro-
duction exceeding 1,148 million tonnes harvested from approximately 205 million hectares (FAO, 2023) [1].
As a C4 crop with a highly efficient carbon-assimilation pathway, maize possesses substantial genetic yield
potential; however, realization of this potential depends strongly on the synchronization of nutrient availabil-
ity, particularly nitrogen (N), with crop developmental demand. In India, maize occupies nearly 10.0 million
hectares with an average productivity of only about 3.2 Mg ha−1, well below the 8–12 Mg ha−1 attainable
under optimum management, reflecting a substantial and persistent yield gap (Cassman et al., 2020) [2].
Nitrogen demand in maize is highly dynamic, rising sharply from the six-leaf (V6) stage through tasseling
(VT) as canopy establishment accelerates, and continuing through grain filling via a combination of contin-
ued root uptake and remobilization of pre-silking N reserves. Commercial hybrids differ substantially in phe-
nological duration - broadly classified as early- (80–90 days after planting, DAP), medium- (95–105 DAP)
and late-maturing (115–130 DAP) - and, consequently, in the timing, intensity and duration of their nutrient-
demand windows (Ciampitti et al., 2012; Bender et al., 2013) [4, 3]. A single calendar-based N schedule may
therefore be poorly synchronized with the physiological demand of hybrids possessing different maturity du-
rations, motivating a phenology-based, stage-scouted approach to nutrient and biostimulant management
rather than one driven purely by calendar days.

Biostimulants - substances or microorganisms capable of stimulating
natural physiological processes independent of their nutrient content
(du Jardin, 2015) [5] - have emerged as a rapidly expanding comple-
ment to conventional fertilization. Seaweed extracts (e.g.,
Ascophyllum nodosum) contribute auxin- and cytokinin-like bioactiv-
ity that promotes root growth and delays senescence (Battacharyya et
al., 2015; Shukla et al., 2019) [6, 7]; plant growth-promoting rhizobac-
teria (PGPR) such as Azospirillum brasilense and Bacillus subtilis en-
hance rhizosphere nutrient mobilization and biological nitrogen fixa-
tion (Bashan et al., 2014) [8]; and humic substances improve root ar-
chitecture and nutrient transport (Nardi et al., 2021) [9]. Encapsulation
of such bioactive consortia within chitosan-based nanocarriers has ad-
ditionally been reported to improve compound stability, controlled re-
lease and physiological uptake efficiency (Oliveira et al., 2016) [10].
Despite growing interest in both precision nitrogen management and
biostimulant technology, limited evidence exists on whether the effec-
tiveness of biostimulant application, when synchronized with hybrid-
specific phenological stages rather than fixed calendar dates, differs
systematically across maize maturity groups and nitrogen environ-
ments. This study therefore examined the combined and interactive
effects of hybrid maturity group (early, medium, late), nitrogen envi-
ronment (low vs. recommended) and a phenology-synchronized
ternary nano-biostimulant consortium (seaweed extract + PGPR + hu-
mic acid in a chitosan nanocarrier) on growth, phenology, yield, grain
quality, soil nutrient dynamics and multi-nutrient-use efficiency of
maize, with the specific objectives of (i) quantifying the main and in-
teractive effects of these three factors on productivity and nutrient-use
efficiency, and (ii) determining whether the magnitude of the biostim-
ulant response scales with hybrid phenological duration.
2. Materials and Methods
2.1 Experimental site and soil characterization
The field experiment was conducted during the kharif season of
2025–26 at the Research Farm of BEST Innovation University, lo-
cated in the semi-arid (Köppen BSh) agro-climatic zone of Andhra
Pradesh, India. Mean annual rainfall in the region ranges from ap-
proximately 700–900 mm, concentrated in the June–September south-
west monsoon, and mean kharif-season temperatures range from ap-
proximately 24–36 °C. Composite soil samples (n = 12, 0–30 cm
depth) were analysed for baseline physico-chemical properties prior
to treatment imposition (Table 1). The soil was a non-saline (EC 0.32
± 0.04 dS m−1), moderately alkaline (pH 7.8 ± 0.1) sandy clay loam
Alfisol with low organic carbon (4.6 ± 0.6 g kg−1) and available nitro-
gen (178 ± 16 kg ha−1), moderate available phosphorus (20.8 ± 2.8 kg
ha−1) and potassium (240 ± 22 kg ha−1), and marginally low available
zinc (0.38 ± 0.05 mg kg−1).
2.2 Plant material and treatments
Three commercial maize hybrids representing contrasting maturity
groups were used: early-maturing (H1, 80–90 DAP), medium-matur-
ing (H2, 95–105 DAP) and late-maturing (H3, 115–130 DAP). All hy-
brids were sown on the same date at a spacing of 60 cm × 25 cm (tar-
get population ≈ 60,000 plants ha−1), with uniform basal application
of phosphorus (60 kg P2O5 ha−1) and potassium (40 kg K2O ha−1) to
all plots. Nitrogen was applied at two levels - low-N (N1, 60 kg N
ha−1) and recommended-N (N2, 120 kg N ha−1) - constituting the sec-
ond experimental factor. Biostimulant treatment (Factor C) comprised
a water-sprayed control (B0) and the nano-biostimulant consortium
(B1), applied as a foliar spray at 500 L ha−1 using a calibrated knap-
sack sprayer at three phenological stages of each respective hybrid:
V6 (six fully expanded leaves), VT (tasseling/anthesis) and R2 (blis-
ter stage). Because the three hybrids differed in developmental rate,
field scouting was used to identify the true physiological stage of each
hybrid rather than applying treatments on fixed calendar dates, ensur-
ing that biostimulant delivery was synchronized with comparable de-
velopmental states across maturity groups.
2.3 Nano-biostimulant consortium: preparation and
characterization
The ternary nano-biostimulant consortium comprised Ascophyllum
nodosum seaweed extract (5% v/v), a PGPR consortium of
Azospirillum brasilense and Bacillus subtilis (≈108 CFU mL−1), and
potassium humate (2% w/v), encapsulated within a medium-molecu-
lar-weight chitosan nanocarrier (190–310 kDa; degree of deacetyla-
tion ≥ 75%). Chitosan nanoparticles were synthesized by the ionic-
gelation technique using sodium tripolyphosphate (TPP) as the cross-
linking agent, with the bioactive components incorporated during
nanoparticle formation under controlled mixing to preserve PGPR vi-
ability. The formulation was characterized for particle size and zeta
potential (Zetasizer Nano ZS, Malvern Panalytical; target zeta poten-
tial +25 to +35 mV for colloidal stability), encapsulation efficiency
(UV–Vis spectrophotometry), PGPR viability (plate-count on Luria–
Bertani agar) and retention of seaweed-extract bioactive constituents
(HPLC) prior to field application.

2.4 Experimental design and treatment structure
The experiment followed a Randomized Block Design (RBD) with a
3 × 2 × 2 factorial treatment structure - Hybrid Maturity Group (H1,
H2, H3) × Nitrogen Environment (N1, N2) × Biostimulant Treatment
(B0, B1) - replicated three times (12 treatment combinations × 3 repli-
cations = 36 plots). Each plot measured 4.0 m × 5.0 m (20 m2 gross),
with a net harvestable area of 3.0 m × 4.0 m (12 m2) after excluding a
0.75 m border on all sides. Treatment codes and combinations are
summarized in Table 2.

2.6 Statistical analysis
Data were analysed by three-factor factorial ANOVA appropriate to
the RBD (Hybrid × N-environment × Biostimulant) using Python
(statsmodels v0.14), cross-verified in R v4.3.2 (R Core Team, 2024)
[11]. Treatment means were compared using critical difference (CD) at
P = 0.05, computed as 𝐶𝐷 = 𝑆𝐸𝑚± ×𝑡 ( 0.05, error df ) × √2 .
Coefficient of variation (CV%) was calculated as the ratio of the
square root of the error mean square to the grand mean. Pearson cor-
relation analysis was used to examine relationships among growth,
yield and nutrient-use-efficiency parameters.
3. Results
3.1 Growth attributes and phenology
Plant height at the R4 stage, leaf area index (LAI) at VT and the
SPAD chlorophyll index at VT were all significantly (P ≤ 0.01) influ-
enced by hybrid maturity group, nitrogen environment and nano-bios-
timulant application (Table 3, Fig. 1). Plant height increased progres-
sively with hybrid duration, from a mean of 168.8 cm in H1 to
187.9 cm in H2 and 202.3 cm in H3, consistent with the longer vege-
tative phase and greater internode number of longer-duration hybrids.
Recommended-N plots were significantly taller (191.5 cm) than low-
N plots (181.2 cm), and nano-biostimulant application increased
mean plant height from 179.1 cm (control) to 193.6 cm — an effect
attributable to the auxin- and cytokinin-like activity of the seaweed-
extract component together with improved nutrient acquisition medi-
ated by PGPR and humic acid (Battacharyya et al., 2015; Nardi et al.,
2021) [6, 9]. LAI at VT increased from 3.57 (control) to 4.05 (nano-
BS; CD = 0.41 at P = 0.05), and SPAD index rose from 44.8 to 50.1
over the same comparison, indicating a genuine improvement in
canopy photosynthetic capacity rather than a response driven solely
by improved assimilate partitioning.

3.2 Yield attributes and grain yield
ANOVA indicated highly significant (P ≤ 0.01) main effects of hybrid
maturity group, nitrogen environment and biostimulant treatment on
grain yield, with significant hybrid × N-environment and hybrid ×
biostimulant interactions (P ≤ 0.05) and a non-significant three-way
interaction (Table 5). Grain yield increased progressively with hybrid
maturity duration, from 3973 kg ha−1 (H1) to 4942 kg ha−1 (H2) and
5936 kg ha−1 (H3) — a 49.4% improvement of H3 over H1 — consis-
tent with the greater total N accumulation and post-silking remobi-
lization capacity of longer-duration hybrids (Bender et al., 2013) [3].
Recommended-N plots yielded 5770 kg ha−1 against 4130 kg ha−1
under low-N (+39.7%), and nano-biostimulant application raised
mean grain yield from 4545 kg ha−1 (control) to 5356 kg ha−1
(+17.8%) — a response broadly comparable to, though more conser-
vative than, the 14.8% gain reported for seaweed extract alone by
Shukla et al. (2019) [7], consistent with the expectation that the addi-
tional PGPR and humic-acid components would compound the sin-
gle-component response.

3.3 Grain quality: protein content
Grain protein content responded most strongly to nitrogen environ-
ment, rising from 9.32% under low-N to 9.78% under recommended-
N (CD = 0.635% at P = 0.05), reflecting the direct structural role of N
in storage-protein deposition. Nano-biostimulant application pro-
duced a smaller but consistent increase (9.45% to 9.66%), attributable
to enhanced N uptake and assimilation via PGPR-mediated biological
N fixation and phosphate solubilization acting in concert with im-
proved root proliferation. Hybrid maturity group had comparatively
little effect on grain protein percentage, consistent with a dilution ef-
fect of higher starch deposition in the larger kernels of longer-dura-
tion hybrids approximately offsetting their greater total N uptake (Fig.
5).

3.5 Multi-nutrient-use efficiency (PFPN, NHI, PUE, KUE)
Partial factor productivity of nitrogen (PFPN) was, as expected on a
per-kg-applied-N basis, higher under low-N (68.84 kg kg−1) than rec-
ommended-N (48.09 kg kg−1). Nano-biostimulant application signifi-
cantly raised PFPN from 53.62 to 63.31 kg kg−1 (CD = 7.24 at P =
0.05), and PFPN increased progressively with hybrid maturity, from
47.15 (H1) to 58.47 (H2) and 69.77 kg kg−1 (H3). Nitrogen harvest in-
dex, phosphorus-use efficiency and potassium-use efficiency were
likewise all significantly improved by nano-biostimulant application
(NHI: 63.49 to 65.30%; PUE: 75.75 to 89.27 kg kg−1; KUE: 113.62
to 133.90 kg kg−1) and increased progressively with hybrid maturity
duration, being consistently highest in H3 (Table 5, Fig. 7).

3.6 Integrated ANOVA summary
A consistent pattern emerged across all growth, yield, quality, soil and
nutrient-use-efficiency parameters examined (Table 6): main effects
of hybrid maturity group, nitrogen environment and biostimulant

4. Discussion
The results provide consistent evidence that a phenology-synchro-
nized, chitosan-nanoencapsulated seaweed extract–PGPR–humic acid
consortium improves maize growth, yield and multi-nutrient-use effi-
ciency, and that the magnitude of this improvement scales positively
with hybrid maturity duration. The combined application of the nano-
consortium with recommended nitrogen in the late-maturing hybrid
(T12) produced the highest values across virtually every growth, yield,
quality, soil-biological and nutrient-use-efficiency parameter mea-
sured, while the unfertilized, non-biostimulant early-maturing hybrid
under low-N (T1) recorded the lowest values for nearly every trait - a
coherent hierarchy across independently measured trait categories that
strengthens confidence in the underlying biological mechanism rather
than reflecting a single confounded observation.
Mechanistically, the parallel enhancement of leaf area index and
SPAD chlorophyll index (source capacity), soil available P and K and
microbial biomass carbon (rhizosphere nutrient mobilization), and
kernel number and kernel weight (sink capacity and grain filling) is
consistent with the three complementary modes of action built into
the consortium: seaweed-extract-mediated hormonal stimulation of
shoot and root growth (Battacharyya et al., 2015) [6], PGPR-mediated
biological nitrogen fixation and phosphate solubilization (Bashan et
al., 2014) [8], and humic-acid-mediated improvement of root architec-
ture and nutrient transport (Nardi et al., 2021) [9]. Because nano-bios-
timulant application did not alter the calendar duration to physiologi-
cal maturity, the yield gains documented here reflect genuine im-
provement in physiological efficiency within an unchanged phenolog-
ical window rather than an extension of the growing season, an im-
portant practical distinction because it indicates the technology can be
adopted without disrupting existing cropping calendars or the sched-
uling of succeeding crops.
The significant hybrid × biostimulant interaction for grain yield and
for PFPN, PUE and KUE - but not for grain protein, plant height or
LAI individually - suggests that the yield-scaling effect of hybrid du-
ration operates principally through the capacity of longer-duration hy-
brids to translate biostimulant-enhanced nutrient acquisition into a
longer post-silking remobilization window, consistent with the physi-
ological framework proposed by Ciampitti et al. (2012) [4] and the
empirical nutrient-partitioning data of Bender et al. (2013) [3]. From
an applied standpoint, these findings imply that the economic and en-
vironmental returns to phenology-synchronized nano-biostimulant
technology are likely to be most pronounced in medium- and, espe-
cially, late-maturing hybrid systems managed under recommended ni-
trogen, where the extended grain-filling period provides the greatest
physiological opportunity for biostimulant-mediated nutrient capture -
a conclusion with direct relevance to sustainable-intensification
strategies that seek to increase productivity while reducing the envi

5. Conclusion
Phenology-synchronized application of a chitosan-nanoencapsulated
seaweed extract–PGPR–humic acid consortium significantly im-
proved grain yield, growth attributes, yield components, grain protein
content, soil biological activity and multi-nutrient-use efficiency of
maize across three contrasting hybrid maturity groups and two nitro-
gen environments. The magnitude of the biostimulant-mediated im-
provement in grain yield and in PFPN, PUE and KUE was signifi-
cantly greater in longer-duration hybrids, confirming that late-matur-
ing hybrids possess a greater physiological capacity to convert bios-
timulant-enhanced nutrient acquisition into recoverable yield and effi-
ciency gains. The combination of a late-maturing hybrid, recom-
mended nitrogen (120 kg N ha−1) and the phenology-synchronized
nano-biostimulant consortium produced the highest overall perfor-
mance among all treatments evaluated and is recommended as the
most agronomically favourable combination identified in this study.
Because nano-biostimulant application did not delay physiological
maturity, this technology can be integrated into existing cropping cal-
endars without disrupting succeeding-crop scheduling. The present
findings derive from a single season at one location; multi-season,
multi-location validation, together with economic analysis of adop-
tion and mechanistic studies at the molecular level (e.g., nutrient-
transporter gene expression across maturity groups), are recom-
mended before extending generalized recommendations to farmers.
