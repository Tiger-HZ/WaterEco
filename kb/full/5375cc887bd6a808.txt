Abstract Traditional separation membranes suffer from inherent limitations of single functionality, severe fouling susceptibility, and incapability of simultaneous removal of dissolved organic pollutants in oily wastewater, which severely limit their advanced treatment and applications. To address the limitations of traditional separation membranes in terms of antifouling and multifunctionality, this study, inspired by the hierarchical structure of nacre, aims to develop a dual-functional inorganic–organic composite hydrogel membrane that possesses both efficient oil–water separation and simultaneous catalytic degradation capacity for organic pollutants.
