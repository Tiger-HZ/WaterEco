CHEM-SAMARA is a conceptual white paper proposing a carrier-assisted, shape-memory foam delivery architecture for distributed material payloads on Mars. The concept builds on passive samara-style descent ideas, but replaces rigid wings with compressed deployable foam bodies inspired by cold hibernated elastic memory (CHEM) foam research. The system is intended for rugged material payloads rather than crew, fragile instruments, or precision landers. A protected carrier or aeroshell would survive the high-energy entry phase, then release pre-warmed or partially deployed CHEM-SAMARA units into a lower-energy descent regime. Each unit combines a useful payload core, such as metal feedstock, water, hydraulic fluid, polymer, ceramic, or other recoverable material, with a larger foam body intended to provide drag area, impact absorption, visibility, and post-recovery utility. The paper frames CHEM-SAMARA as a research hypothesis, not a validated flight system. It identifies key feasibility gates including deployment timing, low-pressure CO₂ aerodynamics, impact survival, recovery efficiency, foam reuse value, carrier overhead, and whole-system mass economics. It also explores passive descent morphologies, internal metallic skeletons, layered payload cores, post-recovery material loops, and staged test pathways from bench deployment to low-pressure chamber testing and Mars-relevant field analogs. This version includes conceptual illustrations, scoping calculations, risk registers, kill criteria, and reviewer questions intended to support technical critique and public prior-art disclosure.
