Cellular agriculture has emerged as a transformative approach to producing animal-derived foods through cell culture and precision fermentation, offering a potential pathway toward more sustainable and ethical food systems. This review provides a comprehensive and critical assessment of next-generation innovations in cellular agriculture, with a particular focus on technological readiness, scalability challenges, and commercialization prospects. Key advancements in cell line development, serum-free culture media, bioreactor engineering, scaffolding technologies, and precision fermentation platforms are discussed in the context of their role in enabling large-scale production of cultivated meat and related products. Despite significant progress, several technical and economic barriers remain. Culture media costs, scale-up limitations, cell line stability, and bioprocess optimization continue to constrain industrial feasibility. Engineering challenges such as shear stress, oxygen transfer, and metabolite accumulation further complicate large-scale cultivation. In addition, continuous production systems introduce new safety considerations, including contamination risks and process control complexities. While cellular agriculture has the potential to reduce land use, greenhouse gas emissions, and reliance on conventional livestock systems, these benefits must be critically evaluated against trade-offs such as high energy demand, resource intensity, and infrastructure requirements. This review also examines regulatory frameworks across major regions, ethical considerations, and factors influencing consumer acceptance, including trust, cultural perceptions, and labeling transparency. The functionally complementary relationship between cell cultivation and precision fermentation is examined specifically, how precision fermentation supplies key inputs (growth factors, functional proteins) that enable scalable cell cultivation. At the same time, both converge in hybrid products as a near-term commercialization pathway. Emerging tools such as artificial intelligence and synthetic biology are expected to accelerate process optimization and cost reduction. Overall, cellular agriculture represents a promising but still evolving field. Its successful transition from laboratory innovation to commercial reality will depend on overcoming key technological bottlenecks, achieving economic viability, and addressing regulatory and societal challenges. This review aims to provide a balanced perspective to guide future research, policy development, and industry strategies.
