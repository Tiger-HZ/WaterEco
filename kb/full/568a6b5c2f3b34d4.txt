Abstract
Polystyrene microplastics (PS MPs) and carbamazepine (CBZ) are frequently detected in freshwater
ecosystems. However, the transgenerational effects of PS MPs and CBZ on the reproduction of aquatic
organisms and the corresponding mechanisms are still unclear. In the present study, D. magna was used
to evaluate the reproductive toxicity in two consecutive generations (F0, F1). Molting and reproduction
parameters, the expression of reproduction, and toxic metabolism genes were examined after 21 d
exposure. A signicantly enhanced toxicity was observed in the presence of 5 μ m PS MPs and CBZ.
Chronic exposure results showed that the 5 μ m PS MPs alone, CBZ alone, and their mixtures exerted
signicant reproductive toxicity of D. magna. The results of RT-qPCR showed transcripts of genes related
to reproduction (cyp314, ecr-b, cut, vtg1, vtg2, dmrt93b) and toxic metabolism (cyp4, gst) were altered in
both the F0 and F1. In addition, for the F0, gene transcriptional changes of reproduction were not fully
translated into physiological performance, probably due to the compensatory responses caused by the
low dose of PS MPs alone, CBZ alone, and their mixtures. Whereas for the F1, the trade-off between
reproduction and toxic metabolism at gene levels was observed, which translated into a signicant
reduction in the total neonate number of F1. These ndings suggest that long-term exposure to MPs and
CBZ can cause serious reproduction damage to aquatic animals, which needs to be given sucient
attention.
1. Introduction
Microplastics (MPs), dened as the size lower than 5 mm, have been ubiquitously detected in
environments, even in table salt and drinking water (Zhang, et al, 2020). Due to their small size and bio-
inert properties, MPs in aquatic environment are easily ingested in large quantities by a variety of aquatic
organisms (Xu et al., 2020). More importantly, MPs can adsorb various contaminants due to their high
specic surface area (Mai, et al, 2020), whose risk is substantially different from pristine ones (Dang et
al., 2022). Therefore, it is necessary to study the combined toxicity of contaminants and MPs.
Carbamazepine (CBZ), an anticonvulsant or anti-epileptic drug, is one of the most frequently detected
contaminants in aquatic environments around the world (Clara et al., 2004). According to previous
studies, CBZ was detected in surface water at concentrations up to 8.05 µg/L (aus der Beek et al., 2016).
This pharmaceutical can induce toxic effects on aquatic animals, including alterations on oxidative
status, metabolic capacity in bivalves and reproductive toxicity in sh (Almeida et al., 2021; Yan et al.,
2018).
Recently, several researchers have reported toxicity effects of MPs and CBZ on aquatic organisms.
Brandts et al. (2018) found that the co-exposure to polystyrene (PS) MPs and CBZ induced a signicant
downregulation of gene expression related to biotransformation, DNA repair, cellular stress response, and
innate immunity in the gills of marine bioindicator mussels (Mytilus galloprovincialis), in comparison
with exposure to PS MPs or CBZ alone. Moreover, co-exposure to PS MPs and CBZ had more severe
synergistic toxicity on shell formation in marine thick-shelled mussels (M. coruscus), thereby threatening
Page 3/21
their health and survival (Yu et al. 2022). Both PS MPs and CBZ were found to have adverse effect on
reproduction, however, whether the reproductive effects of CBZ adsorbed on MPs in aquatic organisms
can be changed by MPs has not been investigated.
Furthermore, in real-life scenarios where contaminants are continuously released into the environment,
aquatic organisms are exposed throughout their entire lifetime over multiple generations. It has been
found that MPs and pharmaceuticals can produce transgenerational reproductive toxicity in the offspring
after their parental exposure (Junaid et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2020). The transgenerational reproductive
toxicity might still not be recovered when the exposure was removed (Liu et al., 2022a; Nogueira et al.,
2022).
Thus, it is of great interest to study whether the transgenerational reproductive toxicity of CBZ can been
inuenced by PS MPs. In the present study, the freshwater planktonic crustacean D. magna was selected
as a model aquatic organism to clarify this issue, due to its unique advantages for transgenerational
studies such as its short life cycle, high fecundity, and parthenogenetic reproduction (Tkaczyk et al.,
2021). To clarify the effects and mechanisms, the following two main aspects were demonstrated: i)
examined the chronic reproductive toxicity in both the parental generation (F0) and the offspring (F1) in
terms of life history parameters (molting and reproduction); ii) determined the expression of target genes
involved in reproduction (cytochrome 314 (cyp314), ecdysone receptor b (ecr-b), cuticle protein (cut),
vitellogenin 1 and 2 (vtg1, vtg2), doublesex-Mab related 93B (dmrt93b)) and toxic metabolism
(cytochrome 4 (cyp4), glutathione-S-transferase (gst)). The results could provide a valuable reference for
the ecological and health risk assessment of simultaneous exposure to PS MPs and pharmaceuticals in
the aquatic environment.
2. Materials And Methods
2.1 Test chemicals and organisms
Carbamazepine (CBZ, C15H12N2O; CAS No. 298-46-4; ≥ 98% purity) was purchased from Aladdin
Industries Inc. (Shanghai, China). Deionised water-ethanol emulsion (1:1, v/v) of PS MPs (700nm, 1 µm, 5
µm) at a concentration of 25 g/L was obtained from BaseLine ChromTech Research Centre (Tianjin,
China). Methanol was HPLC grade and purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). All other
chemicals are at analytical grade and were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.
(Shanghai, China).
D. magna were obtained from the Wuhan Clorox Biotechnology (Wuhan, China), and cultured in a 2 L
beaker containing M4 medium at 20 ± 2°C and 16 h light/18 h dark cycle. M4 medium was prepared on
the day before usage to obtain sucient dissolved oxygen and was renewed three times a week. D.
magna were fed daily with green alga (Scenedesmus obliquus) of 5×105 cells/mL.
2.2 Chemical analysis

The stability of CBZ was determined during the test using high performance liquid chromatography
(HPLC, Waters e2695, Waters Corporation). It was measured in freshly prepared solutions (t0 h, no
D.magna) and in the solution after 48 h exposure (t48 h, D.magna removed), in triplicate. The
measurement was repeated every two days. Analytical methods and nominal and measured
concentrations of CBZ in Supporting Information (SI). From Table SI, t0 h-concentration of CBZ remained
in the range of 90–101% of the nominal. And t48 h-concentration of CBZ remained in the range of 95–
100% of t0 h-concentration during the test, indicating that the attenuation of CBZ during 48 h was
negligible. Therefore, our subsequent results refer to the nominal values.
2.3 Acute exposure
The experiment was design according to OECD TG 202 (OECD, 2004). PS MPs with diameter 700 nm, 1
µm and 5 µm were selected for acute exposure because they can be ingested by D. magna and cause
different toxicity effects (Yang et al., 2021). The test was performed in 30 mL screw-top bottles. Briey,
the concentrations of PS MPs were selected according to the pre-experiments, ensuring the LC50 value
was within the concentration range. Then, 10 neonates (< 24 h) were exposed to a series of 20 mL PS
MPs of different particle sizes with set concentrations (0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 mg/L) for 48 h. The culture
conditions during the exposure were the same as during cultivation, but without feeding. At 48 h, the
organisms whose heartbeats were not observed under the biological inverted microscope (Jiangnan
JSZ6S, Nanjing, China) were considered dead and the results were recorded to calculate the median
lethal concentration (LC50) (OECD, 2004). Before the test, 20 mL of the test solutions were sonicated for
15 min to homogenize the solution. Each treatment group was performed in triplicate. A similar
experimental procedure was performed to obtain the LC50 of CBZ on D. magna, and the concentrations of
CBZ were set at 0, 30, 50, 70, 90 and 120 mg/L.
For the combined exposure, two sets of tests were carried out: i) PS MPs of variable concentrations (0, 5,
10, 15, 20, 25, 30 mg/L) plus 50 mg/L CBZ, ii) 5 mg/L PS MPs plus CBZ of variable concentrations (0, 30,
50, 70, 90 and 120 mg/L). The similar experimental procedure as for the single exposure were
subsequently performed to obtain the LC50.
2.4 Transgenerational chronic exposure
The D. magna chronic reproduction experiment was conducted according to OECD TG 211 (OECD, 2012),
and transgenerational reproduction experiment was conducted based on the protocol from Castro et al.
(2018). The experimental exposure concentrations are shown in Fig. 1. The exposure concentrations of
PS MPs (5 µm) and CBZ were set according to the acute exposure results and the aquatic environmental
relevant concentrations (aus der Beek et al., 2016). The PS size of 5 µm was selected as the chronic
exposure size, since it exerted moderate acute toxicity among the particle sizes studied based on the
acute exposure experiment. In addition, the 5 µm PS plus CBZ led to a signicant variation compared with
the separate tests according to our preliminary study (Table S2). The chronic exposure concentrations of
Page 5/21
PS MPs were set at 5 µg/L (7.18×104 particles/L) and 5 mg/L (7.18×107 particles/L), and CBZ was set at
5 µg/L.
As shown in Fig. 1, six test groups were designed, including blank control (M4; A), low and high
concentrations of PS MPs alone (B1, B2), CBZ alone (B3), and low and high concentrations of combined
PS MPs and CBZ (C1, C2). 15 neonates (< 24 h) of the parental generation (F0) were randomly selected
for each treatment group, and transferred to individual 30 mL screw-cap bottles containing 20 mL of test
medium for 21 d exposure, respectively. The F1 generation experiment was started with the neonates
between the third to fth broods (< 24 h) from F0. The F1 either performed exposure (F1 exposed to the
same concentration as the F0 generation) or recovery (F1 from the exposed parents and then transferred
to the clean medium) treatments for 21 d. The test medium was renewed every 48 h. Both the exposure
and recovery treatments of the F1 generation included 15 D. magna. During exposure of F0 and F1
generations, the following parameters were monitored: the rst brood time, the number of rst brood
neonates, the rst molting time, the total neonate number, the total brood number, and the molting
frequency. And the newborn neonates and molts were promptly removed from the bottles.
2.5 Quantitative real-time PCR assay
The F0 and F1 groups of D. magna for RT-qPCR analysis are shown in Fig. 1 (marked with “ # ”). 30
neonates (< 24 h; F0) were randomly selected for exposure to PS MPs or CBZ alone or their mixture. The
F1 generation were established using the third to fth broods (< 24 h) of the F0 generation. Both F0 and
F1 exposures lasted for 21 d. Three replicates were carried out for each treatment and subjected to the
same experimental procedure as the chronic exposure. At 21 d, F0 and F1 were collected from different
treatments, respectively. The collected D. magna specimens were rinsed with RNase-free water and placed
in 1.5 mL of RNase-free centrifuge tubes, then stored at -80°C until analysis. The total RNA was extracted
from each treatment group using RNA simple Total RNA Kit (DP419, TIANGEN Biotech, China). The
quality and concentration of the isolated RNA were checked and adjusted. The cDNA was then
synthesized using Hifair III 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix for qPCR (gDNA digester plus)
(11141ES10, YEASEN Biotech, China). We evaluated the expression of target genes regarding
reproduction and toxic metabolism, with β -actin as the housekeeping gene. Finally, RT-qPCR was
conducted using Genious 2×SYBR Green Fast qPCR Mix (RK21204, ABclonal Biotech, China). The primer
sequences for the reference and target genes and details of the RT-qPCR are presented in the SI.
2.6 Statistical analyses
Experimental data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation. The value of LC50 was calculated
with Probit analysis by SPSS 22.0 (IBM Crop., IL, USA). For the chronic exposure data, the normal
distribution test (Shapiro-Wilk test) and the chi-square test (Levene's test) were performed at rst. Under
the prerequisite of normal distribution and equal variance, a multiple comparison test (Dunnett's test) was
used to analyze the signicance of the differences (p0.05) between the different groups. If the data did
not conform to a normal distribution or there was unequal variance, then a non-parametric test (Kruskal-
Wallis test) was used to analyze the signicant differences (p0.05). T-test was used to compare
Page 6/21
differences between the exposure groups of F1 and the recovery groups of F1. All statistical analyses of
data were performed on SPSS 22.0 statistical analysis software.
3. Result
3.1 Acute exposure
The LC50 values and 95% condence intervals (CI) for the exposures to PS MPs of different sizes alone,
CBZ alone, or their mixtures for neonatal D. magna are shown in Table S2 and Table S3. For single acute
toxicity, the 48 h LC50 value of CBZ was calculated to be 88.77 mg/L. The 48 h acute toxicity of PS MPs
of different sizes were ranked as 1 µm (LC50 = 14.09 mg/L) > 5 µm (21.66 mg/L) > 700 nm (26.99 mg/L).
Our results indicated that the acute toxicity of PS MPs varied with the particle size.
In the presence of 50 mg/L CBZ, the 48 h LC50 of PS MPs of different sizes were ranked as 700 nm
(10.69 mg/L) > 5 µm (14.56 mg/L) > 1 µm (15.60 mg/L), which is nearly opposite to the exposure results
for single PS MPs of different sizes (Table S2). In the PS MPs of xed concentration with CBZ of variable
concentration group, the toxicity of CBZ increased in combination with 5 mg/L PS MPs (Table S3). And
the toxicity variation was correlated with the particle size of PS MPs, where the 48 h LC50 were ranked as
CBZ with 5 mg/L PS MPs of 5 µm (50.87 mg/L) > CBZ with 5 mg/L PS MPs of 700 nm (75.7 mg/L) > CBZ
with 5 mg/L PS MPs of 1 µm (82.12 mg/L). The strongest enhancement of acute toxicity of CBZ was
observed in the presence of 5 µm PS MPs.
The dose-response curves for 5 µm PS MPs were shown in Fig. 2. The results showed that in the presence
of CBZ or 5 µm PS MPs, the lethality of either 5 µm PS MPs or CBZ increased to varying degrees. For its
chronic toxicity, especially sublethal effects (e.g., transgenerational reproductive toxicity), it deserves
further investigation.
3.2 Transgenerational exposure
3.2.1 Parental exposure
Figure 3 shows the reproduction and molting performance of the F0 exposed for 21 d to PS MPs alone,
CBZ alone, or their mixture. As shown in Fig. 3A and Fig. 3B, there was no signicant difference in the rst
brood time and the number of rst brood neonates between the single groups and the control group.
However, in the co-exposure group, the rst brood time was signicantly prolonged and the number of
rst brood neonates was reduced signicantly (p < 0.05) when PS MPs was increased from 5 µg/L to 5
mg/L. After 21 d, the total neonate number from the co-exposure group of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 mg/L PS
MPs exhibited a signicant decrease (p < 0.05) compared to the other groups (Fig. 3C). However, for the
brood numbers after 21 d, no signicant differences were found in all exposure groups (Fig. 3D). And the
similar situation happened to the molting performance (Fig. 3E, 3F). In conclusion, the exposure of 5
mg/L PS MPs combined with 5 µg/L CBZ signicantly inhibited the fecundity of the F0.

3.3 Gene expression
3.3.1 Parental generation
The expression proles of eight selected genes of the F0 are shown in Fig. 5. Two target genes (cpy4, gst)
were selected to assess the toxic metabolism after 21 d exposure to PS MPs alone, CBZ alone, and their
mixtures. Compared with the control group, cyp4 expression was signicantly up-regulated (p < 0.05) in
the groups exposed to 5 µg/L PS MPs alone, 5 µg/L CBZ alone, and the mixture of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5
mg/L PS MPs, while it was signicantly downregulated (p < 0.05) in the groups exposed to 5 mg/L PS

< 0.05) but signicantly down-regulated (p < 0.05) in the groups exposed to 5 mg/L PS MPs, and the co-
exposure groups.
Six related target genes (cyp314, ecr-b, cut, vtg1, vtg2, dmrt93b) were assessed to clarify the reproductive
toxicological mechanism of F0. Compared to the control group, cyp314, ecr-b and cut were signicantly
up-regulated (p < 0.05) in the group exposed to 5 µg/L CBZ alone. The expression of both cyp314 and ecr-
b was up-regulated signicantly (p < 0.05) in the co-exposure group of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 µg/L PS MPs. A
signicant up-regulation of ecr-b and cut was also found in the co-exposure group of 5 µg/L CBZ with 5
mg/L PS MPs. Also, cut was signicantly up-regulated in the 5 µg/L PS MPs group. Moreover, a
signicant up-regulation of cyp314 and ecr-b was found in the group exposed to 5 mg/L PS MPs. The
expression of cut was signicantly down-regulated with the increasing exposure concentration of PS
MPs. In the co-exposure groups, the expression of cut signicantly up-regulated with the increasing
concentrations of PS MPs, while those of cyp314 and ecr-b did not change signicantly.
The expression of vtg1 and vtg2 was signicantly up-regulated (p < 0.05) in the exposure group of 5 mg/L
PS MPs alone. In the co-exposure groups of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 mg/L PS, both vtg1 and vtg2 was
signicantly up-regulated (p < 0.05), compared to the co-exposure to 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 µg/L PS MPs. In
addition, a signicant downregulation of vtg2 (p < 0.05) was found in the groups of 5 µg/L PS MPs alone,
5 µg/L CBZ lone, and the mixture of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 µg/L PS MPs. In terms of dmrt93b, no signicant
differences were observed between the control group and all exposure groups. However, dmrt93b in the
group of 5 mg/L PS MPs showed a signicant up-regulation (p < 0.05), compared with the group of 5
µg/L PS MPs, 5 µg/L CBZ, and co-exposure of 5 µg/L CBZ with 5 mg/L PS MPs exposure groups.

4. Discussion
Both MPs and pharmaceuticals can be accumulated in aquatic organisms, and transferred to offspring
(Junaid et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022; Minguez et al., 2015), thereby causing toxic effects on both
generations. The MPs can act as carriers of pharmaceuticals and facilitate their exposure to aquatic
organisms (Zhou et al., 2020). In the present study, we investigated the transgenerational reproductive
toxicity of PS MPs alone, CBZ alone, and their mixtures on D. magna, and the mechanisms were revealed
at the transcriptional level.
In the acute toxicity, the 48 h LC50 value of CBZ was 88.77 mg/L, which is within the range identied for
D. magna in previous studies (Di Poi et al., 2018; Oropesa et al., 2016). In terms of the toxicity of PS MPs,
the results indicated that 1 µm PS MPs showed the lowest 48 h LC50, illustrating that it posed the highest
toxicity among all tested PS MPs. Previous studies have demonstrated that 1 µm PS MPs was more toxic
to D. magna than 10 µm PS MPs, most probably because it is more readily ingested by zooplankton, and
then cause more immobilization and physical damage (Zhang et al., 2019). Similarly, it has been
demonstrated that the MPs of nanosize (e.g. 20 nm; 100 nm) are more bioavailable and could persist in
the organism for a longer time than the MPs of microsize (e.g. 1 µm; 2 µm) (Rist et al., 2017;), resulting in
more accumulation in D. magna, thereby, potentially causing a chronic sublethal toxicity rather than an
acute toxicity (Besseling et al., 2014). Such ndings might explain the fact that, in the present study, the 1
µm PS MPs caused the heaviest toxicity in the acute exposure.
In the presence of CBZ, the acute toxicity was enhanced for PS MPs of different particle sizes except for
MPs of 1 µm (Table S2). A similar result was observed in a previous study. Lin et al. (2021) reported that,
in the presence of pyrene, the immobilization of neonatal D. magna increased after 48 h exposure to PS
MPs of different particle sizes. Interestingly, in the present study, the order of acute toxicity of PS MPs
Page 10/21
with different particle sizes in the presence of CBZ was almost opposite to those of PS MPs of different
particle sizes alone. Among those, the acute toxicity of PS MPs of 700 nm nearly doubled in the presence
of CBZ, possibly because the MPs of nanosize had a larger specic surface area than those of microsize,
which facilitates more CBZ to enter into D. magna (Zhang and Goss, 2020). Compared to the MPs of
microsize, the MPs of nanosize may remain longer time in D. magna and consequently had more time to
discharge the carried CBZ, resulting in the enhanced bioaccumulation of CBZ in D. magna (Shi et al.,
2020). Therefore, we speculated that the toxicity variation of PS MPs of different particle sizes in the
presence of CBZ might be attributed to the toxicity amplication effect of CBZ.
In the presence of PS MPs, the acute toxicity of CBZ increased (Table S3). This can be probably attributed
to the vector effect of PS MPs, which can adsorb CBZ and subsequently enter the organism together, thus
elevating the bioaccumulation of CBZ in D. magna (Ma et al., 2016). Additionally, the variation of CBZ
toxicity in the presence of PS MPs was correlated with the particle size of PS MPs in the present study.
Among these, the toxicity of CBZ was signicantly enhanced in the presence of 5 µm PS MPs, followed
by 700 nm, and 1 µm. Moreover, the acute toxicity of CBZ in the presence of 5 µm PS MPs was enhanced
to the largest extent in all combined acute toxicity exposure groups. The sublethal effects, however, still
need further investigation.
The 21 d chronic reproduction tests of F0 generation showed that the combined exposure of 5 µg/L CBZ
and 5 mg/L PS MPs exerted a serious reproductive threat to F0, notably in terms of the total neonate
number (Fig. 3C). The total neonate number is a key and sensitive parameter to measure the reproductive
capacity of D. magna (Dang et al., 2012). The impaired reproduction in organisms caused by
environmental pollutants is also reected in gene expression, especially for the genes related to the
detoxication metabolism and reproduction (Bao et al., 2020). Regarding the toxic metabolism of D.
magna, the expression of cyp4 was measured, which was a vital detoxication enzyme in Phase I
detoxication systems of invertebrates (Snyder, 2000). And gst, which is involved in Phase II
detoxication of various xenobiotics, plays an important role in the detoxication of oxidative stressors
(Kim et al., 2021). Figure 5 observed that the expression of cyp4 from the exposure group of 5 µg/L PS
MPs alone, 5 µg/L CBZ alone, and the co-exposure group of 5 mg/L PS MPs and 5 µg/L CBZ, and gst
from the exposure group of 5 µg/L PS MPs were signicantly upregulated, which indicates that the
metabolism system was activated. Vitellogenin is considered to be the major precursor of vitellin, an egg-
yolk protein, which provides an essential source of energy during the embryogenesis of oviparous
organisms (Tokishita et al., 2006). In the D. magna, vitellogenin was encoded by at least two vtg genes,
vtg1 and vtg2, which share > 99% identity and have been identied on a single cluster in neighboring but
in opposite orientations ( Tokishita et al., 2006). Dmrt93b is associated with the determination of sex in D.
magna and is involved in the process of changing reproductive strategies (Kato et al., 2008). In the
present study, vtg2 from the exposure group of 5 µg/L PS MPs alone, 5 µg/L CBZ alone, and co-exposure
group of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 µg/L PS MPs all expressed a signicant down-regulation (Fig. 5). However,
there was no signicant difference in the total neonate number for these groups (Fig. 3C). It suggests that
the exposure to PS MPs alone, CBZ alone, or their mixture had affected the expression of reproduction
related genes, but not further affected the reproduction physiological outcome. It has been demonstrated
Page 11/21
that the low or moderate doses of toxic compounds may induce compensatory responses in individuals,
which facilitates the activation of repair mechanisms in response to damage (Bao et al., 2020; Rix et al.,
2022). In the present study, the insignicant effects on reproductive physiological parameters of
individuals exposed to PS MPs and CBZ at low doses, which may be attributed to the activation of toxic
metabolism system in D. magna. The compensatory could also be found in exposure group of 5 mg/L PS
MPs alone, where vtg 1 and vtg 2 were up-regulated and cyp4 and gst were down-regulated. This
compensatory effect seemed to be broken in the high dose co-exposure group of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 mg/L
PS MPs. In this exposure group, vtg1 also showed upregulated expression, the reasons behind need
further research.
Successful molting is essential for the growth and development of crustacea, which is predominantly
regulated by ecdysteroids (Sumiya et al., 2014). Cyp314, a subfamily of cytochrome P450, plays an
important role in the biosynthesis of ecdysone and is responsible for the conversion of ecdysone into its
active form 20-hydroxyecdysone (20E), which regulates molting (Liu et al., 2022b). Ecr-b is an ecdysone
receptor that is involved in crustacea growth and development by binding to 20E (LeBlanc, 2007). Both
are upstream genes that regulate molting. Cut is regulated by 20E and responsible for the production of
the neo-epidermal cuticle protein, which belongs to the downstream genes regulating molting (Song et al.,
2017). In 21 d exposure, both the rst molting time and the molting frequency of the F0 showed no
signicant difference compared to those from the control. However, from the transcriptional level, the
expression of molting related genes increased in F0 from all the exposed groups, except ecr-b in the 5
µg/L PS MPs exposed group (Fig. 5). It is probably due to the fact that the molting process of D. magna
involves multiple signal pathway besides the expression of cyp314, ecr-b and cut (Cho et al., 2022; Wei et
al., 2022).
Compared with the control group, a signicant reduction in the total neonate number was observed in the
F1 exposure groups, except for the exposure group of 5 µg/L PS MPs and the co-exposure group of 5
µg/L CBZ with 5 µg/L PS MPs. The result was consistent with the gene transcription, i.e., vtg1 and vtg2
signicantly downregulated in the F1 co-exposure group of 5 µg/L CBZ and 5 mg/L PS MPs), suggesting
that the co-exposure of high-dose PS MPs and CBZ caused more adverse reproductive effects in F1 than
F0. Previous studies have also demonstrated that continued exposure to MPs and PPCPs resulted in
serious reproductive toxicity to offspring (Liu et al., 2022a).. Moreover, the molting frequency of the 5
µg/L PS MPs exposure group was signicantly increased (Fig. 4E), which may be related to the irritant
effect, caused by the low dose of PS MPs (Rix et al., 2022). It has been demonstrated that low doses of
toxicants may promote the evolution of organisms (Costantini, 2019) via inducing transgenerational
excitation and stimulating the biological processes (Rix et al., 2022).
Likewise, the exposure still showed a reproduction inhibition at both physiological and gene
transcriptional level, even when the exposure was removed in the F1, except the offspring that their
parents were exposed to 5 µg/L PS MPs (Fig. 4C and Fig. 6). It indicates a transgenerational reproductive
toxicity under the long-term exposure to PS MPs alone, CBZ alone or their mixture. Previous studies have
obtained similar results for other MPs and pharmaceuticals. For instance, De Liguoro et al. (2019)
Page 12/21
reported that, when F0 generations of D. magna were exposed to umequine, the inhibition of
reproduction could persist up to the three unexposed generations (F1, F2, F3).
Surprisingly, the gene expression of toxic metabolism (cyp4, gst) was signicantly activated in both the
F1-recovery and F1-exposure groups, meanwhile the vtg1 and vtg2 were found to be signicantly down-
regulated (Fig. 6). This was also translated into the physiological performance, according to the results
shown in Fig. 4C that the total neonate number exhibited a signicant decrease in the F1-recovery group
whose parents were exposed to 5 mg/L PS MPs and 5 µg/L CBZ, as well as in the F1 group co-exposed to
5 µg/L CBZ and 5 mg/L PS MPs. It could be explained by the trade-off mechanism between toxic
metabolism and reproduction (Metcalfe and Alonso-Alvarez, 2010), that is, F1 invested more energy in
detoxication metabolism, resulting in the reduced investment of energy to reproduction. It has been
reported that the reduction in reproduction of generations may be due to the increased energy
requirements (e.g., synthesis of antioxidant enzymes) to restore the internal maintenance damaged by
toxic-induced stress (Im et al., 2020; Kim et al., 2014).
5. Conclusion
In the present study, a signicantly enhanced acute toxicity of CBZ was observed in the presence of 5 μ m
PS MPs. Chronic exposure results showed that the 5 μ m PS MPs alone, CBZ alone, and their mixtures
exerted the reproductive toxicity of D. magna. It was also found that the F0 and F1 generations showed a
different pattern in the correlation of the reproduction physiological performance and gene levels, and
even the responses of the F1 were stronger than those in the F0. For the F0, the changes in gene
transcription were not fully translated into reproduction physiological performance, potentially owing to
the compensatory response induced in individuals by low or moderate doses of toxic compounds. For the
F1, the reproduction physiological performance and gene levels were consistently changed in both the
exposure and recovery groups, and the trade-off mechanism between the toxic metabolism and the
reproduction was found. Moreover, the reproductive toxicity of CBZ was aggravated by high doses of PS
MPs in F0, but not in the F1 exposure group. And the reproductive toxicity in F1 was dicult to recover.
Altogether, the results could provide a valuable reference to understand the transgenerational toxicity
mechanisms of PS MPs in combination with CBZ on aquatic organisms.
