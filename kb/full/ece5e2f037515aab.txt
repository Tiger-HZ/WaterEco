Flood overtopping in tropical urban river basins is frequently intensified by extreme rainfall, insufficient river conveyance capacity, sedimentation, floodplain encroachment, and limited flood-control infrastructure. This study develops a hydrologic–hydraulic modelling framework to assess return-period-based flood overtopping severity and establish mitigation priorities along the Pangkajene River, Indonesia. The framework integrates areal rainfall estimation using the Thiessen Polygon method, design rainfall analysis using the Log Pearson Type III distribution, design flood estimation using the HSS Nakayasu synthetic unit hydrograph, and hydraulic simulation using HEC-RAS. The hydrological analysis shows that design flood discharge increases from 296.65 m³/s under the 5-year return period to 526.94 m³/s under the 200-year return period, indicating a substantial increase in hydraulic loading under extreme flood conditions. Baseline hydraulic simulation demonstrates that multiple cross-sections along the investigated 5.5 km reach are unable to convey design flood discharges, resulting in widespread overtopping. The return-period-based severity analysis shows that 86.96% of the evaluated cross-sections are overtopped under Q5 and Q10, while 95.65% are overtopped from Q25 to Q200. Critical cross-section assessment identifies STA 4400, STA 400, STA 4000, STA 200, STA 1400, STA 3400, and STA 4600 as Priority I locations requiring immediate hydraulic intervention. The proposed framework provides methodological added value by converting HEC-RAS outputs, including water surface elevation, overtopping depth, and conveyance limitation, into a severity-based prioritization system that supports more operational and phased flood mitigation planning. The findings support staged flood-control planning through targeted dredging, embankment improvement, channel normalization, selective widening, sediment management, and periodic monitoring in data-limited tropical river basins.
