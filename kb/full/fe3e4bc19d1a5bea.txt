Olive ( Olea europaea L.) cultivation is gaining attention and expanding rapidly in Pakistan, 
predominantly in the Pothwar region. Being a new intervention, optimized nutrient recommendations 
for olive are lacking and need appropriate nutrient recommendation for achieving high fruit yield and 
oil productivity. During this study different nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) fertilizer combinations 
were tested on the yield performance of olive trees (Arbequina cv.) of ten years age at Chakwal, 
Pakistan. Treatments consisted of three N levels (400, 500, and 600 g tree-1) and two P levels (200 and 
300 g tree⁻¹), while potassium was applied uniformly at 300 g tree -1. Two plants were selected under 
each treatment and laid in randomized complete block design (RBCD) arrangement. Fertilizer 
application was completed according to treatment plan, all phosphorus, ⅟3 nitrogen and ½ potash was 
applied in first week of March before flowering, ⅟3 nitrogen in mid -April after fruit formation and ⅟3 
nitrogen and ½ potash was applied in the end of August. Plants were closely monitored during the 
whole period. During the month of October, olive fruits were picked from each tree after ripening, 
yield parameters including fruit weight/tree, average weight/fruit and oil content of fruit were 
recorded fo r each treatment. Results showed that increasing N and P application rates significantly 
improved olive productivity and oil accumulation. Among the tested treatments, NPK application at 
600-300-300 g tree⁻¹ (T 6) produced the highest fruit yield and superi or fruit quality attributes, 
indicating that adequate nutrient supply is critical for maximizing olive performance in Pothwar 
conditions. The results suggest that application of 600 g N, 300 g P, and 300 g K per tree represents 
the most effective nutrient combination for ‘Arbequina’ olive. These findings provide a locally validated 
fertilization strategy to enhance olive productivity and support the sustainable development of 
Pakistan’s emerging olive industry.

Introduction 
Olive (Olea europaea L.) is one of the world's most economically important fruit crops, cultivated extensively in Mediterranean 
and semi-arid regions for its edible oil, table fruit, and environmental benefits. In addition to its economic value, olive cultivation 
contributes to environmental sustainability through carbon sequestration, soil conservation, and adaptation to climate 
variability. Although olive cultivation has a long history in Mediterranean countries, olive farming in Pakistan is a relativ ely 
recent development. The country's first olive harvest was recorded in 1991 in Chakwal, Punjab (Ali et al., 2024). Owing to its 
diverse agro-ecological conditions, Pakistan possesses considerable potential for olive cultivation, particularly in rainfed and 
semi-arid regions. Recognizing the future demand for olive oil and the crop's high economic returns, the Government of Punjab 
has designated the Pothwar region as the “Olive Valley” of Pakistan (Iqbal et al., 2023). 
Several olive cultivars, including Ascolana, BARI Zaitoon -1, Earlik, Gemlik, Hamdi, Hojiblanca, Manzanilla, and Picual, have 
been introduced and promoted for commercial cultivation in Punjab (Iqbal et al., 2022). Despite the rapid expansion of olive 
plantations, achieving high fruit yield and oil quality remains a major challenge due to suboptimal orchard management 
practices, particularly nutrient management. Balanced fertilization is essential for optimizing olive tree growth, canopy 
development, flowering, fruit set, yield, and oil synthesis. Adequate nutrient su pply not only enhances fruit productivity but 
also influences oil content and fatty acid composition, which are critical quality parameters for olive oil production. 
Among essential nutrients, nitrogen is frequently considered the most limiting element in olive orchards because of its central 
role in vegetative growth, leaf development, photosynthetic activity, and fruit production. However, excessive nitrogen 
application may adversely affect oil quality and reduce production efficiency (Jan et al., 2021). Potassium is equally important, 
particularly for fruit development, oil accumulation, osmotic regulation, and drought tolerance under water-scarce conditions 
(Rohi et al., 2023). Recent studies have demonstrated that integrated nutrient management approaches, including the combined 
use of soil-applied NPK fertilizers and foliar applications of potassium and boron, can significantly improve fruit yield and oil 
quality in olive orchards (Çolak et al., 2024). Likewise, fertilizer application at critical growth stages has been reported to 
improve nutrient-use efficiency and stabilize productivity under Mediterranean-like environments (Iqbal et al., 2022). 
The effectiveness of fertilization practices, however, is strongly influenced by soil and environmental cond itions. The soils of 
the Pothwar Plateau are predominantly alkaline, calcareous, low in organic matter, and frequently deficient in essential plant 
nutrients (Shaheen, 2016). High soil pH, calcium carbonate content, moisture limitations, and nutrient inter actions often 
restrict nutrient availability and uptake by olive trees. Consequently, fertilizer recommendations developed for Mediterranean 
regions may not be directly applicable to the edaphic and climatic conditions of Pothwar. Furthermore, many local g rowers 
apply fertilizers without prior soil or leaf analysis, leading to imbalanced nutrient use, reduced fertilizer efficiency, and 
inconsistent yields (Iqbal et al., 2022; Khan et al., 2022). 
Although considerable research has been conducted on olive nut rition in traditional olive -growing countries, information 
regarding the optimum nutrient requirements of olive under the alkaline, calcareous, and rainfed conditions of the Pothwar 
Plateau remains limited. In particular, field -based evaluations of differe nt nitrogen and phosphorus combinations for 
maximizing fruit yield and oil production in commercially cultivated olive varieties are scarce. The lack of region -specific 
fertilizer recommendations represents a significant knowledge gap that limits the produ ctive potential of olive orchards in 
Pakistan. 
Therefore, the present study was conducted to evaluate the response of olive trees to different nitrogen and phosphorus 
fertilization regimes under Pothwar conditions and to identify the optimum nutrient combi nation for maximizing fruit yield 
and oil production. The findings are expected to provide scientifically validated fertilizer recommendations that will enhanc e 
nutrient-use efficiency, improve orchard productivity, and support the sustainable expansion of Pakistan’s emerging olive 
industry. 
Materal And Methods 
Site selection and Experimental Design 
Field experiments were conducted for five consecutive years during 2020-21 to 2024-25 at Chakwal, Pothwar region of Pakistan. 
The climatic conditions of the re gion are semi -arid, heavily influenced by monsoon rainfall, mostly characterized by hot 
summers and cool winters. The soil texture of the experimental site ranges from sandy loam to loam. Soils are non-saline with 
low organic matter. Trials were executed on permanent layout following randomized complete block design (RCBD) consisting 
of total six treatments including, T 1 (400-200-300) NPK g/plant, T 2 (500-200-300) NPK g/plant, T 3 (600-200-300) NPK 
g/plant, T 4 (400-300-300) NPK g/plant, T 5 (500-300-300) NPK g/plant, and T 6 (600-300-300)NPK g/plant with three 
replications (Table 1).

Fertilizer application, Data collection and analysis 
Olive plants of 10 years old age were selected for the experiment. Total 36 plants were selected with two plants in each 
treatment. Fertilizer application was completed according to treatment plan, all phosphorus, ⅟3 nitrogen and ½ potash was 
applied in first week of March before flowering, ⅟3 nitrogen in mid-April after fruit formation and ⅟3 nitrogen and ½ potash was 
applied in the end of August. Plants were closely monitored during the whole period. During the month of October, olive fruits 
were picked from each tree after ripening, yield parameters including fruit weight/tree, average weight/fruit and oil content of 
fruit were recorded for each treatment. 
Soil analysis 
To record the changes in soil chemical characteristics and fertility dynamics, soil samples (0-15 and 15-30 cm) were collected 
in February every year before start of fertilization. The analysis was done by using standard analytical protocols at Soil a nd 
Water Testing Laboratory, Rawalpindi. Soil text ural class was determined by hydrometer method (Gee and Bauder, 1986), 
available phosphorus was measured on spectrophotometer using Olsen’s method (Olsen et al., 1982), and potassium was 
determined with flame photometer by ammonium acetate extraction (Ryan et al., 2001; Estefan et al., 2013). Soil organic matter 
was determined by using Walkley and Black titration method (Nelson and Sommers, 1996). 
Data analysis 
The data of all years was compiled and analyzed statistically using statistix 8.1 software. Treat ment means were compared 
using the least significant difference (LSD) test at a 5% level of significance. The economic analysis of the olive yield data was 
also done to compare the cost of different fertilizer doses and find the best fertilizer recommendation. 
Results 
Effect of variable rates of NPK on soil chemical characteristics 
Soil samples collected annually after fruit harvesting revealed noticeable variations in soil chemical properties across the study 
period (2020–21 to 2024 –25) (Table 2). Overall , fruit production resulted in nutrient removal from the soil; however, the 
continuous application of balanced fertilizers combined with pruning residues returned to the orchard floor helped sustain soil 
fertility and prevented severe nutrient depletion. 
Electrical conductivity (EC) remained within the non -saline range throughout the study, varying from 0.90 to 1.81 dS m⁻¹. A 
gradual increase in EC was observed over the years, particularly during 2023 –24, which may be attributed to the cumulative 
effect of fertilizer additions and nutrient cycling within the orchard system. Nevertheless, EC values remained below the 
threshold considered detrimental for olive growth, indicating that fertilizer management practices did not induce salinity 
problems. 
Soil pH remained moderately alkaline throughout the experimental period, ranging from 7.31 to 8.03. Minor fluctuations among 
treatments and years were observed, but no consistent trend of acidification or alkalization occurred. The relatively stable pH 
suggests that fertilizer applications and orchard management practices maintained a favorable chemical environment for 
nutrient availability and root activity. 
Soil organic matter (OM) exhibited considerable temporal variation. Initial OM contents ranged from 0.57 to 0. 85% during 
2020–21, declined in several treatments during 2021 –22 and 2023 –24, and subsequently increased during 2024 –25. The 
recovery of OM in the final year, particularly under T2, T3, T5, and T6 (0.75 –0.78%), indicates the beneficial contribution of 
leaf litter, pruned biomass decomposition, and root turnover to soil carbon accumulation. These results suggest that orchard 
residue recycling partially compensated for organic matter losses associated with crop production.

Available phosphorus (P₂O₅) showed substantial year-to-year fluctuations, ranging from 6.13 to 11.30 mg kg⁻¹. The highest 
phosphorus concentration was recorded in T3 during 2020–21 (11.30 mg kg⁻¹), whereas the lowest value was observed in T5 
during 2022–23 (6.13 mg kg⁻¹). Despite annual nut rient removal through fruit harvest, phosphorus levels were generally 
maintained within a narrow range across treatments, indicating that fertilizer applications replenished a significant proportion 
of the phosphorus removed by the crop. 
Exchangeable potassium (K₂O) varied considerably during the study period. Potassium levels declined after the initial years, 
reflecting substantial uptake by olive trees during fruit development. However, a marked increase was recorded during 2024–
25, when K₂O concentrations reached 108–117 mg kg⁻¹ across treatments. The highest potassium content was observed in T6 
(117 mg kg⁻¹), followed by T3 (112 mg kg⁻¹) and T5 (111 mg kg⁻¹). This improvement suggests that continued fertilizer 
application and nutrient recycling from orchard residues effectively restored soil potassium reserves. 
Overall, the findings demonstrate that although fruit production continuously removes nutrients from the soil, long -term 
application of balanced NPK fertilizers together with the incorporation of pruned biomass and leaf litter can maintain soil 
fertility, stabilize soil chemical properties, and support sustainable olive production. Among the treatments, T6 consistentl y 
maintained comparatively higher soil nutrient reserves in the later years of the study, indicating a greater capacity to sustain 
soil fertility under continuous orchard production.

increasing doses of NPK. Graded doses of NPK significantly (≤0.05) influenced the olive fruit yield.

Effect of variable rates of NPK on Olive fruit quality 
The data of average fruit weight showed a steady increase with the increasing levels of NPK concluding that experimental 
treatment positively influenced the quality of fruit. However maximum fruit weight was obtained with the application of NPK 
@600-300-300 kg/ha in T6. Elevating the level of P from 200 g/plant to 300 g/plant significantly boosted olive yield. These 
findings are in line with previous studies by Haberman et al., 2021. 
As the experiment was continued for five consecutive years, in the first year of experiment there was 11% increase in fruit 
quality as average fruit weight was increased from 1.268 g in T1 to 1.413 g in T6. Although, a miner reduction in fruit size was 
observed in the subsequent year across all treatments, with values ranging from 1.021 to 1.291 g, however no effect was observed 
on overall yield. Maximum fruit size was picked during the year 2023-24. These yearly variation may be attributed to different 
rainfall pattern in each year.

Effect of variable rates of NPK on olive fruit oil contents 
Oil recovery from olive plants was significantly improved by increasing the dose of P, as 16% increase in oil content was 
observed in T6 when compared with T 3. Raising the sole nitrogen dose caused negligible impact on fruit oi l content, however 
elevating the level of P significantly enhanced the oil recovery. The pooled data of long term study provided further evidences 
in the yield enhancement of olive plants with increasing levels of N and P. Tadayon et al., 2025 reported tha t application of 
balanced fertilization improved nutritional value, yield, and oil quality of olive fruit. 
Maximum fruit oil content was observed with the application of NPK @600-300-300 g/plant. The experiment was concluded 
in 2024-25 by determining the b est fertilizer recommendation for olive plants of age 10 years, giving maximum fruit oil 
recovery. Pairwise comparison of treatment means using Least Significant Difference (LSD) test also revealed significant 
difference in treatments means of different doses of N and P.

Discussion 
The present study was carried out to determine the nutrient requirements of olive plants grown under semi -arid climatic 
conditions of pothwar region of Punjab. The results revealed that application of each nutrient significantly influenced all 
recorded yield parameters including average fruit weight, fruit yield per tree and oil content. Abdulqader and Shaymaa, 2024 
also reported the similar trends. Application of different levels of nitrogen to olive plants caused significant variation in fruit 
yield in response to different nutrient levels. Maximum fruit yield was obtained by increasing the level of nitrogen from 400 to 
600 g/plant which may be attributed to enhanced number of healthy and functional flower clusters. As reported by Silva et al., 
2023 the boosted fruit yield may also be due to enhanced rate of photosynthesis, as nitrogen is a vital component of chlorophyll. 
Kotsias et al., 2023 and Pappas et al., 2023 also narrated the similar trends. 
Application of phosphorus also contributed towards yield improvement of olive plants. As raising the level of phosphorus along 
with nitrogen caused more elevated yield as compared to sole increase in the nitrogen rate. According to Haberman et al., 2021 
increase in yield may be supported by boosted rate of cell division, tissue development, inflorescence initiation, flower formation 
and ultimately developing into higher pulp to stone ratio fruit. These evidences have also been reviewed by Jan et al., 2021 
through several studies. 
Potassium plays a real support system for plants in osmoregulation and stomatal control, as narrated by Colak et al., 2024. Rohi 
et al., 2023 and Gowda et al., 2022 also reported the role of potassium in improving the fruit size and quality of fruit. Potassium 
also plays critical role in fatty acid synthesis which indirectly improves both oil content and overall quality of fruit pulp. Similar 
studies have also been reviewed by Isaac et al., 2020 stating that application of balanced fertilization increased fruit set and 
reduced fruit drop. Improvement in all yield parameters was observed with the application of NPK @ 600-300-300 gram per 
plants suggesting it an optimum recommended dose for the study area. On overall basis the increase in all yield components is 
supported by enhanced photosynthetic activity, fatty acid synthesis and improved sink strength due to sufficient supply of each 
essential nutrient as mentioned by Jan et al., 2021, Zipori et al., 2020 and Ferreira et al., 2020.

Conclusion 
This study evaluated the response of olive trees to different nitrogen and phosphorus fertilization regimes under the agro -
climatic conditions of the Pothwar region. The results demonstrated that graded applications of NPK fertilizers significantly 
influenced fruit yield and oil content, highlighting the importance of balanced nutrient management for olive production. 
Among the evaluated treatments, the application of NPK at 600 -300-300 g tree⁻¹ (T6) produced the highest fruit yield, fruit 
weight, and oil content, with differences confirmed as statistically significant by the LSD test at the 5% probability level. 
The findings indicate that adequate and balanced supplies of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium are essential for maximizing 
olive productivity and improving fruit quality under the alkaline and calcareous soil conditions of the Pothwar Plateau. Based 
on the results of this st udy, the application of NPK at 600 -300-300 g tree⁻¹ can be recommended as an effective fertilization 
strategy for enhancing yield and oil production in olive orchards of the region. 
Furthermore, the study emphasizes that nutrient application at appropriate growth stages is critical for improving nutrient -
use efficiency and sustaining orchard productivity. Balanced fertilization not only increases fruit yield but also contribute s to 
enhance oil content, thereby improving the economic value of olive cultivation. Nevertheless, the present findings are limited 
to the conditions of this study and the evaluated cultivar. Future research should investigate the long -term effects of this 
fertilization regime on soil fertility, nutrient dynamics, and environmental sus tainability, as well as validate its effectiveness 
across different olive cultivars, orchard ages, and agroecological conditions.
