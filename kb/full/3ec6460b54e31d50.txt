Abstract Steel surface defect segmentation requires dense annotations and adapts poorly to rare or newly emerging defects. This paper proposes DFPIFormer, a few-shot segmentation framework that introduces wavelet-based dual-frequency prototype learning. Instead of compressing all foreground cues into a single spatial prototype, DFPIFormer separates low-frequency structural information and high-frequency detail information to reduce prototype frequency entanglement. A response-guided lightweight cross-attention module further adapts the dual-frequency prototypes to each query image, while a noise-aware spatial-frequency decoder uses high-frequency responses as candidate evidence rather than independent decisions.
