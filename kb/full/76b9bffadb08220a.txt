Abstract Background Universal school meal programs are expanding in Canada, but evidence remains limited on how they affect lunch-period consumption. We assessed whether changes in lunch-period energy and nutrient intake, contributions to Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs), and nutrient density differed between intervention and control schools after implementation of a universal school lunch program. Methods This quasi-experimental controlled before-and-after study used repeated cross-sectional samples from four elementary schools in Saskatoon, Canada. Two schools implemented a universal free lunch program and two served as controls.
