Abstract Enteric viruses are major causes of food- and waterborne diseases. Due to the limited cultivability of many human enteric viruses, surrogate viruses are commonly used to model their environmental persistence. This study aimed to evaluate the not-well-understood long-term survival of murine norovirus (MNV), rhesus rotavirus (RRV), and porcine sapovirus (PoSaV), used here as cultivable surrogate viruses for human enteric viruses, in bottled drinking water. Bottled water artificially inoculated with each virus was stored at 4 °C, 25 °C, and 37 °C for up to 365 days, and viral infectivity was quantified using TCID 50 and plaque assays, with the latter successfully applied for PoSaV quantification.
