Abstract
Understanding tropical lake ecosystem responses to anthropogenic disturbance over long timescales is critical for assessing 
resistance, resilience, and regime shifts; however, such perspectives are rare in the Neotropics. We combined paleolimno-
logical, limnological, and remote-sensing approaches to evaluate the response of a shallow tropical maar lake in the Sierra 
de Los Tuxtlas (eastern Mexico) to deforestation-driven disturbance over the last ~200 years, with emphasis on ecosystem 
trajectories following peak impact in the late twentieth century. Forest-cover changes were reconstructed from remote images 
(1976, 2000, 2024), while lake condition was assessed using limnological measurements and diatom assemblages from the 
water column (2000–2024) and sediments (~1808–2025). Sedimentary magnetic susceptibility and diatoms documented 
a major state shift during the 1970s–1980s, coincident with peak deforestation and erosion, characterized by high species 
turnover and the dominance of disturbance-tolerant taxa (Achnanthidium minutissimum– Fragilaria capucina). This transi-
tion contrasts with gradual and elastic responses observed during earlier periods. Despite partial reforestation and landscape 
stabilization since the 1990s, limnological variables indicate persistent eutrophic to hypereutrophic conditions. However, 
sedimentary and water-column diatoms revealed ongoing ecological changes, marked by declines in disturbance taxa and 
increasing abundances of baseline planktonic species (Aulacoseira spp.), indicating an elastic recovery trajectory toward 
pre-disturbance conditions. Comparisons with an earlier disturbance between AD 100–800 (Classic Mesoamerican period) 
highlight the importance of disturbance duration and climatic context in determining ecosystem resilience. Our results 
demonstrate long-term resilience in a tropical lake following intense anthropogenic disturbance, underscoring the value of 
paleolimnological records for evaluating ecosystem recovery.
Keywords Deforestation · Human impact · Paleolimnology · Tropical lakes · Ecological resilience
Introduction
One of the most relevant questions in ecology and envi-
ronmental management is to understand how ecosystems 
respond to disturbance over time, whether they persist 
(resistance) or transition toward new regimes or states, 
and whether they eventually return to their baseline or ref-
erence conditions once the disturbance has ceased (resil-
ience) (Lake 2013). These transitions can be gradual (lin-
ear, with low species turnover) or abrupt (threshold, with 
high species turnover), while elastic responses occur when 
the ecosystem recovers to its previous baseline condition 
(Lake 2013; Van Meerbeek et al. 2021). A basic step toward 
understanding these processes is to document the evolution 
of disturbed or restored ecosystems over a sufficiently long 
time interval. Most perturbation and/or restoration studies 
are limited by the timeframe of the observations that can

represent valuable models for studying ecosystem perturba-
tion because they are among the most sensitive ecosystems 
to human-induced disturbances and are isolated systems 
where changes can be relatively easily quantified (Spears 
et al. 2017; Whittaker et al. 2017). Furthermore, the anal-
ysis of lake sediments offers an alternative for long-term 
lake monitoring, as paleolimnological methods represent 
an exceptional tool for the reconstruction of lake charac-
teristics over long periods of time, allowing for the identi-
fication of lake conditions prior to perturbation (reference 
conditions) and to follow up lake responses through time 
for periods that exceed most monitoring programs (Bat-
tarbee 2000; Smol 2009). This approach is particularly rel-
evant in regions where lake monitoring is not an established 
practice or for time intervals that predate lake monitoring 
programs; for example, paleolimnological studies in Lake 
Ontario, the easternmost of the Laurentian Great Lakes at 
the Canada–USA border, and its long-term eutrophication 
history still to be identified (Pillsbury et al. 2021). Forest 
clearance and increased phosphorous loading to the lake 
caused eutrophication, with a shift from its baseline condi-
tions during the 1920s, favoring high phosphorus tolerant 
taxa. This impacted condition lasted until the 1980s, when 
measures were implemented to reduce phosphorous load-
ings and the phytoplankton community partially recovered. 
Similarly, in Lake Peñasquito, a Neotropical, karstic lake in 
southern Mexico, paleolimnological studies allowed degra-
dation and eutrophication processes associated with expand-
ing agricultural activities during the 1970s to be identified. 
In this case, the degradation process accelerated in the early 
2000s, leading to an abrupt change in the lake conditions, 
with a high species turnover that favored taxa tolerant to 
higher turbidity and nutrient levels (Caballero et al. 2025).
Neotropical ecosystems are some of the most biodiverse 
in the world; however, they have been severely affected by 
deforestation, particularly since the 1970s when regional 
economic development was frequently associated with 
forest clearing and conversion to pastures or farmlands 
(Bianchi and Haig 2013; Guevara and Laborde 2012; Rosa 
et al. 2016). The Sierra de los Tuxtlas (STUX) in eastern 
Mexico (Fig.  1) is a good example of how deforestation 
has altered Neotropical ecosystems. The STUX has been 
severely affected by deforestation and landscape fragmenta-
tion, losing about 40% of its forested area during the 1970s 
and 1980s (Guevara et al. 2004; Von Thaden et al. 2020). 
Even though deforested areas are frequently abandoned after 
a few years, allowing for reforestation processes, until the 
1990s, deforestation in the region occurred at much higher 
rates than reforestation (Guevara et al. 2004). Due to its bio-
logical relevance, in 1998, a large part of the STUX was 
declared a protected natural reserve (Los Tuxtlas Biosphere 
Reserve (Semarnat-Conanp 2006)), and some actions were 
implemented to reduce deforestation in the region, albeit 
with limited long-term success (Von Thaden et al. 2018).
Deforestation and erosion in the Neotropics impact 
aquatic ecosystems, as they lead to an increase in sedi-
ment influx, higher turbidity, and eutrophication (Callisto 
et al. 2014). In the STUX, many lakes have been impacted 
by deforestation and are now eutrophic to hypertrophic 
(Vázquez and Caballero 2013; Vázquez et al. 2004). How -
ever, the previous disturbance history of these lakes or their 
baseline or reference conditions before the modern defor -
estation process is mostly unknown. One exception is the 
maar lake Lago Verde, which offers a unique window into 
the long-term memory of disturbance in the Neotropics. 
In this lake, a paleolimnological study was conducted on 
a sediment core retrieved in 2001 (Caballero et al. 2006; 
Lozano-García et al. 2010; Ortega-Guerrero et al. 2006). 
This work showed a nearly 2000-year record of lake his-
tory in which two periods of disturbance associated with 
high erosion rates, deforestation, and eutrophication were 
identified; the first occurring during the Mesoamerican 
Classic period (AD 100 to 800) and the second associated 
with the modern deforestation process, which peaked during 
the 1970s and 1980s. In the early 1970s, the lake showed a 
sharp shift in diatom assemblages in response to high ero-
sion and remained in this impacted condition until the top 
of the sequence, dating to 2001. Nearly 25 years later, we 
returned to Lago Verde with the objective of assessing its 
present condition to evaluate whether it has remained stable 
in its impacted condition, transitioned to a different regime, 
or returned to its previous reference condition. We hypoth-
esized that landscape stabilization after peak disturbance 
and the establishment of the natural reserve in 1998 could 
have been associated with an elastic response in this lake, 
with its modern diatom community returning to the one 
present before the 1970s. To evaluate this, we used a novel 
approach in which we integrated limnology, paleolimnology, 
and remote-sensing tools. Diatom assemblages were used 
to evaluate lake response to the external forcing represented 
by land use change and erosion, while remote images and 
magnetic susceptibility were used as a tool for quantifying 
these external forgings, therefore bridging the gap between 
aquatic and terrestrial dynamics.
Site description
The STUX is a volcanic field in the Neotropics of east-
ern Mexico; it is an area of great ecological relevance 
because of its high biodiversity (Von Thaden et al. 2020), 
as it holds one of the last remnants of tropical evergreen 
rainforest in Mexico (Guevara and Laborde 2012). The 
STUX has two main volcanoes (San Martín Tuxtla and 
Santa Marta) and at least 368 monogenetic vents (Sieron

et al. 2023, 2021). Most of these monogenetic vents are 
cinder cones, but they also include maar craters, of which 
at least 13 contain a lake. Lago Verde (18° 36′ 43″ N, 95° 
20′ 52″ W, 149 m asl) is a maar lake on the western flanks 
of the San Martín Tuxtla volcano, on the STUX, outside of 
the limits of the Los Tuxtlas Biosphere Reserve (Fig.  1). 
The region has a mean annual temperature of 25 °C and a 
mean annual precipitation of 2500 mm year−1. The lake is 
nearly circular, approximately 380 m in diameter, and has 
a maximum depth of 4 m (Vázquez et al. 2004). It lies in 
a small basin (0.45 km2), with steep rims on the north and 
western shores, and an elongated southeastern catchment 
drained by a small rivulet. In 2000, the lake was hyper -
trophic, characterized by turbid waters (Secchi disk depth 
= 0.4 ± 0.2 m) and a high chlorophyll a concentration (153 
± 55 mg  m−3). Grasslands for cattle ranching dominate 
the vegetation cover in this small basin, with conserved 
patches of secondary tropical trees (mainly Bursera sima-
ruba, Cecropia obtusifolia, Myriocarpa longipes, and 
Pachira aquatica).
Methods
Our methodological approach was to evaluate deforesta-
tion around the lake by analyzing remote images from dif -
ferent time windows from 1976 to 2024, and to contrast 
this information with limnological and paleolimnological 
data. Magnetic susceptibility was used as a physical proxy 
to evaluate sediment delivery to the lake in both modern 
(2023–2024) and historical (last ~200 years) sediment 
samples. The current lake conditions and diatom assem-
blages were studied during two annual cycles in 2023 and 
2024, and contrasted with equivalent measurements taken 
in 2000 and 2003 (Vázquez and Caballero 2013; Vázquez 
et al. 2004), as well as with diatom assemblages from sedi -
ments dated to the last ~200 years (Caballero et al. 2006). 
Changes in diatom assemblages were used as indicators 
of lake response to external forcing; in this case, sedi-
ment delivery that changed lake transparency and nutrient 
loadings.

Forest cover analysis
Remote images were analyzed to assess deforestation around 
Lago Verde, which has been the main driver of environmen-
tal change for this lake. Changes in forest cover over the last 
50 years were analyzed using aerial photographs from 1976 
and 2000 (INEGI 2024; https:// www. inegi. org. mx/ app/ geo2/ 
siiv/) and a high-resolution satellite image from 2024 (Planet 
Team 2022). It was not possible to locate aerial photographs 
dating to an earlier period. Land cover was classified into 
three major categories: forest, non-forest, and water bod-
ies. All imagery was processed using the maximum likeli-
hood algorithm in ArcGIS Pro 3.5.4 (ESRI 2025, https:// 
www. esri. com). The classification results were compared 
to quantify changes in forest cover over time and to identify 
transition patterns between categories.
Limnology
The lake was sampled during two annual cycles (May, Sep-
tember, and January) from May 2023 to January 2025. The 
samples collected in May 2023, September 2023, and Janu-
ary 2024 will be referred to as the 2023 annual cycle, while 
those collected in May 2024, September 2024, and January 
2025 correspond to the 2024 annual cycle. The selected sam-
pling months align with the strategy followed in the previ-
ous 2000 annual cycle, and they characterize the seasonal 
variation in the region; May represents the warmest season, 
September the wettest, and January the coldest. In situ lim-
nological parameters were measured at two locations near 
the center of the lake, which were the same as those sam-
pled in the 2000 study. Samples were collected from the 
surface (0.5 m) and bottom (3–3.5 m) using a Van Dorn 
bottle. Measurements of some variables were taken in situ 
on the surface and bottom using a multiparametric probe 
(Hydrolab DS5). These measurements included water tem-
perature, electric conductivity (EC), and dissolved oxygen. 
Secchi disk visibility (ZSD) was also determined, and surface 
samples for water quality analyses were collected on each 
occasion. Water quality analysis included major ion determi-
nations (carbonates, chlorides, sulphates, Na+, K+, Ca2+, and 
 Mg2+), chlorophyll a (Chla), total phosphorus (TP), soluble 
reactive phosphorus (SRP), ammonium, nitrite, and nitrate. 
Total dissolved nitrogen (DIN) was estimated by adding 
ammonium, nitrite, and nitrate determinations. The samples 
were refrigerated until analyzed using standard methodolo-
gies (American Public Health Association (APHA) et al. 
2005). Samples for cation determinations were acidified with 
 HNO3. Samples for Chla determinations were filtered (What-
man GF/C filters), Chla was extracted with 90% methanol, 
and the extracts were measured spectrophotometrically; 
concentrations were expressed as mg m−3. Samples for 
ammonium and nitrate were acidified with H2SO4. Nutrients 
were determined in a Thermo Scientific GENESYS 20 vis-
ible spectrophotometer (ammonium by Nessler’s method, 
nitrite by diazotization, nitrate by the brucine colorimetric 
method, TP by persulfate digestion, SRP by the ascorbic acid 
method) (APHA et al. 2005).
The trophic level index (TLI) based on Chla and DIN 
concentrations was used to evaluate the changes in trophic 
conditions of Lago Verde. This index was selected because 
unlike other indexes that are based on Carlson’s Trophic 
state index (Carlson 1977), this one was developed for lakes 
with high phosphorous concentrations, where nitrogen 
becomes the limiting element, as in Lago Verde (Kratzer 
and Brezonik 1981; Paulic et al. 1996). The TLI was esti-
mated for the 2023–2024 annual cycle samples, and also for 
the previously published data collected in 2000 (January, 
May, and September) and July 2003 (Vázquez and Cabal-
lero 2013; Vázquez et al. 2004). The LSI limits used were:

Diatom analyses
Diatoms are widely used as bioindicators in modern and 
paleolimnological studies because they meet the following 
criteria: (i) their populations change rapidly in response to 
environmental changes due to their short life cycles, (ii) 
their species have well-documented ecological niches, and 
(iii) their siliceous remains (valves) allow for an accurate 
taxonomic identification (Battarbee et al. 2001; Caballero 
and Maidana 2017; Smol and Cumming 2000). Further -
more, after dying, the siliceous diatom valves are deposited 
in the sediments where they are preserved; therefore they 
are among the few biological groups that can be sampled 
and analyzed identically in both modern and old (archive) 
sediments. In the context of this work, this is particularly 
relevant as the past diatom assemblages provided the nec-
essary historical context for interpretation of the modern 
assemblages collected in water and sediment samples.

Diatoms in surface sediments
Surface sediment samples for diatom analysis and magnetic 
susceptibility measurements were collected from the cen -
tral part of the lake using an Ekman dredge on the same 
dates as the previous samples. Sediment samples for diatom 
analysis were dried, and 0.2 g of dry sediment was treated 
with HCl (10%) and H2O2 (30%) to eliminate carbonates and 
organic matter (Battarbee 1986; Schrader 1973). Slides were 
prepared with 200 µl of the clean suspension and mounted 
with Naphrax. Diatom counts of at least 400 valves were 
undertaken using an Olympus BX50 microscope at 1000× 
magnification. Diatom species were identified using special-
ized literature (Krammer and Lange-Bertalot, 1986; Spauld-
ing et al. 2021). Results were expressed as species relative 
abundances (%).
Magnetic susceptibility of surface sediment samples
Magnetic susceptibility was measured in the same sediment 
samples collected in September 2023 and September 2024 
for diatom analysis, which allowed for a direct correlation 
between physical (magnetic susceptibility) and biological 
(diatoms) variables. Magnetic susceptibility is a physical 
parameter that responds to the entrance of allochthonous 
volcanic sediment from the surrounding slopes, and can be 
used as a proxy for sediment delivery to the lake (Michaud 
et al. 2006; Thompson et al. 1975). The same methodol-
ogy was used for the core sediment samples in our previous 
work (Ortega-Guerrero et al. 2006). For this purpose, 2 cm 
side cubes were filled with sediment, and their net weight 
(A&D HF2000G balance) and bulk density (g cm−3) were 
determined. Magnetic susceptibility was measured using a 
Bartington MS2B system, and the mass-normalized mag-
netic susceptibility ( χ) was determined by dividing by the 
sediment density; the results were expressed as 10–6 m3  kg−1.
Statistical analysis
Annual averages and standard deviations of EC, DIN, TP, 
ZSD, Chla, and LTSI were estimated. Their values were com-
pared with those previously reported for the 2000 annual 
cycle, when samples were collected on similar dates (Janu-
ary, May, and September) and with the single measurements 
made in July 2003, using a one-way analysis of variance 
(ANOVA). This test was performed in paleontological sta-
tistics (PAST) software (Hammer et al. 2001).
To identify trends in the diatom assemblages in the water 
samples and the environmental variables (EC, temperature, 
TP, Chla, DIN, pH, and ZSD), a canonical correspondence 
analysis (CCA) was performed (ter Braak 1986), and a series 
of partial CCAs were also run using single environmental 
variables to evaluate their importance in explaining samples 
and species distribution. The long-term trends in the diatom 
assemblages in sediment samples from Lago Verde were vis-
ualized using a detrended correspondence analyses (DCA) to 
measure the distance of the 2023 and 2024 samples to those 
previously studied (Correa-Metrio et al. 2014). These analy-
ses were run with the Vegan package in R (R Development 
Core Team 2020) using the square root transformation of the 
environmental variables and diatom densities (water sam-
ples), and species relative abundances (sediment samples). 
For the water column samples, analysis included the six 
samples corresponding to the 2023 and 2024 annual cycles, 
and the previously published samples collected in July 2003 
(Vázquez and Caballero 2013) and in 2000 (January, May, 
and September), providing a 25-year long perspective. For 
the sediment samples, analysis included the six samples 
corresponding to the 2023 and 2024 annual cycles, the pre-
viously published sample collected in July 2003 (Vázquez 
and Caballero 2013), and the top 60 cm (10 samples) of the 
210Pb-dated core sequence collected in 2001, with estimated 
ages from ~1808 to 2001 (Caballero et al. 2006), providing 
an ~200-year perspective of lake evolution. The scores of a 
DCA ordination represent standard deviations of ecological 
turnover (Correa-Metrio et al. 2014; Gauch 1982). There-
fore, the distance between samples in the ecological space 
defined by the first two axes of the DCA gives an accurate 
perspective of the species turnover between samples, consid-
ering that ~50% ecological turnover is represented by each 
unit. However, to obtain a more accurate estimate of species 
turnover between samples, the Bray–Curtis dissimilarity was 
also estimated for water and sediment samples. This metric 
varies between 0 (no species turnover) and 1 (100% or total 
species turnover).
Results
Forest cover
Forest cover in the 1 km buffer zone around Lago Verde 
was already low in 1976 (Fig.  2a, b), occupying ~17% of 
the area (73.5 ha), while non-forested land dominated the 
landscape (Table  1). Forest cover continued to decline to

12% (55 ha) by 2000 (Fig. 2c, d), indicating continued land-
use changes during the late twentieth century. Nevertheless, 
in 2024 (Fig.  2e, f) forest cover increased slightly to 18% 
(80 ha), indicating partial vegetation recovery, while non-
forested areas reduced from 84.2% in 2000 to 79.3%. These 
shifts may reflect recent declines in agricultural and graz-
ing activities and a gradual expansion of vegetation over 
abandoned lands. Further, the lake surface decreased from 
~15 ha in 1976 to ~11 ha in 2000. This reduction affected the 
southeastern sector of Lago Verde, where the small stream 
flows into the lake. The sediment delivery from this small 
inflowing stream has progressively clogged this part of the 
lake, contributing to its present configuration.
Remote images of this lake analyzed by Caballero et al. 
(2006) identified a reduction in forest cover occurring 
between 1980 and 1991. In these images, only the area cor-
responding to the lake’s hydrological basin was analyzed; 
however, the 1976 image shows the same forest cover within 
the lake basin as in 1980, while the 2000 image has the same 
forest cover within the lake basin as in 1991 (Electronic Sup-
plementary Material (ESM) Fig. 1). The new remote images 
analyzed in this work allowed for a more detailed timeline 
to be established of changes in the extent of the surrounding 
vegetation over the past five decades, demonstrating high 
deforestation since the 1970s as well as a progressive clog-
ging of the eastern part of the lake that reduced its surface 
during the 1990s.

Discussion
Lago Verde, in the STUX region of eastern Mexico, is an 
ideal site for studying aquatic ecosystem changes through 
time associated with forest clearance disturbance in the Neo-
tropics. The sediments from Lago Verde provide an excel -
lent record of land use changes (deforestation) through the 
magnetic susceptibility signal, while diatom associations 
reflect the lake’s internal dynamics and show state shifts 
occurring in the lake during the last ~200 years. This long-
term approach is further supported in this work by remote 
images covering the last ~50 years, and field data recording 
the lake’s trophic status and the diatom assemblages in water 
samples during the last 25 years (Fig. 8).
Remote images showed that by 1976, there was already 
an ongoing deforestation process that intensified during 
the 1980s, in agreement with historical information on 
high deforestation rates in the STUX during the 1970s and 
1980s (Guevara et al. 2004). The magnetic susceptibility 
record of Lago Verde showed high erosion rates affecting 
this lake between ~1968 and ~1988 (Ortega-Guerrero et al. 
2006), identifying the time of maximum disturbance. This 
disturbance was also related to progressive clogging of 
the eastern part of the lake that receives discharge from a 
stream, leading to a reduction in the lake’s surface during 
the 1990s. After peak disturbance, the magnetic susceptibil-
ity decreased while forest cover around the lake remained 
relatively stable during the late 1990s, with reforestation 
occurring during the last two decades. The pattern of accel-
erated deforestation during the 1970s with a later slowdown 
during the 2000s is common to other important Neotropical 
regions such as the Amazon Basin (Rosa et al. 2016). How-
ever, despite the recovery in the forest cover around the lake, 
field measurements showed that the lake remained turbid, 
albeit with significantly lower nitrogen levels. This reduc-
tion in DIN was the main environmental factor associated

with the observed changes in diatom species over the last 
25 years as the lake changed from hypertrophic to eutrophic. 
Furthermore, nitrogen concentrations in Lago Verde during 
2023 and 2024 were seasonally below the 0.15 mg L−1 star-
vation limit proposed by Reynolds (1999). Lower nitrogen 
levels can have cascading effects in the algal community of 
phosphorus rich lakes, favoring species that are more com-
petitive under low nitrogen conditions (Hayes et al. 2015).
The slowdown in deforestation during the 1990s and the 
recent reforestation trend provided an excellent opportunity 
to explore the response of a Neotropical lake ecosystem 
before, during, and after disturbance. Our results show that 
Lago Verde experienced gradual to elastic responses during 
periods of low deforestation pressure in the late nineteenth 
and early twentieth centuries (stage 1, Fig.  8), suggesting 
relatively low resistance to disturbance. Furthermore, lake 
conditions shifted over a relatively short time interval (pos-
sible threshold transition) when disturbance peaked in the 
1970s (stage 2, Fig.  8). This was the largest change in the 
lake documented in the last 200 years. However, it is diffi-
cult to establish whether this represented a threshold change 
since the time resolution between the analyzed core samples 
was around 4 years, even though this change could have hap-
pened in a much shorter time.
High species turnover was identified in the water samples 
diatom assemblages during the slowdown in deforestation 
occurring in the last 25 years (Fig.  8), with a nearly total 
reduction in the abundance of F. capucina and A. minutis-
simum. The diatom assemblages in sediment samples offer 
a more conservative perspective, with a partial turnover 
scenario during this same time, but with a similar pattern 
of decreasing values of F. capucina and A. minutissima, 
while Aulacoseria spp. increased. These differences between 
assemblage changes in the water and sediment samples can 
be explained because the sediments integrate information 
from a longer period (~6 months to 1 year), in contrast to 
the instantaneous information from the water samples. In 
both cases, however, the evidence points to an ongoing 
transition in lake conditions that involves a reduction in the 
disturbance taxa (e.g., A. minutissimum), while the baseline 
taxa (e.g., Aulacoseira spp.) are recovering (stage 3, Fig. 8). 
This is an ongoing elastic response (resilience), where the 
lake has reached a new condition (Aulacoseria spp. and F. 
capucina) that is closer to its baseline. According to this 
interpretation, our initial hypothesis was partially correct, 
as after 40 years of peak disturbance, the lake has reached a 
new state that is closer to its baseline condition (long-term 
resilience).
Long-term resilience processes such as the one observed 
in Lago Verde can be favored by geographic diversity, as 
species can disperse to adjacent regions where environmen-
tal conditions remain favorable (i.e., better climate or lower 
human impact) and then function as a dispersion source for 
repopulation after disturbance, thereby acting as metacom-
munity units (Benito et al. 2018 ). In this way, the intricate 
topography of central Mexico represents a favorable element 
for ecosystem resilience.
The long-term resilience pattern of change in Lago Verde, 
with a partial return to baseline conditions, is comparable 
to what was observed in Lake Ontario (easternmost of the 
Laurentian Great Lakes), where peak disturbance during 
the early twentieth century transformed the lake from its 
baseline condition to a eutrophic state where pollution toler-
ant diatom taxa proliferated. Thanks to pollution abatement 
programs, the lake recovered during the 1980s, lowering 
its phosphorous levels with a recovery of baseline diatom 
taxa, but also with the incorporation of small centric spe-
cies, which were absent before disturbance and which have 
been associated in the Laurentian Great Lakes with changing 
(warmer) climatic conditions (Pillsbury et al. 2021).
The Lago Verde paleolimnological sequence also 
recorded a previous disturbance event that offers the oppor-
tunity to compare the lake’s recent response (last 200 years) 
to disturbance in the more distant past (last 2000 years), and 
to evaluate another interesting question in ecology: whether 
ecosystem changes associated with human impact during 
the last century are unprecedented (Benito et al. 2022). In 
the STUX, humans have been present for several millen-
nia (Stoner 2017), and specifically around Lago Verde, the 
presence of Zea mays pollen attested for past agricultural 
activities (Lozano-García et al. 2010). The previous human 
disturbance event in Lago Verde occurred during the Classic 
period, between AD 100 and 800, and was also associated 
with deforestation, high erosion, and high magnetic suscepti-
bility (Lozano-García et al. 2010). During the Classic period 
disruption event, the lake responded in a way that was very 
similar to what happened in the 1970s, with a rapid shift in 
lake conditions toward an impacted state that was dominated 
by A. minutissimum. In this case, the baseline condition of 
the lake was defined by the dominance of Discostella stel-
ligera, a diatom species that disappeared from the record 
during its impacted interval (AD 100–800). However, in this 
case, the impacted condition persisted for nearly 600 years 
until agricultural activity, erosion, and magnetic suscep-
tibility declined around AD 800, and as climate changed 
toward wetter conditions and human populations in the 
region declined. At that time, the lake experienced another 
rapid state shift when baseline D. stelligera recovered, but 
only in low numbers, and a new arrival, Pseudostaurosira 
brevistriata, became dominant. The impacted state of Lago 
Verde during the Classic period represents a clear precedent 
of human-induced disturbance that was not only comparable 
in intensity to modern processes, but that also lasted longer 
than the modern one (~600 years versus ~30 years). Fur -
thermore, the partial recovery of baseline conditions after 
disturbance was similar to what happened in Lake Ontario,

and in both cases, it was associated with changing climatic 
conditions that precluded a full elastic response, as post-
impact conditions are different from those at baseline. This 
is of relevance for understanding contemporary responses 
to human impact, as modern ecosystems are also under the 
stress of a changing, global warming scenario. In the case of 
Lago Verde, F. capucina is the new arrival that established 
after peak disturbance, and which might be better adapted to 
modern climatic conditions that most likely differ from those 
during the early twentieth century. The lesson seems to be 
that changing climatic conditions limit ecosystem resilience, 
preventing recovery to baseline conditions after disturbance 
as environmental conditions (temperature, precipitation, 
urbanization, etc.) have also changed. Climate change can 
also limit the distribution of species, or even exclude them 
from the regional metacommunity, even in regions that have 
a great geographic diversity, further hindering ecosystem 
resilience.
Conclusions
Lago Verde provides a valuable long-term record of how 
Neotropical lake ecosystems respond to human disturbance 
and their subsequent recovery processes. The combination 
of approaches presented in this research, including remote 
images, limnology, and paleolimnology, was successful in 
providing evidence of the long-term evolution of the land-
scape and documenting different lake responses (resistance, 
regime-shift, resilience) to disturbance by anthropogenic 
stressors.
The results show that the lake ecosystem transitioned 
through three different stages during the last 200 years. 
Stage 1 represents the baseline condition and included 
gradual changes under low disturbance. Stage 2 represents 
a rapid state shift associated with peak disturbance during 
the 1970s. Stage 3 is an ongoing resilience phase associ-
ated with reduced land-use pressure, recent reforestation, 
and decreasing nitrogen concentrations. Although forest 
cover has partially recovered and nutrient concentrations 
have declined, the lake has not returned completely to its 
original baseline condition; instead, it has reached a new 
ecological state characterized by partial recovery of baseline 
taxa, with the incorporation of new species, showing partial 
long-term resilience.
In a longer time perspective (2000 years), the disturbed 
condition of the lake during the 1970s and 1980s had a 
precedent during the Classic period of human occupation 
of this basin (AD 100 to 800), during which a similar state 
of the lake was established, attesting that Mesoamerican 
cultures also had a significant impact on their environment, 
comparable to modern human-induced changes. Compar -
ing modern conditions with the lake’s conditions during 
the deforestation event of the Classic period highlights the 
importance of disturbance duration and climatic context for 
ecosystem resilience. Past and modern recovery phases indi-
cate that ecosystem resilience is constrained by changing 
environmental conditions, particularly climate variability. 
These results suggest that prolonged human disturbance 
under a changing climate can severely limit ecosystem resil-
ience, making Lago Verde an example relevant to the future 
of Neotropical aquatic ecosystems in the context of global 
warming and sustained land-use pressure. These findings 
emphasize the importance of combining long-term ecologi-
cal records with modern monitoring to better understand 
ecosystem trajectories and to inform conservation and res-
toration strategies in Neotropical regions.
