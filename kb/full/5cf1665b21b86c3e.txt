Irrigation water quality is conventionally reported as a set of separate indices: electrical conductivity, sodium adsorption ratio, soluble sodium percentage, residual sodium carbonate, Kelly's index, permeability index and magnesium adsorption ratio, each classified against its own limit. A water that is acceptable on every index taken singly may nevertheless be close to several limits at once, and that condition is invisible to the index-by-index reading. This study combines the seven indices into a single composite irrigation water quality index and converts the measured salinity into the extra water needed to keep the root zone below a target salinity, for ten observation wells in Durg block, Chhattisgarh.
