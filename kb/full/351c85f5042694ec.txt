Sediment microbial fuel cells (SMFCs) are emerging bioelectrochemical devices that simultaneously generate renewable energy and remediate contaminated aquatic environments. This chapter discusses the principles, mechanisms, and performance factors of SMFCs using sediments from Yamaguchi Bay and Tokyo Bay, Japan. The SMFCs produce bioelectricity through the activity of exoelectrogenic microbes that oxidize organic substances in anaerobic sediments. Marine sediments showed higher power output than freshwater sediments, with peak voltages around 300 mV under laboratory conditions. The SMFCs also demonstrated strong bioremediation potential, including up to a 90% reduction of acid volatile sulfides (AVS) within five weeks. System performance was influenced by sediment type, temperature, and electrode materials, with higher voltage generation in summer and carbon felt identified as an effective electrode material for marine SMFCs.
