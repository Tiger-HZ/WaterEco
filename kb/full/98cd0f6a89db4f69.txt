Abstract Habitat heterogeneity is a key driver of temporal and spatial variability of subtidal marine benthic biodiversity. However, this makes it a challenging environment in which to measure and quantify the factors driving biodiversity in a consistent manner. Current methodologies are either expensive, logistically challenging, require extensive technical knowledge, or are limited to shallow water environments. To overcome some of these limitations, a novel “SeaPen” device was designed as a cost‐effective, safe, easily deployable instrument that can provide repeatable and comparable survey results over the small spatial scales required to describe heterogeneous environments.
