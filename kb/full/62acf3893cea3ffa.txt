This paper examines the association between energy poverty indicators and agricultural productivity in five SAARC countries—Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka—over the period 2000–2023. Specifically, it investigates how access to clean cooking fuels, electricity, rural electricity, urban electricity, renewable energy consumption, renewable electricity output, and financial development are associated with agricultural output. Using panel-data estimation, with Feasible Generalized Least Squares (FGLS) as the primary estimator and OLS, Panel Corrected Standard Errors (PCSE), and Driscoll–Kraay Standard Errors (DKSE) as robustness checks, the results indicate that access to clean fuels, rural electricity, urban electricity, and renewable energy consumption are positively associated with agricultural productivity. In contrast, overall electricity access, renewable electricity output, and financial development show negative associations. The coefficient patterns remain broadly consistent across specifications. These findings suggest that the relationship between energy access and agricultural productivity varies across dimensions of energy poverty, and that aggregate access measures alone may not adequately reflect agriculturally relevant energy conditions. Given the small cross-sectional sample of five countries, the results should be interpreted as associational and with appropriate caution.
