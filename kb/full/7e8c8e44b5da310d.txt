Abstract Groundwater contributions to streamflow significantly influence the structure and function of riverine ecosystems, particularly in glacierized catchments where there are marked differences in water sources and subsurface flow paths. Here, we investigated spatial and temporal variation in relationships between water sources, flow paths, physical and chemical processes, organic matter, microbial biofilms, and macroinvertebrates across groundwater-fed streams in the glacierized Toklat River catchment of Denali National Park, Alaska. Streams fed predominantly by seepage from the valley sides were perennial, whereas streams sustained by glacial meltwater seepage were ephemeral.
