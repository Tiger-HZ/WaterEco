Surface water quality is a crucial determinant of environmental and public health, particularly in arid and semi-arid regions such as northwestern Morocco, where waterways like the Drader Valley support agriculture, ecosystems, and local communities. This study assessed seasonal variations in nitrate contamination and associated human health risks in surface water from three sampling sites (S1–S3) along the Drader River. Nitrate concentrations varied strongly by season, peaking during the dry season and exceeding permissible limits at S2 and S3. Health risk assessment using the Target Hazard Quotient (THQ) revealed values above the safety threshold (THQ &gt; 1) at S2 and S3, indicating potential non-carcinogenic risks for local residents.
