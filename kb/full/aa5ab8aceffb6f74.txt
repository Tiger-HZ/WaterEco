Abstract Water infrastructure projects in fragile and post-conflict states faced risks that conventional project management frameworks often failed to capture. This paper examined risk management and delay mitigation through the case of the Hargeisa Water Supply Expansion Project in Somaliland, a multi-phase, donor-funded initiative marked by schedule overruns, contractor insolvency, and institutional capacity gaps. Drawing on documentary evidence from 42 sources spanning three project phases, the paper identified principal risks: institutional fragility, contractor failures, financing irregularities, demographic pressures, environmental risks, and donor coordination failures.
